# Agency Comparison — Is Abomination Vault the Outlier?

**Question asked 2026-09-10, after S8:** *"Isn't making all enemies elite, and starving your players of hero points, basically just a meat grinder? I'm really questioning if I'm having fun."*

**Method:** every combat encounter across Pax's five active PF2e tables was extracted from the session recaps (plus Foundry roll logs where they exist) into the same schema — enemies and templates, rounds, outcome, Pax-PC damage share, downs, rounds lost, whether the PC's primary mechanic functioned, hero points, and decisive moments. 56 sessions, 175 encounter rows. This file is the comparison; the per-table extractions are the source.

| Table | DM | Pax's PC | Sessions | Encounters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Abomination Vault** | Lap | Argon (S1–4) → **Snagg** (S5–8) | 8 | 15 |
| Setting Sun | Cody | Tivael (S1–8) → **Sovael** (S9–14) | 14 | 66 |
| Revenge of the Runelords | Cody | **Sevek** | 6 | 19 |
| Hellbreakers | Kaazz | **Bai Jian** | 20 | 49 |
| Season of Ghosts | AndeGM | **Zhen** | 8 | 26 |

---

## The one-line takeaway

> **Every table gets hard. Only one table takes the character sheet away to do it.**

The metrics you'd naively reach for — downs, retreats, damage share — do **not** single out Abomination Vault. Setting Sun's Ruby Phoenix arc scores worse on all three. What singles AV out is *where the difficulty comes from*: it is the only table where the dominant reason a fight went badly was **the stat block nullifying the PC's kit**, rather than the dice, the positioning, or the party's own call.

That's the "hard hard vs fun hard" distinction, and it holds up under the numbers.

So the outcome numbers are measuring the wrong thing. If they were what mattered, you'd be questioning Setting Sun tonight, not AV. You aren't. The difference has to be somewhere else.

## 3. Where it actually is — what *kind* of denial

Every extraction produced a "denial inventory": each time the PC's primary mechanic was made irrelevant. Classified by cause:

| Denial type | Meaning | Has an in-fight answer? |
|---|---|---|
| **Immunity / resistance** | The stat block nullifies your effect type: immune grabbed, resist physical, incorporeal, regen only stopped by X | **No** — you needed a different key before the door |
| **Positional** | You can't reach it: flying, teleporting, reach 100, 30-ft fly vs 30-ft reach | Yes — mobility, ranged, positioning |
| **Control** | Paralyzed, stunned, Confused, Suggested — you don't get the turn | Sometimes — saves, allies |
| **Variance** | The die said no: nat-20 save, missed by 1, cover flat check | It's dice |
| **Ruling** | A DM call negated a success | Table conversation |

| | **AV** | Setting Sun | RotR | Hellbreakers | SoG |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Entries | 10 | 24 | 16 | 19 | 17 |
| **Immunity / resistance** | **7** | 3 | 2 | 6 → **answered at S10** (silver) | 3 |
| Positional | 0 | 3 | **7** | 1 | 0 |
| Control | 2 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| **Variance** | 0–1 | **~12** | 1 | 1 | **~12** |
| Ruling | 0 | 2 (Foundry non-applications) | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| **Dominant type** | **Immunity** | Variance | Positional | Immunity, *then solved* | Variance |

**AV is the only table where immunity is the dominant reason a fight went badly.**

- **Setting Sun** is variance-dominant: two rank-5 Thunderstrikes saved, Divine Wrath into a nat 20 and an 18, both Chain Lightnings saved by both worms, Aether Beam erased by a nat-20 Reflex. Sovael cast the right spell at the right save and the die said no. That's a bad night, not a locked door.
- **RotR** is positional-dominant: Runelords teleporting, dragons at 50 ft, a boss that never entered reach. Sevek answered it with Dragon Wings, Ascend, and Group Taunt at 120 ft — and when Baolen was in reach, three Trips burned all three of its Mythic Points. Cody *removed* the immunity layers by hand.
- **Season of Ghosts** is variance-dominant: Earth's Bile crit-saved on a nat 20, Needle Darts foiled by cover, Steal Face saved. The lanes always functioned.
- **Hellbreakers** had real immunity denial — *"resist physical except silver"* for four sessions — and it is the control case: **the player bought the key and the lock opened.** S10 silver falcata; S15 vampires: "silver bypassed both."
- **AV**: Gibbering Mouther (weak to bludgeoning nobody had; can't be flanked). Chandriu (incorporeal + Rejuvenation — unkillable by damage). Jaul (weak silver, nobody had it). Chain woman (regen 10 off only by silver/holy, nobody had it; Impaling Chain anchor). Scalathrax Oily Scales (Combat Grab pops). Volluk (immune grabbed / prone / restrained / precision, resist physical 7, fast healing 7, area weakness behind Reflex +19). **Seven fights. Seven different keys the party didn't have.**

### The silver test

The cleanest side-by-side in the whole dataset:

| | Hellbreakers | Abomination Vault |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Orts/Insidiators: *"resist all physical except silver"* (S6–S9) | Jaul: weak silver 7 (S6). Chain woman: regen 10, off only by silver/holy (S7) |
| Player response | Silvered the falcata, S10 | Bought silver handwraps, S7 |
| Next relevant fight | S15 vampires: **"silver bypassed both"**; Trips landed; killed Hedvend VI | S8 Volluk: **immune to grabs, resist *all* physical incl. silver, weakness gated behind its best save** |
| Result | The key opened the lock | **The lock changed** |

Same problem, same player, same fix. One table let it matter.

## 4. The damage-share shape

Mean share is ~25% at both AV and RotR. The *distribution* is the tell:

**Snagg / Argon (AV):** 0 · 0 · 0 · 8 · 17 · 28 · 31 · 41 · 41 · 47 · 60
**Sevek (RotR):** 9.7 · 11.3 · 16.3 · 17.4 · 22.3 · 22.3 · 23.4 · 25.7 · 27.0 · 27.2 · 31.4 · 33.0 · 37.8 · 38

AV is **bimodal**: either the kit works and Snagg tops the party (S4 reliquary, S5 pirates, S6 shrine, S7 dining room), or the stat block turns it off and he contributes nothing (S3 ghost, S4 last door, S7 chain room). There is almost nothing in between, because there's no partial credit for a mechanic that's been switched off.

RotR is **unimodal with a floor**. Sevek's worst share — 9.7% against Baolen — was a fight where he tripped a mythic kraken three times and consumed every one of its Mythic Points. Low damage, full agency.

Sovael's shares (Setting Sun) are lower than both and that is *correct* — he's a control caster. His three most-agency fights (Wall of Wind cutting archer output 157→44; Roaring Applause forcing a solifugid to clap into four Reactive Strikes; Incarnate Ancient Specter dropping four opponents prone) barely register on damage. Damage share is the wrong ruler for him. **It is the right ruler for a Fighter whose entire kit is Strikes and grabs**, which is why it's diagnostic for Snagg and not for Sovael.

## 5. What Snagg's three downs actually were

Snagg went down three times in S8, and it's worth saying what to: **2d8+10 persistent piercing** that needed a DC 15 flat check to close, rolled 11 / 11 / nat 1 / 5, against a creature immune to his grab and resistant to his bite. Sovael's four downs in Ruby Phoenix: Punch Down for 72, a nat-20 flurry, crits of 60 and 62, Falling Stars. **Burst.** Big numbers from the offense axis, in fights where his spells were landing on the right saves.

Getting hit hard is the fun-hard axis. Bleeding out while your character sheet is inert is the other one.

| | AV | Setting Sun |
|---|---|---|
| Count | 6 | 5 |
| Chosen | 5 | 3 |
| Forced | 1 (S8: attempted, door re-locked) | 2 |

Four of Setting Sun's five retreats are Ruby Phoenix mechanics — conceding is a *move* with a known price, and the DM built the arc around it. AV's are the party walking away from things it had no way to touch, and one of them cost a character.

## 7. What was actually good (for honesty)

The IWD file had this section and Lap earns it more than Drew did.

- **He prepares.** Designed, multi-phase encounters; he tracks his own roster from memory; every session has structure.
- **He is genuinely good at fun-hard when the party's kit engages.** S6 shrine: Extreme, nine rounds, Snagg killed seven things — brutal and fine. S7 Scalathrax and Corpselights: three rounds each, full of texture, three of four wisp kills on reactions. Nobody came out of those asking what the enemy design was.
- **He pulls punches in the moment.** Two rounds on Discorporate instead of one. A free hero point to the whole table mid-fight for not metagaming. *"He is the main boss for this floor"* and *"if he succeeds, you guys make TPK here"* said out loud. *"If I don't pull this punch, I don't think you guys will enjoy it."* The carry-over overkill rule that he credits with preventing a TPK in S6.
- **He takes notes.** Took your crit-effect DC ruling on the spot in S8. Adjusted after S5. Leveled the party early so *"I don't need to hold back."*
- **You get the spotlight.** The entire S8 recap turns on a call you made. S7's set piece was an argument you mediated. That was never the problem at this table.

None of this is the blind spot. The blind spot is that "hard" is one word to him, and the elite template is the only knob.

---

## 8. Verdict

**AV is the outlier, and not on the axis the outcome numbers would suggest.** It's the only table where the dominant failure mode is *the stat block switching your kit off*, and where the party's fixes (silver, learning the weakness) don't carry forward because the next lock is different. Combined with a hero-point economy at roughly a third of every other table you play at, that is — structurally — a meat grinder. The elite-on-a-level-1-crawling-hand is the smoking gun that it's a default, not a design decision.

**But it's the first time one of these has had a lever in it.** IWD was no-prep. Myrrindar was a system you'd outgrown. Neither had a dial. This has two:

1. **"Can you stop eliting everything by default — save it for the actual bosses?"** Restores difficulty signalling. Doesn't touch his lethality doctrine.
2. **"Can we get hero points closer to what the other tables run — even three a session?"** Every other table you play at is at 3–6. You're at 1.

Neither asks him to make the game easy. Both are two-sentence conversations. And he has already told you enjoyment is his metric; he just has no data on where the line is.

**What would change this verdict:** if the conversation happens and the next three sessions still produce immunity-dominant fights with the party's counters not carrying forward — that's architecture, not a dial, and it's the IWD pattern again.

**The rep, again:** the IWD lesson was *"the exit is the rep, not just the outcome."* Here the rep might be the conversation. Both previous times you'd already exhausted that before leaving. This time you haven't.
