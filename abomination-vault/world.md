# Abomination Vault — World & Story

## Setting
**Abomination Vault** (PF2e Remaster AP), levels 1–11, three volumes: *Ruins of Gauntlight → Hands of the Devil → Eyes of Empty Death*. Set in the frontier town of **Otari** on the Isle of Kortos, beside the **Gauntlight** — a dead lighthouse atop a vast megadungeon. The vault is the lair of the long-dead would-be tyrant **Belcorra Haruvex** (final boss, L12, incorporeal undead), whose undead and aberrations are seeping toward the surface.

## Argon Rath's backstory
Argon Rath was born to a scar-marked orc hold on the ragged edge of the borderlands, the kind of clan that tattoos its survival into its skin. Somewhere far back, a dragon's blood crept into his line — and when his fury takes him, it surfaces as raw crackling **force**, sparking off his hide like a storm with nowhere to go. He earned every scar the honest way, defending a hold that never stopped bleeding, until there was nothing left to defend and no war worth staying for. So he shouldered his guisarme and went looking for a better one. Otari promised coin and quiet work — but the dead things clawing up out of the Gauntlight offered something Argon respects far more: an enemy that *deserves* killing. He signed on to go down into the dark, and hasn't looked back.

**Ties to the mechanics:** Hold-Scarred scars = a hardened veteran (Veteran background); dragon-blood = the Barrage/force Draconic Rage; the hold-fighter's brawl = Athletics maneuvers (Slam Down shove/trip, grappling via Titan Wrestler, Raging Athlete), with a fear rider on his hits (Intimidating Strike).

**The end (S4, 2026-07-29):** Died as he lived — holding a doorway on the Gauntlight's third floor so the others could run. A ghast leader's 34-damage crit dropped him; Orc Ferocity and the One Hundred Victories tattoo stood him back up at 10 HP; the paralysis took his legs and the crit took his guisarme, and the party fled at his word: *"I'll hold him back!"* The cult is turning him; the party expects to have to put the ghoul with the diamond scars down. His +1 striking guisarme lies where he fell.

## Snagg's backstory
Born and raised in Otari — a goblin in a town that treats his kind "not very bad, but definitely not good." Left at adulthood for a few years of adventuring, got tired of the life, and came home to a real job: **security team leader for Otari Lumber**, reporting directly to Klorte Hengis, with a four-man team under him (two humans, a goblin, a gnome: Rodman †, Parry, Clive, Danilo). The company gave a goblin an opportunity and treats him well; he repays it with genuine loyalty — *"This is for Otari! For the wood company!"* Razortooth jaws, full plate, a sturdy shield, and a medic's toolkits: he bites people apart and patches them back together, and sees no contradiction in it. He missed the first Gauntlight flare entirely (*"I was away, guys. I had a different job"*) — the one party member with no history with the light.

**Ties to the mechanics:** the jaws = Razortooth heritage + Fang Sharpener; the day job = Field Medic background → Medic free archetype (Battle Medicine, Risky Surgery, Doctor's Visitation); Otari-local = the town-knowledge lane; Warfare Lore from the security work.

## Locations
- **The base** — an abandoned tavern ~30 min NW of Otari the party is refurbishing; **shared with a warren of kobolds** (odd but non-hostile, keep to their own rooms).
- **The Gauntlight** — ~100-ft dead lighthouse in a swamp; the megadungeon entrance. Sealed by a magic seal during daytime — **only accessible after sunset.** First floor cleared; secret dungeon entrance on the 2nd floor; the prior party got halfway through the 3rd floor (where they hit the ghouls). **Teleport circles on floors 2 and 3 are both reactivated (S4, S6)** — the party now rides straight to floor 3.
- **The pavilion over the underground lake** *(below the floor-3 stairs the armored knight used, S6)* — a stone pavilion on pillars 10 ft above a cold underground lake; brown fungus on the west shore; water flows in from a natural cave to the south; a **metal pipe runs near the cave ceiling** (Alder); passages lead **northeast, northwest, and southwest** (unexplored). Jaul Mezmin camped here. A north door opens on a tight **spiral stair going both up and down**; the party went up.
- **The domed shrine of Nhimbaloth** *(off the spiral stair's landing, S6)* — a 30-ft dome whose walls are carved as a graveyard, ghosts rising toward a giant **eye** at the apex; a 15-ft raised platform holds an altar of assembled stone slabs shaped like a **seven-fingered hand** clutching a **blue light**, with a small object on top (unexamined). **Room effect**: DC 22 Will each turn while inside — fail Frightened 1, crit fail 2, crit success immune; the fear lasts until you leave. Crawling hands crawl out of the altar; **stone sarcophagi stand upright in the walls** — two burst open (Cairn Wights) when a body came within **15 ft**; the goblin saw **more, some still closed**.
- **Dawnflower Library** — SW Otari, by the sea; a central library flanked by twin chapel wings. The **Rose Blade of Vol Rajadi** (a Roseguard hero) is displayed in a glass case at the entrance.
- **Chester** — small port town ~1.5 days west of Otari; receives regular Otari Lumber shipments. Has a harbor able to dock a large ship.

## Lore
- **Belcorra Haruvex** — would-be tyrant slain **500 years ago by the Roseguard**; suspected of reactivating the Gauntlight (its light woke undead in the Otari graveyard two weeks ago). **Four tapestries** of her life recovered so far; the library learns more from each. Her **tomb / boss room** was found on the 3rd floor in S2 — a carved double stone door (a ghost-lady rising from a grave); the party stopped at the threshold.
- **Otari (the hero)** — the Roseguard member the town is named for **did not die** in the battle. He survived, went **deeper into the dungeon**, and left **messages** the party keeps finding hidden in corners (two so far).
- **The Gauntlight's light** — a pillar of **negative energy** rising from a lake on the 3rd floor (near a huge **purple-worm corpse**) up to the top of the lighthouse. Undead that touch the energy are **healed**; it will kill the living.
- **The Gauntlight is an artifact** (Wrin's blueprint report, S5) — built by **Belcorra**: the 115-ft lighthouse over **eight dungeon levels** (terminating at the ninth), with **four magical lenses** so potent Belcorra handled them personally. Activated from the bottom level, it can **observe anything its light touches**, **reanimate all undead within a mile**, and **teleport creatures along its beam** — currently one creature of level 4 or lower (the graveyard "spider-thing"); fully restored, **any number of creatures of level 15 or lower, with light reaching Absalom** and the millions dead of its historic sieges. Working frame: stop it **before "stage four"** — it sits at roughly stage two.
- **The second flare (S5)** — past midnight after the pirate raid, the Gauntlight abruptly **doubled in brightness** and stayed there: about **half as bright as the graveyard night** per Wrin, and the first sudden jump in her nightly observations. The party connects it to the cult's dwarf sacrifice; Xurl's read: they sacrificed *because* they knew it would brighten, not to brighten it.
- **The teleport array** — dormant teleport circles on the lower floors (found on negative-1 and negative-2; none on the surface). Alder believes he can **reactivate** them with the right **ritual instructions** (a book or scroll), as long as the circle isn't defaced — a potential shortcut through the megadungeon.
- **The flesh-eating cult** — ghoul cultists operating from the 3rd-floor "restricted collection" library. They **eat their victims** (bickering over raw vs. fungus-poisoned flesh) and can **heal by consuming corpses**. Includes a robed intelligent ghoul and a **spellcasting ghoul** (Grim Tendrils, Harm, Command, Phantom Pain). They **recruit** — the caster offered the party immortality to "drop your weapon and join us."
- **The captured red-haired dwarf** — held by the cult, to be **sacrificed at midnight in two days, on the floor below** the party's current position (the same day as the planned pirate raid). **Presumed dead (S5)**: the party was four hours out at sea when midnight passed, and the Gauntlight flared. No body confirmed.
- **The lumber-pirate ring** — magical **Kholo-language notes** are launched from the Otari cliffs, flying to pirates at sea; each note carries a shipment's **departure time + destination** so raiders can intercept Otari Lumber convoys. The party intercepted two. Not confirmed an inside job. The party has agreed to hunt the pirates directly (over the Arsenal Navy handling it). **S5: the decoy ambush worked** — one pirate ship captured (sailed home to Otari), seven boarders dead, the raid commander and navigator taken prisoner, at least two swimmers gone into the storm. The lumber continued to Chester on the original ship.
- **Black Eel** — the Kholo pirate captain behind the raids, operating from a **small island ~2 hours' sail south** of the ambush point. Nobody in the party — or Klorte, who knows everyone — has heard the name.
- **The Sons of Montriarch** — what Black Eel's crew calls itself. Theological anomaly (Ruben, Religion 25): Kholo worship **Lamashtu**, Mother of Monsters, and style themselves *sons of Lamashtu*. Who or what is **Montriarch** — a rebel, another god, something else?
- **"Has defense"** — the S5 signal note carried a third line the older ones didn't: time, destination, and *"has defense."* Someone in Otari watched the mayor's guards board the decoy and reported it. The ring has a **watcher on the docks**, and the notes' author is still unidentified.

- **The flesh statue** — a 15-ft statue of a woman made of stitched squares of preserved flesh, in the floor-3 reliquary (smashed display cases, spotlights). The cult feeds from it — the priestess ate a piece mid-fight to heal. Left intact; the party suspects it should be destroyed.
- **The cult's origin (mirror vision, S4)** — the Cracked Magic Mirror showed the reliquary in the deep past: intact-but-empty cases, no statue, and 20–30 robed librarians kneeling with heads bowed — then devouring one another. The library's own keepers became the flesh-eating cult.
- **The armored knight** — full plate, visibly not human; twice walked past the party ignoring them; took a **huge book** from a hidden wall-library and descended toward the floor below. **Both the knight and the book appear in the recovered tapestries** (Xurl + Alder made the connection). Identity unknown.
- **The laughing figure ("the jokester")** — appears in doorways, laughs, vanishes; pranked the *previous* party mid-fight (per Ruben); its laugh can deal psychic damage. First contact with this group in S4.
- **The teleportation network** — **nine circles**, roughly one per floor. Alder reactivated the floor-3 circle with a nat-20 ritual (S4) using the Folio of Teleportation Research; each circle must be reactivated on-site, and destroying its inlaid silver disables it. **The ground-floor circle's connection is severed** — reason unknown.
- **Nhimbaloth** *(spelling tentative)* — **outer god of emptiness, despair, and ghosts** (Ruben, S6: "the empty death"). Her shrines are empty except for the altar — that emptiness is the tell. The domed shrine below floor 3 is dedicated to her alone, and Belcorra's floor-3 tomb door (S3) is a Nhimbaloth shrine too.
- **Jaul Mezmin** *(spoken at the table, S6)* — a **werewolf** who camped on the pavilion over the lake with a wolf: pale, skinny, two katars, a cracked voice from not talking; the word "job" frightened him. Killed Kaledo's wife Isla ~a year ago; 300 gp bounty. **Killed S6** by Alder's natural-20 chakram + Critical Hit Deck save-or-die (failed the Fort save by one at 109 HP remaining). Run as level 7 elite, 140 HP, weakness silver 7 — nobody had silver. Why he was three floors down under the Gauntlight is unknown.
- **The werewolf curse (Lap's rules, S6)** — Jaul's bite **cursed Xurl** (DC 25 Fort, failed). It is a curse, not a disease — no incubation. The cursed turn under **direct moonlight on the full moon**, lose control, and rampage under GM control; **underground (no moonlight) is safe**; a windowless room works. The full moon was **the night of S6 or the next** — the graveyard night was a new moon exactly two weeks earlier ("I'm not making this up"). No cure known; Ruben knows the transmission only.
- **The Cairn Wights** *(S6)* — two, from the shrine's sarcophagi: longsword +16, Drain Life (Drained stacks), temp HP on hit, **Funereal Dirge** (party-wide Frightened), Final Spite. **They speak Common** — "Leave." / "Leave this place." — and **four times one of them could not advance on a downed PC**; each rolled a Will save at the top of its turn (escalating −2), held on high results, attacked on low. The DM never named the effect.
- **Will-o'-wisps (Foundry: Flickerwisp)** *(S6)* — five in the shrine: **immune to magic** (only specific spells like Force Barrage hurt them), Shock, **Flicker** (DC 20 Will → Confused), and they **heal by feeding on a confused creature**. Melee only.
- **Aller Rosk** *(Foundry name — never spoken at the table)* — the **ghast leader** on floor 3; claw +17 (2d6+9), Paralysis DC 24, far above the floor's other undead. Recognizes the three veterans from a fight they fled. **Killed Argon (S4).**
- **Nhakazarin** *(Foundry name — never spoken)* — the cult's spellcasting **ghoul priestess** (Chilling Darkness, Harm, Grim Tendrils, Vampiric Touch, Daze; AC 23, DC 23–24). Offered the party membership twice. Slain and beheaded in S4; her staff (unidentified, "probably magical") is in the party stash.

## The DM
- **"Lap"** — runs deliberately **over-level, lethal combat**; standard/trivial encounters bore him, so most fights are 1–2 levels above the party. Warned players not to get attached — and as of S4 **every player at the table has lost a character** (Argon is the second death). Handled Argon's death graciously: "I don't want you to feel bad because I killed your character… you guys didn't heal it up back to full."
- **House rules seen in S6**: **overkill from a killing blow carries over to an adjacent enemy** (fired four times; the DM's own post-mortem: "if I'm not doing that carry damage over it might be a TPK"); a **potion on a downed ally is 2 actions** (played as 1 both times), own potion = free draw + 1 action; **Shove an ally = no MAP**; the **Critical Hit / Fumble Decks** are live for everyone (cards have killed a boss, made a wolf bite itself, and thrown a wisp's Shock into its own hand). Retraining feats/spells between sessions is allowed on request. Foundry scene names are not room names (the "Library" scene was the shrine).
