- **Hero-point house rule (confirmed S4, re-confirmed S9):** spend = **reroll OR bump one degree of success**. **⚠️ This table does NOT use the "+10 on a reroll of ≤10" rule documented in the repo-root CLAUDE.md — that belongs to a different table.** Lap stated his own rule plainly in S9 when offering to revert it: *"I will also remove every house rule about hero point, saying that automatically increased by success — it will be just reroll"* / *"if he's able to roll a success, he can use a hero point to pump him up to a critical success."* ~~S9 compromise: a PC with zero hero points at the start of a session gets one~~ — **WITHDRAWN by Lap at the top of S10**, replaced by: **(1) a Heroic Buff at the start of every combat** for every PC regardless of hero points held — spend before a roll for advantage (roll twice, keep better) or after for a reroll (second result stands); **no degree bump**; expires with the combat (auto-applied in Foundry); and **(2) Hero Actions** — gaining a hero point draws a card from Paizo's optional **Hero Point Deck** (no trading; ignore an irrelevant card and spend the point normally). Regular hero points keep the degree bump and may also be spent for advantage. Long-rest hero points continue, cap **3**. **Roll-log consequence: a Heroic Buff reroll or advantage roll also renders as a two-d20 line — don't count every two-d20 line as a hero point from S10 on.** **Consequence for roll-log analysis: degree bumps leave no second d20, so a reroll count is always a FLOOR** — S9 logged 5 reroll lines against 8 actual in-fight spends. Degree bumps are invisible in the Foundry export (no second d20). The DM also hands out mid-session hero points for discoveries (the mirror vision). **CHANGED — as of S7 hero points refresh on a LONG REST, not per session** (*"that was the start and then we changed to long rest"* — Xurl); the overnight rest granted one each, and both were spent in round 1 of the next fight.

This table has **no blanket nat-1 auto-reroll**: S7 had **seven natural 1s and only two rerolls**, both PC hero-point spends confirmed in the transcript. **Verification method: count natural 1s against rerolls in the same log.** If most nat-1s stand un-rerolled, every two-d20 line is a hero point.

- **Knowing a weakness ≠ being able to use it.** Alder's Exploit Vulnerability rider applies to **his own Strikes only**. His S7 fire read on the Scalathrax was broadcast to the party and turned out worthless — *"has anyone got any fire for these guys?"* / nobody did. Read the party's actual damage types before treating a weakness as actionable.
- **Reactions are the answer to fleeing enemies.** Three of four Corpselight kills landed on wisps trying to fly clear — **Xurl's Stand Still, Snagg's Reactive Strike**, and Ruben's standing Sepulchral Mask aura. The wall works when the enemy has to leave.
- **Luck note**: the wights rolled five natural 20s in 18 swings (~1 in 650) while Snagg rolled zero in 50 dice all night and still topped damage (187) — the +16 vs AC 20–22 math carries him even when the d20 doesn't.

- **S8 — the lockout, and the lesson that replaces "nobody has silver."** Volluk (level 8 elite, PL+3, a **Severe** encounter by the book) nearly TPK'd a party that has cleared Severe and Extreme fights before, because it wasn't a difficulty spike — it was a **capability lockout**. `immune[grabbed, prone, restrained]` deleted both control PCs' primary lanes; `immune[precision]` and `resist[physical 7]` taxed a nearly all-physical party twice (119 of 293 rolled damage eaten); `fast healing 7` added 70 HP over 10 procs. **The new rule: knowing and even owning a weakness is not enough — you have to beat the save.** Ruben *had* an area spell, cast **Inner Radiance Torrent** straight into `weak[area 7]` while the target sat at **Clumsy 3**, and it **critically succeeded its Reflex +19 for zero.** Before buying bombs, check what save the weakness is gated behind.
- **Persistent damage is the real killer at this table.** A 2d8+10 persistent Tendril (doubling to ~40/round on a crit) put Snagg down three times and Alder to Dying 3 — healing cannot outpace a wound that reopens, and DC 15 flat checks fail. Compare the chain woman's Impaling Chain in S7. **Expect the pattern to continue.**
- **Hero points, S8 ledger**: seven spent (Xurl 3, Ruben 3, Alder 1, Snagg 0) on a long-rest allocation of one each — because the **DM handed the whole table an extra one mid-fight** for not looking the monster up online. Remember that **degree-bump spends leave no second d20 in the export**, so the roll log's reroll count undercounts. Confirmed again: **this table does not use the +10-on-a-low-reroll house rule.**
- **The Critical Hit Deck is a tactical resource, not a bonus.** Snagg traded double damage for Clumsy 3 + Enfeebled 3 (AC 27 → 22 off-guard) and it directly bought Xurl a hit; the same triple-damage card came up **twice in one night** and the second one ended the fight. Xurl made the same trade in S7 (Stunned 2 over 10 damage).
- **The AP itself is the problem, not the DM's scaling.** Lap's post-mortem: *"the only thing I did is add a spell to his list — Stinking Cloud. Everything else is from the AP."* The elite template scaled the numbers (+20 HP, +2 AC, +2 attack, **+2 DCs**); the immunity/resistance stack is Paizo's.
- **S9 — the accuracy gap IS the difficulty, and it is measurable.** The floor's guardian (a **level 9 elite will-o'-wisp**, PL+3, AC 32) **connected on 21 of 24 attack rolls — 87.5%, four crits, zero critical misses. The party connected on 9 of 45 — 26.7%, with fourteen critical misses.** Snagg's +17 jaws needed a **15**; Xurl and Alder at +15 needed a **17**. **388 damage absorbed against 274 total party HP**, and **all four PCs went down**. When judging tuning at this table, **compute the connect rate, not the XP budget** — the encounter was a by-the-book Severe (PL+3) and still nearly a TPK, because AC out of band converts a grind into a coin-flip.

The problem is with Abomination Vault and its abilities — every fucking monster has either some kind of resistance or some kind of rider ability on its normal attack."* Lap's own words: *"this is one of the fights that people complain about Abomination Vault. It's unfair."* **The AP is the problem, not Lap's scaling** — S9 confirmed it a second time: every number was a textbook elite template, both brutal mechanics (magic immunity, at-will rank-4 Darkness) were native to the stat block, and his only two deviations *helped* the party.
- **"Knowing a weakness isn't enough, and neither is owning one — you have to have it in your repertoire."** The S8 lesson evolves. The wisp's `immune[magic]` has an exception list of just four spells plus anything with the **`light` trait**: **Faerie Fire, Revealing Light, Force Barrage, Quandary**. *(The block also lists glitterdust/magic missile/maze — pre-remaster names for three of those, not extra options.)* **Faerie Fire and Revealing Light are both rank-2 DIVINE spells** — squarely available to Ruben, who does not know either. Snagg found this mid-fight and asked; the answer was *"I do not have revealing light."* **This is the third magic-immune enemy** (S6 Flickerwisps, the Corpselights, now this). **Action item: get a light-trait or exception-list spell into Ruben's repertoire before the next descent.**
- **Blindness is the real weapon at this table, not damage.** Rank-4 Darkness blinded the whole party **rounds 3–9** and put a **DC 11 flat check on every single party attack** — costing at least three confirmed hits including **one of Xurl's two natural 20s**. It also **blanked the party's brand-new Lantern of Empty Light**, because a level-6 item is only rank-3 magic. **Buy light that beats rank 4, or a way to see without light.** Note the corollary: the Darkness ended in round 9 only because the creature's own **Feed on Fear reignited its Glow** — which also cleared its own Clumsy 2/Enfeebled 2. Rounds 10–12 had **zero** flat checks, and that is why the kill landed.
- **Enemy self-healing off the party's own conditions.** **Feed on Fear** (3d4+4, **1d4-round cooldown**) heals off anything **Frightened or Dying** — and the creature's own Fearful Strike rider generates the Frightened. A closed loop. It healed 22 back, and Lap volunteered afterward that **he rolled the cooldown as a 4 twice** and *"if I roll a one he will be still up at this point."* Pure luck decided this fight.
- **The incapacitation trait, settled (S9).** For a **non-spell** effect the rule is simply *"a creature of higher level than the item, creature, or hazard generating the effect"* gains one degree better — **no doubling, no effect-level arithmetic.** So a level-9 creature outlevels a level-6 monk and is entitled to the bump. Xurl's **Stunning Blows** saves were adjusted **twice** (round 6 success → critical success; round 7 natural-1 critical failure → plain failure), which turned **three actions of denial into one** in the round before he was dropped. **Correct, by the book — do not re-flag this.**
- **The Critical Hit Deck decided the fight again.** **Card #47** off Xurl's round-7 natural 20 applied **Clumsy 2 + Enfeebled 2**, taking the boss from 39 HP to 11 in one turn. *(Note the interaction: the debuff was cleared by the creature's own heal in round 9 — "until healed" cards have a real expiry at this table.)*
- **Shields are consumables.** Snagg's **Sturdy Shield (Minor)** (Hardness 8, HP 64, **BT 32**) took 15 then 30 and **broke past its Break Threshold** in round 11 — and the **lost +2 circumstance AC** is what put him down, turning rolls of 26 and 25 into hits. Shield Block is also once per round. **Repair the shield before the next descent.**
