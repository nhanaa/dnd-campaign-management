## Session Summary

Pirate day. The morning after Argon's death, the party reported to the port at noon, told Iqbal what the Gauntlight had claimed, and met the decoy shipment's escort: Klorte Hengis, the lumber company's shirtless, feather-boa'd half-orc manager — and behind him, the mayor's hired security team and its leader, a short, well-armored goblin who smiled to show every one of his very sharp teeth. *"Snagg? Yeah, because I'll snack your head off. Well — not you specifically. As long as you stay on my good side."* Pax's new character is an Otari local, born and raised, security lead for the lumber company — and by the end of the night the party's unanimous review was Ruben's: *"I love this guy. Can we keep him?"*

The trap half-worked. Two open d-rolls before departure came up maximally cursed — the worst weather on the table and the latest possible attack — so the party spent four seasick hours in a building storm (everyone but the ocean-raised Snagg fought at Sickened 1 all day) before the pirate ship rammed them at sunset. What followed was a chaotic deck brawl: eight pirate boarders — all women, commanded by a human "captain" with a slide pistol — against four PCs, Klorte, and the player-piloted deck crew. Snagg's first combat turn set the tone for the whole fight: a jaws critical that ripped a pirate's leg artery out (*"Who is the fucking villain here?"* — the DM), then a hero-pointed Combat Grab crit on a second. Xurl and Clive muscled the enemy captain over the rail; Ruben angled a 60-foot Inner Radiance Torrent at him while he dangled; Klorte long-jumped onto the pirate ship like Superman and hurled boarders into the sea; and when the captain shoved his last pirate at the party and Snagg's Reactive Strike bit her down in one hit, the man's nerve broke entirely — *"I'm surrender! I'm surrender!"* Two captives taken, at least two swimmers gone into the storm, and one real cost: Rodman, one of Snagg's own men, cut down by a parting blade as he tried to run.

The back half of the night belonged to Ruben. With Snagg kneeling at eye level beside the prisoners — blood still dripping from his jaws — Ruben ran the interrogation in full Pharasmin magistrate voice (*"Your hands are stained with blood, even if you didn't hold the knife"*), turned a public nat 1 into a hero-pointed 30, and walked away with everything: the pirates answer to a Kholo captain called **Black Eel**, operating from a small island two hours south, and they call themselves the **Sons of Montriarch** — a name that should be "Sons of Lamashtu," if Ruben's theology is right, and isn't. The Navigator flipped outright, trading her piloting for a lighter sentence. The new signal note carried a third line the old ones didn't: **"has defense."** Someone in Otari watched the guards board.

Then the bill came due. Sailing the captured pirate ship home past midnight, the party watched the **Gauntlight double in brightness** — the same omen that preceded the graveyard dead rising two weeks ago, and almost certainly the cult's midnight sacrifice, completed while the party was four hours away at sea. The graveyard was quiet; Wrin and Vandy were already conferring outside the star-painted tent. Ruben handed Wrin the Gauntlight Blueprints, and by morning she had the shape of the nightmare: the Gauntlight is an **artifact Belcorra built** — eight dungeon levels under a 115-foot tower, four lenses only Belcorra could handle — and fully restored it can watch anywhere its light reaches, **reanimate every undead within a mile**, and **teleport creatures across its beam**... with light that would reach Absalom and its millions of siege-war dead. *"We definitely need to stop it before it goes up to stage four."*

## Key Events

### Act I — The Port at Noon
- The party told **Iqbal** of Argon's fate — *"The Gauntlight claimed another"* (Xurl) — and confirmed they'll see the contract through: *"I was summoned here to do a job. We will get it done"* (Alder).
- Met **Klorte Hengis** *(spelling tentative)*: half-orc, mid-40s, braided beard, no shirt, white feather boa, the lumber company's mark tattooed across his back. Greeted the party with bear hugs and told Alder to his face he's too weak (*"That's bullshit. You can still spend some time training"*). Alder: *"I like this guy already."*
- **Snagg's introduction**: Pax's new PC is a local goblin, a former adventurer who came home and took the security-lead job under Klorte; his four-man team (two humans, a goblin, a gnome — Rodman, Parry, Clive, Danilo) shipped out with him. The name pun landed immediately, and the height ribbing with Xurl started the session's best double act (*"I finally see someone that's shorter than me"* / *"Bumbling fool"* — Snagg is actually a foot and a half taller). Xurl's theory of goblin temperament brought the house down: *"People are closer to hell, and that makes them more violent."*
- Quick shopping: Xurl grabbed a level-2 talisman; Snagg bought smelling salts.
- Two open rolls set the day's difficulty: the weather d6 came up **1** (worst possible) and Xurl's d4 for the attack timing came up **4** (latest possible — four hours out). The DM later confirmed: a 1 on the d4 would have had them back in time for the sacrifice.

### Act II — The Storm Crossing
- Departed ~1 PM into a rising storm. The seasickness Fort save (at −2) caught everyone except the ocean-raised local: **Xurl, Ruben, and Alder fought the entire day Sickened 1.** Ambush positions: Alder eyed a barrel, Ruben hid below with the cargo, Xurl loitered in plain sight ("no one's going to look twice at a random fucking halfling") — and Snagg stood in the open, his boss on deck: *"I'm a real fighter. I don't need to hide."*
- **Snagg spotted the pirate ship first** (Perception 27), picking one sail out of the storm-blurred traffic of the Inner Sea: *"This probably is the one... Just my gut feeling."*
- **The cannon duel** (the mayor's two borrowed cannons vs. the pirates'): Xurl's first shot nearly blew up the gun (Halfling Luck: *"I'm happy with not exploding, actually"*); Klorte lucked one hit and declared himself a professional. Snagg's rally speech — *"This is for Otari! For the wood company!"* (DM: *"For our corporate overlords"*) — cratered on the morale check, and he dropped mid-sentence; he later wiped the puke off the cannon while aiming it.
- The party's one earned artillery hit was a **three-man production**: Xurl asked to fire on **Warfare Lore** (ex-sailor), Alder Aided with a **nat-20 Esoteric Lore** (Diverse Lore stretching to naval gunnery), and the 23 struck home. Two hits total on the pirate ship — enough to matter later.
- The pirates rammed the ship at sunset. Brace-and-Reflex: Xurl (hero point, still 19) and Ruben went prone with most of the deck crew; Snagg and Alder kept their feet.

### Act III — The Deck Fight *(see Combat Encounters)*
- Eight boarders, a pistol-armed enemy captain, and a navigator against the party, Klorte, and the crew. The DM handed each player one of Snagg's guards to pilot — a number lottery gave Ricardo Rodman, Pax Parry, Ben Clive, and Twan Danilo.
- Highlights: Snagg's three jaws kills and the surrender cascade they caused; Xurl and Clive putting the enemy captain over the rail; Klorte's flying double-hatchet boarding action; Rodman's death; two swimmers escaping into the storm; **captain and navigator captured**.

### Act IV — The Interrogation
- Post-fight, Snagg counseled caution before mercy: the letter was in Kholo but no Kholo was aboard — *"are these actually the real people that we need to defeat? Or there's more?"* — and proposed binding and questioning the survivors. Xurl stripped the prisoners of everything: *"Anything which isn't made out of pants is coming off. They can keep their pants, begrudgingly."*
- Ruben led the interrogation, with Snagg as the visual aid: *"Perhaps you'd rather talk to my little friend here"* — the DM gave the +2 and the kneeling captain found himself eye to eye with a blood-soaked goblin smile. Ruben's openly-rolled nat 1 sparked a table debate about secret rolls; the DM offered a reroll-take-lower, and Ruben hero-pointed it to a **30**.
- The take: the raiders answer to **Black Eel**, a Kholo pirate captain (nobody at the table recognized the name — four Recall Knowledge rolls of 4–8: *"Who the fuck is anyone?"*); the hideout is a **small island ~2 hours south**; the crew styles itself the **Sons of Montriarch**.
- **Ruben's Religion check (25)** flagged the anomaly: Kholo worship Lamashtu, Mother of Monsters, and call themselves *sons of Lamashtu*. "Sons of Montriarch" is either a rebel, a different god, or something worse. *"Perplexing... More to research on."*
- **The Navigator flipped**: over the captain's protest (*"the captain will skin you alive"*), she agreed to pilot the party to the hideout — *"I'm going to die anyway. I'd rather risk it."* Ruben, magnanimous in judgment: *"You are not going to meet Lady Pharasma this night. Congratulations to you both."*
- The new Kholo signal note had **three lines** — time, destination, and *"has defense."* Someone in Otari saw the mayor's guards board the decoy. The interception now points at a watcher on the docks.
- Strategy call: **Snagg argued to hit the hideout immediately** — *"Now is the best time to attack them. We have time to prepare... They'll be prepared next time"* — but yielded to the older clock: the dwarf in the Gauntlight. Ruben: *"I do not like my odds of challenging an entire stronghold"*; Xurl: *"take the prisoners back, hand them over to the mayor, and actually get a proper force."* Klorte sent the lumber on to Chester with the original ship and sailed the **captured pirate ship** home with the party and prisoners.

### Act V — The Beacon
- Past midnight, approaching Otari: **the Gauntlight abruptly doubled in brightness** and stayed there. The veterans knew the omen; Snagg failed the "were you even in town" flat check — *"I was away, guys. I had a different job"* — and heard about the graveyard dead for the first time.
- The party sprinted for the graveyard: quiet, empty. **Alder ran the deduction**: Vandy wasn't on the shortcut path, so she wasn't headed to the graveyard — *"the only two places I can think of is Wrin or the mayor"* — and led the party straight to Wrin's Wonders.
- The **disused cliff path** down to the Dawnflower Library (DC 22 Reflex): Ruben and Alder slipped and caught themselves; *"the goblin with heavy plate armor doesn't fucking care."* Snagg, before free-soloing it: *"There's no such thing as too dangerous for Snagg."*
- Library: Vandy already gone. Xurl to the acolyte: *"It is bright like the night the dead rose. You should wake Vandy."* They found **Vandy and Wrin already conferring** outside Wrin's Wonders. Wrin's read: about **half as bright as the graveyard night** — a first in her nightly observations.
- **Ruben connected it to the cult's sacrifice** (Wrin: *"Ruben, what are you talking about? What kind of ritual?"*) and Xurl sharpened the theory: they weren't sacrificing to brighten the light — **they sacrificed because they knew it would brighten**. With the pirate detour running four hours, the midnight ritual is presumed complete and the red-haired dwarf presumed dead. *"We went for pirates instead of this ritual sacrifice... It is what it is"* (Xurl).
- Ruben handed **the Gauntlight Blueprints** to Wrin overnight (*"Oh gods... my Pharasma"*).

### Act VI — Wrin's Report (session close)
- Next morning at the mayor's, Wrin laid it out: the Gauntlight is an **artifact built by Belcorra** — the 115-ft lighthouse above **eight dungeon levels**, with **four magical lenses** so potent Belcorra handled them personally. Activated from the bottom level, it can **observe anything its light touches**, **reanimate all undead within a mile**, and **teleport creatures along its beam** — currently one creature of level 4 or lower (the "spider-thing" that appeared in the graveyard), but fully restored: **any number of creatures of level 15 or lower, with light reaching Absalom**.
- **Snagg's local knowledge (Society 21) supplied the stakes**: Absalom has weathered five or six major sieges across thousands of years — millions of dead in the ground outside its walls, every one of them in reach of a restored beam.
- The closing frame on the clock: stop it **before it reaches "stage four"** — with the light sitting at roughly stage two now.

## Character Spotlight

### Snagg · the Company Man with the Company Teeth — *debut*
- The debut worked on every axis at once. In fiction he's an Otari local made good — a goblin from a town that "treats you not very bad, but definitely not good," who came home from adventuring to lead the lumber company's security team, and who repaid the first employer to respect him with *"This is for Otari! For the wood company!"* (then dropped mid-speech as the rally roll cratered — and later calmly wiped the puke off the cannon he was aiming).
- The name pun — *"Snagg? Yeah, because I'll snack your head off... as long as you stay on my good side"* — turned out to be a promise, not a joke. First combat turn: a jaws crit that **ripped a pirate's artery out** and killed her outright, then a hero-pointed Combat Grab crit on a second. By fight's end he'd bitten three pirates dead — including the beheading-by-two-bites the DM narrated in horrified detail, and the **Reactive Strike one-shot that broke the enemy captain's morale** and ended the battle. The DM's review: *"Who is the fucking villain here?"*
- And then the same jaws declined the easy ending: offered the DM's deadpan out (*"you're in the middle of the ocean, no one knows what happened"*), he refused it — *"I'm just not going to go for kills here"* — and later argued the party should strike the hideout **now**, while it was unwarned, before conceding to the dwarf-rescue clock. The ferocity has a spine and a code attached.
- Sharp above the table from minute one: he reminded Ruben that Protector's Sacrifice was **heightened** (tripling its value on the crit aimed at Snagg himself), called the *Grab an Edge* reaction for the falling captain, argued the rail-hanger should be off-guard (*"School of cool for the win"* — the DM improvised a −2), and litigated Reactive Strike triggers all night — even when the ruling went against him.
- The **Medic** kit debuted both ways: a nat-20 Risky Surgery on himself, a *"just need to roll a 2"* crit-heal on Alder — and one botched procedure that stabbed Xurl for 8 and healed nothing: *"It was an accident, okay? Trust me. I'll get you next time." / "It's a procedure, okay?"*
- And the cost column is his too: the security team is *his* team, and **Rodman died** on the deck he was hired to hold. *"Poor Rodman. He found out the bad way."*

### Xurl · the Sailor on a Cursed Sea
- The ex-sailor finally got a naval session and spent it half-competent, half-cursed: seasick (Sickened 1 all day), a cannon that nearly blew up in his hands (*"Brother, I don't know where the fuck I'm aiming"* — saved by Halfling Luck: *"I'm happy with not exploding, actually"*), and then, when the DM let him fire on **Warfare Lore**, the party's one earned artillery hit. He also called the ram — *"Incoming ram! Brace!"* — and braced, and went prone anyway on a hero-pointed 19.
- **The Clive arc** was the session's running delight: handed one goblin crossbowman to pilot, Ben turned him into a legend — a nat-20 flat check to **bodycheck the enemy captain over the rail** alongside Xurl's own Athletics (*"because this man is the king"*), then *"Clive, you've done God's work, son,"* and finally: *"If I die, I'm taking Clive as my next character."*
- His own fists did their usual work: an Assurance Demoralize sold as pure body language (a shoulder-check fake-out — Frightened 1), then a Crane Wing **crit that "exploded" the pirate Snagg had grabbed** — followed by a **nat 1 on the follow-up swing**, which fired the deck's standing rule (nat 1 near the rail = over you go): the DM narrated the dying pirate's counter-kick, and Xurl caught the railing on a crit-success Grab an Edge.
- He spent the whole session running a wary side-eye at the new hire — from the intro's *"People are closer to hell, and that makes them more violent"* through *"This guy's just ripping people apart with his face. Are you sure you want to recruit this guy?"* — which curdled into the night's best line after the botched surgery: *"I don't know if I like you. I've only seen you cause damage."*
- Sharpest read of the finale: the cult didn't sacrifice to brighten the Gauntlight — *"they're sacrificing because they knew it would become brighter."* And the sailor's ledger on the day, unsentimental as ever: *"We went for pirates instead of this ritual sacrifice. It is what it is."*

### Ruben Falmoor · Pharasma's Magistrate
- His combat was all judgment calls made for other people: a rank-2 Heal that put **28 HP** back into a crit-shredded Alder (*"Holy shit. That's a bit of health"*), and **Protector's Sacrifice** thrown in front of the cutlass crit on Snagg — a level-appropriate 9 damage eaten for a goblin he'd met that morning (the DM, watching the mechanic: *"It's magic. Don't question it"*). His one attack was characteristically theatrical: a 60-foot **Inner Radiance Torrent** angled to catch the enemy captain *while he dangled from the rail* — both targets saved, and Ruben shrugged: *"They just have swords and guns and shit, and we're bringing magic nukes."*
- **The interrogation was a set piece** and he ran every beat of it: the opener (*"Now that you have been thoroughly trounced, now you're going to tell us everything"*), the staging (*"Perhaps you'd rather talk to my little friend here"* — pointing at the blood-jawed goblin, +2 from the DM), the honesty flex when his nat 1 landed in public (*"I would have done the same thing if I rolled a nat 20. Just to let you know"*), and the hero point that turned it into a 30.
- Then the magistrate voice: sentencing (*"Your hands are stained with blood, even if you didn't hold the knife"*), clemency (*"You are not going to meet Lady Pharasma this night. Congratulations to you both"*), and recruitment — flipping the Navigator into the party's pilot for the price of a lighter sentence, with a rider for the captain: *"And for you to not do anything stupid."*
- The night's two biggest lore beats were his: the **Religion 25** that exposed "Sons of Montriarch" as theological nonsense for Lamashtu-worshipping Kholo (*"Perplexing. Maybe he's just a rebel"*), and connecting the Gauntlight flare to the cult's sacrifice in front of Wrin before handing her the blueprints.
- Verdict on the new guy, delivered mid-carnage: *"I love this guy. Can we keep him?"*

### Alder Ungram · the Scholar Who Aids in All Things
- Opened the session carrying the party's resolve to Iqbal — *"We are here to do it. I was summoned here to do a job. We will get it done"* — and closed it running the night's cleanest deduction: Vandy wasn't on the shortcut, so she wasn't at the graveyard — *"the only two places I can think of is Wrin or the mayor"* — which put the party in front of Wrin's tent without a wasted step.
- The session's most improbable clutch roll was his: a **nat-20 Esoteric Lore Aid on naval artillery** — Diverse Lore stretched to "how to aim a cannon from a book" — which is the only reason Xurl's shot hit the pirate ship. The thaumaturge lane also ran in combat: **Exploit Vulnerability on the enemy captain** read out the officers' weakness (strong Reflex from a life at sea, **weak Fortitude** under the muscle), and his chakram crit — *"That is a critical hit. Let's go. Big boy damage"* — plus the backswing killed a boarder outright.
- Character comedy in his signature register: the accent-restart bit (*"Once we catch them with their proverbial pants down — oh wait, I'm using the completely wrong accent"*), the instant fondness for the man who called him weak (*"I like this guy already"*), and the anthropology of an all-female crew with one male commander: *"He created his own harem. A pirate harem."*
- Piloting **Rodman** went worse: a Reactive-Strike sap crit that rolled a 1 on its damage die (*"He's so sad he hates himself"*), a second crit off the deck (he took the double damage), and then the fatal retreat into a waiting Reactive Strike — seconds after the DM's *"don't sacrifice your people"* warning. The table gave Rodman a soldier's eulogy; the DM's ruling on cowardice stood: *"It's called tactical retreat, okay?"*
- Quietly useful everywhere else: the Command scroll on the Navigator (saved — a scroll spent for nothing), the empty-ship deduction (the pirates run light to haul more loot and sail faster), and the closing argument that beat the storm math: deliver first, *"by the time they get word that their assault failed, we would have already made the delivery."*

## Combat Encounters

### The Decoy Run — Pirate Boarding Action (~78 min, two rounds, ended by surrender)
**Field**: two rain-lashed decks in a storm, 15 feet above the waterline — nat 1s near the rail risk a swim; the "on the edge" penalty was invented live.
**Sides**: Snagg, Xurl, Alder, Ruben (three of four Sickened 1), Klorte (GM-run), and the player-piloted deck crew (Rodman, Parry, Clive, Danilo) vs. **8 pirate boarders** (cutlasses, Boarding Action, all women), an **enemy captain** (slide pistol + pike; the Foundry "Bosun"), and a **Navigator** (slide pistol). Two "Diver" tokens rolled initiative and never visibly surfaced.
**Initiative (top of order)**: Xurl 27 = Rodman 27 · Snagg 25 (delayed) · pirate cluster 22–24 · Ruben 21 = Navigator 21 · Alder 20 · Captain 19 · Klorte 18 = Clive 18 · Parry 11 · Danilo 10. Prone from the ram: Xurl, Ruben, Rodman, Parry, Danilo.

The fight ran exactly **two rounds** — each nearly forty minutes of table time with sixteen combatants — and ended mid-round-two when the surrender cascade beat the bottom of the order: Clive, Parry, and Danilo never got a second turn.

- **Round 1 — the boarding wave.** Rodman's opening crossbow shot went into the ocean; he dropped it and drew his sap. Xurl stood from prone and entered **Crane Stance**; Snagg raised his shield and **delayed**, waiting for targets. **Two pirates swung across on Boarding Actions** — one opened Clive for 10; the other **crit Xurl for 12**, and her follow-up missed him **by exactly 1: the Crane Stance AC bought the miss**.
- **Snagg's debut turn** (re-entering off delay): jaws **crit — 32 damage**, one pirate dead on the spot, artery torn out (*"You see this small little goblin in heavy armor just jump up and bite — and he just kills this guy right off"*). Then **Combat Grab**: nat 1, **hero point**, rerolled to a **second crit — 26**, target **Grabbed and Slowed 1** (failed the brawling crit-spec Fort by 7). Two actions, 58 damage, the table's *"Nom!"* in the chat log.
- **The second wave.** Two more pirates boarded: one **crit Alder for 18**; the other swung past Rodman — **Rodman's Reactive Strike** answered with a sap **crit** that rolled a 1 on its damage die (8 total; the roll so sad Foundry mis-targeted it at Rodman himself) — and then she carved him up with two strikes, **7 and 10**. A fifth pirate crossed but ran out of actions. **Ruben's rank-2 Heal restored Alder 28.** The Navigator advanced; **Alder stood and read a scroll of Command** at her — saved (28 vs DC 20). Mid-round Wisdom checks landed the reveal: the letter was Kholo, but the man commanding the all-women crew is **human**.
- **The captain and the rail.** The **captain** strode out — pike in one hand, slide pistol in the other — and fired at Alder through cover; the rocking deck took the shot wide (miss by 2). One pirate whiffed twice on Xurl (Crane Stance again, miss by 1), and his **Stand Still** counter came up a crit-miss fumble — the card rolled him aside, prone, off balance on the pitching deck. Then the allies answered: **Klorte's crit-success running long jump onto the pirate ship**, twin hatchets drawn mid-air, a **38-damage crit** that swatted a pirate into the ocean (*"He is fucking Superman over here"*) — and the play of the fight, **Xurl and Clive together muscling the captain over the rail**: Clive's nat-20 "is he smart enough" flat check (*"this man is the king"*), resolved as a Reposition off Xurl's Athletics (success by 4 vs the captain's Fortitude DC). The captain **Grabbed an Edge** (31) and hung there, **dropping his slide pistol into the sea** — Alder mourned it (*"that's a damn good pistol"*).
- **Round 1's bottom.** Watching a teammate get instant-killed, one pirate passed the DM's improvised **morale check** and piled onto the big half-orc anyway (hit Klorte for 8). Parry and Danilo stood, whiffed their crossbows, and Parry drew his sap (*"Parry is useless"*). The last pirate's Boarding Action **crit Snagg for 28** — **Ruben's heightened Protector's Sacrifice** ate 9 of it (Snagg took 19, Ruben 9; Snagg is the one who reminded him it was heightened).
- **Round 2 — collapse.** Rodman again: the DM's warning came first, seconds before the end — *"don't sacrifice your people... if you can keep him alive, maybe something good's going to happen"* — then a second sap crit (crit-deck: chose double damage — 8), and then **the run**: both adjacent pirates were entitled to Reactive Strikes, one was rolled, and **7 damage dropped him**. Rodman died on the deck. Xurl stood (a Reactive Strike crit-missed him), sold an **Assurance-Intimidation Demoralize** as a shoulder-check fake-out (Frightened 1), and split a **Flurry of Blows**: a **Crane Wing crit — 24 — killed the grabbed pirate**, and the follow-up came up **nat 1** — the rail rule fired, the DM narrated her dying counter-kick, and Xurl caught the railing on a **crit-success Grab an Edge**.
- **The bites and the break.** Snagg's jaws **crit (18)** left a frightened pirate Slowed 1 again; his **Snagging Strike** follow-up (16) took her head off — *"with two bites, the whole head went off."* A pirate landed one of three swings on Alder (8); another **failed her morale check** — *"'Fuck this!' And she jumped off"* — the first swimmer. The Navigator's pistol clipped Alder for 7. **Ruben's Inner Radiance Torrent** (60-ft line, angled at the rail) caught the **still-dangling captain** and the Navigator — both saved for 7 each, the captain at the DM's improvised **−2 "on the edge"** (argued for by Snagg). **Alder's chakram crit (29) + backswing (15)** killed another boarder, and his **Exploit Vulnerability** read the captain: strong Reflex, **weak Fort**.
- **The surrender cascade.** Only then did the captain **nat-20 his climb** back aboard — the DM waived **Alder's Implement's Interruption** for the natural 20 (Alder had spotted the trigger) and hand-waved the prone litigation — now armed with nothing but the pike; Snagg: *"Just fist fight, man. Come. Fist fight."* He shoved his last pirate at the party and ordered her in; her path crossed Snagg's reach — **Reactive Strike: jaws crit, 32, one bite, dead.** The captain watched his order get someone instant-killed, **failed his morale check, dropped his weapon**: *"I'm surrender! I'm surrender!"* Klorte, mid-rampage, **crit the last fighting pirate for 36 and hurled her into the sea** (*"I feel like this was supposed to be a hard fight"* — the DM, deflated), and the **Navigator surrendered** rather than share the ocean. The DM rolled one last morale check — for whether **Klorte** would stop killing. He did.
- **End state**: enemy captain and Navigator **captured**; Snagg's Perception 30 spotted the pirate who broke and jumped riding the waves out — plus **at least one more figure** swimming with her (likely one of the "Diver" shapes that never surfaced during the fight; a mid-fight *"You don't see shit"* Perception check was the only other trace of them). The party let them go — *"you're in the middle of the ocean, no one knows what happened,"* offered the DM, deadpan; Snagg declined: *"I'm just not going to go for kills here."* The rest were dead: seven of eight boarders. **Rodman was the only allied loss.** *"Good job, everyone. No one died. I mean, poor Rodman."*

**Damage boards** (per the roll log): **Snagg 124** across five jaws connections — **four of them crits** — plus three kills and the fight-ending morale break; **Klorte 74** (two crits, two pirates into the sea); **Alder 44** (chakram crit + backswing kill); **Xurl 24** (one crit kill, one captain over the rail, one Demoralize); **Ruben 14** by spell plus **28 healed** and **9 redirected** onto himself. Damage taken: Alder 33 (before 53 of healing), Snagg 19 + 3 self-inflicted (surgery), Xurl 12 (+8 from friendly surgery, after), Ruben 9 (all voluntary), Clive 10, Klorte 8, Rodman — all of it. Kill ledger: Snagg 3, Klorte 2, Xurl 1, Alder 1, one fled — **four of the seven were one-shots**, matching Xurl's post-mortem: *"four of them just got one shot."*
