The party returned to the Gauntlight's third floor to finish clearing it before tomorrow's twin deadlines — the pirate shipment and the midnight sacrifice of the captured dwarf. Behind the heavy stone doors of the floor's central chamber they found a reliquary desecrated into a shrine: smashed display cases, and a 15-foot statue of a woman stitched together from squares of preserved flesh. Kneeling before it was the cult's spellcasting ghoul priestess, who turned, smiled, and made the same offer as before — "Drop your weapon and join us." Ruben answered for the party: "Pitiful creatures. You will receive your judgment from Pharasma." The fight that followed was an 80-minute war of attrition against a caster who healed herself by eating pieces of the flesh statue, dropped Alder outright with an upcast three-action Harm (a hero-point reroll that came up nat 1 twice — "one in 400," said the DM), and finally died to Argon's guisarme: a called vertical cut past Alder's shoulder, straight down through her skull, for a 50-damage critical.

The middle of the night belonged to exploration — and to two mysteries walking the halls. An armored knight, visibly not human, twice crossed the party's path and ignored them; it left carrying a huge book taken from a hidden wall-library the party then found for themselves. Inside were the Gauntlight Blueprints and a Folio of Teleportation Research — Ruben's nat-20 find — which by night's end had the third floor's teleportation circle reactivated (one of nine in the dungeon). The Cracked Magic Mirror showed the room's past: twenty or thirty robed librarians kneeling before empty cases, and then, when the image cleared, devouring one another — the birth of the flesh-eating cult, centuries ago. A second fight against two Canker Cultists mid-ritual went the party's way (Ruben's Vitality Lash zapped one dead; Argon's 38-damage crit sent the other flying into the flesh mound), though it cost Argon a round paralyzed and Xurl a faceful of Phantom Pain.

Then, with the session winding down and the party healing up to leave, one last unopened door. Xurl said what everyone was thinking — "Surely that won't go wrong." Argon stepped forward: "Let me do it." Behind the door: a small corpse on a table, three ghouls feeding on it, and the ghast leader the veterans had once fled from, who looked up in annoyance — "You guys again? Why don't you leave me alone?" The party ran. Argon held the doorway so they could — took a 34-damage crit that dropped him, until Xurl called across the map, "You have Ferocity, right?", and the One Hundred Victories tattoo stood him back up at 10 HP — then failed the paralysis save. Pax made the call above the table: "No, just leave. Leave him. He's dead. Don't even try." The last the party heard, Argon was still fighting. The DM's ruling on ghoul custom: they won't eat him — they'll turn him. Argon Rath is gone. The party leveled to 5 on the walk out. Everyone at the table is now on their second character.

Timeline

Act I · RP · The Reliquary of Flesh

"Either way, you will be mine."

  • The corpses the party left behind yesterday were gone — "They are aware of our presence, probably" (Argon).
  • Session-opening banter: Argon clocked that Xurl's bioluminescent stripes trace the One Hundred Victories pattern — the same tattoo Argon wears in scar. Both carry it.
  • The door-duty exchange that would bookend the night: Xurl asked who goes first through doors. Argon: "You have more AC than me. You should be first, actually."
  • Behind the double stone doors: smashed, empty display cases under spotlights, and a 15-ft statue of a woman made of stitched flesh squares — the source of the room's stench.
  • The ghoul priestess and two ghoul attendants knelt before it. She repeated the cult's offer: "Drop your weapon and join us." Ruben spat back Pharasma's judgment; she smiled — "Either way, you will be mine" — and initiative was rolled.
Act II · Combat · Killing the Priestess

An 80-minute war of attrition

  • Alder dropped at 46 damage on a double-nat-1 hero-point reroll against her upcast three-action Harm — revived by Ruben's 30-point Heal.
  • The priestess healed herself by eating a piece of the flesh statue, went Quickened for a 4-action turn, and made Ruben her favorite target all night (79 damage taken).
  • Argon ended her with a called shot — blade curved past Alder, straight down through the skull — nat 20, 50 damage.
  • Ruben spat on the corpse; Xurl took her head off with Argon's axe afterward — "I'm not leaving her mostly intact in one room."
  • Mid-fight, a figure appeared in the doorway, laughed, and vanished — Ruben later identified it as the "jokester" that pranked the previous party during their fights. First contact for this group.
Act III · Mystery · The Knight

A creature best left ignored

  • On the way to the safe room, footsteps: a knight in full plate — visibly not human — walked past the battered party, ignoring them entirely, and went into the reliquary. They shut the secret door and holed up — "encountering an enemy we don't know anything about, while we're injured, would be suicide" (Ruben).
  • Argon, uneasy: "I wonder if we're doing something that we would regret later, though."
  • After the healing round it appeared a second time, now carrying a huge book. Xurl called out — "Where'd you find that book?" — no answer. The party debated jumping it and stood down: "The last time we picked a fight with a creature who was ignoring us, it went poorly" (Xurl). It descended the stairs toward the floor below and disappeared.
  • Perception checks connected it: both the knight and the book appear in the recovered tapestries. Argon, who joined after the tapestries were found, couldn't place it.
Act IV · Explore · The Hidden Library & the Mirror

What the knight left behind

  • Ruben found a hidden wall-niche library (nat-20 Perception) — the room the knight took its book from; a conspicuous gap remained in the shelf. "It seems Pharasma has smiled upon me very well today."
  • Recovered: the Folio of Teleportation Research and the Gauntlight Blueprints (a full-day downtime project — about the lighthouse itself, not the dungeon). Ruben's plan: bring them to Lady Wrin.
  • The Cracked Magic Mirror (cracking further with use) showed the room's past: 20–30 robed librarians kneeling before intact-but-empty cases — then devouring each other. The cult's origin. The DM awarded everyone a hero point.
  • End of night: Alder performed the activation ritual — a nat-20 Arcana ("That's funny. You activate this.") — and brought the third-floor teleportation circle online. The nat 20 bought extra insight: nine circles total, roughly one per floor, each needing on-site reactivation — and the ground-floor circle's connection is severed.
  • Ruben, shelving the loot: "Come. We have a sacrifice to find."
Act V · Combat · The Ritual Chamber

Bone figurines around a dead flesh-mound

  • A room with a dead flesh-mound (like the gibbering mouther, but dark red and dead) ringed by dozens of tiny bone figurines wrapped in hair — two Canker Cultists mid-ritual.
  • Corpse-stench at the door: DC 20 Fort — fail is sickened 1, success is immune for 24 hours. Ruben failed, then retched it off with a nat 20 ("Nat 20, baby").
  • Ruben's Vitality Lash killed one cultist ("No, not slash. Zap."); Argon crit the other for 38 and sent it flying over Xurl into the flesh pile.
  • Cost: Argon a full round paralyzed, and Xurl a faceful of Phantom Pain (persistent mental + a fight-long Sickened 1).
Act VI · Explore · The Mushroom Door

Not a fun mushroom

  • One more door: a soil-floored room with two Violet Fungi. One tentacle-slashed Xurl for 16 + 5 poison (Violet Rot, Enfeebled 1).
  • Nobody identified them — the whole table failed the Recall Knowledge; the DM narrated what they'd met on pre-campaign travels ("this is not a fun mushroom").
  • The party slammed the door and did not go back. Xurl: "Who puts hostile mushrooms in their basement? I ain't paid enough to deal with this bullshit."
  • Precautionary healing all around; the plan was: activate the circle, check one last door, and leave.
Act VII · Combat · The Last Door — Argon's Death

"Let me do it."

  • Heading out, one unexplored door. Xurl: "Surely that won't go wrong." Argon stepped forward: "Let me do it" — deliberately taking point because, as he said after, "at least I can tank if needed."
  • Inside: a small corpse on a table, three ghouls feeding, and the ghast leader — a creature the veterans had once fled from. It recognized them: "You guys again? Why don't you leave me alone?" Argon, who'd never met it: "I don't know what you're talking about. I don't know you."
  • The party fled; Argon held the doorway. A ghoul's jaws and claw, then the ghast leader's claw — critical, 34 damage. Argon dropped — until Xurl called across the map: "Is he done? You have Ferocity, right?" Argon had forgotten his own feat. One Hundred Victories + Orc Ferocity stood him back up at 10 HP.
  • But the crit had torn the guisarme from his hands, and the Paralysis save (DC 24) failed by 8. "I don't think it matters that much to me."
  • Xurl: "Pax, are we trying?" Pax: "No, just leave. Leave him. He's dead. Don't even try." Boots of Elvenkind — "and we're fuckin' runnin'!" — as Argon shouted "I'll hold him back!"
  • The DM, as the three escaped: "You hear that Argon keeps fighting, holding the ghouls as best as he can."
  • Recall Knowledge on the run: the cult will turn him into a ghoul, not eat him — "a couple days to save him." Xurl said the quiet part: "We're going to have to put him down next time we come down here."
  • Above table: Pax retired Argon on the spot. Every player has now lost a character in this campaign. The party leveled to 5. Next week: the pirate raid.

  • Alder read a Bless scroll and tagged Ghoul A with Exploit Vulnerability.
  • Argon (still Stupefied 1 from the Furnace): Intimidating Strike missed — hero point, bumped to a hit (13, Frightened 1) — realized Rage never auto-fired, switched it on (7 temp HP, +4 force retroactive), and his second swing crit Ghoul A for 42 — dead on the spot.

Round 2 — "Quick work of these two"

  • Ruben's Spiritual Armament hit Ghoul B for a max-roll 16; Guidance on Xurl. The priestess Dazed Ruben (9, failed by 1).
  • Xurl's Flurry of Blows finished Ghoul B (8+15) — "quick work of these two," Argon noted — and Xurl slid behind her.
  • Alder's Exploit Vulnerability on the priestess succeeded but learned only that she's not a regular ghoul — no weaknesses to find.

Rounds 3–7 — The Long War

  • Her three-action Harm (upcast rank 3): Alder hero-pointed the Fort save and rolled nat 1 twice ("one in 400"), took 46, dropped dying, and lost chakram and buckler; Argon failed (23); she healed herself 23 off the same pulse. Ruben's two-action Heal (30) brought Alder back.
  • She ate a piece of the flesh statue to heal 16 — which also scrubbed off the Clumsy 2 / −5 speed that Xurl's nat-20 crit card had pinned on her ("until healed," taken literally).
  • Her Grim Tendrils got argued down to a single target by Argon's line-geometry catch (Ruben saved, took 3); her Vampiric Touch forced Ruben to hero-point the save to a success ("that would have put me out") on a Quickened 4-action turn that also put a staff into his head (12).
  • Her second 3-action Harm went out at rank 2 — a DM misclick the table held to no take-backs — and still crit-caught Argon (22).
  • Xurl went 0-for-3 on grapples (two hero-pointed rerolls, both still crit fails, prone twice — he blamed Ruben for one) but Demoralized her twice (one success by exactly the tattoo's +1) and crit for 20. Alder's Implement's Interruption punished her casting once (18) and backfired once — a fumble card left him Clumsy 2; his nat-20 chakram crit offered bleed-or-double and he took double (27).
  • A rules summit established she's a unique creature, RK DC 32 — even Alder's Scholarly Recollection double-roll (28) couldn't crack her.

The Kill

  • After a full whiff cycle (Xurl's readied flank-Flurry missed twice — one nat 1 saved by Halfling Luck, still a miss by 1 — and Argon's nat-1 fumble card shoved him 10 feet), Argon stepped back into Xurl's flank and called the shot: blade curved past Alder, straight down through the top of her skull — nat 20, 50 damage, dead.
  • Ruben spat on her; Argon stabbed the chest "to make sure she's dead for good"; Xurl took the head.

Damage dealt: Argon 123 · Alder 58 · Xurl 53 · Ruben 26 — the priestess self-healed exactly 50 and put 79 into Ruben. Six hero points spent; only the two degree bumps changed an outcome.

Character Spotlight

Argon Rath · the Wall That Held — final session

  • The session bookended itself on him. At the door-duty question in the first five minutes he deferred — "You have more AC than me. You should be first, actually" (to Xurl). By the last door he'd stopped deferring: taking point so the squishier three didn't have to. He volunteered for the fatal door in plain words — "If you're going to open that door, let me open it. At least I can tank if needed" — and when it went wrong, his first and only instinct was "I'll hold him back! Run!"
  • Before the end, he was the session's executioner: a 42-damage crit that dropped a ghoul in fight 1, the 50-damage called-shot crit that killed the priestess ("curve my blade, just slightly past Alder, and slash straight down on the top of her head"), and the 38-damage crit that ended fight 2 by launching a cultist into the flesh mound. Every boss-tier enemy that died to a weapon died to his.
  • Sharp above the table too: talked the table through the counteract rules, argued the priestess's Grim Tendrils line down to a single target with a geometry catch, and litigated the incapacitation trait on his own paralysis before accepting the DM's "trust the system, please." Read the room at the last door before anyone rolled: "It's not too late to run away. Or maybe it is too late."
  • Fought the whole first fight Stupefied 1 — the Furnace's memory-burn from last session only lifted mid-session, giving him two clear-headed hours before the end.
  • His body — weaponless, paralyzed, at 10 HP — was left among four undead. The cult will raise him as one of their own.

Xurl · the Doctrine of Closed Doors

  • The party's conscience and its Cassandra. He spent the whole session warning about doors — "Don't open that door. Just keep walking. There's some bullshit behind all these doors, man" — and delivered the night's standing wisdom to talk the party out of jumping the knight: "The last time we picked a fight with a creature who was ignoring us, it went poorly."
  • Corpse-disposal doctrine: borrowed Argon's axe to behead the dead priestess and chucked the head into a far corner — "I'm not leaving her mostly intact in one room." In a dungeon where a pillar of void energy heals the undead, this is not paranoia, it's protocol.
  • In combat, the grapple curse continued (two hero-pointed grapples, both still failed — he blamed Ruben for one) but the fists never stopped: killed a ghoul with Flurry of Blows, landed a 20-damage crit on the priestess, stunned a Canker Cultist, and delivered "shockingly, we're going to punch him a bunch" as an actual mission statement.
  • At the end he was the one still watching Argon's sheet: when the 34 crit landed, it was Xurl who called "Is he done? You have Ferocity, right?" — Argon had forgotten the feat that stood him back up. Then, when Pax released them, he made the exit cleanly: Boots of Elvenkind, "and we're fuckin' runnin'!" — and, first of anyone, named what comes next: putting ghoul-Argon down.

Ruben Falmoor · Pharasma's Weary Instrument

  • Opened the session in full Pharasmin voice — "Pitiful creatures. You will receive your judgment from Pharasma" — and spent the boss fight as her favorite target: Chilling Darkness (21), Daze (9), a staff to the head (12), Vampiric Touch (hero-pointed down to survivable), and two Harms. He healed through all of it and still brought Alder back from dying with a 30-point Heal.
  • The night's best luck ran through him: the nat-20 Perception that found the hidden library and the blueprints — "It seems Pharasma has smiled upon me very well today" — and the mission-defining line as he shelved the loot: "Come. We have a sacrifice to find."
  • Kill credit with style: his Vitality Lash dropped a Canker Cultist, and he corrected the DM's narration in real time — "No, not slash. Zap."
  • Parented his kid Skylar off-mic through the entire boss fight — "Okay, Skylar, I'm sorry. It just tickles me, okay?" — without dropping a single heal.
  • Carried the retreat's grief: "Dammit, I hate this" — then, bitterly, the session's epitaph: "Every door we open is a freaking encounter. Why do we keep opening doors?"

Alder Ungram · the Scholar Who Already Knows How This Goes

  • The quiet one had the night's darkest arc. Dropped to dying by a double-nat-1 hero-point reroll against the priestess's Harm (46 damage) — his whole reaction: "Yeah, yeah. What the fuck?" When she wound up the second Harm, he deadpanned: "Yeah, I guess I'll die. That's what happened to me" — this is the player already on his second character.
  • The RK engine ran all night: Exploit Vulnerability on every enemy, and the party's standing orders delivered in his broken deadpan — "Don't use will save on them... They have much mind power." His Implement's Interruption punished the priestess's casting; his nat-20 chakram crit helped bring her down (offered bleed-or-double, he took double — 27).
  • He's the one holding the clock: "Tomorrow we need to save the person from the ritual." And he performed the nat-20 ritual that brought the third-floor teleportation circle online — the party's first real infrastructure in the megadungeon.
  • One of the two minds (with Xurl) that placed the armored knight and its stolen book in the recovered tapestries — the night's biggest lore connection, made on a post-fight Perception check.
