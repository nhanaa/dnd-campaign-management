## Session Summary

The session opened in the wreckage of the last one — Alder at Drained 3, Ruben at Drained 1, Xurl at one hit point and a werewolf curse — with Snagg running the surgery line. Risky Surgery is not a gentle art, and Xurl found that out at seven damage a go: *"I have one health left. Just punch me directly in my wound."* Ruben, deadpan: *"No, he cuts you open. He cuts you and then restitches you back up."* Snagg's whole bedside manner was *"It's just surgery. You never done surgery before?"* Half an hour of ten-minute cycles later the party was topped off, and the ceiling of the empty shrine did something it had never done before: the light gathered into a message — **"Gauntlight is a weapon, built to raze Absalom — it must be brought down!"** — and then, instead of streaking off deeper into the dungeon the way the veterans had always been told it did, it just sat there and faded. Ruben's read: *"It appears someone is speaking to us... Perhaps a weary soul who was cut down before their prime."* His verdict on being lectured: *"I need no embodied spirit to tell me my job."*

Ruben spent a natural 20 trying to purify Nhimbaloth's altar with sanctified water and got the worst possible good news — the water evaporated, the seven-fingered hand is tied straight to the goddess, and the gap between him and cleansing it isn't training, it's *scale*: **"You are looking at the deity itself."** He turned the failure into the session's biggest idea anyway — a theory that **the Gauntlight is powered by Nhimbaloth's own power**, channelled and stored here, which would explain why the undead are so thick through the whole catacomb. Then the party went exploring, and the hallway of upright stone coffins turned out to have a party trick — step within range and a lid goes **transparent**, showing the armoured corpse inside. Xurl spent several minutes lobbing severed crawling hands from last session down the corridor to map the trigger radius (*"I have plenty of hands to throw"*), got nothing, walked in himself, and got jump-scared by the first one anyway. *"Xurl's had a day of discoveries. First, no balls. Second, transparent stone."*

Then they forced a door that had been chained shut from the inside, and the night's real problem was waiting: a room where **every surface was chain** — ceiling, walls, floor — a caged spider-thing from the underworld, and a blue-skinned, hairless, pierced woman leaning on the far wall who wanted them gone and did not much care to negotiate about it. She never gave a name. She raised one hand and both cages unlocked; she waved another and the chains came off the walls to attack from anywhere in the room; she waved a third and chained the door open so it couldn't be closed. Her second hit on Snagg was a **critical for 30 that impaled him in place** — grabbed, anchored, unable to stop the doubled bleed until he escaped a DC 25 — and when he finally tore free she caught his eye and showed him **his own parents being beheaded in front of him**. Snagg dropped from full to **2 hit points**. Xurl grappled him and hauled him bodily out of the chains — *"I ain't leaving you to die twice in effectively two days"* — Ruben landed a 40-point Heal, and the party fought a withdrawal to the door and slammed it. In roughly forty minutes of table time and **two rounds**, the party dealt **thirteen damage**, all of it Alder's, none of it to her.

What followed was the most important nine minutes of the session, and it wasn't a fight. Xurl made the case to leave, and made it properly: *"Every time we've stopped to consider if we should fight or not, it's gone cataclysmically wrong when we try to fight."* / *"Our tank got chunked down from 100 to, like, 2 HP in one turn."* / *"If we look at that fight, we made how many attacks that entire fight? One?"* Ruben, who still had spell slots, argued to press on and then deliberately stood down twice — *"I've already put in my two cents... if y'all think we can't, I'm not going to argue."* Snagg mediated, asked for a summary he could act on, and called it. They went home. On the way out the sky was clear and the moon was up and the second floor is full of ceiling holes, so the plan became *"Step one: swaddle the halfling"* — Xurl sat on the stairs below the magic barrier, wrapped in three donated bedrolls, and made the Fortitude save against the call. The DM then offered him a deal: **resist it three times and he controls it, and gains the abilities.** Xurl didn't hesitate. *"Fuck it. I'm going to greet it. I want the werewolf abilities. It just sounds rad as hell."*

Otari in the morning was the session's best roleplay. **Kaledo** took Jaul Mezmin's necklace out of Snagg's hand, grabbed his shoulder, demanded to know where Jaul was, opened the locket onto the portrait of his dead wife — and started laughing, louder and louder, while he cried. He paid the 300 gold, said something gracious about good deeds, turned back to his stall, and then, very quietly, with his back to them: *"Thank you. Thank you for taking care of that son of a bitch."* Snagg's Society check filled in the rest — Kaledo used to be a nicer man. Then came a shopping run built entirely on Alder's undead expertise (*"the best thing is just destroying them in whatever way you're best at. So if you're good at biting, it's usually a pretty good option"*), the DM's off-the-cuff floor bestiary — Mitflits, Morlocks, a great many underworld things, Corpselights, and **demons, meaning "Belcorra definitely has some contract with demons"** — and a silver-versus-cold-iron decision that ended with Snagg in **silver handwraps** and Alder in cold iron. Snagg also learned that the level-5 armour rune he wants is a **week-long special order**, because the party once failed to save a delivery courier and Otari's express service died with him.

And on the road back, at the fork by the stone pond, a gnome druid named **Waluwin** stopped them and asked for Jaul Mezmin's body. Jaul was one of her circle. His lycanthropy was no secret among the druids; he hid himself in the forest every full moon precisely because he knew he couldn't hold it; Isla was in the wrong place on the wrong night. *"He's not a bad person."* Snagg, who had just been paid 300 gold for the man's necklace, immediately asked what was in it for them — and when the panicking gnome dug an **armour potency rune** out of her pocket, flipped 180 degrees in a single breath: *"Snagg is very strong. Snagg can bring two of them at the same time. No problem."*

They did not go back for the chain woman (*"No, let's leave her alone"*). Instead the empty sarcophagus at the end of the hall turned out to have a false bottom — Alder couldn't shift the lid, Snagg shoved him aside and lifted it one-handed (*"Got to train better, man. Got to go to the gym."* / *"Remind me not to wrestle you."*) — and behind it was a natural passage into a bone-strewn feeding ground with **two more of the spider-things**. This time the party had a Recall Knowledge, a fire weakness, a fresh set of dailies and a tank at full, and they took it apart: Alder killed the first with the chakram and finished the second with a **Demoralize into a 39-damage critical hit**. Past a chimney-less kitchen lay a **dining room** — chandeliers still burning, a banquet table still set, skeletons on the floor where they'd fallen trying to reach the doors, and **seven exits**. *"There has been much murder here. These bodies, they tried to get away. They couldn't."* The bodies got up. They were **Corpselights** — parasites wearing corpses, so that every time the party destroyed a body a blue light came out of its mouth and the thing was still alive. Four of them, four rounds, and four kills split around the table, the last one landed by a goblin who jumped off a dining chair to punch a floating light out of existence. Xurl's summary: *"It turns out when we don't have to keep fighting extreme enemies, we're doing okay."*

## Key Events

*The party spent the whole session on the level below the Nhimbaloth shrine — which the DM referred to in passing as **the fourth floor**, the first time that number has been attached to this area.*

### Act I — Surgery, and a Message That Stayed
- **Snagg ran the recovery line** off the last session's Extreme fight: Risky Surgery + Continual Recovery on ten-minute cycles, **four critical successes** across the opening half-hour. Xurl went from 1 HP to full (a natural 20 the DM upgraded to a full heal outright). Ruben treated Alder and refocused in parallel; Snagg patched himself.
- **Xurl's objection to the method** became the session's opening bit: *"So this is a rather important order of operations. I take seven damage. I have one health left. Just punch me directly in my wound."* Ruben explained the procedure; Snagg's total defence was *"It's just surgery. You never done surgery before?"* and *"You get used to it. Don't worry about it."*
- **Snagg's condition audit**: he cleared the party's wounded conditions, then flagged the real problem himself — *"Still drained, though... the problem is more the Drained 3 on Alder"* — and followed it with *"Alder, make sure you don't get bitten by anything, because you are Drained 3."*
- **The light-message.** The party had been *told* about this by the previous group; this was the first time they **saw it themselves**: **"Gauntlight is a weapon, built to raze Absalom — it must be brought down!"** Every previous time, the light re-formed and fled deeper into the dungeon and was followed. This time **it did not move at all** — it hung there and vanished.
- **The party's read**: Ruben — *"It appears someone is speaking to us... Perhaps a weary soul who was cut down before their prime"*; Alder — *"Maybe it's one of those heroes who was killed here"*; Xurl connected it to Wrin's blueprint briefing (*"it can transport large amounts of enemies directly to the city"*) and floated the reason it hasn't fired yet: *"Or it simply has not charged far enough yet. I can't imagine you could just wave your hand and raise an undead horde."* Snagg's contribution: *"Yeah, Snagg will pretend that he understands. He doesn't understand shit."*
- **Ruben's attempt on the altar.** He used **Sanctify Water** on his waterskin, poured it over the seven-fingered stone hand and around its base, and prayed to Pharasma. **Natural 20 on the Religion check.** The water beaded, ran into the gaps — and **evaporated**. Three findings: *seven* is Nhimbaloth's number; the altar holds power "way beyond your ability"; and the gap isn't a training problem — **"you are looking at the deity itself."** Ruben: *"It's almost as if this monument has a direct link to Nhimbaloth herself. We must be very wary."*
- **Ruben's theory, built on the spot** — and the most consequential idea anyone had all night: *"I can propose a theory that this Gauntlight itself is, well, could be powered by **Nhimbaloth's power herself**. Someone was able to possibly channel it and store it here. That could be the reason why the undead is very prominent in these entire catacombs."*
- Xurl immediately found the corroboration: one of the recovered paintings showed **Belcorra born under seven stars** — seven being Nhimbaloth's number. *"She could be blessed or a champion of some sort."* Ruben: *"Or a priestess."* Xurl: *"That would fit with the entire chapel on the ground floor."* Alder's contribution was the warning: *"You should be careful. The influence of Nhimbaloth is very powerful here."*

### Act II — The Hallway of Transparent Coffins
- The corridor off the shrine is lined with **upright stone sarcophagi**, and stepping close makes a lid go **fully transparent** — you can still feel the stone, you just see through it. Inside: skeletons in armour *at least a couple of hundred years old*, each in a different style, one with a shattered skull (the fatal wound), the others unreadable.
- **Xurl's science experiment**: rather than walk into another sarcophagus ambush, he threw **severed crawling hands** from last session's fight at them one at a time to find the trigger radius — three throws, nothing, *"I have plenty of hands to throw."* The DM offered an escalation (*"you can try to cut your hand off and throw it"*), Xurl declined on professional grounds (*"Monks famously not known for kneading their hands"*), stepped forward himself, and got jump-scared by the lid going clear. *"Who makes see-through stone?"*
- **Alder's cracked mirror** got its one use of the night on the shrine: a vision of **Belcorra kneeling in prayer** at the altar, and nothing else. *"Eh, maybe it just went into some random day of prayer."* Xurl: *"Not every attempt can be successful. Bound to get some duds eventually."*
- At the end of the hall: **stairs going down**, sealed behind something. Alder called the turn — *"Oh, hell no. You know what? We should turn around"* — and the party went for the side door instead.
- Snagg's audit of the veterans, after learning nobody had searched the shrine for secret doors: *"And I thought you guys experienced. What's going on?"* Xurl, deflecting on his own behalf (third person, as always): *"He's also a little baby adventurer. He doesn't know what he's doing yet."*

### Act III — The Chain Room *(see Combat Encounters)*
- **The door wouldn't open** — something metal was holding it, and Snagg forced it on the third try. Behind it: **chain over every surface of the room**, two cages, a hissing creature in one.
- **Alder's Recall Knowledge named them: Scalathrax** — not of this world, out of the **underworld**, famous for their violence, spider-shaped, and *intelligent enough to talk.*
- A woman's voice from the dark, in Common, in *"the most laziest way you can think of"*: **get out.** Ruben cast **Light** — and there she was: **blue skin, no hair, piercings across her face, chain wound over her whole body,** leaning on the back wall. Alder, reaching for a precedent: *"Are you like the red one from upstairs? Demon?"*
- Her reply to the light was *"Why? Why do you have to do this?"* — and then initiative. **She never gave a name.**
- **What she could do**: raise a hand and both cages unlock; wave and the chains rip off the walls to strike **anywhere in the room**; wave again and the chains bolt the open door to the wall so it can't be shut. Snagg, on the receiving end of Impaling Chain: *"What kind of fucking dumbass ability is that?"*
- **The party ran.** Snagg called it at *"Jesus. Time to run"*; Xurl answered *"Yep, time to use the funnel."* They executed a fighting withdrawal into the corridor, Xurl closed the door on her, something hammered on it for a while, and then stopped.

### Act IV — The Nine-Minute Argument
This is the load-bearing scene of the session and it was entirely a conversation.
- **Xurl's case to leave**, made in stages: *"Every time we've stopped to consider if we should fight or not, it's gone cataclysmically wrong when we try to fight."* / *"It feels like every time we pick up debuffs and spend our dailies, and then we try and pick another fight, we get kicked in the teeth and someone dies."* / *"All of my defensives have already been spent, and our tank got chunked down from 100 to, like, 2 HP in one turn."* / — the closer — *"The problem is, we go from 100 to 0 in one or two turns, and we can't kill anything in those one or two turns, because after that, everyone else is stuck on defensive healing duty. So if we look at that fight, we made how many attacks that entire fight? One?"*
- **Ruben's case to stay**: he still had half his spell slots and *"a couple more dailies I haven't used yet."* He made it twice and then explicitly stood down both times: *"I've already put in my two cents... if you all think that we can't, then I'm not going to argue."*
- **Snagg mediated and then decided.** He'd already framed the actual constraint before the argument started — *"I don't think that matters. What matters is when we run into another encounter, I think we're just going to not have enough gas to go through"* — stepped out mid-debate rather than steamroll it, and came back and asked for a summary he could act on: *"I need some context. Can you guys give me some context?"* Xurl's one-sentence version — *"I've burned every single defensive layer I have. I don't want to open another door. Twan hasn't burned all of his spell slots yet, so he kind of wants to open another door"* — plus Snagg's own accounting (*"I have used up all my Battle Medicine on you guys"*) settled it. **They left.**
- **The tail of the argument turned into tactics**: Xurl pitched pre-buffing before kicking doors (*"why not just pre-cast them before we kick the door? We found a fight in almost every fucking room"*); Ruben pushed back on burning slots into empty rooms; and **Snagg killed it on the rules** — *"if we're talking about rules as written, you cannot pre-buff with a one minute spell."* Ruben's parallel: *"Just like how monks can't get into stances outside of combat."*

### Act V — The Moon, and Xurl's Decision
- Going up, the party sent someone other than Xurl to check the sky first (*"Yeah, obviously"*). The roll came up **clear** — moon visible — and the second floor is a colander: a **hole in the ceiling** in one room, a **window** in the next. Nowhere safe.
- **"Step one: swaddle the halfling."** Xurl proposed being wrapped in bedrolls; Ruben refined it (one on the body, one over the head, tied); Ruben and Alder both donated theirs. Xurl settled on the **stairs below the magic barrier** with a bedroll and something to dive under, and Ruben posted up nearby to watch him.
- **The save.** The DM handed Xurl a **Fortune** effect on the Fortitude save — roll twice, keep the better. He made it. *"There's no direct moonlight, but you can feel it. There's a calling to you... You resist the urge to just run out and answer whatever it is calling to you."*
- **The DM's offer**: resist it **three times** and Xurl **controls the curse and gains additional abilities** — one down, two to go, with the honest caveat that he doesn't know whether the campaign will run three in-game months. Removing it instead means **Cleanse Affliction upcast to rank 4**, which needs a **level-8 caster in Absalom**; nobody local can do it. Also permanent, starting now: **Xurl has weakness to silver.** (*"I'm sure that won't be relevant."*)
- **Xurl chose the curse.** *"Fuck it. I'm going to greet it. I want the werewolf abilities. It just sounds rad as hell."*
- The night's rest restored everyone to full, gave the party **a hero point each**, cleared Ruben's Drained entirely and knocked Alder's from 3 to 2.

### Act VI — Otari: The Bounty, the Shopping, and the Druid
- **Kaledo and the locket.** In the early-morning market crush, Snagg presented Jaul Mezmin's necklace. Kaledo grabbed his shoulder and demanded to know where he'd found it — *"Where is that son of a bitch? Where is he?"* Xurl: *"Very dead."* Snagg: *"Calm down. Let's talk."* Alder: *"If you want to find him, you can go down there."* Kaledo took the necklace, opened it onto the portrait of **Isla**, and **began laughing louder and louder while crying**. He paid the **300 gp**, thanked them for a ton of good deeds, turned back to his stall — and then, quietly, back turned: *"Thank you. Thank you for taking care of that son of a bitch."*
- **Alder's Perception and Snagg's Society** both confirmed it: genuine. And Snagg knows the man — **Kaledo was nice to everyone before his wife's death.** The incident changed him.
- **The undead lecture.** Snagg asked Alder what undead are weak to. Alder's own answer was spirit-touching effects and holy water, with silver for lycanthropes; the DM filled in the rest as knowledge Alder already has — *"that is your specialty, right?"* — namely that undead as a category have **no universal weakness** and a very long immunity list. Alder's practical advice: *"Really, the best thing is just destroying them in whatever way you're best at. So if you're good at biting, it's usually a pretty good option. I'll let you know whenever it's not, you know?"*
- **The DM's floor bestiary, given freely**: **Mitflits** on floor 1, **Morlocks** on floor 2, a great many creatures **from the underworld** (the chain woman included), **Corpselights** — *"like Will-o'-Wisps"*, and therefore *usually immune to magic* — and **demons**, which produced the session's biggest lore drop: **"So Belcorra definitely has some contract with demons."**
- **Silver vs. cold iron.** Both 40 gp. The DM checked and reported demons and fiends run mostly **silver** — so **Snagg bought silver handwraps** and **Alder took cold iron** to cover the gap. (Snagg's own aside on Corpselights being magic-immune: *"This is more a problem with Ruben than with me."*)
- **Rune economics**, worked out at the table: transferring a rune costs **10% of that rune's price** and takes **one day per rune** — free if you do it yourself with Magical Crafting and expert Crafting. **Alder transferred his +1 striking onto a composite shortbow** (6 gp 5 sp). Snagg came up **3 gold short**; **Alder covered it** (*"I can give you three gold. That's fine."*), and the party sold a spare item — explicitly *not* Alder's striking weapon (*"No, that's what I'm using"*) — and split **12 gp each**.
- **The delivery problem.** The armour potency rune is a **level 5 item**, and Otari only stocks up to level 4 — it's a **special order taking a week** (Otari to Absalom and back is three days each way). There *used to be* an express service at three days for a premium. There isn't now: **the party failed the side quest to save the courier.** *"The person is dead, so it's no longer available."*
- **Waluwin at the Stone Pond** *(spelling tentative)*. On the road back, at the fork between the graveyard and the Gauntlight, a **gnome druid of the Stone Pond** stopped them. Snagg knew of her: Otari and the druids used to be on good terms, and the relationship broke **one year ago when Jaul killed Kaledo's wife**.
- **Her ask**: bring Jaul's body back for a proper burial. **Her case**: Jaul was one of their circle; *"among our druids, he's a werewolf — it's not a secret to us. He's been really open to us. He's able to control himself most of the time."* Every full moon he deliberately went into the forest to hide, because he knew he'd lose control. **Isla was simply there.** *"He's not a bad person."* / *"We are not trying to do anything, any funny business. I just want to give him a proper burial."*
- **The negotiation.** Xurl raised the logistics (*"He's kind of a big dude"* — gesturing at the party; the DM, surveying them: *"Two short people. Two nerdy-looking guys"*). Snagg went straight for the fee — *"we might need some bonus, you know?"* — and Xurl set the ask (*"if we got anything which helped with killing monsters, that'd be great. Place is infested"*). She panicked, dug through her pocket, and produced **an armour potency rune**: *"That's all I have."* **Snagg's attitude flipped instantly** — the DM called it 180 degrees — *"Yeah, don't worry. Snagg is very strong. Snagg can bring two of them at the same time. No problem."* She'll be at the stone ring pond by the fork.

### Act VII — The Barrier and the False Bottom
- **They did not go back for her.** Snagg: *"Do we want to storm the room again?"* Ruben: *"No, let's leave her alone."*
- **The barrier over the down-stairs.** Ruben detected magic on it; with no Arcana at all he asked to substitute **Religion at a penalty** and got it: the fog is **inert right now**, but **powerful** — he couldn't identify its nature, its activation conditions, or dispel it. *"For all we know, it could activate as soon as we step into it."*
- **The decision to go sideways.** Ruben framed it and handed it over: *"It is a risk, but we still need to go down further to reach the core of this place. I leave it in your hands."* Alder: *"I feel like there's still a lot of information we could find on this floor."* Ruben: *"Very well. Then we will return to this once we have more info."*
- **The empty sarcophagus.** One lid went transparent on **nothing** — no corpse. Alder reached in, found a **gap at the bottom**, and needed the lid off to get at it. He failed the Athletics check; Snagg stepped in — *"You got this, little one"* — and the DM played it straight: *"this tiny little goblin just steps, pushes you aside... and then, without any effort."* Snagg: *"Got to train better, man. Got to go to the gym."* Alder: *"Remind me not to wrestle you."*
- Underneath: a **false bottom over a natural passage**, opening into a wide chamber with **bones and rotten meat scattered across the floor** — and two creatures already in it.

### Act VIII — The Feeding Ground *(see Combat Encounters)*
- Two more **Scalathrax**, in what the party correctly read as their **larder** (*"they were just eating people. This is like a feeding ground for them"*).
- The rematch went the other way: Exploit Vulnerability up front, **fire weakness** exploited all fight, Snagg holding the line with Snagging Strike and Combat Grab, and Alder closing with a **Demoralize into a 39-damage crit**.
- New rules discovery mid-grapple: **Oily Scales** — the Scalathrax treat Escape results one degree better, which turned a plain success into a critical success and popped Snagg's Combat Grab. *"This looks more like a crab than a spider to you... there's some very sticky oil on the shell."*
- Alder's Exploit Vulnerability on the second one read **Will as its weakest save** and he immediately passed the ball: *"Hey, Ruben. They're not the strongest-willed creatures, so maybe you can control them."* Ruben's honest self-assessment: *"He's not a controller. He's a debuffer."*

### Act IX — The Dining Room *(see Combat Encounters)*
- Past the passage: an **abandoned kitchen** with a cast-iron stove and **no chimney** — the DM noted the smoke would have filled the whole room when it was in use — and a skeleton on the floor. Nothing to loot.
- Through the east door: a **formal dining room**, still lit by **two burning chandeliers**, with a long banquet table, chairs (some upright, some fallen), a full setting of empty plates, **skeletons on the floor**, and **seven doors**.
- Ruben read the room before it read them: *"There has been much murder here. These bodies, they tried to get away. They couldn't."*
- Then **the corpses stood up** — and turned out not to be the enemy at all. They were **Corpselights**: a light that wears a body. Every time a corpse dropped, a **blue light came out of its mouth** and the thing kept fighting. Xurl called it inside a round: *"It's a parasite which is controlling a body. You killed the body, you didn't hit the parasite."*
- **All four destroyed**, and the session ended on Snagg jumping onto a dining chair to punch the last one out of the air.
- **The wind-down.** Snagg gambled the last heal on an expert DC and crit it, maxing the Risky Surgery damage roll on the way — Xurl: *"I like how you maxed the damage roll. Just out of spite."* Then the night's last exchange: *"Is Snagg a civilized goblin, or is he doing a little bit of snacking while doing surgery?"* — *"He's using his mouth to, like, carry the tools."* — *"I don't know if that's better or worse than what I said. Probably better. Very hygienic."* The party ended standing in the dining room with six doors unopened. **No cliffhanger.**

## Combat Encounters

### Encounter 1 — The Chain Room *(≈21:12–21:52, **2 rounds**)* — **the party retreated**

**Enemies**: the chain woman (unnamed; level 7 elite, **110 HP, AC 26, regeneration 10 stopped only by holy or silver**, weak holy 5 / silver 5, immune cold, chain +19) + **2× Scalathrax** — and they were *not* identical tokens: one at **60 HP / AC 21 / attack +13**, the other at **75 HP / AC 23 / attack +15**.

**Initiative**: chain woman 31 · Alder 29 · Scalathrax (the bigger one) 29 · Ruben 28 · Snagg 26 · Scalathrax 22 · Xurl 22 → corrected to **23** mid-fight when Ben caught a missing +1 from Scout. That moved him ahead of **the smaller Scalathrax only** — he still acted sixth of seven. (*"I thought it might. I was suspicious."* — the DM: *"You are correct."*)

| Stat | Value |
|---|---|
| Rounds | **2** |
| Damage dealt by the party | **13** (all Alder) |
| Damage dealt to the chain woman | **0** |
| Damage taken | Snagg **82** (+8 shield-blocked) · Xurl **50** · Alder 0 · Ruben 0 |
| Hero points spent | **0** — the party had none left |
| Outcome | **Fighting withdrawal; door closed** |

**Round 1**
- **Chain woman**: raised a hand — **both cages unlocked**. Waved — a chain came off the wall and hit **Snagg** (+19 vs AC 25, hit by 3) for **22**; the sturdy shield ate 8 and took 14, so 14 got through, plus 1d6 persistent bleed. Third action: chained the open door to the wall so it **couldn't be shut**.
- **Scalathrax**: put its mouth to a hole in the cage and **Sprayed Toxic Oil** (DC 21 Reflex). **Xurl failed** (20), **Snagg failed** (17), **Alder critically succeeded** (32, taking nothing). 13 poison each — plus the oil rider: **weakness to fire for a minute, and drop your weapon on a critical fumble.** Xurl, unarmed: *"Now it's going to be a weird interaction with fist weapons."*
- **Alder**: **Exploit Vulnerability**. He rolled a critical success (31) — and the DM **voided it over a targeting mix-up and had him roll again**, landing a plain **success by 1** (20). So he got the **highest weakness only — fire** — not the full stat block. He popped a **mirror duplicate inside the cage** and delivered the intel in stereo: *"You hear from both of my mouths at the same time: those guys, they're pretty weak to fire. I don't like being burnt, so chuck whatever you got at them. But not poisonous things — I don't care about that."* Chakram hit for 8 → **13 with the weakness**. *(The only damage the party dealt all fight — and note the weakness rider from Exploit Vulnerability applies to **Alder's own Strikes**, not the party's. ## Character Spotlight

### Snagg · the Goblin Who Decides
- **He was the tiebreaker, and he ran the room to get there.** The nine-minute retreat argument had a player who wanted to leave and a player who wanted to stay, and Snagg did three things in order: he named the real constraint before anyone else (*"What matters is when we run into another encounter, I think we're just going to not have enough gas to go through"*), he **physically left the table mid-debate rather than lean on it** (*"Okay. You guys decide, I'm going to go to the bathroom"*), and when he came back he asked for the argument to be summarised so he could actually rule on it: *"I need some context. Can you guys give me some context?"* Then he added the one fact only he had — *"I have used up all my Battle Medicine on you guys"* — and called it. That is a triage lead, not a tank.
- **He also killed the bad plan.** When the post-mortem turned to pre-buffing before kicking doors, Snagg was the one who knew why it doesn't work: *"If we're talking about rules as written, you cannot pre-buff with a one minute spell."* Same instinct later stopped Ruben from putting a patient into an hour-long Treat Wounds lockout that Snagg's Continual Recovery couldn't shorten.
- **The medic engine ran all night.** Four critical-success Treat Wounds cycles before the first fight, **ten across the session**; Battle Medicine and **Doctor's Visitation** mid-combat while Frightened; three more Risky Surgery cycles after the feeding ground and one at the end of the night. He also ran the condition audit nobody asked for — *"You guys shouldn't be wounded anymore, by the way. Still drained, though... the problem is more the Drained 3 on Alder."* His entire attitude toward stabbing his patients as a healing technique: *"It's just surgery. You never done surgery before?"* / *"You get used to it. Don't worry about it."*
- **He ate the whole night's incoming.** **112 points of combat damage, plus 24 more absorbed by the sturdy shield across three Shield Blocks.** In the chain room alone he took **82** — a 22-point chain, 13 poison, a **30-damage critical** that anchored him so he couldn't stop the bleeding, the 12-point bleed itself, and a leg quill — **from full to 2 hit points**, with a vision of **his own parents being executed** on the way down. His running commentary was not dignified — *"My 2 HP. Woohoo!"* / *"Fuck yeah, leave me. I'm gonna die"* / *"What kind of fucking dumbass ability is that?"* — and it did not stop him from being the last one out of the room.
- **And then he was the party's top scorer in the dining room**: **99 damage and two kills**, one of them a **Reactive Strike** on a wisp trying to fly clear, the other the finish — a run-up, a jump onto a dining chair, and a punch through a floating light. **143 damage on the night**, in the two fights he was allowed to actually fight.

And the standing complaint, delivered at least three separate times: *"Oh my god, when will I ever get a flank again?"*
