Session Summary

The session opened in the wreckage of the last one — Alder at Drained 3, Ruben at Drained 1, Xurl at one hit point and a werewolf curse — with Snagg running the surgery line. Risky Surgery is not a gentle art, and Xurl found that out at seven damage a go: "I have one health left. Just punch me directly in my wound." Ruben, deadpan: "No, he cuts you open. He cuts you and then restitches you back up." Half an hour of ten-minute cycles later the party was topped off, and the ceiling did something the veterans had only been told about: the light gathered into a message — "Gauntlight is a weapon, built to raze Absalom — it must be brought down!" — and then, instead of streaking off deeper into the dungeon the way it always had, it just sat there and faded.

Ruben spent a natural 20 trying to purify Nhimbaloth's altar with sanctified water and got the worst possible good news — the water evaporated, seven is her number, and the gap between him and cleansing it isn't training, it's scale: "You are looking at the deity itself." He turned the failure into the session's biggest idea anyway — a theory that the Gauntlight is powered by Nhimbaloth's own power, channelled and stored here, which would explain why the undead run so thick through the whole catacomb. Xurl found the corroboration inside a minute: Belcorra, in one of the recovered paintings, born under seven stars.

Then they forced a door chained shut from the inside, and the night's real problem was waiting: a room where every surface was chain, a caged spider-thing from the underworld, and a blue-skinned, hairless, pierced woman leaning on the far wall who wanted them gone. She never gave a name. She raised one hand and both cages unlocked; she waved another and the chains came off the walls to strike from anywhere in the room; she waved a third and chained the door open so it couldn't be shut. Her reaction to Snagg's attempt to leave was a critical for 30 that impaled him in place — grabbed, anchored, unable to stop the doubled bleed until he escaped a DC 25 — and when he tore free she caught his eye and showed him his own parents being beheaded in front of him. Snagg went from full to 2 hit points. Xurl grappled him and hauled him out — "I ain't leaving you to die twice in effectively two days" — Ruben landed a 40-point Heal, and the party fought a withdrawal to the door and slammed it. In two rounds the party lost 132 hit points and dealt thirteen damage, all of it Alder's, none of it to her.

What followed was the most important nine minutes of the session, and it wasn't a fight. Xurl made the case to leave and made it properly: "Every time we've stopped to consider if we should fight or not, it's gone cataclysmically wrong when we try to fight." / "If we look at that fight, we made how many attacks that entire fight? One?" Ruben, who still had spell slots, argued to press on and then deliberately stood down twice. Snagg mediated — stepping away from the table rather than lean on it — came back, asked for a summary he could rule on, and called it. On the way out the sky was clear and the moon was up, so the plan became "Step one: swaddle the halfling." Xurl sat on the stairs below the barrier in three donated bedrolls, made the save against the call, and was then offered a deal: resist three times and he controls the curse. He didn't hesitate. "Fuck it. I'm going to greet it. I want the werewolf abilities."

Otari in the morning was the session's best roleplay. Kaledo took Jaul Mezmin's necklace out of Snagg's hand, grabbed his shoulder, opened the locket onto the portrait of his dead wife — and started laughing, louder and louder, while he cried. He paid the 300 gold, turned back to his stall, and then, very quietly, with his back to them: "Thank you. Thank you for taking care of that son of a bitch." Then a shopping run built on Alder's undead expertise, the DM's off-the-cuff floor bestiary — and the lore drop of the night: "Belcorra definitely has some contract with demons." Snagg bought silver handwraps; Alder took cold iron. And on the road back, a gnome druid named Waluwin asked for Jaul Mezmin's body, because Jaul was one of hers, hid himself every full moon, and Isla was simply in the wrong place. Snagg asked what was in it for them, and flipped 180 degrees the instant she produced an armour potency rune.

They did not go back for the chain woman. Instead a false-bottomed sarcophagus opened onto a bone-strewn feeding ground with two more spider-things — and this time the party had knowledge, dailies, and a tank at full, and took them apart, Alder closing with a Demoralize into a 39-damage critical hit. Past a chimney-less kitchen lay a dining room: chandeliers still burning, a banquet table still set, skeletons where they'd fallen trying to reach the doors, and seven exits. The bodies got up. They were Corpselights — a light that wears a corpse, so every body they dropped simply released the thing still fighting. Four of them, three rounds, four kills split around the table, three of them landed on wisps trying to flee. Xurl's summary: "It turns out when we don't have to keep fighting extreme enemies, we're doing okay."

Timeline

Act I · Recovery · Surgery and a Message That Stayed

The Empty Shrine, the Morning After

  • Snagg ran the recovery line off the last session's Extreme fight — Risky Surgery + Continual Recovery on ten-minute cycles, four critical successes in the opening half-hour (ten across the night). Xurl went from 1 HP to full on a natural 20 the DM upgraded to a complete heal.
  • Xurl's objection became the opening bit: "So this is a rather important order of operations. I take seven damage. I have one health left. Just punch me directly in my wound." Snagg's entire defence: "It's just surgery. You never done surgery before?" and "You get used to it. Don't worry about it."
  • Snagg's own condition audit named the real problem: "You guys shouldn't be wounded anymore, by the way. Still drained, though... the problem is more the Drained 3 on Alder" — followed by "Alder, make sure you don't get bitten by anything, because you are Drained 3."
  • The light-message. The party had been told about this by the previous group; this was the first time they saw it: "Gauntlight is a weapon, built to raze Absalom — it must be brought down!" Every previous time the light re-formed and fled deeper and was followed. This time it did not move at all — it hung there and vanished.
  • The party's read: Ruben — "It appears someone is speaking to us... Perhaps a weary soul who was cut down before their prime"; Alder — "Maybe it's one of those heroes who was killed here"; Xurl on why it hasn't fired yet — "Or it simply has not charged far enough yet. I can't imagine you could just wave your hand and raise an undead horde." Snagg's contribution: "Yeah, Snagg will pretend that he understands. He doesn't understand shit."
  • Ruben's attempt on the altar. Sanctify Water on his own waterskin, poured over the seven-fingered stone hand with a prayer to Pharasma. Natural 20 Religion. The water beaded, ran into the gaps — and evaporated. Three findings: seven is Nhimbaloth's number; the altar holds power "way beyond your ability"; and the gap isn't training — "you are looking at the deity itself."
  • Ruben's theory, built on the spot — the most consequential idea anyone had all night: "I can propose a theory that this Gauntlight itself could be powered by Nhimbaloth's power herself. Someone was able to possibly channel it and store it here. That could be the reason why the undead is very prominent in these entire catacombs."
  • Xurl's corroboration: a recovered painting showed Belcorra born under seven stars. "She could be blessed or a champion of some sort." Ruben: "Or a priestess." Xurl: "That would fit with the entire chapel on the ground floor." Alder's contribution was the warning: "You should be careful. The influence of Nhimbaloth is very powerful here."
Act II · Exploration · The Hallway of Transparent Coffins

See-Through Stone

  • The corridor off the shrine is lined with upright stone sarcophagi, and stepping close makes a lid go fully transparent — you can still feel the stone, you just see through it.
  • Inside: skeletons in armour at least a couple of hundred years old, each in a different style — one in heavy plate, one in leather. The first had a shattered skull (the fatal wound); the others gave nothing away.
  • Xurl's science experiment: rather than walk into another sarcophagus ambush, he threw severed crawling hands from last session's fight down the corridor one at a time to find the trigger radius — three throws, nothing. "I have plenty of hands to throw."
  • The DM offered an escalation ("you can try to cut your hand off and throw it"); Xurl declined on professional grounds — "Monks famously not known for kneading their hands" — stepped forward himself, and got jump-scared by the lid going clear anyway. "Who makes see-through stone?"
  • Alder volunteered the cracked mirror unprompted ("I also have the cracked mirror if we want to check what's up with this altar") and got a vision of Belcorra kneeling in prayer — and nothing else. "Eh, maybe it just went into some random day of prayer." Xurl: "Not every attempt can be successful. Bound to get some duds eventually."
  • Alder was also the one who remembered the party is supposed to be doing something while it walks: "By the way, should we be doing exploration activities?"
  • At the end of the hall: stairs going down, sealed behind something. Alder called the turn — "Oh, hell no. You know what? We should turn around" — and the party took the side door instead.
  • Snagg's audit of the veterans, on learning nobody had searched the shrine for secret doors: "And I thought you guys experienced. What's going on?" Xurl, deflecting on his own behalf (third person, as always): "He's also a little baby adventurer. He doesn't know what he's doing yet."
Act III · Combat · The Chain Room

"Get Out"

  • The door wouldn't open — something metal was holding it — and Snagg forced it on the third try. Behind it: chain over every surface of the room, two cages, a hissing creature in one.
  • Alder's Recall Knowledge named them: Scalathrax — not of this world, out of the underworld, famous for their violence, spider-shaped, and intelligent enough to talk. Ruben's first impression: "That's a whole lot of nope on a rope."
  • A woman's voice from the dark, in Common, in "the most laziest way you can think of": get out. Ruben cast Light — "Well then, let there be light" — and there she was: blue skin, no hair, piercings across her face, chain wound over her whole body, leaning on the back wall.
  • Alder, reaching for a precedent: "Are you like the red one from upstairs? Demon?" And when she demanded to know why they'd lit her room up: "It's dark in here. What did you expect?"
  • Her reply was "Why? Why do you have to do this?" — and then initiative. She never gave a name.
  • What she could do: raise a hand and both cages unlock; wave and the chains rip off the walls to strike anywhere in the room; wave again and the chains bolt the open door to the wall so it can't be shut. Snagg, on the receiving end of Impaling Chain: "What kind of fucking dumbass ability is that?"
  • The party ran. Snagg called it at "Jesus. Time to run"; Xurl answered "Yep, time to use the funnel." They executed a fighting withdrawal into the corridor, Xurl closed the door on her, something hammered on it for a while — and then stopped.
Act IV · Roleplay · The Nine-Minute Argument

The Load-Bearing Scene of the Session

  • Xurl's case to leave, made in stages: "Every time we've stopped to consider if we should fight or not, it's gone cataclysmically wrong when we try to fight." / "It feels like every time we pick up debuffs and spend our dailies, and then we try and pick another fight, we get kicked in the teeth and someone dies." / "All of my defensives have already been spent, and our tank got chunked down from 100 to, like, 2 HP in one turn."
  • The closer: "The problem is, we go from 100 to 0 in one or two turns, and we can't kill anything in those one or two turns, because after that, everyone else is stuck on defensive healing duty. So if we look at that fight, we made how many attacks that entire fight? One?"
  • Ruben's case to stay: half his spell slots and "a couple more dailies I haven't used yet." He made it twice and explicitly stood down both times: "I've already put in my two cents... if you all think that we can't, then I'm not going to argue."
  • Snagg mediated and then decided. He'd already framed the constraint before the argument started — "What matters is when we run into another encounter, I think we're just going to not have enough gas to go through" — stepped away from the table mid-debate rather than steamroll it, and came back asking for a summary he could act on: "I need some context. Can you guys give me some context?"
  • Xurl's one-sentence version — "I've burned every single defensive layer I have. I don't want to open another door. Twan hasn't burned all of his spell slots yet, so he kind of wants to open another door" — plus Snagg's own accounting ("I have used up all my Battle Medicine on you guys") settled it. They left.
  • The tail turned into tactics: Xurl pitched pre-buffing before kicking doors ("why not just pre-cast them before we kick the door? We found a fight in almost every fucking room"); Ruben pushed back on burning slots into empty rooms; and Snagg killed it on the rules — "if we're talking about rules as written, you cannot pre-buff with a one minute spell." Ruben's parallel: "Just like how monks can't get into stances outside of combat."
Act V · Roleplay · The Moon, and Xurl's Decision

Step One: Swaddle the Halfling

  • Going up, the party sent someone other than Xurl to check the sky first ("Yeah, obviously"). The roll came up clear — moon visible — and the second floor is a colander: a hole in the ceiling in one room, a window in the next. Nowhere safe.
  • "Step one: swaddle the halfling." Xurl proposed being wrapped in bedrolls; Ruben refined it (one on the body, one over the head, tied); Ruben and Alder both donated theirs. Xurl settled on the stairs below the magic barrier, with something to dive under, and Ruben posted up nearby to watch him.
  • The save. The DM handed Xurl a Fortune effect on the Fortitude save — roll twice, keep the better. He made it. "There's no direct moonlight, but you can feel it. There's a calling to you... You resist the urge to just run out and answer whatever it is calling to you."
  • The DM's offer: resist it three times and Xurl controls the curse and gains additional abilities — one down, two to go, with the honest caveat that he doesn't know whether the campaign will run three in-game months.
  • The alternative: Cleanse Affliction upcast to rank 4, which needs a level-8 caster in Absalom. Nobody local can do it. Also permanent, starting now: Xurl has weakness to silver. ("I'm sure that won't be relevant.")
  • Xurl chose the curse. "Fuck it. I'm going to greet it. I want the werewolf abilities. It just sounds rad as hell."
  • The night's rest restored everyone to full, gave the party a hero point each, cleared Ruben's Drained entirely and knocked Alder's from 3 to 2.
Act VI · Social · Otari: The Bounty, the Shopping, the Druid

The Best Roleplay of the Night

  • Kaledo and the locket. In the early-morning market crush, Snagg presented Jaul Mezmin's necklace. Kaledo grabbed his shoulder and demanded to know where he'd found it — "Where is that son of a bitch? Where is he?" Xurl: "Very dead." Snagg: "Calm down. Let's talk." Alder: "If you want to find him, you can go down there."
  • Kaledo took the necklace, opened it onto the portrait of Isla, and began laughing louder and louder while crying. He paid the 300 gp, thanked them for a ton of good deeds, turned back to his stall — and then, quietly, back turned: "Thank you. Thank you for taking care of that son of a bitch."
  • Alder's Perception confirmed the sincerity; Snagg's Society filled in the rest — Kaledo was nice to everyone before his wife's death. The incident changed him.
  • The undead lecture. Alder's own answer was spirit-touching effects and holy water, with silver for lycanthropes; the DM filled in the rest as knowledge Alder already has — "that is your specialty, right?" — namely that undead as a category have no universal weakness and a very long immunity list. Alder's practical advice: "Really, the best thing is just destroying them in whatever way you're best at. So if you're good at biting, it's usually a pretty good option. I'll let you know whenever it's not, you know?"
  • The DM's floor bestiary, given freely: Mitflits on floor 1, Morlocks on floor 2, a great many creatures from the underworld (the chain woman included), Corpselights — "like Will-o'-Wisps", and therefore usually immune to magic — and demons, which produced the lore drop of the night: "So Belcorra definitely has some contract with demons."
  • Silver vs. cold iron. Both 40 gp. The DM checked and reported demons and fiends run mostly silver — so Snagg bought silver handwraps and Alder took cold iron to cover the gap.
  • Rune economics, worked out at the table: transferring a rune costs 10% of that rune's price and takes one day per rune — free if you do it yourself with Magical Crafting and expert Crafting. Alder transferred his +1 striking onto a composite shortbow (6 gp 5 sp). Snagg came up 3 gold short; Alder covered it.
  • The delivery problem. The armour potency rune is a level 5 item and Otari only stocks up to level 4 — a special order taking a week. There used to be express delivery at three days. There isn't now: the party failed the side quest to save the courier. "The person is dead, so it's no longer available."
  • Waluwin at the Stone Pond (spelling tentative). At the fork between Otari Graveyard and the Gauntlight, a gnome druid of the Stone Pond stopped them. Snagg knew of her: Otari and the druids used to be on good terms, and it broke one year ago when Jaul killed Kaledo's wife.
  • Her ask: bring Jaul's body back for a proper burial. Her case: Jaul was one of her people; "among our druids, he's a werewolf — it's not a secret to us. He's able to control himself most of the time." Every full moon he deliberately went into the forest to hide, because he knew he'd lose control. Isla was simply there. "He's not a bad person."
  • The negotiation. Xurl raised the logistics ("He's kind of a big dude" — gesturing at the party; the DM, surveying them: "Two short people. Two nerdy-looking guys"). Snagg went straight for the fee — "we might need some bonus, you know?" She panicked, dug through her pocket, and produced an armour potency rune: "That's all I have." Snagg's attitude flipped 180 degrees — "Yeah, don't worry. Snagg is very strong. Snagg can bring two of them at the same time. No problem."
Act VII · Exploration · The Barrier and the False Bottom

The Way Down, and the Way Around

  • They did not go back for her. Snagg: "Do we want to storm the room again?" Ruben: "No, let's leave her alone."
  • The barrier over the down-stairs. Ruben detected magic on it; with zero Arcana he asked to substitute Religion at a penalty — "It can't be much worse penalty than a zero in Arcana" — and got it: the fog is inert right now, but powerful. He couldn't identify its nature, its activation conditions, or dispel it. "For all we know, it could activate as soon as we step into it."
  • The decision to go sideways. Ruben framed it and handed it over: "It is a risk, but we still need to go down further to reach the core of this place. I leave it in your hands." Alder: "I feel like there's still a lot of information we could find on this floor." Ruben: "Very well. Then we will return to this once we have more info."
  • The empty sarcophagus. One lid went transparent on nothing — no corpse. Alder reached in, found a gap at the bottom, and needed the lid off to get at it.
  • He failed the Athletics check; Snagg stepped in — "You got this, little one" — and the DM played it straight: "this tiny little goblin just steps, pushes you aside... and then, without any effort." Snagg: "Got to train better, man. Got to go to the gym." Alder: "Remind me not to wrestle you."
  • Underneath: a false bottom over a natural passage, opening into a wide chamber with bones and rotten meat scattered across the floor — and two creatures already in it.
Act VIII · Combat · The Feeding Ground

The Rematch

  • Two more Scalathrax, in what the party correctly read as their larder ("they were just eating people. This is like a feeding ground for them").
  • The rematch went the other way: Exploit Vulnerability up front, Snagg holding the line with Snagging Strike and Combat Grab, and Alder closing with a Demoralize into a 39-damage critical hit.
  • Both hero points went in round one — Ruben talked Alder into spending his on a natural-1 Exploit Vulnerability, and Snagg burned the party's last on a natural-1 Snagging Strike.
  • New rules discovery mid-grapple: Oily Scales — the Scalathrax treat Escape results one degree better, which turned a plain success into a critical success and popped Snagg's Combat Grab. "This looks more like a crab than a spider to you... there's some very sticky oil on the shell."
  • Alder's Exploit Vulnerability on the second read Will as its weakest save and he immediately passed the ball: "Hey, Ruben. They're not the strongest-willed creatures, so maybe you can control them." Ruben's honest self-assessment: "He's not a controller. He's a debuffer."
  • The fire weakness Alder found in the first fight turned out to be worth nothing: "Has anyone got any fire for these guys?" — "I don't have any fire spells. I have a lot of holy spells." — "We used to have a guy that could do a lot of fire." — "Yeah, someone left him behind and got eaten."
Act IX · Combat · The Dining Room

Seven Doors and a Banquet

  • Past the passage: an abandoned kitchen with a cast-iron stove and no chimney — the smoke would have filled the whole room when it was in use — and a skeleton on the floor. Nothing to loot.
  • Through the east door: a formal dining room, still lit by two burning chandeliers, with a long banquet table, chairs (some upright, some fallen), a full setting of empty plates, skeletons on the floor, and seven doors.
  • Ruben read the room before it read them: "There has been much murder here. These bodies, they tried to get away. They couldn't."
  • Then the corpses stood up — and turned out not to be the enemy at all. They were Corpselights: a light that wears a body. Every time a corpse dropped, a blue light came out of its mouth and the thing kept fighting.
  • Xurl called it inside a round: "It's a parasite which is controlling a body. You killed the body, you didn't hit the parasite." Snagg's tactical summary: "Punch it in the ghost."
  • All four destroyed, three of them on wisps trying to fly clear — two of those to reactions.
  • The wind-down. Snagg gambled the last heal on an expert DC and crit it, maxing the Risky Surgery damage roll on the way — Xurl: "I like how you maxed the damage roll. Just out of spite." Then the night's last exchange: "Is Snagg a civilized goblin, or is he doing a little bit of snacking while doing surgery?" — "He's using his mouth to, like, carry the tools." — "I don't know if that's better or worse than what I said. Probably better. Very hygienic."

Character Spotlight

Snagg · the Goblin Who Decides

  • He was the tiebreaker, and he ran the room to get there. The nine-minute retreat argument had a player who wanted to leave and a player who wanted to stay, and Snagg did three things in order: he named the real constraint before anyone else ("What matters is when we run into another encounter, I think we're just going to not have enough gas to go through"), he physically left the table mid-debate rather than lean on it ("Okay. You guys decide, I'm going to go to the bathroom"), and when he came back he asked for the argument to be summarised so he could actually rule on it: "I need some context. Can you guys give me some context?" Then he added the one fact only he had — "I have used up all my Battle Medicine on you guys" — and called it. That is a triage lead, not a tank.
  • He also killed the bad plan. When the post-mortem turned to pre-buffing before kicking doors, Snagg knew why it doesn't work: "If we're talking about rules as written, you cannot pre-buff with a one minute spell." The same instinct later stopped Ruben from putting a patient into an hour-long Treat Wounds lockout that Continual Recovery couldn't shorten.
  • The medic engine ran all night. Four critical-success Treat Wounds cycles before the first fight, ten across the session; Battle Medicine and Doctor's Visitation mid-combat while Frightened; three more Risky Surgery cycles after the feeding ground and one at the end of the night. He also ran the condition audit nobody asked for. His entire attitude toward stabbing his patients as a healing technique: "It's just surgery. You never done surgery before?" / "You get used to it. Don't worry about it."
  • He ate the whole night's incoming. 112 points of combat damage, plus 24 more absorbed by the sturdy shield across three Shield Blocks. In the chain room alone he took 82 — a 22-point chain, 13 poison, a 30-damage critical that anchored him so he couldn't stop the bleeding, the bleed itself, and a leg quill — from full to 2 hit points, with a vision of his own parents being executed on the way down. His running commentary was not dignified — "My 2 HP. Woohoo!" / "Fuck yeah, leave me. I'm gonna die" / "What kind of fucking dumbass ability is that?" — and it did not stop him being the last one out of the room.
  • And then he was the party's top scorer in the dining room: 99 damage and two kills, one a Reactive Strike on a wisp trying to fly clear, the other the finish — a run-up, a jump onto a dining chair, and a punch through a floating light. 143 damage on the night, in the two fights he was allowed to actually fight.
  • Otari is his lane and he keeps proving it. He presented the bounty, calmed Kaledo down ("Calm down. Let's talk"), and read the man afterwards — the Society check that told the party Kaledo used to be kind before Isla died. He knew the druid of the Stone Pond on sight, knew why her people and the town fell out, and then negotiated her up from nothing to an armour potency rune by simply asking what was in it for them.
  • The strength gag landed twice. He picked the sarcophagus lid Alder couldn't budge, one-handed, without effort — "Got to train better, man. Got to go to the gym" — and closed the session by punching a ghost off a chair. And the standing complaint, delivered at least three separate times: "Oh my god, when will I ever get a flank again?"

Xurl · the Halfling Who Said Yes to the Curse

  • He argued the party into surviving. The retreat case was his, he built it properly, and he built it out of evidence: the tank went from full to 2 in a turn, every defensive layer he owns was spent, and — the line that ended it — "if we look at that fight, we made how many attacks that entire fight? One?" He also framed the pattern above the specific fight: "Every time we pick up debuffs and spend our dailies, and then we try and pick another fight, we get kicked in the teeth and someone dies." And when Snagg asked for the summary, he gave both sides fairly, including Ruben's.
  • The rescue. Snagg at 2 hit points, anchored in the chains, bleeding and unable to stop it. Xurl spent two actions — Grapple, then Reposition — to haul him out of reach and put himself in the gap: "I ain't leaving you to die twice in effectively two days. I can take at least a couple hits for you." It's the best line of the session, and it's also a direct reference to Argon dying in a doorway five sessions ago.
  • He took the curse on purpose. Offered a route to remove it (a level-8 caster in Absalom) and a route to master it (resist three full moons and control it, with abilities attached), he made the call in one breath: "Fuck it. I'm going to greet it. I want the werewolf abilities. It just sounds rad as hell." One resist banked, two to go, and a permanent weakness to silver he waved off with "I'm sure that won't be relevant." He'd already spent the night wrapped in three people's bedrolls at the bottom of a stairwell — his own proposal, delivered as "Step one: swaddle the halfling."
  • He is the party's most careful thinker and its least serious person, simultaneously. He tested the sarcophagus trigger radius by lobbing severed crawling hands down a corridor rather than walking into it, declined the DM's offer to escalate to his own hand on professional grounds ("Monks famously not known for kneading their hands"), and then diagnosed the entire Corpselight fight from one turn of evidence: "It's a parasite which is controlling a body. You killed the body, you didn't hit the parasite."
  • Top damage in the dining room — 103 points, including a critical hit that came after a critical fumble on the same Flurry of Blows, which he then argued correctly should still land because Flurry is one action; the resulting Stunned 3 cost the last Corpselight its whole turn. His kill was a Stand Still on a wisp trying to escape. The one thing that still doesn't work is Combat Grab, and he knows it: "Don't know why I bother. It's never going to happen. I'm never going to grab anything."
  • The two lines that bookend his night: "Xurl's had a day of discoveries. First, no balls. Second, transparent stone." and, after the party finally won a fair fight, "It turns out when we don't have to keep fighting extreme enemies, we're doing okay."

Ruben Falmoor · Pharasma's Working Theory

  • He produced the single biggest idea of the session, and it came out of a failure. The natural-20 Sanctify Water attempt on Nhimbaloth's altar told him he can't win — "You are looking at the deity itself" — so he turned it into a hypothesis instead: "I can propose a theory that this Gauntlight itself could be powered by Nhimbaloth's power herself. Someone was able to possibly channel it and store it here. That could be the reason why the undead is very prominent in these entire catacombs." That is a new campaign-level thread, built at the table from a failed check, and nobody else was thinking at that altitude. Xurl found the corroboration inside a minute — Belcorra born under seven stars, seven being Nhimbaloth's number.
  • He argued his side and then stepped back — twice — on purpose. He had spell slots and wanted to keep going, said so clearly, and then declined to fight about it: "I've already put in my two cents... if you all think that we can't, then I'm not going to argue." Later, at the barrier, he did it again: "It is a risk, but we still need to go down further to reach the core of this place. I leave it in your hands." / "Very well. Then we will return to this once we have more info." Both times the party was better off for it.
  • The 40-point Heal that saved the session. Snagg at 2 hit points with a boss's chain in him; Ruben upcast a two-action Heal with Sorcerous Potency for 40, then spent the rest of the fight feeding Lay on Hands and a Healer's Gloves activation into Xurl. "Not dead yet — the joys of having an actual healer. We love healing magic in this house."

Asked to identify the barrier with zero Arcana, he requested to substitute Religion at a penalty — "It can't be much worse penalty than a zero in Arcana" — and came back with a real answer: inert now, powerful, undispellable, activation conditions unknown. And in a pitch-black chain room where only the goblin could see, he fixed it in four words: "Well then, let there be light."
  • His in-character narration is doing more work than anyone gives it credit for. "I need no embodied spirit to tell me my job" on the Gauntlight's message; "Perhaps a weary soul who was cut down before their prime" on its sender; "That's a whole lot of nope on a rope" on first sight of the caged Scalathrax; and, seconds before the dining room turned into a fight, the line that framed the whole encounter: "There has been much murder here.   • Sepulchral Mask is his new engine and it earned its keep — 21 damage applied off a single cast across three bodies in round 1, and then the standing emanation ticked one of them down and killed it on its own turn without him spending an action. 63 damage in the dining room, plus the Vitality Lash that dropped the last body for Snagg to finish. He also knows exactly what he is: handed "maybe you can control them" by Alder, he answered "He's not a controller. He's a debuffer."

Alder Ungram · the Man with Two Voices

  • He kept the party in the dungeon at the top of the session, and he was right to. Snagg opened with "I thought we wanted to run the hell out of them" and Alder shut it down flat — "No. No, I don't think so. We still have a bunch of resources, don't we?" — then organised the recovery: rest here, camp in the cave, "It's fine to be a little bit on the edge." Two fights and a bounty later, that call was worth 300 gold and a rune.
  • The stereo delivery — his best beat in this campaign. Mid-fight, he put a mirror duplicate inside the monster's cage so he could see what was happening in there, and delivered his Exploit Vulnerability read out of both bodies at once: "While I'm over here, you hear from both of my mouths at the same time: those guys, they're pretty weak to fire. I don't like being burnt, so chuck whatever you got at them. But not poisonous things — I don't care about that." It was also the mirror's second unprompted use that night — he'd volunteered it on the altar without being asked, got a vision of Belcorra kneeling in prayer and nothing else, and shrugged it off: "Eh, maybe it just went into some random day of prayer."
  • He is the party's brake, and he pulls it at the right moments. "Oh, hell no. You know what? We should turn around" at the sealed stairs. "I feel like there's still a lot of information we could find on this floor" at the barrier. "You should be careful. The influence of Nhimbaloth is very powerful here." He's also the only one who remembers the party is supposed to be doing something while it walks: "By the way, should we be doing exploration activities?"
  • He asks the question the others don't. While the table was working out what the Gauntlight is, Alder went past it: "But I think also the more important question is how they control this weapon." Nobody has an answer yet. It's the right question.
  • His expertise is real and he doesn't oversell it. Asked what undead are weak to, he gave the honest professional answer — spirit-touching effects and holy water help, silver is for lycanthropes — and then the genuinely useful part: "Really, the best thing is just destroying them in whatever way you're best at. So if you're good at biting, it's usually a pretty good option. I'll let you know whenever it's not, you know?" Which is precisely what he then did, twice, in two fights.
  • He was the party's top killer and its top damage dealer — 169 on the night, at Drained 2 the whole time. Both Scalathrax in the feeding ground, the second via Demoralize into a 39-damage critical hit; the critical chakram that opened the dining room; and the natural-1 Exploit Vulnerability he spent a hero point to save when Ruben pushed him into it.
  • His register for the small ones is a running character note, and it never slips: "Careful, little one. Don't know if we'll be able to pull you back up", "Lead the way, little ones", "You got this, little one" — the last said to Snagg immediately before the goblin shoved him aside and lifted a stone lid he couldn't move. "Remind me not to wrestle you." And to a woman made of chains who asked why they'd lit a torch in her room: "It's dark in here. What did you expect?"
