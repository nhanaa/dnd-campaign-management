## Session Summary

Half this session was about kobolds, and it was the good half. **Fiverr**, the leader of the warren the party shares its base with, asked for something nobody in this campaign has asked them for: help being allowed to exist near a town. He had a reason he was reluctant to give — **his sister wants to take Otari by force**, they fought over it, and he lost. Integration is his alternative to her war. The clan also turned out to have lived on **the Gauntlight's first floor until three months ago**, when something evicted them.

So the party walked two kobolds into Otari in broad daylight, talked their way past guards who levelled spears at them on the bridge, and put the clan's case to Wrin Sivinxi. Ruben ran the diplomacy and drew a hard line on how it would be done — *"If you're going to do good, it is best not to hide behind trickery to do so"* — after Snagg floated a plan involving disguises and a dramatic reveal. Xurl gave the clan the only practical plan anyone offered: take the jobs nobody wants, run messages, feed horses, kill the rats in people's basements, *"just let people see you helping the town."* And Alder did the single best thing anyone did all night: he looked at the townsfolk giving the kobolds the stink eye, told them to *"get back to work or whatever you're supposed to be doing,"* got told to mind his own business, answered *"Listen to your own advice, buddy,"* offered the man a fight, watched him leave — and then looked down and said, **"I got you, buddy."**

Then an hour at Wrin's tent that was a shopping montage with a body count. Alder volunteered to identify everything — *"I'm touching everything"* — and the magic items fought back: the Lantern of Empty Light nearly Drained him for staring into it, and the Oil of Mending was destroyed outright when he rolled a 2 uncorking it and Ruben rolled a 1 catching it. Alder was also the only PC who *could* have used the Staff of Necromancy, via Trick Magic Item, and chose to sell it instead — 115 gp, after nobody would even touch it (*"Don't click me. Don't click me. Don't click me"* — Snagg; *"Ruben isn't even going to talk to that staff"*). **Snagg took delivery of the ghost touch rune** he's wanted since Argon died, and immediately asked the question that tells you where his head is: *"Can we come back to fight the ghost lady? I have ghost touch now."*

The second half was the Gauntlight's unexplored rooms, and they were extraordinary. A hidden alcove with a **teleport circle whose silver inlay had been stripped out** — which answers a question open since S4 about why the ground-floor circle is severed. A room of five animated dioramas showing **Absalom destroyed five different ways** — fire, tsunami, earthquake, tentacled things, an undead army — modelled on a version of the city centuries out of date, the **third** wrong-Absalom object this campaign has turned up. A **war room** wrapped in spider webs with no spiders anywhere in it, where Xurl pulled the webs off the centrepiece and found it wasn't a model at all but a live illusion projecting Absalom. A room they had already cleared where **both corpses and every blood splatter were simply gone** — Snagg: *"Interesting. Something has cleaned."* And behind a faintly glowing secret door that **Ruben** found on his exploration roll, **Belcorra's private suite**: four rooms, each replaying a harmless illusion of the woman herself — briefing a drow, reading the same book the armoured undead carried off, eating a cooked humanoid head, and stepping out of a portal licking blood off her hand.

Two more tapestries came out of it, and **tapestry seven is the most important object recovered in this campaign so far**: six figures at the foot of the Gauntlight. Belcorra. Volluk. The armoured knight. A Seugathi. A demon. And a floating ball of light with eyes and **seven-fingered hands**. Xurl had called two of them before the Recall Knowledge came back.

They met the sixth figure forty minutes later.

Xurl got his brand-new crowbar stuck in a pool of slime in the trophy room, the whole party rolled Perception to check on him, every single one failed — and a voice at the door said *"What are you doing here?"*, then, before anyone could answer, **"I guess you are the group that our mistress want to kill."** Snagg tried *"I don't know, we're friends with the mistress."* The DM: *"Good try."* Everyone rolled initiative at −2.

What followed was a **twelve-round, two-hour-ten-minute fight against a level 9 elite creature, and every single member of the party hit the floor.** A will-o'-wisp — AC 32, immune to magic, Intelligence +6. It opened by webbing Snagg and Alder to the floor, and it went at Ruben harder than anyone, which Ruben noticed out loud: *"he keeps fucking targeting me. Specifically."* At 61 HP it cast **rank-4 Darkness** and blinded the whole party for **rounds 3 through 9**, putting a DC 11 flat check on every single party attack. Ruben's entire kit bounced off the magic immunity; he dealt **zero damage in twelve rounds** and took **166**, three times his own maximum, going down three separate times.

They won it anyway, on three natural 20s and a cascade of medicine checks. Snagg spent almost the whole fight as a field hospital rather than a damage dealer — **four successful Treat Wounds/Battle Medicine applications against eleven attack rolls, standing three different people up**: Ruben twice, Xurl once off Dying 2 via Doctor's Visitation, and Alder once. Alder, when he finally got free of the web in round 7, spent a turn feeding Xurl a healing potion off Dying 3 — the exact thing Xurl had lectured the party about carrying two hours earlier. And in round 12, with **Snagg and Ruben both unconscious on the floor** and Alder delaying to hand him the flank, Xurl jumped onto a bed and punched a creature sitting on 7 hit points for 9. It screamed, its eyes and claws collapsed inward, and it vanished. **It never got a round-12 turn.**

The DM asked Alder and Xurl to Recall Knowledge on whether it had actually died. Both rolled badly. Nobody knows.

The party finished with **20 hit points between all four of them**. Then Lap volunteered two things. First: the creature's heal recharges on a 1d4-round cooldown, *"I rolled twice a four — if I roll a one he will be still up at this point."* Second: *"good thing you guys did not come here before you level to 6. If you guys are level 5, I'm pretty sure there's no way you can kill this guy. This is one of the fights that people complain about Abomination Vault. It's unfair."* Xurl: *"I wonder why."* Ruben: *"What they need to do is fucking remaster Abomination Vault."*

**Lap inflated nothing.** Every number is a textbook elite template on the book's level-8 Voidglutton, the magic immunity is native to the stat block, and so is the at-will Darkness at rank 4. His only two deviations both *helped* the party: he ran the counteract DC at the book's 26 rather than the elite 28. Pax's read was therefore exactly right, and he turned it into a rules change: constant PL+3 elites plus AV's rider-stacked monsters make missing feel unfair rather than hard — *"these fights are winnable on paper; the problem is with Abomination Vault and its abilities"* — and the table agreed that **from next session, any PC starting with zero hero points automatically gets one.**

### Act VI — The Post-Mortem and a New House Rule (5:25 – 5:41)
- Xurl, counting up: **"Alright, so we have a cool 20 HP between the entire party."**
- Lap volunteered how close it was: the creature's heal **recharges on a 1d4-round cooldown**, and *"I rolled twice a four. If I roll a one he will be still up at this point."*
- And then the encounter's provenance: **"good thing you guys did not come here before you level to 6. If you guys are level 5, I'm pretty sure there's no way you can kill this guy. This is one of the fights that people complain about Abomination Vault. It's unfair."** — *"This thing is level 9."* Xurl: **"I wonder why."** / *"Why the fuck is there a 9th level creature down here?"* Ruben: **"What they need to do is fucking remaster Abomination Vault."**
- Lap gave the stat block outright: **AC 32, immune to magic, Fort +15 / Ref +20 / Will +18**, and **Intelligence +6**, which he cited to explain why it played so well.
- ⭐ **Pax's read, which the DM accepted**: *"These fights are winnable on paper. The problem is with Abomination Vault and its abilities — every fucking monster has either some kind of resistance or some kind of rider ability on its normal attack. It's just crazy."* Lap: *"remember when you guys create new characters, when I look at your new ability, I was like, fuck, this thing is so fucking OP. But when you actually play it against those monsters, then I can see that, oh, yeah."* Snagg: *"it works, it works, but like half of the time doesn't work."*
- **The hero-point negotiation.** Pax asked for one hero point at the start of each session, arguing that constant above-level elites make missing feel unfair rather than hard. **Lap countered by framing it as a trade** — per-session *or* per-rest, not both — and by offering to revert to RAW entirely — one per hour, but *"I will also remove every house rule about hero point… it will be just reroll"* — and priced his own homebrew: *"In that fight, Ruben, if he's able to roll a success, he can use a hero point to pump him up to a critical success. In a normal game, you will need to reroll."*
- Pax declined to make it a house-rule fight and landed the compromise: one hero point at session start **only if you have none**, because *"it feels bad — you start a new session, if you haven't returned to town, you have nothing to use."*
- Lap's one condition: he will not reward ending a session early to bank the refresh. Xurl: **"Yeah, no, that's bitch mode."** Snagg: *"We're here to play."*
- ⭐ **AGREED, effective S10: all existing hero-point house rules stay, plus — if a PC has zero hero points at the start of a session, they automatically get one.** Long-rest hero points continue; cap stays 3; players add it themselves in Foundry.
- Lap's closing notes: **"I'm not here to babysit you guys. I am going to kill you guys. Just saying. Again and again."** And, on the party's build choices: *"why do you guys have so much healing now, are you guys so afraid to die? … now I see it after this fight. I can't imagine if, for example, Snagg doesn't have Battle Medicine — everything relies on Ruben, you guys are dead already."*

## Character Spotlight

### Snagg · the Field Hospital That Bites
- **He played this fight as a medic first and a fighter second, and it was the right call.** **Four successful Treat Wounds / Battle Medicine applications against eleven attack rolls** — and he stood **three different people** up: **Ruben twice** (a crit for 22, then 18), **Xurl once** off Dying 2 via **Doctor's Visitation** (Stride + Battle Medicine in one action), and **Alder once** (7, Battle Medicine). A fifth attempt critically failed and stabbed him for 2.
- **He also quietly won every lore beat of the session.** His **Crafting 29** identified the **Lantern of Empty Light** — the item Alder had been staring into — and he backed the Recall Knowledge lane on the dioramas and the tapestries with the party's highest Intelligence.
- **Best mechanical read of the night, and he lost the argument on purpose.** Blinded, he made the case that Reactive Strike should still trigger on a creature he couldn't see, worked the rules aloud — and then conceded cleanly: *"That's why I'm saying rules as written, because the GM has the right to rule and interpret how he wants. So I will accept that. I just wanted to bring it up as a contest… as a chance to get us an advantage in this fight that seems pretty hard."* He was also right about the mechanic that mattered: **Darkness is a manipulate action**, so his Reactive Strike triggered on the cast, and the DM rewound to let both reaction Strikes resolve before the blindness landed.
- **He also found the one thing that could have broken the fight open, while it was still live.** The creature's magic immunity has a short exception list, and two entries on it — **Faerie Fire** and **Revealing Light** — are rank-2 *divine* spells. Seconds after Ruben's Dispel failed, Snagg asked him straight out: *"you have revealing light?"* The answer was *"I do not have revealing light."* Nobody else in the party went looking for the loophole.
- **Round 2's natural 20** was his one big hit — a critical for 18 and Crit Deck #24. **52 damage on 45% accuracy against AC 32**, and **two clean jaws hits in round 3 were erased by failed DC 11 flat checks** in the darkness.
- **He went down in round 11 — and then argued himself back onto the floor.** A 38-point crit blew his Sturdy Shield past its **Break Threshold** (15 + 30 = 45 damage against BT 32), and the broken shield's lost **+2 circumstance AC** turned the next two rolls of 26 and 25 from misses into hits. He caught the error first and **won it on exact arithmetic** — *"it still had 19 HP left"* (64 − 45 = 19) — and the DM reverted both hits. Then **Alder** pointed out that a shield breaks at half, not at zero, and **Snagg reversed himself against his own interest**: *"So yeah, I am down. I'll take the damage, and then I'll take this damage."*
- **Dark comedy, perfectly timed**: right after a rare hit, *"At least this thing doesn't have fucking like fast healing and some bullshit."* It healed twice more.
- **The RP half was his too.** He's the one who trails the kobolds home at the end of a near-death night *"because, you know, there's a guy in the party that still cares"* — and the one who took delivery of a **ghost touch** rune and immediately started planning a return trip for the ghost he's never been able to hurt. His Diplomacy on the bridge failed; the humans carried it.
- **And he turned a bad night into a rules change.** The hero-point argument was his, start to finish: he diagnosed the problem as system-plus-AP rather than DM malice, refused the DM's offer to revert everything, proposed the narrow fix, and got the table to sign off.

### Xurl · the One Who Kept Swinging
- **He killed it.** Round 12, up onto a bed, a flurry punch into a creature on 7 HP for 9 — with Alder delaying to hand him the flank. Second session running that Xurl lands the finishing blow on a floor boss. **It never got another turn.**
- **The round-7 natural 20 is the turn that made it survivable.** A critical for **28** plus Crit Deck **#47**, sticking it with **Clumsy 2 + Enfeebled 2** — and its Fortitude save came up a **natural 1** on top. It went from 39 HP to 11 in one turn. *(The critical failure only produced Stunned 1 rather than Stunned 3, because incapacitation correctly bumped the save a degree — the creature outlevels him.)*
- **He led the party in damage with 62 — and threw 25 attacks, 12 of them critical misses.** Three in one turn in round 4 (*"Three critical misses in a turn. Great"*), three more in round 6. He also had a **second natural 20 in round 9 wiped out by back-to-back failed flat checks** — a natural 1, then an 8 — in the dark.
- **He was honest about being locked out and kept acting anyway**: *"There's literally nothing I can do to help. So, fishing for crits."* / *"I would love to do something to get it off you, but I can't fucking touch it."* / *"Fucking already tried to intimidate. I can't do that again. Can't do shit."* Then: **"We at least got to kill this fucking guy or die trying."**
- **Orc Ferocity bought the party a turn** — round 8, held at 1 HP instead of dropping. He still went down twice and ended on Wounded 3.
- **The best tactical joke of the night was also a real decision**: *"So observe the elite tactical move of fuck this shit. I'm going out of line to fight him."* Wounded 3 and one hit from death — *"It's shown that it loves Coup de Grâce-ing one person at a time"* — he deliberately broke off and came back in when it was safe.
- **Out of combat he is the party's deduction engine, not just its comedian.** He solved the **war room** (pulled the webs off and identified the centrepiece as a live illusion, not a model), **found and routed around the elevator**, remembered the second set of blueprints existed at all, and **called two of tapestry 7's six figures before the Recall Knowledge came back**.
- **His advice to the kobolds was the most useful thing anyone said to them** — small jobs, messages, horses, pest control, *"just let people see you helping the town."* And his lecture about carrying healing potions (*"Tip your medics"*) is the reason he was alive in round 8 to take the round-7 crit at all.
- Consistently the funniest person at the table, and always attached to something true: *"We love our Recall Knowledge. We don't know shit, but we like rolling the dice."* / *"Yeah, well, we touch doors. That's what we do."* / on an empty dresser, *"If anything, that's more suspicious."* / *"The martials are chilling over here having a beer."*

### Ruben Falmoor · the Man Who Held the Line on Principle
- **He found Belcorra's private suite.** The whole Act IV centrepiece — four illusion replays, both tapestries, the seven-fingered-hand lead — came off **his** exploration roll, when Xurl's and Snagg's came up a 3 and a 4. The single most consequential roll of the session was his.
- **He is the party's conscience and he used it twice.** He killed Snagg's disguise-the-kobolds plan on ethics alone — *"If you're going to do good, it is best not to hide behind trickery to do so"* — and talked the clan down from marching everyone into town, because *"the entirety of the clan coming out at once for the first time would most likely be seen as an invasion."*
- **He ran the negotiation that got them in.** The DC 18 Diplomacy at the bridge is the only reason two kobolds walked into Otari instead of getting speared: *"Calm yourselves. We have been hired by the mayor to investigate the Gauntlight. That is above your pay grade, unfortunately."* Then he doubled back to commend the guards to the mayor.
- **Asked whether Belcorra's ultimatum changed the job**, he gave the answer that defines him: **"There's still much work. I've been paid for a job. I will not cancel a contract just because of this."**
- **His natural 20 on Medicine dated the butcher-room remains to 4–5 days** — the only hard timeline the party has on what's still living down there.
- **Then the fight happened to him.** **166 damage taken** — three times his 56-point maximum — **three knockdowns**, Dying 3, Wounded 3, and **zero damage dealt**, because a creature immune to magic turns a divine sorcerer into a healer with a shield. He spent **three hero points**: two on Dispel Magic counteracts trying to strip the Darkness, and his last at Dying 3 to stay alive.
- **He read the targeting correctly while it was happening** — *"he keeps fucking targeting me. Specifically."* He was right; it put more Strikes into him than anyone.
- **He kept himself in the fight the whole time** — Lay on Hands on himself twice, **Benediction's +1 status AC aura up for the entire party from round 1 to the end**, Rousing Splash on Snagg, and a 49-point Heal the round he got back up. He also **declined a hero point** when the DM named the counteract DC and the maths stopped working: *"You know what? I'm not going to waste a hero."*

#### Hero points and luck
- **8 spent in the fight — Snagg 2, Ruben 3, Xurl 2, Alder 1** — plus Alder's pre-combat reroll on the Lantern, for **9 across the session**. The DM had **handed the whole table one** five minutes before initiative, for Alder's mirror vision.

The creature rolled two of its own, including the 50-damage crit on Xurl.
- **The incapacitation trait did real work, correctly.** **Stunning Blows is an incapacitation effect**, and because the creature is **level 9 against a level 6 monk**, it was entitled to one degree of success better on both saves — RAW for a non-spell effect is simply *"a creature of higher level than the item, creature, or hazard generating the effect."* It fired **twice**: in round 6 a plain success became a **critical** success, and in round 7 Xurl's **natural 1 critical failure** became a plain failure. Since Stunning Blows reads *"Stunned 1 (or Stunned 3 on a critical failure),"* that second one turned **three actions of denial into one** in the round before the creature put Xurl to 1 HP and then down — the single most expensive rules interaction of the fight, and entirely by the book.
