Session Summary

Half this session was about kobolds, and it was the good half. Fiverr, the leader of the warren the party shares its base with, asked for something nobody in this campaign has asked them for: help being allowed to exist near a town. He had a reason he was reluctant to give — his sister wants to take Otari by force, they fought over it, and he lost. Integration is his alternative to her war. The clan also turned out to have lived on the Gauntlight's first floor until three months ago, when something evicted them.

So the party walked two kobolds into Otari in broad daylight, talked their way past guards who levelled spears at them on the bridge, and put the clan's case to Wrin Sivinxi. Ruben ran the diplomacy and drew a hard line on how it would be done — "If you're going to do good, it is best not to hide behind trickery to do so" — after Snagg floated a plan involving disguises and a dramatic reveal. Xurl gave the clan the only practical plan anyone offered: take the jobs nobody wants, run messages, feed horses, "just let people see you helping the town." And Alder did the single best thing anyone did all night: he looked at the townsfolk giving the kobolds the stink eye, told them to "get back to work or whatever you're supposed to be doing," got told to mind his own business, answered "Listen to your own advice, buddy," offered the man a fight, watched him leave — and then looked down and said, "I got you, buddy."

Then an hour at Wrin's tent that was a shopping montage with a body count. Alder volunteered to identify everything — "I'm touching everything" — and the magic items fought back: the Lantern of Empty Light nearly Drained him for staring into it, and the Oil of Mending was destroyed outright when he rolled a 2 uncorking it and Ruben rolled a 1 catching it. Alder was also the only PC who could have used the Staff of Necromancy, via Trick Magic Item, and chose to sell it instead — 115 gp, after nobody would even touch it. Snagg took delivery of the ghost touch rune he's wanted since Argon died, and immediately asked the question that tells you where his head is: "Can we come back to fight the ghost lady? I have ghost touch now."

The second half was the Gauntlight's unexplored rooms, and they were extraordinary. A teleport circle whose silver inlay had been stripped out. A room of five animated dioramas showing Absalom destroyed five different ways, modelled on a city centuries out of date. A war room wrapped in spider webs with no spiders in it, where the centrepiece turned out to be a live illusion projecting Absalom. A room they had already cleared where both corpses and every blood splatter were simply gone — Snagg: "Interesting. Something has cleaned." And behind a secret door that Ruben found, Belcorra's private suite: four rooms replaying harmless illusions of the woman herself, including one of her eating a cooked humanoid head.

Two tapestries came out of it, and tapestry seven is the most important object recovered in this campaign so far: six figures at the foot of the Gauntlight. Belcorra. Volluk. The armoured knight. A Seugathi. A demon. And a floating ball of light with eyes and seven-fingered hands. They met the sixth figure forty minutes later.

Xurl got his brand-new crowbar stuck in a pool of slime, the whole party rolled Perception to check on him, every single one failed — and a voice at the door said "What are you doing here?", then, before anyone could answer, "I guess you are the group that our mistress want to kill." Snagg tried "I don't know, we're friends with the mistress." The DM: "Good try." Everyone rolled initiative at −2.

What followed was a twelve-round, two-hour-ten-minute fight against a level 9 elite creature, and every single member of the party hit the floor. A will-o'-wisp — AC 32, immune to magic, Intelligence +6. At 61 HP it cast rank-4 Darkness and blinded the whole party for rounds 3 through 9, putting a DC 11 flat check on every party attack. Ruben's entire kit bounced off the magic immunity: zero damage dealt in twelve rounds, 166 taken, three times his own maximum, and three knockdowns.

They won it anyway, on three natural 20s and a cascade of medicine checks. Snagg spent almost the whole fight as a field hospital rather than a damage dealer — four successful heals against eleven attack rolls, standing three different people up. Alder, freed from the web in round 7, immediately fed Xurl a healing potion off Dying 3 — the exact thing Xurl had lectured the party about carrying two hours earlier. And in round 12, with Snagg and Ruben both unconscious on the floor and Alder delaying to hand him the flank, Xurl jumped onto a bed and punched a creature sitting on 7 hit points for 9. It screamed, its eyes and claws collapsed inward, and it vanished. It never got a round-12 turn.

The party finished with 20 hit points between all four of them. Lap then volunteered that the creature's heal recharges on a 1d4-round cooldown and "I rolled twice a four — if I roll a one he will be still up at this point," and that "if you guys are level 5, there's no way you can kill this guy. This is one of the fights that people complain about Abomination Vault. It's unfair." He had inflated nothing: every number is a textbook elite template, and the magic immunity and the rank-4 Darkness are both native to the stat block. Pax's read was exactly right — "these fights are winnable on paper; the problem is with Abomination Vault and its abilities" — and he turned it into a rules change the whole table agreed to.

Never named at the table. The DM only ever called it "this thing." The party's working identification — a will-o'-wisp type — comes from Alder's Recall Knowledge on tapestry 7, not from the fight: his in-combat RK failed, and both post-fight attempts rolled low. (Foundry's actor name is "Voidglutton," which nobody spoke aloud.)

The stat block, and who built it

  • Level 9 elite — HP 110, AC 32 (30 off-guard, 28 off-guard + Clumsy 2), Fort +15 · Ref +20 · Will +18, Perception +20, attack +22, Intelligence +6.
  • Three claws a round at +22/+18/+14 — 1d6+4 piercing + 4d6 force, 15–29 a hit, 38–50 on a crit, each carrying Fearful Strike (DC 28 Will → Frightened).
  • Ectoplasmic Web (+22 ranged, Immobilized, DC 26 Escape) — opened the fight by pinning Snagg and Alder to the floor.
  • At-will rank-4 Darkness, and Feed on Fear: 3d4+4 healing off anything Frightened or Dying, on a 1d4-round cooldown.
  • Lap inflated nothing. This is a textbook elite template on the book's level 8 Voidglutton (HP 90 → 110, AC 30 → 32, attack +20 → +22, saves +2 each). Both of the brutal parts — the magic immunity and the rank-4 Darkness — are native to the AP stat block. His only two deviations: Feed on Fear ran 3d4+4 rather than a flat 3d4, and he used the book's counteract DC 26 instead of the elite 28, which helped the party.

The magic immunity was beatable, and Snagg found the key mid-fight. The block's exception list resolves to four spells plus anything with the light trait: Faerie Fire, Revealing Light, Force Barrage and Quandary. (The block also lists glitterdust, magic missile and maze — those are the pre-remaster names for the first three, not extra options.) Faerie Fire and Revealing Light are both rank-2 divine spells. In round 3, right after the Dispel failed, Snagg asked Ruben directly whether he had Revealing Light — "you have revealing light?" — and the answer was "I do not have revealing light." This wasn't a lockout; it was a gap in the repertoire, identified while the fight was still live.

The accuracy gap is the fight

  • It connected on 21 of 24 attack rolls — 87.5%, four criticals, and not one critical miss.
  • The party connected on 9 of 45 — 26.7%, with 14 critical misses. Snagg 45.5% (11 rolls), Xurl 24.0% (25 rolls, 12 critical misses), Alder 11.1% (9 rolls, one hit).
  • Snagg's +17 jaws needed a 15 against AC 32. Xurl and Alder at +15 needed a 17.
  • Party HP bars total 274; they absorbed 388. The creature healed 22 back over two Feed on Fear procs.

Round by Round

  • R1 — It webs Snagg and Alder to the floor and claws Ruben for 22. Ruben puts up Benediction (+1 status AC, up all fight). Alder's Fear lands Frightened 1 on a successful save — dropping its AC to 31 — then Exploit Vulnerability succeeds on the nose, Esoteric Lore 26 vs DC 26. Snagg escapes the web and leaves it Grabbed and Off-Guard for 11, spending a hero point to bump a miss into a hit.
  • R2 — It escapes the grab and claws Snagg for 23 (shield eats 8) and Xurl for 15. Alder's chakram connects for 20 — his only hit of the night. Xurl's Demoralize critically fails. Snagg's Snagging Strike comes up a natural 1, hero-pointed into a 3 — still a critical miss — then Combat Grab rolls a natural 20: critical hit, 18, Crit Deck #24. → 61 HP.
  • R3 — the lights go out. Snagg lands a Reactive Strike on the Darkness cast (casting is a manipulate action) for 9. Ruben's Dispel fails, and he declines a hero point once the DM names DC 26: "I'm not going to waste a hero." Alder rolls a natural 1, hero-points to an 18, and still fails by 1. Rank-4 Darkness blinds the entire party — a DC 11 flat check on every attack. Snagg lands two clean jaws hits and loses both to failed flat checks (3 and 7).
  • R4 — All four Perception checks into the dark fail (18/15/13/8). It rolls a natural 20 on Stealth for 40 to go fully undetected. Sepulchral Mask is critically succeeded against for zero. Xurl critically misses all three Crane Wings. Ruben critically fails a fear save. → 52 HP.
  • R5 — Feed on Fear heals it 10. A claw for 29 puts Ruben down, Dying 1. Snagg's Treat Wounds critically succeeds for 22 and stands him straight back up. Alder burns his whole turn on three failed Escapes.
  • R6 — the worst round. A 46-point critical on Ruben takes him to 10, then an 18 puts him down again. Snagg's second Treat Wounds (18) stands him up. Ruben's second Dispel — natural 1, hero-pointed to a 22 — fails again. Xurl hero-points a miss into a hit for 9.
  • R7 — the turn. Two claws put Ruben down a third time, Dying 3, and he spends his last hero point to stay alive. A 50-point critical drops Xurl to 13. Alder finally escapes the web off his own Guidance — and immediately feeds Xurl a healing potion off Dying 3. Then Xurl rolls a natural 20: critical hit for 28, Crit Deck #47, applying Clumsy 2 + Enfeebled 2, with its Fortitude save a natural 1. → 11 HP.
  • R8 — A claw for 23 would have dropped Xurl; Orc Ferocity holds him at 1 HP. The next for 25 puts him down, Dying 2. Snagg uses Doctor's Visitation — Stride + Battle Medicine — for 3. Alder pulls a Sure Strike scroll and misses anyway.
  • R9 — Xurl rolls his second natural 20 and fails both flat checks (a natural 1, then an 8). The critical is erased by the darkness. A claw puts him down again, Dying 3. Feed on Fear heals it 12 — and that heal reignites its own Glow, ending the Darkness and clearing its own Clumsy 2 / Enfeebled 2. The DM: "You see that light appear in front of you. You two are no longer blind. The light is coming from him." Snagg's Treat Wounds critically fails on a natural 1 and stabs him for 2.
  • R10 — no more flat checks. Xurl chips it to 14. Then a 48-point critical on Alder leaves him on 8, and a claw for 24 takes him down — dropping his chakram, cracked mirror and buckler. Snagg's Battle Medicine (7) stands him back up, and Alder picks the chakram up.
  • R11 — Snagg falls. Xurl chips it to 7. Then it critically hits Snagg for 38 — the Sturdy Shield absorbs 8 but breaks past its Break Threshold (45 cumulative against BT 32), and the lost +2 circumstance AC turns the follow-up rolls of 26 and 25 into hits for 18 and 21, putting Snagg down at 0.
  • R12 — the kill. With Snagg and Ruben both unconscious and Alder delaying to give the flank, Xurl connects on a natural 15 and rolls 9 damage into a creature with 7 HP. "Ben, as he jumps up on the bed, you punch this thing. You see that it scream. And all the eyes and the claw suck back into it. It disappears." It never got a round-12 turn.

Hero points: 8 spent in the fight — Snagg 2, Ruben 3, Xurl 2, Alder 1 — plus Alder's pre-combat reroll on the Lantern, for 9 across the session. The DM had handed the whole table one five minutes before initiative, for Alder's mirror vision. Only 5 show as two-d20 reroll lines; three were degree-of-success bumps, which Lap's house rule allows and which leave no second d20 in the export. (A sixth reroll in the log is Xurl's Halfling Luck on the crowbar, not a hero point.)

Luck audit: 17 natural 1s against 6 reroll lines confirms this table has no blanket nat-1 auto-reroll — every reroll is a deliberate spend. Three natural 20s went the party's way (Snagg 1, Xurl 2), and one of Xurl's was erased by a failed flat check. The creature rolled two of its own, including the 50-damage crit on Xurl.

The incapacitation trait did real work, correctly. Stunning Blows is an incapacitation effect, and because the creature is level 9 against a level 6 monk, it was entitled to one degree of success better on both saves — RAW for a non-spell effect is simply "a creature of higher level than the item, creature, or hazard generating the effect." It fired twice: in round 6 a plain success became a critical success, and in round 7 Xurl's natural 1 critical failure became a plain failure. Since Stunning Blows reads "Stunned 1 (or Stunned 3 on a critical failure)," that second one turned three actions of denial into one in the round before the creature put Xurl to 1 HP and then down — the single most expensive rules interaction of the fight, and entirely by the book.

Character Spotlight

Snagg · the field hospital that bites

  • He played this fight as a medic first and a fighter second, and it was the right call. Four successful Treat Wounds / Battle Medicine applications against eleven attack rolls — and he stood three different people up: Ruben twice (a crit for 22, then 18), Xurl once off Dying 2 via Doctor's Visitation, and Alder once. A fifth attempt critically failed and stabbed him for 2.
  • He quietly won every lore beat of the session. His Crafting 29 identified the Lantern of Empty Light — the item Alder had been staring into — and he backed the Recall Knowledge lane on the dioramas and the tapestries with the party's highest Intelligence.
  • He found the one thing that could have broken the fight open, while it was still live. The creature's magic immunity has a short exception list, and two entries on it — Faerie Fire and Revealing Light — are rank-2 divine spells. Seconds after Ruben's Dispel failed, Snagg asked him straight out: "you have revealing light?" The answer was "I do not have revealing light." Nobody else went looking for the loophole.
  • Best mechanical read of the night, and he lost the argument on purpose. Blinded, he made the case that Reactive Strike should still trigger on a creature he couldn't see — then conceded cleanly: "That's why I'm saying rules as written, because the GM has the right to rule and interpret how he wants. So I will accept that. I just wanted to bring it up as a chance to get us an advantage in this fight that seems pretty hard." He was also right about the mechanic that mattered: Darkness is a manipulate action, so his Reactive Strike triggered on the cast, and the DM rewound to let both reaction Strikes resolve before the blindness landed.
  • Round 2's natural 20 was his one big hit — a critical for 18 and Crit Deck #24. 52 damage on 45% accuracy against AC 32, and two clean jaws hits in round 3 erased by failed flat checks in the darkness.
  • He went down in round 11 — and then argued himself back onto the floor. A 38-point crit blew his Sturdy Shield past its Break Threshold, and the lost +2 circumstance AC turned the next two rolls from misses into hits. He caught the error first and won it on exact arithmetic — "it still had 19 HP left" (64 − 45 = 19) — and the DM reverted both hits. Then Alder pointed out that a shield breaks at half, not at zero, and Snagg reversed himself against his own interest: "So yeah, I am down. I'll take the damage, and then I'll take this damage."
  • Dark comedy, perfectly timed: right after a rare hit, "At least this thing doesn't have fucking like fast healing and some bullshit." It healed twice more.
  • The RP half was his too. He's the one who trails the kobolds home at the end of a near-death night "because, you know, there's a guy in the party that still cares" — and the one who took delivery of a ghost touch rune and immediately started planning a return trip for the ghost he's never been able to hurt. His Diplomacy on the bridge failed; the humans carried it.

Xurl · the one who kept swinging

  • He killed it. Round 12, up onto a bed, a flurry punch into a creature on 7 HP for 9 — with Alder delaying to hand him the flank. Second session running that Xurl lands the finishing blow on a floor boss. It never got another turn.
  • The round-7 natural 20 is the turn that made it survivable. A critical for 28 plus Crit Deck #47, sticking it with Clumsy 2 + Enfeebled 2 — and its Fortitude save came up a natural 1 on top. It went from 39 HP to 11 in one turn.
  • He led the party in damage with 62 — and threw 25 attacks, 12 of them critical misses. Three in one turn in round 4 ("Three critical misses in a turn. Great"), three more in round 6. He also had a second natural 20 in round 9 wiped out by back-to-back failed flat checks — a natural 1, then an 8.
  • He was honest about being locked out and kept acting anyway: "There's literally nothing I can do to help.   • Orc Ferocity bought the party a turn — round 8, held at 1 HP instead of dropping. He still went down twice and ended on Wounded 3.
  • The best tactical joke of the night was also a real decision: "So observe the elite tactical move of fuck this shit. I'm going out of line to fight him." Wounded 3 and one hit from death — "It's shown that it loves Coup de Grâce-ing one person at a time" — he deliberately broke off and came back in when it was safe.
  • Out of combat he is the party's deduction engine, not just its comedian. He solved the war room (pulled the webs off and identified the centrepiece as a live illusion, not a model), found and routed around the elevator, remembered the second set of blueprints existed at all, and called two of tapestry 7's six figures before the Recall Knowledge came back.
  • His advice to the kobolds was the most useful thing anyone said to them — small jobs, messages, horses, pest control, "just let people see you helping the town." And his lecture about carrying healing potions ("Tip your medics") is the reason he was alive in round 8 at all.

We don't know shit, but we like rolling the dice." / "Yeah, well, we touch doors. Ruben Falmoor · the man who held the line on principle

  • He found Belcorra's private suite. The whole Act IV centrepiece — four illusion replays, both tapestries, the seven-fingered-hand lead — came off his exploration roll, when Xurl's and Snagg's came up a 3 and a 4. The most consequential roll of the session was his.
  • He is the party's conscience and he used it twice. He killed Snagg's disguise-the-kobolds plan on ethics alone — "If you're going to do good, it is best not to hide behind trickery to do so" — and talked the clan down from marching everyone into town, because "the entirety of the clan coming out at once for the first time would most likely be seen as an invasion."
  • He ran the negotiation that got them in. The DC 18 Diplomacy at the bridge is the only reason two kobolds walked into Otari instead of getting speared: "Calm yourselves. We have been hired by the mayor to investigate the Gauntlight. That is above your pay grade, unfortunately." Then he doubled back to commend the guards to the mayor.
  • Asked whether Belcorra's ultimatum changed the job, he gave the answer that defines him: "There's still much work. I've been paid for a job. I will not cancel a contract just because of this."
  • His natural 20 on Medicine dated the butcher-room remains to 4–5 days — the only hard timeline the party has on what's still living down there.
  • Then the fight happened to him. 166 damage taken — three times his 56-point maximum — three knockdowns, Dying 3, Wounded 3, and zero damage dealt, because a creature immune to magic turns a divine sorcerer into a healer with a shield. He spent three hero points: two on Dispel Magic counteracts trying to strip the Darkness, and his last at Dying 3 to stay alive.
  • He read the targeting correctly while it was happening — "he keeps fucking targeting me. Specifically." He was right; it put more Strikes into him than anyone.
  • He kept himself in the fight the whole time — Lay on Hands twice, Benediction's +1 status AC aura up for the entire party from round 1 to the end, Rousing Splash on Snagg, and a 49-point Heal the round he got back up. He also declined a hero point when the DM named the counteract DC and the maths stopped working: "You know what? I'm not going to waste a hero."
  • And he knows when to let go. On whether the party should keep carrying the kobolds: "They must learn. They must learn to fly from the nest on their own." (Xurl, immediately: "Fuck it, yeet the child.")

Alder Ungram · the man who stood up for somebody

  • The session's best roleplay beat is his, and nobody asked him to do it. The Intimidating Glare on the townsman heckling the kobolds: "get back to work or whatever you're supposed to be doing" → "Mind your own business, who are you, shut up, go away" → "Listen to your own advice, buddy." → "Want to fight me, huh?" He volunteered for all of it — "I'm touching everything" — identified the Staff of Necromancy and the scrolls on a −1 Intelligence, corrected the DM on the identification rules, and spent Scholarly Recollection to confirm Belcorra's illusions were harmless recordings rather than a haunt.

  • He's the one who remembers the loose threads. He raised the armoured knight with Wrin when nobody else did, and his stated goal for the return trip is the broadest in the party: "get rid of all of the ones that we couldn't before… Destroy all of those abominations."
  • His round-1 Fear landed and set up the entire opening. Fear on a successful save still applies Frightened 1 — which is what dropped the creature's AC to 31 and made Snagg's opening sequence possible. Then Exploit Vulnerability succeeded on the nose, Esoteric Lore 26 against DC 26.
  • Then he was webbed to the floor for six rounds — blind on top of it, Drained, burning whole turns on Escape attempts failing at DC 12: "I can't fucking do anything except trying to fucking escape." It took a Guidance cantrip on himself to finally get a critical success in round 7.
  • The first thing he did with his freedom was save Xurl's life. Out of the web in round 7, he spent his turn feeding Xurl a healing potion off Dying 3 — "I'll rabbit and feed it to you" — the exact contingency Xurl had lectured the party about two hours earlier. And in round 12 he delayed his turn to give Xurl the flank for the kill.
  • 1 hit in 9 attack rolls, and in round 10 he took a 48-point critical into a 56-point bar, went down, and dropped his chakram, his cracked mirror and his buckler. He got back up, picked the chakram up, and kept throwing.
  • He tracked the fight's real shape better than anyone, in two lines: "Has this guy missed a single attack yet?" and, on a 99% roll going wrong, "This is some XCOM-related bullshit."
  • And he found a rules improvement mid-fight that will matter every session from here: Scroll Thaumaturgy lets him draw and activate scrolls with the hand already holding his esoterica — "so I don't have to stow any of my stuff." His cracked mirror also solved the trophy room, showing the seven-fingered hand that pinned three bodies to the wall.

What Snagg is thinking about

  • Medic-first was correct, and that is the problem. Against AC 32 his jaws were a 45% proposition and his Medicine was better than 90%. The wall only works if the wall can also hurt things. Accuracy, not damage, is the gap.
  • The magic immunity was beatable and nobody had the key — and Snagg worked that out during the fight. The exception list is four spells plus anything with the light trait, and two of them, Faerie Fire and Revealing Light, are divine. He asked Ruben for Revealing Light in round 3 and got "I do not have revealing light." That is a repertoire conversation, not a dead end — and it is the third magic-immune enemy this campaign. Raise it with Ruben before the next descent.
  • Darkness is the real weapon, not the claws. A DC 11 flat check on every attack for seven rounds cost the party at least three confirmed hits including one of Xurl's natural 20s. The party's own light sources were blanked because the Lantern is only a level-6 item. Rank 4 or better, or a way to see without light.
  • The shield needs repairing before the next descent, and Snagg carries a Repair Toolkit.
  • Rider-stacking is the AP's signature, and he named it to the table: every monster down here has a resistance or a rider on its basic attack. Plan the kit around riders, not hit points.
  • The hero-point rule is banked. Don't waste it. Starting a session at zero now means starting with one.
  • Twenty hit points between four people, deep in a dungeon. Getting out is the first problem next session, not going further.
