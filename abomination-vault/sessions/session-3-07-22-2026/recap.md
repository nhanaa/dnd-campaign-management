## Session Summary

A quiet, careful floor turned loud twice — and both times the smart move was to leave. Picking over Floor 3's book-choked scriptorium rooms, the party catalogued tapestries, a carved door to **Nhimbaloth** (goddess of emptiness), and a model of an Absalom that doesn't match the city Alder knows. Then Argon, hunting for a secret door, pried open a hatch in a too-hot little chamber and woke the **Vengeful Furnace** — a haunt whose burning ghosts *ate a piece of his memory.* He spent the rest of the night not remembering why he'd come to the Gauntlight at all, leaning on companions he only half-recognized.

Deeper in, a scribe's ghost rose from a chain-shirted skeleton and ordered the party back to their desks. Bluffs failed, she screamed, and the fight that followed had no version the party could win: she went first, wailed them into sorrow, shrugged off everything they threw (six damage, total), and dropped Argon with a single fifty-point strike. But Argon had noticed the pendant in the dead scribe's hand — and instead of swinging, he held it up: *"Is this your love?"* She stopped. She reached for it. It fell through her incorporeal hand, and she went back to weeping for a man who betrayed her. The party ran, closed the door, and only *afterward* learned the truth: ghosts like her aren't meant to be beaten. You talk them down, or you lay them to rest — and they'd been holding the key the whole time.

They regrouped in Otari: delivered a pristine tapestry to Wrin, planned a decoy-shipment ambush for the lumber pirates with Mayor Osif, and — broke to the bone — bought a few scrolls. Alder spent his downtime befriending the priestess Vandy, angling for the library's restricted collection on ghosts. The DM closed on the thesis the whole night had been building toward: *"Not everything should be resolved with combat."*

## Key Events

### Act I — The Scriptorium Rooms (exploration)
- Picked up where S2 ended: the big bookshelf chamber with five doors. Searched bodies (nothing) and shelves — necromancy, sacrifice-ritual, and regional-history texts, too vague to pin the cult's ritual without knowing *which* ritual.
- **Alder floated an infiltration plan:** use his **Masquerade Scarf** to disguise as a ghoul, slip through, and scout the ritual. Shelved when the rooms turned out empty of ghouls.
- Recovered a **well-preserved tapestry** — a woman over a slain-but-breathing bear in a snowy forest; in the blood's reflection, serpentfolk building a shrine around a **mossy stone skull**; a second pool showing a woman before a half-built lighthouse. Added to the stash for Wrin to interpret.
- **Alder identified the carved north double-stone door:** a representation of **Nhimbaloth, goddess of nothing/emptiness**, taking the souls of her followers — **this is the boss door: Belcorra's tomb** (confirmed later as a point of no return). *(Nhimbaloth spelling tentative — confirm with the DM.)*
- Explored a cracked-mirror study, a suspiciously **clean washroom** with a mossy-skull fountain, and a **model of Absalom** that Alder — who has Absalom Lore — swears looks *nothing* like the real city.

### Act II — The Vengeful Furnace (haunt)
- In a small, unnaturally **hot** chamber, Argon searched for a secret door, lifted a barrel, and opened a **metal hatch in the floor** — releasing **two burning ghosts.**
- **Burn Knowledge** ate Argon's memories in stages: the first failed save took *why he was in the Gauntlight*; a second burning lash took his companions' faces too (they flickered back as he fled). Left **Stupefied 1 for 24 hours.**
- The ghosts lashed him for **24** on a crit. Argon, confused, swung his guisarme twice and connected (23 damage into the furnace — not enough to destroy it), then made the only sane call and **ran** as Ruben and Alder pulled him out.
- **The party never learned it was a haunt.** No one was told there were disable options (Intimidation to cow the spirits, Religion to exorcise, or destroying the furnace) — it read as "a wall spat fire and stole Argon's memory."

### Act III — The Weeping Scribe (the unwinnable ghost)
- A scriptorium with writing desks, dried inkwells, and a **skeleton in a shiny chain shirt.** Stepping in, a **ghost** appeared: *"Sit down, start working! You're late!"*
- Bluffs to placate her failed — Alder's "the day's ended, shift change," then **Argon's deadpan "I'm here to hand in my resignation letter."** She screamed and combat began.
- **She was a wall the party couldn't climb:** won initiative (31), opened with **Despairing Cry** (sorrow AoE — Ruben and Xurl slowed), and the party's entire offensive output was **six damage.** Alder's Exploit Vulnerability failed and his chakram critically missed; Argon's Intimidating Strike missed; only Ruben's **Vitality Lash** chipped her.
- **She crit Argon for 50** with a ghostly hand. Ruben poured in a Heal to keep him up.
- **The real turn:** Alder pulled a **pendant** from the dead scribe's hand — inscribed with the name **Volluk**, whom Alder recognized as *Belcorra's assistant*, the ghost's lost love. **Argon held it up to her: "Is this your love?"** She calmed, focused on it — but when he tried to pass it over, **it fell through her hand** and she resumed weeping: *"Why did you betray me, my love?"*
- Recognizing there was no win here, **the party fled and sealed the door.**

### Act IV — The Lesson, Late (knowledge)
- Safely out, Alder's Recall Knowledge finally paid off: **ghosts are put to rest, not beaten down** — talk them into letting go, or force it via a **Religion exorcism** (or, brutally, grind them to 0). And the pendant/lost-love story is exactly the lever to talk *this* one down.
- The DM noted Alder could use his **scrying mirror** to look into the past and reconstruct what happened between the scribe and the assistant.
- Confirmed the **Nhimbaloth-carved north door is the point of no return** — the DM warned "something important is behind it; once you open it, there's no turning back." This is **Belcorra's tomb / the boss arena.** Left unopened; the party isn't ready.
- Belatedly identified the S2 **Gibbering Mouther** as an import from the **underworld** — a thinking blob that doesn't belong on the surface.

### Act V — Otari (town, planning, downtime)
- Back to town, low on everything. Transferred/appraised the **mossy magic pick** (a +1 pick, similar make to Argon's weapon) and sold it off; split ~17 gp.
- Delivered the pristine tapestry to **Wrin Sivinxi**, who was delighted; Alder made a warm impression thanking her.
- **Mayor Osif** reported he'd borrowed **two cannons.** **Argon** pitched a **decoy-shipment ambush** — hide aboard, let the pirates board, spring the trap (Alder refined it: hide in the barrels, hit them as they board). Launch: **noon, two days out** (the same day as the midnight dwarf-sacrifice — the timing conflict still looms).
- **Downtime:** Alder courted **Vandy** at the Dawnflower Library as a contact, angling for the restricted collection on ghosts ("knowledge is power"). She'll see what she can dig up.

## Character Spotlight

### Argon Rath · the Amnesiac Who Kept His Head
Pax's orc got the worst of the night mechanically and turned it into the session's emotional spine. The Furnace wiped his memory in stages — first he forgot *why he'd come to the Gauntlight*, and after a second burning lash he forgot his companions' faces too, before they flickered back as he fled. He **played it straight the whole way through**, half-recognizing his own party and *choosing to trust them anyway:* "Well, I guess I'll just have to follow you guys. I'll just trust you guys." He carried it clean into the next day's downtime — "I got a good rest, I still don't really remember why we went there, but I'll just follow you guys." It's loyalty written without a memory to justify it, and it's the best RP at the table this session.

He was also the party's **comic pressure valve and its conscience.** The deadpan "I'm here to hand in my resignation letter" to a screaming ghost is an all-timer. But under the jokes he was the one steering risk all night — "I don't want to go fight at this HP," "we need to be better prepared than this," "we're low on resources" — the amnesiac orc was the most cautious head in the room. And in town, it was Argon who **pitched the decoy-shipment ambush** for the pirates.

And crucially, **Argon found the answer nobody else reached for.** While the others traded attacks, he clocked the pendant, connected it to the ghost's grief, and tried *empathy* — "Is this your love?" — the exact non-combat solution the DM later confirmed. He couldn't complete it (the pendant fell through her), but he was the first to see that this was a person to be reached, not a monster to be killed. Then he called the retreat with feeling: "The chance to run, that is. Fuck this shit."

*(The frustration is real and separate: in the actual combat rounds he had almost no agency — memory-wiped, one missed swing, then a 50-damage crit. His lone mechanical bright spot was hero-pointing a crit-failed Despairing Cry save into a nat-20 to shrug off her sorrow-wail. The RP was rich; the fight gave him little to do. Both things are true.)*

### Alder Ungram · the Answers Guy
The party's investigator ran the knowledge lane all night and did it with warmth. He **IDed the Nhimbaloth door**, recognized the mismatched Absalom model, pitched the **ghoul-disguise infiltration** plan, and — after the ghost fight — pieced the whole tragedy together: "This might be Belcorra's assistant… I guess she loved him? I don't know if he loved her back, though." That deduction, plus the RK on how to lay ghosts to rest, is what converted a bad fight into a plan.

He was also the party's **face and its glue.** He bluffed the ghost ("the day's ended, shift change"), thanked Wrin sincerely ("it's a collaborative effort — between us all, we'll solve this mystery"), gently offered to catch amnesiac Argon up "over a beer," and spent his downtime cultivating Vandy for the library's restricted ghost collection. His thesis, delivered to a priestess: "Knowledge is power — knowing anything about these creatures is the best way to deal with them."

### Ruben Falmoor · the Pharasman Anchor
Running on almost no sleep, Ruben still held the party together as **healer and moral center.** His clearest faith beat landed early — surveying the necromantic rooms: "There's a lot of blasphemy here. Raising the dead? It spits on the concept of life." Pure Pharasma.

In the fights he was the one keeping people upright — the **only clean damage** on the ghost (Vitality Lash, since void heals her but vitality doesn't), a big Heal to keep a battered Argon on his feet, and the discipline to **hero-point a crit-failing Despairing Cry save** down to a mere fail ("I can't risk it — as a spellcaster, I can't risk it") — one of *two* hero points burned on that wail, alongside Argon's. His bedside manner with the panicking, memory-wiped Argon — "Easy there. Easy there. Breathe, breathe." — was the quiet heart of the Furnace aftermath; he then diagnosed the memory loss as magical and temporary, promising Argon he'd be fine by tomorrow. And he named the mood honestly when they regrouped: "A second time we got our shit rocked since we've been down here."

### Xurl · in absentia
Ben was out, so Xurl was a DM-moved token — and the running gag survived even under DM control: his lone grapple attempt on the ghost was a crit-fail the DM didn't even bother to roll. Argon dragged his token along by hand ("I'll bring him in — let's just pretend he's following us").

## Combat Encounters

### The Vengeful Furnace (Complex Haunt, Hazard 4)
Not a fight so much as an ambush the party couldn't identify. Argon triggered it opening the floor hatch; **Burn Knowledge** hit him with the memory-loss Stupefied (24-hour duration on the failed save), and a **Burning Lash crit for 24**. Argon landed two guisarme hits (**23 damage** into the furnace) before fleeing — the party never learned it was a haunt with disable options, so they simply left the room (a haunt resets when empty).

- **Effective threat:** an on-level Hazard 4; fair on paper, but run without the "this is a haunt" read, which removed all counterplay.
- **Outcome:** left active; Argon stupefied for 24h.

### The Weeping Scribe — Chandriu Invisar (Elite ghost; unwinnable as fought)
*The party never learned her name in-fiction. She is Chandriu Invisar, run with the **Elite template** — a book level-6 ghost pushed to level 7, i.e. **PL+3** against a level-4 party of three. As a solo with **Rejuvenation**, she cannot be permanently killed by damage; the intended resolution is a Diplomacy talk-down (over her grief for **Volluk** — Belcorra's assistant, whose name the pendant bears) or a Religion exorcism.*

**Initiative:** Chandriu **31** · Ruben 22 · Alder 16 · Xurl 12 · Argon 12.

**Round 1**
- **Chandriu — Despairing Cry** (DC 26 Will, sorrow AoE): Alder **crit success** (nat 20). Argon **crit-failed**, then **hero-pointed the reroll into a nat 20 → crit success.** **Ruben** hero-pointed his own crit-fail down to a plain **fail** → Slowed 1. **Xurl failed** → Slowed 1. *(Two hero points spent on this one wail.)*
- **Alder:** Exploit Vulnerability vs DC 23 → **failed** (personal antithesis only); **Instructive Strike** chakram vs AC 25 → **critical miss.**
- **Ruben:** **Vitality Lash** — she made the basic Fort save → **6 damage** (the party's only damage on her all fight).
- **Argon:** **Intimidating Strike** guisarme vs AC 25 → **miss.**
- **Xurl** (DM-run): Slowed 1 — managed only Crane Stance and a move.

**Round 2**
- **Chandriu — Ghostly Hand** vs Argon (AC 21) → **crit for 50 void.** It nearly folded him; Ruben answered with a **24-point Heal** to keep him up.
- **Xurl** (DM-run): two **Crane Wing** strikes vs AC 25 → miss/miss; a **grapple** attempt → **critical failure.**
- **The pendant beat:** Alder retrieved the lost-love pendant from the skeleton; **Argon showed it to her — "Is this your love?"** She calmed and fixed on it, but the hand-off **fell through her incorporeal grasp** and she resumed grieving.
- **Party fled** and sealed the door.

- **Total party damage to Chandriu: 6 (of ~84 HP).** No win condition was available in the room (Rejuvenation + Elite +3 solo). Fleeing was correct.
