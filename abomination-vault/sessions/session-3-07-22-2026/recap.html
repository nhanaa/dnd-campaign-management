A quiet, careful floor turned loud twice — and both times the smart move was to leave. Picking over Floor 3's book-choked scriptorium rooms, the party catalogued tapestries, a carved door to Nhimbaloth (goddess of emptiness), and a model of an Absalom that doesn't match the city Alder knows. Then Argon, hunting for a secret door, pried open a hatch in a too-hot little chamber and woke the Vengeful Furnace — a haunt whose burning ghosts ate a piece of his memory. He spent the rest of the night not remembering why he'd come to the Gauntlight at all, leaning on companions he only half-recognized.

Deeper in, a scribe's ghost rose from a chain-shirted skeleton and ordered the party back to their desks. Bluffs failed, she screamed, and the fight that followed had no version the party could win: she went first, wailed them into sorrow, shrugged off everything they threw (six damage, total), and dropped Argon with a single fifty-point strike. But Argon had noticed the pendant in the dead scribe's hand — and instead of swinging, he held it up: "Is this your love?" She stopped. She reached for it. It fell through her incorporeal hand, and she went back to weeping for a man who betrayed her. The party ran, closed the door, and only afterward learned the truth: ghosts like her aren't meant to be beaten. You talk them down, or you lay them to rest — and they'd been holding the key the whole time.

They regrouped in Otari: delivered a pristine tapestry to Wrin, planned a decoy-shipment ambush for the lumber pirates with Mayor Osif, and — broke to the bone — bought a few scrolls. Alder spent his downtime befriending the priestess Vandy, angling for the library's restricted collection on ghosts. The DM closed on the thesis the whole night had been building toward: "Not everything should be resolved with combat."

Timeline

Act I · Explore · The Scriptorium Rooms

Book-choked rooms, five doors

  • Picked up where S2 ended — the bookshelf chamber with five doors. Searched bodies (nothing) and shelves: necromancy, sacrifice-ritual, and regional-history texts, all too vague to pin the cult's ritual without knowing which ritual.
  • Alder floated an infiltration plan — use his Masquerade Scarf to disguise as a ghoul and scout the ritual. Shelved when the rooms turned out empty of ghouls.
  • Recovered a well-preserved tapestry: a woman over a slain-but-breathing bear in a snowy forest; in the blood's reflection, serpentfolk raising a shrine around a mossy stone skull; a second pool showing a woman before a half-built lighthouse.
  • Alder identified the carved north double-stone door as Nhimbaloth, goddess of nothing/emptiness, taking the souls of her followers — later confirmed to be Belcorra's tomb: the boss door.
  • Explored a cracked-mirror study, a suspiciously clean washroom with a mossy-skull fountain, and a model of Absalom that Alder — who has Absalom Lore — swears looks nothing like the real city (though he couldn't say why; he rolled a 2).
Act II · Combat · The Vengeful Furnace (Haunt)

A hatch better left shut

  • In a small, unnaturally hot chamber, Argon searched for a secret door, lifted a barrel, and opened a metal hatch in the floor — releasing two burning ghosts.
  • Burn Knowledge ate his memories in stages: the first failed save took why he was in the Gauntlight; a second burning lash took his companions' faces too (they flickered back as he fled). Left Stupefied 1 for 24 hours.
  • The ghosts lashed him for 24 on a crit. Confused, Argon swung his guisarme twice and connected (23 damage into the furnace — not enough to destroy it), then made the only sane call and ran as Ruben and Alder pulled him out.
  • The party never learned it was a haunt. No one was told there were disable options — Intimidation to cow the spirits, Religion to exorcise, or destroying the furnace — so it read as "a wall spat fire and stole Argon's memory."
  • Ruben, playing medic, talked the panicking amnesiac down — "Easy there. Breathe, breathe" — and diagnosed the loss as magical and temporary.
Act III · Combat · The Weeping Scribe

The ghost that couldn't be beaten

  • A scriptorium with writing desks, dried inkwells, and a skeleton in a shiny chain shirt. Stepping in, a ghost appeared: "Sit down, start working! You're late!"
  • Bluffs to placate her failed — Alder's "the day's ended, shift change," then Argon's deadpan "I'm here to hand in my resignation letter." She screamed, and combat began.
  • She was a wall the party couldn't climb: won initiative (31), opened with Despairing Cry (a sorrow AoE — Ruben and Xurl slowed), and the party's entire offensive output was six damage. Only Ruben's Vitality Lash chipped her; Alder's chakram critically missed and Argon's Intimidating Strike missed.
  • She crit Argon for 50 with a ghostly hand; Ruben poured in a Heal to keep him standing.
  • The real turn: Alder pulled a pendant — inscribed with the name Volluk, Belcorra's assistant — from the dead scribe's hand. Argon held it up: "Is this your love?" She calmed and fixed on it, but when he tried to pass it over, it fell through her incorporeal hand and she resumed weeping: "Why did you betray me, my love?"
  • Recognizing there was no win here, the party fled and sealed the door.
Act IV · Mystery · The Lesson, Late

The answer they were holding

  • Safely out, Alder's Recall Knowledge finally paid off: ghosts are put to rest, not beaten down — talk them into letting go, or force it via a Religion exorcism (or, brutally, grind them to 0). The pendant/lost-love story is exactly the lever to talk this one down.
  • The DM noted Alder could use his scrying mirror to look into the past and reconstruct what happened between the scribe and the assistant.
  • Confirmed the Nhimbaloth-carved north door is the point of no return — "once you open it, there's no turning back." This is Belcorra's tomb. Left unopened; the party isn't ready.
  • Belatedly identified the S2 Gibbering Mouther as an import from the underworld — a thinking blob that doesn't belong on the surface.
Act V · Social · Otari

Town, planning, downtime

  • Back to town, low on everything. Appraised the mossy magic pick (a +1 pick, similar make to Argon's weapon) and sold it off; split ~17 gp.
  • Delivered the pristine tapestry to Wrin Sivinxi, who was delighted; Alder made a warm impression thanking her — "it's a collaborative effort — between us all, we'll solve this mystery."
  • Mayor Osif reported he'd borrowed two cannons. Argon pitched a decoy-shipment ambush — hide aboard, let the pirates board, spring the trap (Alder refined it: hide in the barrels). Launch: noon, two days out.
  • The timing conflict looms: the midnight dwarf-sacrifice falls the same day as the pirate raid.
  • Downtime: Alder courted Vandy at the Dawnflower Library as a contact, angling for the restricted collection on ghosts — "knowledge is power." She'll see what she can dig up.

The party never learned her name in-fiction. She is Chandriu Invisar, run with the Elite template — a book level-6 ghost pushed to level 7 (PL+3) against a level-4 party of three. With Rejuvenation, she can't be permanently killed by damage; the intended resolution is a Diplomacy talk-down (over her grief for Volluk, the pendant's owner) or a Religion exorcism.

Initiative: Chandriu 31 · Ruben 22 · Alder 16 · Xurl 12 · Argon 12.

Round 1

  • Despairing Cry (DC 26 Will): Alder crit-success (nat 20); Argon crit-failed, then hero-pointed the reroll into a nat 20 → crit success; Ruben hero-pointed his own crit-fail down to a plain fail → Slowed 1; Xurl failed → Slowed 1. Two hero points on one wail.
  • Alder: Exploit Vulnerability vs DC 23 → failed (personal antithesis only); Instructive Strike chakram vs AC 25 → critical miss.
  • Ruben: Vitality Lash — she made the basic Fort save → 6 damage (the party's only damage all fight).
  • Argon: Intimidating Strike guisarme vs AC 25 → miss.
  • Xurl (DM-run): Slowed — managed only Crane Stance and a move.

Round 2

  • Ghostly Hand vs Argon (AC 21) → crit for 50 void. It nearly folded him; Ruben answered with a 24-point Heal.
  • Xurl (DM-run): two Crane Wing strikes vs AC 25 → miss/miss; a grapple → critical failure.
  • The pendant beat: Alder retrieved the pendant; Argon showed it to her — "Is this your love?" She calmed and fixed on it, but the hand-off fell through her incorporeal grasp and she resumed grieving.
  • Party fled and sealed the door.

Total party damage to Chandriu: 6 of ~84 HP. No win condition was available in the room — fleeing was correct.

Character Spotlight

Argon Rath · the Amnesiac Who Kept His Head

Pax's orc got the worst of the night mechanically and turned it into the session's emotional spine. The Furnace wiped his memory in stages — first why he'd come to the Gauntlight, then his companions' faces — and he played it straight the whole way through, half-recognizing his own party and choosing to trust them anyway: "Well, I guess I'll just have to follow you guys. I'll just trust you guys." He carried it clean into the next day — "I got a good rest, I still don't really remember why we went there, but I'll just follow you guys." Loyalty written without a memory to justify it, and the best RP at the table this session.

He was also the party's comic pressure valve and its conscience. The deadpan "I'm here to hand in my resignation letter" to a screaming ghost is an all-timer — but under the jokes he was the one steering risk all night ("I don't want to fight at this HP," "we need to be better prepared"), and in town it was Argon who pitched the decoy-shipment ambush.

And crucially, Argon found the answer nobody else reached for. While the others traded attacks, he clocked the pendant, connected it to the ghost's grief, and tried empathy — "Is this your love?" — the exact non-combat solution the DM later confirmed. He was the first to see a person to be reached, not a monster to be killed.

The frustration is real and separate: in the actual combat rounds he had almost no agency — memory-wiped, one missed swing, then a 50-damage crit. His lone mechanical bright spot was hero-pointing a crit-failed save into a nat-20. The RP was rich; the fight gave him little to do. Both things are true.

Alder Ungram · the Answers Guy

The party's investigator ran the knowledge lane all night and did it with warmth. He IDed the Nhimbaloth door, recognized the mismatched Absalom model, pitched the ghoul-disguise infiltration plan, and — after the ghost fight — pieced the whole tragedy together: "This might be Belcorra's assistant… I guess she loved him? I don't know if he loved her back, though." That deduction, plus the RK on how to lay ghosts to rest, is what converted a bad fight into a plan.

He was also the party's face and its glue — bluffing the ghost ("the day's ended, shift change"), thanking Wrin sincerely, gently offering to catch amnesiac Argon up "over a beer," and spending his downtime cultivating Vandy for the library's restricted ghost collection. His thesis: "Knowledge is power — knowing anything about these creatures is the best way to deal with them."

Ruben Falmoor · the Pharasman Anchor

Running on almost no sleep, Ruben still held the party together as healer and moral center. His clearest faith beat landed early, surveying the necromantic rooms: "There's a lot of blasphemy here. Raising the dead? It spits on the concept of life." Pure Pharasma.

In the fights he kept people upright — the only clean damage on the ghost (Vitality Lash), a big Heal to keep a battered Argon on his feet, and the discipline to hero-point a crit-failing save down to a mere fail ("I can't risk it — as a spellcaster, I can't risk it"), one of two hero points burned on that wail. His bedside manner with the panicking, memory-wiped Argon — "Easy there. Breathe, breathe" — was the quiet heart of the Furnace aftermath. And he named the mood honestly: "A second time we got our shit rocked since we've been down here."

Xurl · in absentia

Ben was out, so Xurl was a DM-moved token — and the running gag survived even under DM control: his Crane Wing strikes and a grapple all whiffed (the grapple a crit-fail). Argon dragged his token along by hand — "I'll bring him in — let's just pretend he's following us."
