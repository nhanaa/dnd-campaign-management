## Session Summary

Argon Rath came to the party the hard way — face-down in the dark. A displaced orc warrior taking patrol work on the Isle of Kortos, he'd heard of Otari from a fellow orc on the trail and set out to find new work there. Two days short of town he was ambushed at night by things with cold claws and a dead smell, beaten unconscious, and dragged into the basement of the **Gauntlight**. He came to in a black room, struck blind into his captors, and ran — feeling his way door to door until he lost them. He also lost something to their bite: the first fatigue of **ghoul fever** settling into his blood. Half-starved and sick, he passed out again, and woke to torchlight and voices — the very party hired to purge this place, doubling back down the stairs. They nearly braced for a fight ("You don't look particularly rotting"), then took him in instead.

With Argon confirmed bitten (and Alder admitting he'd caught a bite of his own earlier), the group scrapped the night's expedition and hauled their new stray back toward town — first to their base (an abandoned tavern they share, uneasily, with a warren of kobolds), then in the morning to the **Dawnflower Library**, where the priestess Vandy burned the fever out of Argon as a goodwill gesture to the town's new problem-solvers. Between the diagnosis and the cure, Argon signed on: he'd repay the healing by lending his guisarme to their contract. The job, they explained, is the Gauntlight itself — a dead lighthouse whose light woke undead in the Otari graveyard two weeks ago, tied to the long-dead tyrant **Belcorra Haruvex**, slain five centuries past and maybe stirring again.

But the Gauntlight is sealed until nightfall, and a day is a long time to a party with idle hands. Xurl and Argon spotted a **paper note flying eighty feet over the ocean** — the second such note in two days, launched from the cliffs above town, written in a language none of them read. That thread swallowed the rest of the session: a translation from the fortune-teller Wrin (a time, a place, in the gnoll tongue Kholo), a day's march west to the port town of **Chester**, Alder reading old malice off the paper with a psychometry scroll, Xurl dead-reckoning a pirate lane onto a bartender's scrap-paper map, and a long walk back to lay it all before **Mayor Osif Mahimis** — whose lumber convoys the notes have been selling to raiders. By session's end the party had talked their way into the pirate problem on purpose: fight pirates by day, the Gauntlight by night. Not one blade was drawn all night. The DM admitted, cheerfully, that he'd invented the entire side-quest on the spot when the players wandered off the map.

## Key Events

**Act I — The Man in the Dark (Argon's arrival)**
- Character and player introductions all around; Argon is the new face at an established table, and the DM ran his cold open solo before the others found him.
- Argon's backstory: orc warrior of a destroyed borderland clan, ex-captive turned wanderer, taking short patrol contracts on Kortos. A fellow orc patrolman's tale of Otari (and a slain baby dragon) pointed him here.
- Ambushed on the road by ghouls, dragged into the Gauntlight's basement, he woke, struck blind, and escaped by feel through the dark — losing the ghouls but catching **ghoul fever**.
- The party (returning from deeper in the dungeon) found him; a tense first contact ("Are you guys not my captors?") resolved into a rescue once they smelled the rot on his story.
- **Ruben's** medicine check confirmed the ghoul fever; **Alder** then read its progression (esoteric lore via diverse lore) — stage 2–3 of 6; stage 6 means death and rising as a ghoul. They aborted the expedition to save him.
- Full character descriptions traded in the hallway — Ruben's Pharasma amulet, Alder's bandolier of oddities and star-weapon, Argon's one-shouldered breastplate and tusks, and Xurl's inexplicable bioluminescence.

**Act II — Base, Cure, and the Lay of Otari**
- The party led Argon out of the swamp to their base — a half-cleaned abandoned tavern shared with kobolds — and gave him a room. **Ruben swore on Pharasma** that Argon would not perish.
- Next morning: to the **Dawnflower Library** (the Rose Blade of Vol Rajadi on display at the door), where the priestess **Vandy** cured the ghoul fever free of charge.
- Lore dump: the Gauntlight woke and threw undead into the Otari graveyard two weeks ago; the prior party cleared them; the light dimmed but may be reactivating. **Belcorra Haruvex** — slain 500 years ago by the Roseguard — is the suspected cause. Four tapestries of her life have been recovered so far.
- **Wrin Sivinxi** introduced — white-eyed tiefling fortune-teller running Wrin's Wonders. Alder introduced himself and angled for future business and lore.
- **Ruben** identified a salvaged wand — a **wand of quench** (primal; useless to a divine caster) — and sold it plus a sturdy shield for 130 gp, split to the party.

**Act III — The Flying Note**
- Xurl and Argon watched a **note fly ~80 ft over the ocean**, launched from the cliffs — the second in two days (the first, caught last session by Xurl swimming out for it).
- Written in an unreadable script; the party has lost the multilingual members (Victor and the departed prior PCs) who could have read it. **Wrin** identified the paper as a magical "homing" object and translated it for 7 gp: a **date, a time, and a place** (a small town west of Otari), in **Kholo** (the gnoll tongue).
- The whole party rolled catastrophically low on Intelligence checks (a nat 1, several 0-modifier rolls) — the running "we are a group of zero intelligence" gag was born here.
- **Argon** made the key leap: the note's time is *past*, so it can't be a meeting time — "Maybe something happened, like a report." The group voted to chase the note over returning to the Gauntlight.

**Act IV — Chester (the improvised pirate hunt)**
- A day-and-a-half march west to the port town of **Chester** (camp overnight; safe patrolled road; hero points topped to max).
- At the town inn, **Alder** ran the investigation: cast **object reading** (psychometry) off a scroll and sensed the note's author as **"evil, greedy, and determined"** → deduced a pirate.
- Talking to the bartender, Alder learned **Otari Lumber** (the mayor's company) ships to Chester; a shipment arrived two days ago. He pieced together that the notes broadcast shipment routes to raiders.
- **Xurl** — a former sailor — tried to **triangulate the pirate lane** by dead reckoning on a bartender's rough map ("liberal sailing navigation on a coastline; this is what I do"). Sloppy, wrong-direction, and hilarious, but it produced a rough coastal area.
- Ruben owned the misfire gracefully ("I saw the problem, used the completely wrong formula, and got the wrong answer") and the party turned back for Otari.

**Act V — The Mayor's Office**
- Back in Otari, a multi-hour stakeout of the cliff and pier turned up nothing (excellent perception all around — "some freaking Hawkeyes"), but reconstructing the two note-sightings cracked it: **note time = when a ship leaves; note place = its destination**, so pirates can intercept the convoy.
- To the West Wing of the lumber works, greeted by the mayor's elderly elf aide **Iqbal**, then **Mayor Osif Mahimis** himself (owner of Otari Lumber, shipments raided twice last week).
- The party laid out the whole scheme. The mayor — who'd normally hand it to the Arsenal Navy — offered to let them take the pirates themselves (harder, no navy escort, maybe a couple of guards and an under-equipped ship). They took it.
- Reward: 40 gp now; next shipment in two days. Plan set: **pirates by day, the Gauntlight by night.**

## Character Spotlight

### Argon Rath · the Newcomer with a Code
Argon spent the session being onboarded — literally waking into the campaign as a prisoner — but he was never passive. His whole arc is a man deciding, fast, what kind of ally he's going to be. The moment the party pledged to cure him, he flipped the ledger and offered his arm to their contract before he even had a weapon back: *"Since you guys are planning to help me, maybe I can repay the favor."* His bravado is dry and soldierly rather than boastful (*"I have survived many battles. What is one more?"* → *"What is many more? Bring it on."*), and it sits on top of a quiet steadiness. His first words on breaking free of the ghouls set the tone — *"I stand now free. At least I hope so, until I can get out of this place"* — wary, unbroken, already scanning for the exit. Playing the deliberately clueless new guy, he still made the deduction that actually reframed the note mystery (that the timestamp was a report, not a meeting time). There's a welcome dry comedy to him — demanding the party's mystery beer *"after this whole kidnapping"* — but the truest note he struck was trust: pulled from a cell into a stranger's oath, he answered Ruben's vow with a simple *"I trust you,"* a displaced man deciding on the spot that these three were worth following. By session's end, offered his pick of enemies, he was unbothered about which — *"Not really, to be honest"* — because to Argon one fight is as good as another, so long as there's a fight coming.

### Alder Ungram · the Man with the Answers
Alder is the party's occult investigator, and this was his session to prove it. Every time the plot needed a fact, Alder produced it: he read Argon's fever stage off diverse lore, he chatted up Wrin and the Chester bartender for the town-and-shipment intel, and — the single most important play of the night — he cracked the entire pirate mystery by casting **object reading** on the note and sensing its author as *"evil, greedy, and determined,"* then connecting the harbor, the greed, and the lumber routes into *"this might be a pirate."* He's the deductive engine, and he plays it with an unbothered curiosity — introducing himself around town, asking Wrin to read his fortune sometime, methodically working the barkeep two ales deep. His one persistent bit is that his answer to every obstacle is a purchasable scroll (Translate, helpful steps, water breathing), which the party started ribbing him for — but even that is the thaumaturge's toolbox at work.

### Ruben Falmoor · the Oathbound Diplomat
Ruben is the party's voice and its conscience. He carries the courtly register — *"gentlemen," "I suppose," "it would behoove you"* — and he does the load-bearing social work: consoling a frightened, diseased stranger, brokering with the priestess, and laying the full pirate case before the mayor. His defining moment is a vow: to a man he'd known for an hour, still sick and scared, he swore *"on the grace of Pharasma that you will not perish this day."* That's who Ruben is — a healer who treats a promise as a sacred instrument. He's also the session's most gracious loser: he drove the "follow the note" plan, watched his deductions turn out mostly wrong, and owned it without flinching (*"I saw the problem and used the completely wrong formula"* / *"my apologies for the wild duck chase"*). And he mines real comedy from his own build — a divine sorcerer who dumped Intelligence for faith and charisma, cheerfully conceding he has *"a zero in Intelligence"* while Xurl notes he *"has the power of God and anime on his side."*

### Xurl · the Sailor Who Knows Things
Xurl is a three-foot, forty-pound, faintly glowing halfling wrestler-monk who talks like a deckhand and thinks like a navigator. His comedic timing anchored the table — the deadpan role summary (*"He drinks and he knows things… I'm here to punch things"*), the honest job description (*"I'm not so good at hitting. Pretty good at grabbing, though"*), and the session mantra *"Plan is no plan. Fuck it, we ball."* But it would be a mistake to file him as comic relief, because Xurl did the most actual *reasoning* of anyone. He's the one who worked out the full pirate mechanism — that the note goes to the raiders, not the meeting, so you either stow away on the convoy or find the hideout — and he threw his old sailor's trade at the problem, dead-reckoning a search cone onto a scrap-paper map ("this is a new level of fuckery… liberal sailing navigation on a coastline"). Sloppy and wrong-way, but it was real navigation, and it was his idea. He carries the party's institutional memory too (he caught the first note last session, swimming out and nearly drowning), and quietly ran point on most of the tactical planning while cracking jokes the whole way.

## Combat Encounters

**None.** No initiative was rolled this session — it was entirely introduction, roleplay, social investigation, and travel. The one "danger" (Argon's ghoul fever) was resolved by a cure, not a fight. The party has lined up their first real fights for future sessions: the pirate convoy raid (by day) and the return to the Gauntlight dungeon (by night).
