Session Summary

The cliffhanger resolved fast and ugly. Seven Trinity Initiates were still standing behind their overturned tables when the session picked up, and Galan opened the round with a Divine Wrath that caught all seven and produced four critical failures at once — sickened, slowed, and suddenly unable to spend three actions on anything. He then walked the DM through his own spell's degrees of success to make sure the riders actually stuck. That one burst decided the encounter; everything afterwards was collection. Guar spent a Mythic Point to push a 4th-rank Fireball, recast as cold, to DC 37 and dropped it on the pile, and from there it was cleanup: forty minutes and four Foundry rounds turned seven entrenched casters into seven bodies, with Liu killing three, Kudu two, and Guar two. The initiates' entire answer was to chip Liu for 8, 11 and 7. He described it accurately at the time: "I'm just getting chipped down."

The last one didn't die on the floor. She flew fifteen feet up onto a balcony where nobody could reach her — and she was, by some distance, the luckiest thing in the room. She cast the Vision of Death that opened the session, she was the only initiate to save against Divine Wrath, she rolled a natural 20 against the Fireball, and she went the entire fight without ever carrying a single condition. Galan reached for the right idea — "I want to try to dispel her flying effect" — but the Dispel Magic he had prepared was too low-rank to counteract a 4th-rank Fly, which he didn't yet know, and she was already on the balcony by then anyway. He threw a Divine Lance instead and missed. So it fell to the two people who could physically get up there. Kudu Sudden Charged straight up a lava flow on Raging Athlete's climb speed and rolled a critical hit that came to nothing at all; she was still sitting on a full 70 HP seven minutes later. Liu — who had never climbed anything in this system — had the DM walk him through the rules line by line, worked out that climbing needs both hands, and then made the call the whole session turns on: he dropped his tome and his falcata on the floor and spent his entire turn — Climb, Climb, punch — hauling himself up the lava flow weaponless to buy one attack. The Mythic Strike fist critically hit for 60. She Stepped clear and reached for the door, and opening it drew Kudu's Reactive Strike for 124.

The hallway beyond was coated in a rank-7 illusion. Galan identified it on a Religion 38 and named exactly what it was — "extremely powerful illusion magic" — and then, to his credit, immediately said the useful thing rather than the flattering one: "I don't think I'm powerful enough to dispel it." His best prepared Dispel was rank 4, and he committed on the spot to fixing it. It was an Endless Reality, a mythic hazard whose only listed answers were legendary Thievery, legendary Arcana, or a 7th-rank Dispel against DC 39 — nothing a level-12 party has without spending mythic power. Guar priced the cost out loud and pre-committed to it — "if I have to, I absolutely will" — then paid, and cleared a rank-7 hazard with a rank-6 Dispel carried over the DC by his mythic proficiency. Galan closed the scene with the deduction of the night: "someone who was mythic placed the trap there in the first place, right?" The DM confirmed it. Clearing it refilled the whole party's mythic pool.

Behind the last door was an office full of star charts and incense, and behind the desk was a man in green with a polearm. Liu and Galan both read him before initiative, and the answer came back off his gear and his polearm: a runelord of greed. Kudu critically failed the same check by 21, and reported what his character actually got: "It's a guy in a green shirt." He had a Lamia Matriarch and three greedspawn soldiers with him, and he opened with "It's unfortunate you came snooping around. Now you gotta die." The fight ran five rounds and was the hardest of the campaign so far. Liu took 24, 26, 24 and 26 in the first round alone and finished it at 65 of 165. Kudu ate a 62-point critical Reactive Strike for swapping weapons mid-melee, then a 66-point falchion crit later. Gimmel put a Disintegrate into Galan for 75 — and Galan, whose first instinct was to check whether anyone else needed the Mythic Point more and to hold it, was talked into spending it by Kudu's "mythic that motherfucker." He critically succeeded the Fortitude save and took nothing.

It ended with the two reactions Liu has now used to close three of the campaign's five fights. Vesinra's Falchion Swipe crit Kudu for 66; Liu's Retributive Strike cut it to 46 and answered with a 90-damage crit that killed her outright. Then, with Gimmel walled off behind his own Wall of Stone and the party grinding at one inch of masonry, Kudu simply walked around the end of it and crit him for 116, and Liu spent his last turn doing the thing he was built to do — and it was the free roll that paid. His Exploit Vulnerability succeeded by exactly zero and bought almost nothing; the tome's no-action Recall Knowledge, made at the top of the same turn against a rare DC of 33, came back with a name: Risen Runelord Gimmel, dream trait, mythic trait. He asked his follow-up question about lore and got the whole entry. Then he Mythic Struck for 106 and the runelord came apart.

What fell out of the corpse was the mission. A comprehensive intelligence briefing on Xin-Eurythnia — written throughout under the city's ancient name, Xin-Shalast — proving the cult intends to strike the portal ceremony, and a paired sending slate carrying a message from someone who is still out there: "As feared, our city is crawling with heroes… If you don't hear back soon, assume the worst." Signed with one word: the Rune of Greed.

Act II · Exploration · Cleanup and the Storage Rooms

Twenty minutes on the clock, and salvage turned into ammunition

  • Detect Magic on the bodies turned up nothing beyond +1 striking bec de corbins on every initiate — worth four gold apiece under Automatic Rune Progression.
  • Galan critically succeeded both Treat Wounds (Assurance, no dice at all), and Ward Medic let each cover two people: the first healed Liu and Guar 28 apiece, the second rolled 43 and topped up Liu (33, capped) and Kudu.
  • Two storage rooms, stripped to dust and fragments, gave up a Greater Skeleton Key and assorted Dawnsilver oddments — a pen nib, a button, a monocle frame.
  • Galan turned salvage into ammunition: "Can I take some of this Dawnsilver to use it as, what's it called, Needle Darts?" The DM smashed the monocle frame into a Dawnsilver chunk for him.
  • Liu asked about Dawnsilver full plate; it needs a full ingot, and the party had one. He ultimately voted to sell it so everyone could shop: "if the money will help everyone else then I'm fine with just selling it."
  • Kudu raised the same thing twice, unprompted: "Next time we get back to town, remind me to buy some magical storage." He also personally logged every find into the party stash, and dug out the ingot that made Galan's request possible.
Act III · Mystery · The Endless Reality

A hazard built so only mythic characters could pass it

  • Guar's Detect Magic found a rank-7 aura coating the entire hallway. "I'm going to let everyone know that there's a big-boy spell coating that entire hallway."
  • Galan identified it with a Religion check of 38 — a complex magical illusion mythic trap — and relayed it in character: "I always tell everyone that this is extremely powerful illusion magic." Then, immediately: "I don't think I'm powerful enough to dispel it, unfortunately."
  • The disable options, in full: legendary Thievery, legendary Arcana, or a 7th-rank Dispel Magic against DC 39. Nobody at level 12 has a legendary skill. Guar's read: "So it's literally designed to only be passable by mythic characters."
  • Guar pre-committed, then paid. "I don't want to have to use a second Mythic Point, but if I have to, I absolutely will." He beat DC 39 — with a rank-6 Dispel, not the rank 7 the hazard nominally called for. His mythic proficiency carried it over.
  • Galan drew the conclusion that matters: "someone who was mythic placed the trap there in the first place, right?" The DM: "That's a pretty good guess."
  • House rule confirmed: clearing a mythic hazard refills everyone's mythic pool. The DM remembered a round into the next fight.
Act IV · Combat · The Office

Risen Runelord Gimmel, Vesinra, and three greedspawn soldiers

  • The room: four tables of scrolls, star charts and maps, an incense burner spewing spicy smoke, glowing stones in the ceiling. Behind the desk, a man in green with a polearm.
  • Liu and Galan both read him before initiative — identical Arcana successes: he is a runelord of greed. Kudu critically failed the same check by 21, hence "It's a guy in a green shirt."
  • Liu's Battle Cry critically succeeded on initiative (+10), frightening a greedspawn to 2 before anyone had acted.
  • One Recall Knowledge rewrote his whole turn. Vesinra identified as a mythic Lamia Matriarch; her Caress is a curse that leaves you feeble. Liu abandoned his plan and pulled a scroll of Invisibility from his retrieval belt instead.
  • Round one was as bad as it gets for Liu, and the invisibility is what makes it hurt. He'd spent an action and a scroll to go undetected and it bought him nothing — the greedspawn made every DC 11 flat check and put 24, 26 and 24 through an AC 34, and Vesinra spent Sure Strike to waive the check entirely for another 26. Four attacks, four flat checks passed, four hits. He ended the round on 65 of 165 — and the next round, the one flat check that would have gone his way, on Galan's 93-point Heal, was the one that failed.
  • Galan's biggest heal of the fight was eaten by his own ally's invisibility — a rank-6 Heal for 93 lost to a DC 11 flat check. Liu took it on himself: "I could have healed myself. I should have said that."
  • Kudu used Dragon's Rage Breath for the first time in twelve levels. "I've never done an AoE. How do I fucking select them all?" Guar talked him through the template; the cone hit four for a critical failure of 80 and a kill.
  • Gimmel answered with a Wall of Stone to the ceiling, cutting off Galan's Guardian, Guar's Floating Flame and every melee line at once. The party spent two rounds on one inch of masonry with hardness 14.
  • Liu killed Vesinra with a reaction — Retributive Strike cut Kudu's 66-point crit to 46 and countered for 98 applied. Then he named Gimmel off the tome's free Recall Knowledge and Mythic Struck for 106.
Act V · Social · The Evidence and the Slate

The mission accomplished, and a message from someone still out there

  • The room proved the mission. A comprehensive intelligence briefing on Xin-Eurythnia — "though the notes stubbornly refer to it by its ancient name, Xin-Shalast" — granting a +2 item bonus to Recall Knowledge about the city, worth ~650 gp, and the hard evidence Aurora asked for in Session 1.
  • A paired sending slate fell out of Gimmel when he died. "As feared, our city is crawling with heroes. One group is getting close to us. If you don't hear back soon, assume the worst." Signed the Rune of Greed.
  • Guar was the only one who tried to run it down — proposed Scrying, looked up his own spell and ruled himself out, then tried a second angle. Neither worked.
  • Liu chose the client over the market: "We can go talk to the client first. I think it's more important." Then, a beat later: "I mean, also, she might just give us more things for us to use in the market."
  • The priestess took the audience immediately: "This is exactly what I was afraid of… I will schedule a meeting with her first thing tomorrow morning." → an audience with Queen Sorshen tomorrow, and two days to the ceremony.
  • Two distributions — 111 platinum, then 120 — roughly 2,300 gp each. Xin-Eurythnia is an 18th-level city: any common item up to level 18, uncommon and rare negotiable.
  • Liu spent the last of the session working out the pre-buffing rules with the DM, restating the case concretely and taking the misunderstanding on himself. The ruling: an encounter begins when both sides are aware of each other, and "you're casting in a long, loud, strong voice."
