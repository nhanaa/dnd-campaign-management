## Session Summary

The cliffhanger resolved fast and ugly. Seven Trinity Initiates were still standing behind their overturned tables when the session picked up, and Galan opened the round with a **Divine Wrath** that caught all seven and produced four critical failures at once — sickened, slowed, and suddenly unable to spend three actions on anything. He then walked the DM through his own spell's degrees of success to make sure the riders actually stuck. That one burst decided the encounter; everything afterwards was collection — the slowed rider bit immediately, and the DM had to retract a Force Barrage mid-application because a slowed caster can't pay for the three-action version. Guar spent a Mythic Point to push a **4th-rank Fireball, recast as cold**, to **DC 37** and dropped it on the pile, and from there it was cleanup: forty minutes and four Foundry rounds turned seven entrenched casters into seven bodies, with **Liu killing three, Kudu two, and Guar two**. The initiates' entire answer was to chip Liu for 8, 11 and 7. He described it accurately at the time: *"I'm just getting chipped down."*

The last one didn't die on the floor. She flew fifteen feet up onto a balcony where nobody could reach her — and she was, by some distance, the luckiest thing in the room. She cast the Vision of Death that opened the session, she was the only initiate to save against Divine Wrath, she rolled a natural 20 against the Fireball, and she went the entire fight without ever carrying a single condition. **Galan reached for the right idea** — *"I want to try to dispel her flying effect"* — but the Dispel Magic he had prepared was too low-rank to counteract a 4th-rank Fly, which he didn't yet know, and she was already on the balcony by then anyway. He threw a Divine Lance instead and missed. So it fell to the two people who could physically get up there. Kudu **Sudden Charged straight up a lava flow** on Raging Athlete's climb speed and rolled a critical hit that came to nothing at all; she was still sitting on a full 70 HP seven minutes later. Liu — who had never climbed anything in this system — had the DM walk him through the rules line by line, worked out that climbing needs *both hands*, and then made the call the whole session turns on: he **dropped his tome and his falcata on the floor** and spent his entire turn — Climb, Climb, punch — hauling himself up the lava flow weaponless to buy one attack. The Mythic Strike **fist** critically hit for 60. She Stepped clear and reached for the door, and opening it drew Kudu's Reactive Strike for 124.

The hallway beyond was coated in a rank-7 illusion. Galan identified it on a Religion 38 and named exactly what it was — *"extremely powerful illusion magic"* — and then, to his credit, immediately said the useful thing rather than the flattering one: *"I don't think I'm powerful enough to dispel it."* His best prepared Dispel was rank 4, and he committed on the spot to fixing it: *"once we rest I will prepare a higher level one."* It was an **Endless Reality**, a mythic hazard whose only listed answers were legendary Thievery, legendary Arcana, or a 7th-rank Dispel against DC 39 — nothing a level-12 party has without spending mythic power. Guar priced the cost out loud and pre-committed to it — *"if I have to, I absolutely will"* — then paid, and cleared a rank-7 hazard with a **rank-6** Dispel carried over the DC by his mythic proficiency. Galan closed the scene with the deduction of the night: *"someone who was mythic placed the trap there in the first place, right?"* The DM confirmed it. Clearing it refilled the whole party's mythic pool.

Behind the last door was an office full of star charts and incense, and behind the desk was a man in green with a polearm. Liu and Galan both read him before initiative, and the answer came back off his gear and his polearm: **a runelord of greed**. Kudu critically failed the same check by 21, and reported what his character actually got: *"It's a guy in a green shirt."* He had a **Lamia Matriarch** and three greedspawn soldiers with him, and he opened with *"It's unfortunate you came snooping around. Now you gotta die."* The fight ran five rounds and was the hardest of the campaign so far. Liu took 24, 26, 24 and 26 in the first round alone and finished it at **65 of 165**. Kudu ate a 62-point critical Reactive Strike for swapping weapons mid-melee, then a 66-point falchion crit later. Gimmel put a **Disintegrate** into Galan for 75 — and Galan, whose first instinct was to check whether anyone else needed the Mythic Point more and to hold it, was talked into spending it by Kudu's *"mythic that motherfucker."* He critically succeeded the Fortitude save and took **nothing**.

It ended with the two reactions Liu has now used to close three of the campaign's five fights. Vesinra's Falchion Swipe crit Kudu for 66; Liu's **Retributive Strike** cut it to 46 and answered with a **90-damage crit that killed her outright**. Then, with Gimmel walled off behind his own Wall of Stone and the party grinding at one inch of masonry, Kudu simply walked around the end of it and crit him for 116, and Liu spent his last turn doing the thing he was built to do — and it was the **free** roll that paid. His Exploit Vulnerability succeeded by exactly zero and bought almost nothing; the tome's no-action Recall Knowledge, made at the top of the same turn against a *rare* DC of 33, came back with a name: **Risen Runelord Gimmel**, dream trait, mythic trait. He asked his follow-up question about lore and got the whole entry. Then he Mythic Struck for 106 and the runelord came apart.

What fell out of the corpse was the mission. A **comprehensive intelligence briefing on Xin-Eurythnia** — written throughout under the city's ancient name, **Xin-Shalast** — proving the cult intends to strike the portal ceremony, and a **paired sending slate** carrying a message from someone who is still out there: *"As feared, our city is crawling with heroes… If you don't hear back soon, assume the worst."* Signed with one word: **the Rune of Greed**.

## Key Events

### Act I — The Common Room, Concluded (Encounter 1)
- **The session resumed mid-round** with seven Trinity Initiates alive behind cover. Foundry's counter continued from Session 1 — the observed rounds were **4 (partial), 5, 6 and 7**.
- **Galan's Divine Wrath opened the session**: a 20-foot burst, 26 spirit damage, **all seven initiates caught**. Four **critical failures** (natural 1, 2, 2 and 4), two failures, one success. Crit-fail meant **sickened 2 and slowed 1** — which cost the cult its three-action turns for the rest of the fight. Galan then **ran the degrees of success for the DM himself** so the riders landed correctly. His own verdict on the spell: *"I should have prepared more of that."*
- **The slowed rider bit within seconds.** A Force Barrage rolled 12 into Liu and the DM took it straight back off: *"No, no, wait. Don't take that… They're slowed. I forgot. So they can't do a three action."*
- **The initiates' entire answer was Liu.** Three Force Barrages landed — **8, 11 and 7, for 26 total**. Nothing else on the party took a single point in this encounter. Liu: *"I'm just getting chipped down."* He floored at **104 / 165**.
- **Guar spent a Mythic Point on Mythic Casting** to raise a **4th-rank Fireball, recast as cold**, from DC 31 to **DC 37**, then dropped it on the cluster: **two critical failures at 54, five failures at 27**, and the first kill of the night.
- **Kudu's flail hit for 55** and killed a second.
- **Liu killed two in one turn.** The DM caught that his first target shouldn't have had table cover and retracted it; the Strike then hit by **exactly +1** — with the circumstance bonus off his tome's free Recall Knowledge sitting in that modifier list — and then cleared the DC 5 concealment flat check by a single point: *"Oh, oof. It just made it."* 29 damage into 10 remaining. His second Strike finished another initiate sitting on exactly 10.
- **Guar's second burst — a plain rank-3 Fireball, no Mythic Point spent** — finished a fourth off 3 HP. **That one was the initiate Session 1 flagged as the probable leader** (Reflex +18 against the others' +16); he died in the middle of the fight to a rank-3 spell, which closes the thread.
- **Liu's Overwhelming Combination** took a fifth for 41.
- **Liu caught a rider he was owed and wasn't getting.** *"I have Sympathetic Vulnerabilities on that person… Wait, it's not on her, I think… I think it didn't apply to the previous one, which is weird."* **Sympathetic Vulnerabilities** should have been spreading his personal antithesis to every other cultist in the room, and it hadn't been applied to any of them.
- **Fly went up three times** across the fight — the cult's answer to a melee party was simply to leave the floor. The last survivor took it **15 feet up onto a balcony** and out of everyone's reach. Liu, arriving underneath: *"Right in front of the balcony. That is a 15 foot tall balcony. Oh — 15 foot tall balcony. Oh, shit."*
- **The DM had already established this was a real problem** — a round earlier, Kudu needed **Lunge** just to reach something ten feet up.
- **Galan reached for the right idea**: *"I want to try to dispel her flying effect."* It wouldn't have worked — the Dispel Magic he had prepared was too low-rank to counteract a 4th-rank Fly, a gap in how counteract works that he'd diagnose and fix himself an hour later at the hazard — and she was already up on the balcony regardless. He threw a Divine Lance instead and missed.
- **Kudu went up the wall — and it produced nothing.** He used **Raging Athlete's climb speed** to Sudden Charge up a lava flow, 70 feet of movement, and landed a **critical hit that never dealt damage**: no damage roll, no application, and the target sat on a full **70 HP** until Liu reached her seven minutes later. *(Most likely a failed DC 5 concealment flat check — she was Concealed, and flat checks don't appear in the export. Flagged for confirmation.)* Guar: *"You do like a backflip over."* Kudu: *"I'm a Raging Athlete, dude."*
- **So Liu dropped everything and climbed.** He had the DM walk him through the Climb rules from scratch, learned it needs **both hands**, and talked himself into it out loud: *"But I don't want to just drop my sword here. I have no sword to use."* He released tome and falcata as free actions and spent **two Climb actions** — regular successes of 26 and 21, ten feet each on speed 45 — hauling himself level with the balcony lip.
- **His whole turn was Climb, Climb, punch.** *"Do you get to draw a weapon? Or do you get a natural weapon? I have Fist, yeah. You know what? To guarantee that, I'll use Mythic Strike."* The Spirit Warrior fist **critically hit for 60** — thrown from the lip, still prone — plus 8 more from personal antithesis, leaving her on **2 HP**.
- **Liu checked whether Implement's Interruption could fire and it couldn't** — that reaction only triggers off the target of his Exploit Vulnerability, and she wasn't it. Kudu took the reaction instead: she **Stepped** clear and reached for the door, and **opening it** drew **Kudu's Reactive Strike**, a critical hit for **124**. Seven initiates down, encounter over.

### Act II — Cleanup, and the Storage Rooms
- **Detect Magic on the bodies turned up nothing magical** beyond *+1 striking* **bec de corbins** on every initiate — worth four gold apiece under Automatic Rune Progression.
- **Galan critically succeeded both Treat Wounds** (Assurance, so no dice at all), and **Ward Medic let each one cover two people**: the first healed **Liu and Guar** 28 apiece, the second rolled 43 and topped up **Liu (33, capped) and Kudu**. Twenty minutes off the clock, whole party green.
- **Two storage rooms**, stripped to dust and fragments, gave up a **Greater Skeleton Key** and assorted Dawnsilver oddments — a pen nib, a button, a monocle frame.
- **Galan turned salvage into ammunition**: *"Is Dawnsilver — can I take some of this Dawnsilver to use it as, what's it called, Needle Darts?"* The DM smashed the monocle frame into a Dawnsilver chunk for him.
- **Liu asked about Dawnsilver full plate**; it needs a full ingot, and the party had one. He ultimately voted to sell it so everyone could shop: *"if the money will help everyone else then I'm fine with just selling it."*
- **Kudu raised the same thing twice, unprompted**: *"Next time we get back to town, remind me to buy some magical storage. The party storage is on somebody. I've got all kinds of bulk that I can carry."* He also personally logged every find into the party stash, and dug out the ingot that made Galan's Needle Darts request possible.

### Act III — The Endless Reality (mythic hazard)
- **Guar's Detect Magic found a rank-7 aura coating the entire hallway.** *"I'm going to let everyone know that there's a big-boy spell coating that entire hallway."*
- **Galan identified it** with a Religion check of 38 — a **complex magical illusion mythic trap** — and relayed it in character: *"I always tell everyone that this is extremely powerful illusion magic."* Then, immediately: *"I don't think I'm powerful enough to dispel it, unfortunately."* His highest prepared Dispel Magic was rank 4; countering a rank-7 effect with it would have required a critical success against an unknown DC.
- **The hazard's disable options were, in full**: legendary **Thievery**, legendary **Arcana**, or a **7th-rank Dispel Magic against DC 39**. Nobody at level 12 has a legendary skill. The DM explained the design out loud — you auto-fail the skill attempt and then get a mythic reroll. Guar's read: *"So it's literally designed to only be passable by mythic characters."*
- **Guar pre-committed, then paid.** *"I don't want to have to use a second Mythic Point, but if I have to, I absolutely will, and I will frickin' hit it with a mythic level Dispel Magic."* He did, and beat DC 39 — with a **rank-6** Dispel, not the rank 7 the hazard nominally called for. His mythic proficiency is what carried it over. *(The log shows the raw roll at 36; the DM added the +6 mythic bonus by hand for a final 42.)*
- **Galan drew the conclusion that matters**: *"I think we would know that that means that, like, someone who was mythic placed the trap there in the first place, right?"* The DM: *"That's a pretty good guess."*
- **House rule confirmed: clearing a mythic hazard refills everyone's mythic pool.** The DM remembered a round into the next fight.

### Act IV — The Office (Encounter 2)
- **The room**: four tables of scrolls, star charts and maps, an incense burner spewing spicy smoke, glowing stones in the ceiling. Behind the desk, a man in green with a polearm.
- **Liu and Galan both read him before initiative** — identical Arcana successes, and the answer came off his gear and his polearm: **he is a runelord of greed.** **Kudu critically failed the same check by 21**, which is why his contribution to the briefing was *"It's a guy in a green shirt."*
- **Gimmel's only line before the fight**: *"It's unfortunate you came snooping around. Now you gotta die."*
- **Liu's Battle Cry critically succeeded on initiative** (+10), frightening a greedspawn to **2** before anyone had acted.
- **Liu identified the second boss on turn one**: a **Lamia Matriarch** with the beast, humanoid and **mythic** traits, and — from his Know-It-All follow-up — her action list: **Chain Shade, Falchion Swipe, Vesinra's Caress**. Told the last one was a curse that leaves you feeble, he changed his whole turn: *"either way, I will actually do this instead, since this looks dangerous"* — pulled a **scroll of Invisibility** from his retrieval belt and cast it on himself.
- **His Exploit Vulnerability then failed by exactly 1** (29 against DC 30), leaving him with personal antithesis only.
- **Gimmel's opening play was Enlarge** on a greedspawn — Large, **+5 reach**, +2 melee damage — which is what let the thing reach Liu at all.
- **Round one was as bad as it gets for Liu, and the invisibility is what makes it hurt.** He'd spent an action and a scroll to go undetected. It bought him **nothing**: the greedspawn made every DC 11 flat check and put **24, 26 and 24** through an **AC 34**, and Vesinra simply spent **Sure Strike** to waive the check entirely for another **26**. Four attacks, four flat checks passed, four hits. He ended the round on **65 of 165** — and the next round the one flat check that would have gone in his favour, on **Galan's 93-point Heal**, was the one that failed.
- **Guar's Ice Storm** covered the room in difficult terrain and 2 cold per turn — but four of five saves succeeded, and he said so: *"Huh, I should've just fireballed them."*
- **Liu's Overwhelming Combination killed the first greedspawn** with a **122-damage** falcata crit into fist, the shock rune arcing 12 into Vesinra and a second greedspawn.
- **Galan's biggest heal of the fight was eaten by his own ally's invisibility.** He cast a rank-6 **Heal for 93** on Liu, hit the DC 11 miss chance for an invisible target, and **failed the flat check** — spell gone. Liu took it on himself immediately: *"I could have healed myself. I should have said that."* He did, the following round, for 64.
- **Kudu swapped to a reach spear mid-melee and paid 62 for it.** The interrupting Reactive Strike critically hit; **Guar burned Correct the Story** to force a reroll and it **crit again**; **Liu's Retributive Strike** brought it down to 42. Kudu's summary was pure honesty: *"I got a really cool spear, guys — that's why I did it. I can attack all these guys. But I had to swap to it, so now I can't do that."*
- **Vesinra's Synaptic Pulse — a rank-5 incapacitation effect on a 30-foot emanation — caught Galan, Kudu, Guar and Gaston, and every single one critically succeeded.** It did nothing at all.
- **Kudu used Dragon's Rage Breath for the first time in twelve levels.** *"I've never done an AoE. How do I fucking select them all?"* Guar talked him through the cone template — *"The cone is great. There is no shame in the cone."* — and the 30-foot cone hit four: a critical failure for **80**, a failure that killed a greedspawn outright, and 20 each on Gimmel and Vesinra. Guar narrated it for him: *"That's like just the image of Godzilla shooting the beam directly down the monster's throat."*
- **Gimmel's Disintegrate hit Galan for 75.** Galan's first move was to check whether it would catch anyone else and, on hearing it wouldn't, to **hold** his Mythic Point: *"I'm just asking. Okay, well, I'll save it."* Kudu changed his mind for him — *"Mythic that motherfucker"* — and Galan spent it, **critically succeeded the Fortitude save, and took zero.**
- **Guar's amped Ignition hit for 62** — his single biggest hit of the night, stacked out of Unleash Psyche's +12 and Oscillating Wave's +6.
- **Gimmel answered the party's momentum with a Wall of Stone to the ceiling**, splitting the room and cutting Galan's Spiritual Guardian, Guar's Floating Flame and Liu's line of sight off from him at once. Liu: *"Wall of Stone. One of the most OP spells in the game."* The party spent the next two rounds beating on one inch of masonry with hardness 14.
- **Liu killed Vesinra with a reaction.** Her Falchion Swipe hit both frontliners with no multiple attack penalty — 27 on Liu, a **66-point critical on Kudu**. Liu's **Retributive Strike** cut Kudu's damage to 46 and countered with a **critical hit, 90 rolled and 98 applied**, killing her where she stood. *(She appeared to shrug it off until the DM found the cause — Liu's own Champion's Resistance had been applied to her token by mistake. Cleared: "Yeah, that kills her.")*
- **Kudu ate a piece of her.** *"I'm going to kind of bend down and take a chunk out of that snake lady."* Absorb Strength, 11 temporary hit points, and a straight-faced explanation: *"That's how we honor our dead, my civilization."* Liu, watching: *"Liu's kind of like — okay, I guess I see everything."*
- **Kudu simply walked around the end of the wall and crit Gimmel for 116** at mythic proficiency, and the thundering rune landed for the first time all campaign — Gimmel **failed the Fortitude save and went deafened for a minute**. Liu, immediately: *"That may actually affect his spells."*
- **Then Liu named him.** His **Exploit Vulnerability** succeeded by **exactly zero** (28 against DC 28); Gimmel has no printed weakness, so it bought the 8-point antithesis rider. **Diverse Lore should have paid out here too** — Foundry printed the comparison DC as **28** on the same card, exactly matching the result — but the comparison was never called for, so it went unclaimed. The name came instead off the **tome's free Recall Knowledge at the top of the same turn: a 34.** The DM: *"He is rare. Makes it very hard… You still make it. Success."* **Risen Runelord Gimmel** — **dream trait, mythic trait**. Liu spent his Know-It-All follow-up on **lore** rather than a tactical edge, and got the full entry.
- **And killed him.** *"The only way I can miss is literally a nat 1."* Mythic Strike, critical hit, **98 rolled and 106 applied** into 49 remaining HP.
- **Vanishing Death**: Gimmel detonated on death, a DC 27 Will save against 24 hours of stupefied. **Liu and Kudu both critically succeeded.** Liu, over the corpse: *"You're trying to pull a fast one on us, but we're just strong, so just clean them up."*

### Act V — The Evidence, and the Slate
- **The room proved the mission.** A thick collection of documents, scrolls and annotated maps: *"a comprehensive intelligence briefing on the city of Xin-Eurythnia, though the notes stubbornly refer to it by its ancient name, Xin-Shalast."* It grants a **+2 item bonus to Recall Knowledge about the city and its history**, is worth roughly **650 gp**, and is **the hard evidence Aurora asked for in Session 1**.
- **A dark rectangular slate fell out of Gimmel when he died** — one of a matched pair, writing on one appearing on the other. Its message: *"As feared, our city is crawling with heroes. One group is getting close to us. If you don't hear back soon, assume the worst."* Signed **the Rune of Greed**. Guar, instantly: *"We've been trying to reach you, but your car's extended warranty."* Then he was the only person who tried to run it down — proposed **Scrying**, looked up his own spell and ruled himself out (*"Oh, yeah, one creature"*), and immediately tried a second angle: could the slate count as one of Gimmel's possessions? It couldn't; it was Gimmel's, not the other holder's.
- **Liu chose the client over the market**: *"We can go talk to the client first. I think it's more important."* Then, a beat later, the pragmatic rider: *"I mean, also, she might just give us more things for us to use in the market."*
- **The priestess took the audience immediately** at Midnight's Gallery: *"This is exactly what I was afraid of, and this is what we need to talk to the queen about. I will schedule a meeting with her first thing tomorrow morning."* → **an audience with Queen Sorshen tomorrow, and two days to the ceremony.**
- **The sell-off**: the Dawnsilver greatpick from Session 1, eight flaming glaives, Vesinra's falchion, six bec de corbins, the Warshard Spear, leather armour. Nobody wanted the flaming runes; Guar asked if he could put one on his dragon. **Two distributions — 111 platinum, then 120 — roughly 2,300 gp each.**
- **Xin-Eurythnia is an 18th-level city**: any common item up to level 18, uncommon and rare negotiable. Kudu bought **greater obsidian goggles** (1,250 gp) and two **spacious pouches**, donating one to the party stash and waving off Galan's offer to split it: *"300 gold. I got it."* He also tried to hand round his old mask first — *"I've never used it. Haven't even breathed on it. Might have a little blood from this last encounter."*
- **Liu spent the last of the session working out the pre-buffing rules with the DM.** The first answer was situational and didn't land, so he restated the case concretely — *"we're in front of the door and we're going to immediately open the door, can I just cast Heroism?"* — took the misunderstanding on himself (*"Okay, so I probably misunderstood that"*), and got the clean ruling: an encounter begins when both sides are aware of each other, and *"you're casting in a long, loud, strong voice — so just as you can hear them on the other side of the door, they can hear you."* Exploration rounds are ten minutes, so most buffs burn out before a door opens.
