Session Summary

Liu Heifeng caught up with the queen's other honored guests in the worst possible way — mid-brawl, on the threshold of a ruined Dawnsilver workshop, where a blue-skinned snow oni was refusing them entry by beating them senseless. He stepped into a fight already two rounds gone and, within his first turn, did the thing that would define his evening: named the creature, read out its whole defensive profile, and handed the party its weak point.

The fight ended in a surrender rather than a kill, and it was Liu who closed it. Guar pushed Gaston into the oni's reach to re-establish flanking and ate the reaction for it; Liu answered unprompted with a Retributive Strike that cut the oni from 81 HP to 10 in a single counter-blow. The oni threw up his hands: "This isn't worth any amount of gold." He was a hired doorman with a family, and once the pressure was off he talked. A cult called the Risen of the Triad had taken the workshop's back rooms — a dozen or more, all spellcasters — led by a haughty man whose name he could only half-remember. Liu spent a Recall Knowledge chasing that half-name and came back with something worse than useless: there was a Runelord of Envy called Gimel, seven thousand years dead. Kudu, playing an absent friend's character, proposed patching the beaten man up; Galan did the actual healing; Liu waved him off down the street.

What followed was a long, ugly push inward. Four greedspawn — glaive-wielding aberrations whose bite clawed at the mind — spent five rounds trying specifically to kill Liu, landing three natural 20s on him. He survived on Galan's healing, a Delay Consequence that postponed an entire bite, and a Mythic Point that wasn't his: Guar burned Correct the Story to erase the crit that would have dropped him. All four died — one to Guar's Blazing Bolt, one to a single 98-damage crit from Kudu, two to Liu, the last to a 101-damage combination.

Beyond that room, an undetected haunt boiled the eastern wall into lava and hurled phantasmal molten Dawnsilver at everyone standing in it. It could not be fought — the party did zero damage to it all fight — only talked down, and only by Religion or Intimidation. Galan, who had been safely outside, was told plainly that there was no running: "You gotta go face those that need to be exorcised." He walked in, ate 31 damage for it, and exorcised the thing in three successes with Liu feeding him aid.

Then the noise brought the cult. The party opened the last door onto eight robed initiates already dug in behind overturned tables, and Guar — down to his last sixth-rank slot and out of Mythic Points entirely — dropped a Fireball into the middle of them: eight targets caught, seven damaged, 340 total. The session ended there, mid-round, with one initiate dead, seven alive, and the party untouched.

Act 0 · Table · The Group Assembles

Introductions and a Debut

  • The DM — Tom Brister, a professional GM running eight Pathfinder campaigns a week — has run Revenge of the Runelords three times through. He runs rules-as-written with minimal house rules and high player agency.
  • Pax joins as Liu Heifeng, his first thaumaturge and the highest-level character he has ever played (previous ceiling: level 10).
  • Guar debuts. Levi had built a tanking Commander, looked at a party already three deep in melee, and swapped to an Oscillating Wave Psychic with the Drake Rider archetype — riding Gaston, a Riding Dragonet with an INT of 0 who is, per his owner, "a smart boy."
  • Aaron is out sick. The group votes to run Tinzen as a shared NPC rather than write him out; Kudu's player takes the controls for the first fight.
  • The DM recaps what Liu missed: missives from Queen Sorshen, VIP treatment in Xin-Eurythnia, and a magical sleep in which Lirilaru — "a runelord that never was," dwelling in the Dreamlands — awakened the party's mythic power, before the Ashen Man hijacked the dream and showed them Golarion destroyed.
  • The priesthood, led by Aurora, had the same nightmares and traced them to a cult, the Risen of the Triad. The party's task: get hard evidence they threaten the portal ceremony — three days out.
Act I · Combat · The Doorman

An Elite Snow Oni, Two Rounds In

  • Liu arrives to find the party already brawling with a blue-skinned snow oni who has told them to piss off. It is level 14 against the party's 12 — a PL+2 solo — invisible, and standing in a self-cast freezing cloud that conceals everything and hurts anyone who lingers.
  • Liu's Recall Knowledge names it and pulls the full save profile — Will is lowest — and Know-It-All buys a second answer revealing two reactions: Reactive Strike and Icy Deflection. He confirms its 10-foot reach before committing.
  • Exploit Vulnerability → spirit weakness 15. The astral rune on Liu's falcata was already the right answer.
  • Galan's rank-2 Dispel can't touch a rank-7 effect. The DM rewinds the action after explaining counteract ranks, and Galan casts Bless instead — a party-wide +1 to attacks for the rest of the fight.
  • Guar spends a Mythic Point on Correct the Story, rewriting a crit on Kudu into a 37-point hit. Minutes later Liu spends his own on a crit against himself — and the reroll crits again. Liu finishes the night 0-for-1.
  • Liu's fist crit is erased by the DC 5 concealment flat check, prompting the ruling of the night: "You can't Mythic a flat check."
  • Guar's mythic Dispel Magic strips the oni's 7th-rank invisibility. He then lines up a sixth-rank Fireball, measures the template, finds Galan inside it, and cancels.
  • The oni steps into his own cloud and flurries Kudu for 78, then 31 more. Kudu's Mythic Strike crits for 87 and the oni crit-fails Reflex — knocked prone.
  • The finish: Guar moves Gaston into reach to restore off-guard and accepts the reaction. It hits the dragonet for 35 — Liu calls Retributive Strike on his own, Champion's Resistance cuts it to 11, and his counter-Strike lands for 71. Haruto drops from 81 to 10.
Act II · Social · The Interrogation

"This isn't worth any amount of gold."

  • Top of round 5, the oni throws up his hands and surrenders. Liu is suspicious first — "But at first, he talked so much. For someone giving up so quickly."
  • He douses the freezing cloud to save his own life and explains himself: he doesn't even like the cultists, he needed the work, and he has 70 kids to feed back home.
  • Galan asks the question that opens him up — "Do you have any idea how many might be in there?" — and gets the number: "a dozen, maybe more."
  • What he gives up: the cultists are next door, they never let him inside, they're all spellcasters, and they believe they are bringing back runelords. Their leader is burly, mustached, goateed, haughty — and paid him personally. The name, half-remembered: "Limmel, Gimmel, Gabby, something."
  • Liu chases it — and, on his first night at a strange table, argues a −2 penalty onto his own roll that the DM had skipped. He makes it anyway. The answer: a Runelord of Envy named Gimel, dead seven thousand years — "but it couldn't possibly be him." He relays it immediately: "Maybe a fan? A wannabe?"
  • Kudu, playing Tinzen, proposes healing the man who just surrendered — "because I think he would." Galan performs it, his Treat Wounds crit bringing the oni from 10 back to 35.
  • Liu seals it: "There was no one drop today, so we can end it peacefully." The oni declines to help against the cult, and leaves. Liu waves him off.
  • Galan then spends roughly 40 in-game minutes running the party back to full — rolling every Treat Wounds, with Ward Medic letting him treat two at a time and Continual Recovery removing the hour lockout.
Act III · Exploration · Choosing a Door

Four Doors, One Sound

  • None of the doors are locked or trapped. Voices behind the two eastern doors. Something moving behind the southern one. Silence to the north.
  • Guar's Thoughtsense counts four hostile creatures beyond the door before anyone opens it, and he passes the count on.
  • Exploration activities set: Kudu scouting, Liu defending (he checks with the DM that he can enter combat with his shield already raised — he can), Guar on Detect Magic when nobody else has it, Galan searching once his fatigue is cleared.
  • Nothing in the room registers as magical. The party chooses the sound.
Act IV · Combat · Greedspawn

Five Rounds, Four Aberrations, and Three Natural 20s

  • Four glaive-wielding aberrations in a room half-buried under an ancient wall of hardened lava. They hiss and attack without a word. Not a uniform group — two at 200 HP, two at 180, from two distinct stat variants.
  • Liu opens on initiative with Battle Cry — a free-action Demoralize that critically succeeds by +11: frightened 2 before anyone takes a turn.
  • Exploit Vulnerability critically succeeds by +10, revealing the whole profile: no weaknesses, resistance 10 mental. Liu takes personal antithesis instead, and Sympathetic Vulnerabilities spreads it to all four — a flat +8 on every hit he lands for the rest of the fight.
  • Kudu opens with Slam Down → Crashing Slam, using his flail's d12 instead of a d6 for the fall damage — a feat he picked up specifically for that interaction right before the session.
  • One greedspawn taunts the party in Aklo — "You wish you were this pretty" — which Liu can read and Galan reads aloud. Half a minute later Galan answers it, casting Stasis with "you have a face only a mother could love." The target leaves time entirely: untargetable, unable to act, cut off from Liu's shock-rune chain, and it loses its round-2 turn.
  • The pack rolls four natural 20s in a row. Three land on Liu (63, 40, 54). The fourth hits Guar for 45 — an enemy Reactive Strike provoked by Gaston — and Liu's Retributive Strike cuts it to 29.
  • Liu survives on Galan's 80-point heal, a Delay Consequence that postpones an entire bite — damage and its mind-clawing rider — by a full round, his own Retributive Strikes (a second protecting Galan), and finally Guar's Correct the Story, which turns the 54-damage crit into a miss.
  • Sinful Bite leaves Liu sickened; he spends an action retching it off, and Guar casts Prestidigitation to pat him on the back while he does.
  • Kill ledger: Liu → Guar (sixth-rank Blazing Bolt, 46 fire) → Kudu (a single natural-20 crit for 98) → Liu, closing with Intensify Vulnerability into Overwhelming Combination for 101.
Act V · Hazard · The Haunted Forge

A Fight You Cannot Win by Hitting It

  • Nobody detects it. The eastern wall transforms into molten lava and dozens of shrieking human spirits made of fire fly out to scorch the room.
  • The opening blast is not universal. Four targets roll DC 34 Reflex: Kudu and Liu succeed (7 and 2 damage). Guar and Gaston fail — 32 each, plus persistent fire and frightened 1. Galan is outside the room and takes nothing.
  • Guar realizes a beat too late that his Sixth Sense grants a secret check to detect haunts — and flags it himself rather than letting it slide. The DM adds it as a permanent reminder on his sheet, along with Hydra's Bond.
  • The haunt attacks with Molten Dawnsilver at +29, striking four randomly determined targets per round from out of reach, applying slowed 1.
  • An unnoticed +4 AC carries the whole party — the old lava flow grants greater cover, auto-applied by the DM's cover module. It turns roughly six hits in ten into four. Nobody at the table remarks on it.
  • It cannot be fought. The party deals zero damage to it all fight. Kudu misses five times against AC 36, three critically, and his weapon simply bounces off the masonry.
  • Disabling it takes three successes at Master Religion (exorcise) or Intimidation (quell). Guar has neither — so rather than force a bad roll he casts Thermal Stasis twice for party-wide fire resistance 12, and steps a single square to take himself and Gaston out of the random target pool permanently, dropping the haunt from four attacks a round to three.
  • Galan is safe, and the DM makes him walk into the fire: "No running. You gotta go face those that need to be exorcised." He enters and takes 31 for it.
  • The three successes: Galan's Religion (a 36 lifted to 40 by Liu's Aid — "I wouldn't have made it otherwise"), then Liu's Intimidation, then Galan again — a 37 that was one short until a Mythic Point reroll turned it into 47.
  • Liu fails two Intimidation attempts (by 3 and by 1), spending a Mythic Point rerolling each, and critically succeeds all three of his Aid checks. The night's only natural 1 lands on one of those Aids. His Retributive Strike in round 3 cuts Kudu's incoming 29 to 15.
  • Throughout, the party can see cultists in the next room flipping tables into cover and casting protective spells on themselves. The clock has started.

  • Guar rides Gaston to the doorway and lets go his last sixth-rank slot — a straight Fireball, no Mythic Point left to spend on it. Eight caught in the burst, seven damaged: 340 total.
  • Two critical failures take 80 apiece (on a natural 1 and a natural 2), four failures take 40, one success takes 20 — and one initiate rolls a natural 20 and walks out of it untouched.
  • Kudu advances to the doorway and Lunges for 66, taking one initiate from 90 down to 24 — careful not to commit his token deeper under the DM's vision rules.
  • Liu moves up, Exploits Vulnerability (no weaknesses — plain humanoids, so personal antithesis again for +8), and finishes Kudu's target with Overwhelming Combination: 84 rolled, 92 applied.
  • The cult's answer is Vision of Death (8d6 mental), cast three times, and it does nothing. Kudu critically succeeds twice on natural 19s; Liu once — a natural 6 for 28 against DC 26, upgraded by Disciplined Mind.
  • One initiate screams "You killed Janet!" before throwing the spell that bounces.
  • The party takes zero damage. Galan never gets a turn. One initiate is dead; seven are still standing, none below roughly half health — and one of them is measurably sharper than the rest.

Character Spotlight

Liu Heifeng · the Recording Blade

  • He arrived and immediately became the party's eyes. In his first turns he had the oni's creature type, its full save profile, both of its reactions, its reach, and spirit weakness 15. He did the same to the greedspawn and to the haunt. Guar's read on him — "we're fighting for our lives and he's off to the side re-writing a book" — landed, and Liu owned it: "Gotta record everything."
  • He also ended the first fight. When the oni's reaction struck Gaston, Liu's Retributive Strike cut the hit from 35 to 11 and answered for 71, dropping Haruto from 81 HP to 10 and straight into surrender. He accounted for 183 of the 300 damage the party dealt after he arrived — 61% of it — in a fight he joined two rounds late.
  • His defining moment was mercy, not damage. When the oni gave up, Liu's first instinct was suspicion, his second was to interrogate properly, and his third was to let the man walk: "There was no one drop today, so we can end it peacefully." A justice-cause Champion playing his edicts straight — and it bought more than the fight did.
  • He argued a penalty onto his own roll. Chasing the leader's half-remembered name, he stopped the DM to point out he should be taking a −2 for the repeat Recall Knowledge, on his first night at a table full of strangers. Then he made it anyway, and surfaced Gimel.
  • He was the crit sponge and stayed useful. Three natural 20s landed on him in the greedspawn fight while he continued to Recall Knowledge, Retributive Strike on his allies' behalf — once for Guar, once for Galan — and set up his own kills.
  • He closed two of the three finished fights: 101 damage on the last greedspawn, and 92 on the first cultist to fall.

Kudu · the One Who Stood in Front

  • He absorbed the boss. The oni picked him and stayed on him — a 78-damage flurry in a single turn, then 31 more — and Kudu kept swinging through it. He was the body between a PL+2 solo and the rest of the party.
  • His build is deliberate, not incidental. He picked up improved Slam Down immediately before the session specifically so Crashing Slam would use his flail's d12 instead of a d6 — and opened the greedspawn fight with exactly that combination.
  • When he connects, the fight changes. His Mythic Strike crit for 87 and knocked the snow oni prone; his single natural-20 crit for 98 killed a greedspawn outright. His own summary is the honest one: "When I do hit, man, it hurts."
  • He played Tinzen with real generosity. Handed an absent friend's sheet he'd never read, he ran the grapples and Needle Darts competently — then made the call that gave the encounter its ending: "before he goes, I'm gonna treat his wounds, because I think he would." Playing someone else's character in character, on their behalf, is a quietly classy thing to do.
  • His comic timing is the table's metronome — calling his shot on the Mythic Strike, diagnosing his own luck as "feast or famine around this motherfucker," and refusing to accept that a building could dodge him.
  • He ran Scout in exploration and was the one who spotted the cultists rousing in the far room and relayed it.

Guar · the Right Tool at the Right Time

  • He rebuilt his character to fit a party he'd never played with. He arrived with a tanking Commander, looked at three bodies already in melee, and swapped to an Oscillating Wave Psychic on the spot — then took Drake Rider on top of it.
  • He spent his own Mythic Points on other people's problems. Correct the Story to rewrite the crit on Kudu in the first fight, and again in the greedspawn room to erase the 54-damage critical hit that would have dropped Liu. Neither was his turn, neither was his damage.
  • The Fireball he didn't cast came first. In the first fight he lined one up, measured the template, found Galan inside it, and cancelled. He spent the rest of the session hunting a clean shot — and got one on eight cultists behind a table, with his last sixth-rank slot and no Mythic Points left: 340 damage. His own verdict: "Didn't feel too powerful until I freaking threw that Fireball."
  • He found a lane when his kit had nothing. "Religion and intimidation are two of the only skills that I do not have" — so rather than force a bad roll at the haunt he handed the party fire resistance 12 twice, and worked out that a single square of movement would take him and Gaston out of its random target pool for good.
  • He filled the utility gaps: a mythic Dispel Magic that stripped the oni's 7th-rank invisibility, Thoughtsense counting four hostiles beyond a closed door, and Detect Magic as his exploration activity when nobody else had it. He also volunteered his own missed Sixth Sense haunt check to the DM rather than letting it pass.
  • He is generous with what he knows — mentoring Pax through first-session overwhelm ("they're all just tools in a toolbox. You just need to remember to use the right thing at the right time") and catching Galan's mythic action economy mid-turn.

Galan Magani · the Power of Milani

  • He is the reason Liu is alive. Three natural 20s landed on the thaumaturge and Galan answered every one: an 80-point two-action heal, a Delay Consequence that deferred an entire bite — damage and rider — by a round, and a second Retributive-Strike setup by staying in reach.
  • He walked into the fire. He was outside the haunt's room and took nothing from its opening blast. The DM told him there was no exorcising from safety — "You gotta go face those that need to be exorcised" — and he went in, took 31, and rolled the checks. Two of the three successes that killed the haunt were his, the last rescued from a 37 by a Mythic Point reroll.
  • His comedy is a comeback, not a non sequitur. A greedspawn taunted the party in Aklo — "you wish you were this pretty" — Galan read it out, and half a minute later answered it while freezing the thing out of time: "you have a face only a mother could love."
  • He asked the question that cracked the interrogation. "Do you have any idea how many might be in there?" is what produced "a dozen, maybe more" — the party's only estimate of what they're walking into.
  • He thinks past the current fight. After the haunt he pointed out what everyone had missed: "I feel like we should have just came into this room to the left and made them come in here with the haunt. Because they probably would have walked through here." Using a hazard as a weapon against the next encounter is a level above what the fight asked for.
  • He builds for his role deliberately. He took Elf Step for exactly one reason — "as a healer, I need to be able to get away from people" — and runs Ward Medic and Continual Recovery so he can treat two patients at a time without the hour lockout. He also caught and fixed a sheet error between sessions where every ability increase had silently failed to apply.

Pax's Notes — Liu Heifeng's Journal

Private notes, observations, and things to think about. Pax's notes only — not shared with the rest of the party.

What Liu did this session

  • Joined the party mid-fight at the Dawnsilver Workshop on Aurora's writ. The brawl was two rounds old before he arrived; no introductions were made.
  • Identified the doorman: elite snow oni, level 14 — two levels above the party. Lowest save Will. Two reactions: Reactive Strike and Icy Deflection. 10-foot reach. Spirit weakness 15 — the astral rune on the falcata was already the right answer.
  • Spent Correct the Story on a critical hit taken from the oni. The reroll crit again. 0-for-1 on the night; the Mythic Point bought nothing.
  • Ended the first fight with a reaction. Guar pushed Gaston into reach for the flank and took the hit; Retributive Strike cut it to 11 and answered for 71, taking the oni from 81 HP to 10. Dealt 183 of the 300 damage the party landed after arriving.
  • Argued for accepting the surrender and interrogated instead of killing. Got: cult strength (a dozen, maybe more), composition (all spellcasters), intent (bringing back runelords), and a partial name.
  • Corrected the DM onto a −2 penalty he had skipped, then made the Recall Knowledge anyway. Result: Gimel, Runelord of Envy, dead 7,000 years. Relayed immediately.
  • Opened the greedspawn fight with a critically successful Battle Cry Demoralize — frightened 2 before initiative resolved.
  • Exploit Vulnerability crit by +10: no weakness, resistance 10 mental → personal antithesis, spread to all four by Sympathetic Vulnerabilities. Worth a flat +8 on every hit for the rest of the fight.
  • Took three natural 20 critical hits (63, 40, 54) and floored at 68 of 165. Spent two Retributive Strikes on other people — one saving Guar 16 damage, one for Galan.
  • Killed two greedspawn, closing with Intensify Vulnerability → Overwhelming Combination — 101 damage rolled.
  • In the forge: failed two Intimidations, landed the third, and critically succeeded all three Aids on Galan's Religion. Found the haunt's cold weakness; nobody got to use it.
  • Killed the first cultist to fall — 92 damage onto a target Kudu had already taken to 24.

Things Liu knows that the party does not

  • Nothing material is being withheld. Every Recall Knowledge result was read out loud, including Gimel. That is deliberate: the tome exists to be read from.
  • What Liu holds privately is the reading, not the fact. The doorman described a leader who is burly, mustached, goateed, haughty, and personally handling payments. That is a man who wants to be seen paying. A genuine seven-thousand-year-old runelord does not hire ogres at the door with his own hands.
  • Gimel does not fit the map. The Runelord of Envy is supposed to be Belimarius, alive and ruling western New Thassilon. Either the name is a coincidence, an alias, or somebody's deliberate misdirection — and Liu is the only one at the table likely to notice the discrepancy.
  • Shizuru's anathema is a live constraint the others may not be tracking. Do not abandon a companion in need. Do not harm the innocent. The oni was a hired doorman with dependents, not a cultist. Liu did not explain the reasoning. He just made the call.

What Liu is thinking about

  • The tome is working, and it's free. The adept benefit's Recall Knowledge happens automatically at the start of each turn — no action, no cost. Take it every round regardless of the escalating DC: the real prize is the +1 circumstance bonus to the next attack on a success, and a failure costs nothing. The genuine action competition is between Exploit Vulnerability, Intensify Vulnerability and Overwhelming Combination, which all want the same three actions.
  • Dubious Knowledge is a trap on a failed RK. One truth and one lie, with no way to tell them apart, is worse than no information. Correctly declined this session.
  • Being the crit magnet is survivable and not entirely bad. The greedspawn ignored everyone else. AC 34, Parry, Champion's Resistance and a dedicated healer mean Liu can hold attention that would kill Guar outright. But it consumed Galan's biggest heal, a reaction spell, and one of Guar's Mythic Points.
  • Correct the Story is 0-for-1 and cost a Mythic Point. Guar's two uses both landed. Save it for hits that actually threaten a drop, and remember it can be spent on someone else's behalf — Guar demonstrated that twice.
  • The reaction game is the real engine. Retributive Strike ended the oni fight, saved Guar 16 damage, saved Galan, and cut Kudu's haunt damage nearly in half. It fired in every encounter it could. Keeping a dense melee line adjacent is worth more than another Strike.
  • Guar is the aim-point. The Fireball proved it. Telling him fire-or-cold before he commits a sixth-rank slot is worth more than anything the falcata does that turn. Note he is now out of sixth-rank slots and out of Mythic Points going into next week's fight.
  • The party has no wall and no reliable control. Galan has a wall spell and never cast it. Against seven entrenched casters at greater cover, that is the gap — and Galan already identified the better line: bait them through the haunt instead of walking into their room.
  • Scrolls of False Vitality — flagged for purchase. Also worth pricing: something that answers massed casters at range.
