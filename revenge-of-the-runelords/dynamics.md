## Character notes

**Tinzen** — heavy-armor frontline *caster*, not a pure melee body. Titan Wrestler, Binds That Tie, Flying Tackle, Punishing Shove, Reactive Shield, Shield Block, Guarded Advance. Also has **Fly on Shadowed Wings**. Debuff kit: **Bon Mot, Intimidating Glare, Battle Cry, Terrified Retreat**.

**Kudu** — **+2 Greater Striking Astral Thundering Spirit Thresher** (1d12 advanced flail, sweep, versatile S). Slam Down, Vicious Swing, Lunge, Crashing Slam, **Reactive Strike**, Dragon's Rage Breath/Wings, Terrifying Howl, Unbelievable Interception. Roughly **38 damage per hit** — a peer striker to Liu, not behind him.

**Galan** — a healing engine: Battle Medicine, Continual Recovery, Ward Medic, Treat Condition, Assurance (Medicine), Restorative Channel, Healing Hands, Magic Hands, Fast Recovery. Plus **Wall of Roses** for control. Also runs Fiery Rebirth (same as Liu).

**Guar** — Oscillating Wave granted spells alternate elements at nearly every rank: fire (Breathe Fire, Blazing Bolt, Fireball, Volcanic Eruption) and cold (Ice Storm, Howling Blizzard, Frozen Fog, Arctic Rift). Covers the party's AoE and ranged needs. **He chooses which element starts** each encounter, then must alternate (Conservation of Energy); refocusing resets it.

Also: **Psi Burst**, **Thermal Stasis** (party fire/cold resistance), **Frostbite**, **Lose the Path** (reaction — difficult terrain on a failed Will save), **Dispel Magic** and **Detect Magic** as mythic/known spells, **Thoughtsense** (30 ft, detects thinking creatures through walls), **Sixth Sense** (secret check to find haunts while exploring), **Hydra's Bond** (chance to notice an ally under domination).

**Gaston** — the Riding Dragonet gets his own action each round even uncommanded, which Guar uses as free movement: *"I'm essentially quickened."* Commanding him grants a second action. INT 0 (not −4, via Drake Rider), so he can take exploration activities. Riding checks are Nature, but Guar doesn't roll them for his own companion.

## Synergies

### 1. The stand-up punish (best combo in the party)
Kudu **Slam Downs** → enemy prone → it **Stands** (a move action) → provokes **both** Kudu's **Reactive Strike** and Liu's **Implement's Interruption**. A prone enemy standing up eats two premium reaction Strikes. Happens every time he trips something.

### 2. Share Weakness → Kudu
Liu's mortal weakness rides every one of Kudu's 3d12 Spirit Thresher hits — roughly **+12 damage per turn** to the party's other big striker. Highest-leverage thing Liu does for someone else's sheet.

### 3. Exploit Vulnerability aims the psychic
Oscillating Wave chooses between fire and cold constantly. Liu's round-1 EV reveals the enemy's highest weakness (crit = the full resistance/immunity list). **"Cold 10, not fire"** is worth an entire spell slot to that player — and stops a wasted Fireball on a fire-immune target.

### 4. Fear lowers the grapplers' DCs
Liu's Battle Cry frightened 1 drops the target's Fort/Reflex DC — exactly what Kudu's Slam Down trip and Tinzen's grapples roll against.

### 5. Dense melee line = reliable Retributive Strike
With Tinzen and Kudu both in melee beside Liu, Justice's Retributive Strike **always** has a valid trigger and a reachable target (Nimble Reprisal's Step covers repositioning). Ally gets resistance 14, Liu counter-Strikes.

### 6. Natural flanking
Three melee bodies means Liu and Kudu flank without spending anything — on top of Liu's self-contained CHCE off-guard chain.

## ⚠️ Overlaps to manage

**Battle Cry duplicated (Liu + Tinzen).** Frightened does **not** stack — you take the highest value. If both auto-Demoralize the *same* enemy at initiative, one is wasted.
→ **Fix: agree on a target split before session 1.** Tinzen takes the boss, Liu takes a second threat (or skips and Strikes).

**Intimidating Glare duplicated** (Liu + Tinzen). Harmless.

**Liu is not the party's debuffer.** Tinzen owns that lane more completely — he has Bon Mot *and* Terrified Retreat, which Liu doesn't. Liu's unique lanes are **intel, weakness amplification, arcane scrolls, and reaction punishment**.

**Correct the Story duplicated ×3** (Liu + Tinzen + **Guar**). Confirmed in play S1 — Guar used it twice, Liu once. Three crit-negations is deep insurance, but it's the most-duplicated resource in the party. **Unrivaled Retort** is the alternative if Liu wants uniqueness.
→ **Worth noting:** it can be spent on *anyone's* incoming crit. Guar used both of his on other people (Kudu, then Liu). Call the target out loud before spending, so two people don't burn Mythic Points on the same hit.

**Grapple lane is crowded** — Kudu and Tinzen both have Titan Wrestler + Binds That Tie. This is why Liu dropped Sword of Sealing.

**Fire overlap** — Liu's runes should avoid fire; the psychic already owns that element, and fire is the most-resisted damage type (11.7% at level 10+). Astral + shock keeps Liu's damage in rare types.

**Net:** the party is a fortress with strong control and healing. Liu's contribution is **not** more tanking or more debuffs — it's the intel that aims everyone else, the weakness multiplier on Kudu, arcane utility nobody else has, and reaction punishment on a dense melee line.

## Observed in play

### Session 1 (2026-07-21)

**Liu is a crit magnet.** The greedspawn ignored the rest of the party for five rounds and put three natural 20s into him (63, 40, 54). AC 34 + Parry + Champion's Resistance + Galan means he can hold that attention — but it consumed Galan's biggest heal, a reaction spell, and one of *Guar's* Mythic Points. Budget for it rather than being surprised by it.

**Reaction punishment is the real engine, and it over-delivered.** Retributive Strike ended the snow oni fight (71 damage on the counter, 81 → 10 HP), saved Guar 16, saved Galan, and halved Kudu's haunt damage. It fired in **every encounter it could**. Keeping a dense melee line adjacent is worth more than another Strike.

**⚠️ The party has no battlefield control.** Galan has a wall spell and never cast it; nobody has reliable AoE control beyond Guar's blasts. Against entrenched casters behind greater cover this is the visible gap. Galan spotted the better line himself: **bait enemies through a hazard** instead of walking into their prepared room.

**Skill-check hazards are a real failure mode.** The Haunted Forge could only be disabled by **Master Religion or Intimidation** — Guar had neither, Kudu never rolled Intimidation, and the party dealt **zero damage** to it across three rounds. Two PCs carried the whole encounter. Worth knowing who can and can't contribute before the next complex hazard.

**Exploit Vulnerability aims Guar, as designed.** Synergy #3 works — but Guar burned both his sixth-rank slots and all three Mythic Points in one session. Getting him the element *before* he commits a top slot is the highest-leverage thing Liu does for someone else's sheet.

### Session 2 (2026-07-28)

*Tinzen absent again. The party has now played two full sessions as a four-body group and has not yet seen its own shield wall.*

**Liu is still the only target, and it still holds.** Every point of damage the party took in the initiate fight went into Liu (8 + 11 + 7). In the office he took 24/26/24/26 in round one and floored at **65 of 165**. AC 34, Parry, Bless and Galan's healing carried it.

**The Invisibility scroll was the correct play and the dice ate it.** Liu was **Hidden** (greedspawn have imprecise scent), so every attack against him needed a **DC 11 flat check — a flat 50% miss chance.** Four attacks connected that were subject to it, for **99 damage**; the expected prevention was **~50 points**, and it prevented **zero**. That is a **6.25%** outcome. Add **Galan's 93-point Heal** failing the same DC 11 and it's **five coin flips in a row going the wrong way — 3.1%** — a swing of roughly **142 HP** of expected value. The scroll also forced **Vesinra to spend Sure Strike** just to bypass it. *The plan was right: halve the incoming damage and stand in front of three glaives. It failed on variance, not on judgement — and it cost Liu the tanking position the plan was built around.*

**The lost Heal is not a Liu problem, and it isn't a metagaming question.** Galan was in the room and watched Liu pull a scroll and vanish — that is **in-fiction knowledge**, freely available to his character, and squarely inside the table's own S1 agreement that *"characters in the same room can obviously talk."* Pax deliberately avoided calling mechanical state out loud to stay clear of quarterbacking, which is stricter than the agreement requires. **The flat check is the DM's to prompt when someone targets an invisible ally, or Galan's to ask about before committing a sixth-rank slot.** Nothing to change on Liu's side.

**Retributive Strike is the best action Liu doesn't spend a turn on.** In Encounter 2 alone it **mitigated 59 damage off Kudu** across three triggers (28 → 9, 62 → 42, 66 → 46) *and* the third one's counter-Strike **critically hit for 98 and killed Vesinra**. It has now closed three of the campaign's five fights. **Standing adjacent to Kudu is worth more than an extra Strike.** Note the resistance applies **per damage type**, which is why it wipes small elemental riders (the fire component) entirely.

**⚠️ Share Weakness did not fire once all session.** Both Exploit Vulnerability rolls went to bosses — failed by 1 on Vesinra, succeeded by 0 on Gimmel — so Kudu never got the rider. Synergy #2 is the highest-leverage thing Liu does for someone else's sheet and it produced **zero** value this week. Worth deciding deliberately whether to Exploit the boss or the thing Kudu is actually hitting. *(Neither Exploit bought a real weakness either: Vesinra's failure gave personal antithesis, and Gimmel has no printed weakness at all. Both fights ran on a flat +8.)*

**⚠️ Diverse Lore fires on every successful Exploit and has to be asked for.** Foundry printed the comparison DC on the Exploit card at **28** against a result of **28** — a success, and a free Recall Knowledge on a *rare* creature. Nobody called for the comparison, so it went unclaimed. Its **first** clause also matters more out of combat than in: Esoteric Lore at **−2 for any topic** is **+23**, well ahead of Arcana/Occultism at +14 and Society at +0.

**Kudu opens, Liu closes — and the raw gap is smaller than it looks.** In the office Kudu removed **518 HP (55%)** to Liu's **271 (29%)** and Guar's **146 (16%)**. But **122 of Kudu's total was a single Dragon's Rage Breath**, a **once-per-10-minutes** ability used for the first time in twelve levels. Strip it and the sustainable picture is **Kudu 396 (49%) · Liu 271 (33%) · Guar 146 (18%)** — a **1.46:1** ratio, not 1.91:1.

Liu also **rolled 429 and only landed 271**: 158 points went to overkill, because his job is finishing targets others have already opened (122 into a greedspawn with 86 left, 98 into Vesinra's last 33, 106 into Gimmel's last 49). **A damage-share metric structurally undercounts whoever swings last.** He still took **three of the five kills**, including both bosses.

**Kudu's AoE ceiling is real and was untouched for twelve levels.** The breath caught four enemies and rolled a critical failure for 80. Worth building rounds around when the enemy clusters.

**⚠️ The party has no answer to terrain.** Gimmel's **Wall of Stone** (one action-and-a-half) cut off Galan's Spiritual Guardian, Guar's Floating Flame and every melee line simultaneously, and the party spent two full rounds hitting one inch of masonry with **hardness 14**. This is the Session-1 "no battlefield control" gap seen from the other side: nobody can *make* terrain and nobody can *remove* it. Kudu solved it by walking around the end.

**Mythic economy is a gate, not just a resource.** The Endless Reality was passable **only** by spending mythic power, and Guar's Dispel was the single point of failure for the whole dungeon. Clearing it refilled the pool, so the cost was temporary — but the party needs to know who can open which kind of gate.

**Guar keeps spending his own Mythic Points on other people.** Correct the Story on Kudu's incoming crit (third use across two sessions, all on other players; it rerolled into another crit for nothing). He also detected *and* cleared the hazard.

**Galan's kit is bimodal.** Divine Wrath opened the initiate fight with four critical failures and effectively decided it; Identify Magic cracked the hazard; a mythic-spent Fortitude save ate a 75-point Disintegrate whole. Then a Wall of Stone stranded him and he had nothing — Needle Darts into masonry. **He is enormously effective when he has line of effect and nearly idle when he doesn't.** He has also identified his own gap: his prepared Dispel Magic was too low-rank to counteract either the 4th-rank Fly or the rank-7 hazard, and he committed to preparing higher.

## Table agreement — metagaming (session 1, out of character)

The DM raised this explicitly at session end. **He takes no position himself and will enforce the group majority.** All four players landed in roughly the same place:

- **Tactical table talk is fine within limits.** Characters in the same room can obviously talk; six-second rounds don't allow ten-minute strategy sessions.
- **Nobody should be omniscient.** Reading a creature's stat block, resistances, or spells **without a Recall Knowledge** is over the line. (Pax's framing: strategy discussion fine, quoting a monster's sheet is not. Kudu's: fine "as long as it doesn't become a blocker… someone's trying to quarterback." Galan's: no one should have "read the bestiary and know everything.")
- The DM's stake: if table talk replaces everything, **Message, Mind Link and similar spells never get used**.
