Session Summary

The party came into this session having spent all of Session 17 refusing to kill anybody. They end it standing in a corridor full of dead prison guards, agreeing out loud that they are the bad guys now.

The night opened quietly. Bai Jian, standing watch outside the Soldier's Aegis, heard a single set of boots in the snow — Jenverick, who had been told to stay put and hadn't — and then a second man who made no sound at all. Judge Leoz Balake stepped out of the shadows: once a genuinely well-loved magistrate, high integrity, tough on crime, never took a bribe, until somebody threw acid in his face. He has been a vigilante ever since, and that is how the Hellbreakers heard of him. The party looted fifteen feet of jagged pit for eternal eruptions and frozen lava, argued cheerfully about who got to carry the bomb (Zanthar won, and put it up his sleeve "so it's kind of protected from going boom on accident"), and asked the new man whether he could tell them where to plant charges. Leoz answered by drawing his pistol: "I've got a detonator." Panacea rolled a natural 20 on Society and picked the placements herself. They took a room at the White Stag, slept through the sound of boots marching past the window all night, and in the morning Jenverick pulled a spare Masquerade Scarf out of her back pocket for the one man at the table who wasn't wanted yet.

Then they blew a ten-foot hole in the wall of the Gyve, and the tone of the campaign changed inside of one round. A sniper on the guard tower opened with an arquebus crit for 36 into Bai Jian's chest before anyone had taken a turn. Zanthar — the gnome who introduced himself an hour earlier as "a do-gooder" — looked at the tower and said, "I think this is a we-don't-care-if-they-die situation." Leoz put a bullet clean through the sniper for 42, twirled the pistol, told him "you're guilty," and reloaded John Wick-style. Zanthar hero-pointed a Blazing Bolt down the same hole the bullet had made. Panacea killed both artillerists — one with a Moonlight Ray that went "right over the dead guy," one with a hex she narrated as Serrafina's ray bouncing off his skull and taking his head off. Nobody on the tower survived, and nobody at the table hesitated.

Inside, it got worse before it got better. The basement was a torture chamber, and the two things working in it were an interrogator with a long slender razor and a bruiser with a greatclub. The interrogator hit Bai Jian with a 27-damage crit, filled the room with an aura of blood and fear, and then hit him again for 25. Cyrathul's Draconic Frenzy finally put it down ("boss, no!" from the back of the room), and Bai Jian vaulted off a desk to open the bodyguard's chest while Ren tore at his leg. Chained to the wall were Sarevial, the League's own courier, sobbing that he had told them everything — and Osney, who looked up and said "me say nothing. They can get nothing from me." Upstairs, the first door Zanthar picked released a stampede. The party did not just take their own people. They decided to free everyone in the building.

The last fight was a corridor lined with arrow slits and nothing to hit. Ren pointed a paw at a blank wall — a gesture his handler couldn't actually see — Bai Jian burned a hero point on the dog's Perception and then found the scratch marks himself anyway ("I shouldn't have used the hero point"), and a hidden door swung open onto three elite archer sentries and a hired mage. And in the middle of it the argument the whole session had been building to finally happened out loud. The last guard standing dropped his sword, fell to his knees, and said he only makes two gold a week. Judge Leoz flipped a coin for his life. It came up in the guard's favour, and the man handed over his keyring and offered to lead them to the third floor. Bai Jian, watching him go: "I guess we're the bad guys now." Cyrathul: "We're the bad guys."

The Night

Act I · Roleplay · The Night Judge

Session zero, and a man who makes no sound

  • The GM ran a short session zero for the new player: lines and veils, fade-to-black on romance, and an explicit warning that this is a grim AP — Cheliax as Isger's mother country, slavery abolished in name and running as indentured servitude in fact.
  • Panacea: a catfolk witch-medic from a cult of catfolk who keep human familiars; she and Serrafina fell into a portal while playing in the jungle as adolescents and came out near Breachill. And, still: "this is my first time ever playing a role-playing game."
  • Bai Jian: from Tian Xia, five years on the trail of a missing family member, joined the League voluntarily — "hasn't paid out so far, but it has been a journey." Zanthar: "a gnome that's particularly small," a wizard with healing, "a do-gooder." Cyrathul, absent, described by the GM: a Lunar Dragon Thaumaturge who underwent the Lux Aeterna ritual to help throw off Cheliax's chains.
  • Panacea took the recap hero point unprompted ("I'll do it if nobody else wants to"). House economy: one at start, one at the break, one for the recap.
  • Outside the Aegis, Bai Jian's Perception (21) caught one set of boots in the snow — Jenverick, disobeying orders to stay put. The second arrival made no sound at all.
  • Judge Leoz Balake introduced himself: "some in certain circles refer to him as the Night Judge." A well-loved, high-integrity, tough-on-crime magistrate who never took a bribe — until he was hit in the face with acid, and turned to vigilantism. He was supposed to be at the meeting; arriving late is the only reason he isn't in the Gyve with everyone else.
  • Bai Jian tried to bench Jenverick ("I think you should stay back"). She pulled the thinnest needle of a dagger anyone had ever seen out of her belt loop, looked scared and determined, and took a lookout post — blending into the corner of the building so well she nearly vanished.
Act II · Planning · The Pit, the Plan, and the White Stag

Enough explosives to open a prison

  • The munitions pit was ~15 feet deep and jagged. Contents: enough unused frozen lava and eternal eruptions to blow the prison wall, stage several diversionary explosions across town, and leave the party spares. Everything went into Cyrathul's spacious pouch.
  • Zanthar claimed the eternal eruption because "that looks like a fun toy to me," then stowed it in his sleeve "so it's kind of protected from going boom on accident." Bai Jian: "Don't have too much fun, though."
  • Leoz tied his first mechanical contribution to his backstory — asking whether his knowledge of prisons, "from being who he is," could place the charges. He had Underworld Lore, not Warfare Lore. When Zanthar worried they had bombs but no way to set them off remotely, Leoz produced the answer: "I've got a detonator," he says as he pulls out his pistol.
  • Panacea rolled a natural 20 on Society (32) and personally chose where the diversionary charges went; Zanthar's Crafting (13) and Leoz's untrained Society (8) added nothing. The GM gave her the credit outright — "Panacea really seems like she knows what she's doing… almost like she's done this before."
  • Panacea kept dragging the planning back to the part nobody wanted to answer: "are we also planning on what we're going to do with the people that we free?" → "this is why we should be discussing it. We need a good plan."
  • The boat is gone as an escape route. Quoster sails as soon as he's loaded. The replacement: the military stables next to the Gyve. Jenverick took the horses, clearing the stables after the wall goes up, since the blast pulls the stable guards toward the breach.
  • GM warning: "Once we move to the next map, there'll probably be no long rest until you guys are done." They took a room at the White Stag under Zanthar's last Shared Invisibility. One bed, everyone on the floor. Bai Jian took first watch and Zanthar second; both heard boots marching in formation up and down the street all night.
  • In the morning Jenverick handed Leoz a Masquerade Scarf — "I know that you aren't actually wanted yet, but maybe you could use this as well."
  • Recon over breakfast: Panacea's Society (24) turned up a torture chamber in the basement; Bai Jian's Gather Information Diplomacy (26) put the prisoners on the second and third floors.
Act III · Combat · The Wall Breach

Ten feet of wall, and the doctrine dies

  • Charges against an outer wall blew a 10-foot section open. Shouting from the ramparts: "Wall breach! To arms!" Then a loud crack of a firearm from the top of a tower.
  • The courtyard was empty. Normally it would be full of prisoners forced to make arms for the army; there was no labour detail running. Panacea shouted Milani's recognition phrase into it anyway — "if you are friends of the rose, come over here to the prisoners" — and nobody heard her.
  • Doctrine flipped in one line. Zanthar, looking up at the tower: "I think this is a we-don't-care-if-they-die situation." Nobody argued. Panacea, a round later: "apparently we're going all the way with these guys, huh?… We are going to kill them." Leoz: "That's the only way that Leoz starts, unfortunately."
  • Post-fight the party stripped the tower: two dueling pistols, an arquebus, four silencers, two siege-weaponry toolkits, repair toolkits, forty-odd rounds. Leoz claimed the silencers and a spare pistol. Cyrathul, reading the item text: "Four completely silenced shots."
  • Snowgeen arrived at the break and took Cyrathul back from the GM. His first line: "seems to be working pretty well." Leoz's welcome to the campaign's ethics: "Yeah, that whole non-lethal thing you guys apparently tried last time. I figured as much."
Act IV · Combat · The Torture Chamber

"No, no more."

  • Down into a round subterranean chamber: black stone, dripping water amplified into echoes, rusty shackles on the ceiling and walls, an iron smell of blood under a grave-scent of earth, a rack under a pulley system, cruel pokers and blades on the back wall.
  • Leoz, under his breath, before anyone rolled anything: "This is injustice."
  • Bai Jian's boots gave the party away. Two figures turned: one sharpening a long slender razor, one with a nasty gauntlet and a huge club. And from the wall, a voice — "no, no more."
  • Bai Jian's answer to "coming to join the party?": "Yeah, no, we're here to disrupt the party. You gotta stop this madness."
  • Leoz crit-failed initiative and made a joke of it in character: "He's slow. He's new. He's got to get with the rhythm."
  • The prisoners on the wall were Sarevial, the Hellbreaker courier who has hosted the party at two safehouses, and Osney, the goblin burglar who taught them how to stage a robbery. Sarevial fell to his knees sobbing when Cyrathul unlocked his second hand — "I couldn't help myself… I was telling them everything. He was so wicked." Osney gave up nothing: "me say nothing. They can get nothing from me."
  • Bai Jian: "Yeah, we won't abandon our comrades." Sarevial confirmed the S17 raid hit within a minute of the party leaving, that everyone was there, and that Jenverick went out a window with a Hellknight after her. The rest are upstairs, brought down one at a time.
  • Leoz, as the party turned for the stairs: "No time to waste. Justice waits for no one." Bai Jian, approving it out loud: "I like the sound of that." The Night Judge set the tone and the fighter signed off on it.
Act V · Hazard · The Prison Break

The decision to free everyone

  • Zanthar took the Skeleton Key — which doubles as a bonused thieves' toolkit, a fact Bai Jian had to point out after failing to Force Open at DC 27 — and picked the first cell lock in two successes.
  • The door opened on a mass cell: stale sweat, mounds of straw for beds, people packed shoulder to shoulder with nowhere to lie down, all trying to push past the party at once. It was a hazard, not a fight. Bai Jian, deadpan on seeing initiative rolled: "Oh yeah, it's a hazard. Of course."
  • Panacea cast Calm into the mob — not to stop them, but "so they can get out orderly like" — scoring the first success toward disabling it. Her follow-up Recall Knowledge never happened: her Foundry client crashed to a black screen, and the GM covered it in fiction.
  • Cyrathul closed it out with No Cause for Alarm, a 3-action skill feat he'd evidently never had a use for ("I can't think of another situation which could use it"), hero-pointed for safety. The mob froze "like deer caught in headlights," then began politely asking to squeeze past: "Excuse me, can I squeeze through right here?"
  • The party banked an Uprising point.
  • The scope decision. These weren't Hellbreakers — the party recognised nobody. Zanthar: "some criminals maybe." Cyrathul: "Yeah, but it's what the current Chelaxian regime views as criminals, so I don't know if I trust their judgment on this matter." Then, to the cell: "Yes, we're freeing everyone in this prison. So if you want to get out of here, now's your chance."
  • Panacea gave them the route and one instruction — "there's a hole downstairs in the wall… but don't go all the way downstairs into the torture chamber."
  • A dozen prisoners pointed the way: the Hellbreakers are in high security on the third floor. Panacea, failing to list her own recruits: "a little dwarf veteran and a… oh gosh, I can't remember all of the people."
  • Rooms cleared: a tool room (Bai Jian took a crowbar) and an evidence locker of confiscated prisoner property — Leoz: "This seems to be the evidence locker. Evidence of injustice." Cyrathul talked them into looting it anyway: "it's just a lost and found. It's been there too long." Bai Jian took bracers granting a 20-foot climb speed.
Act VI · Combat · The Arrow-Slit Corridor

"Silence is compliance"

  • A winding corridor hung with manacles, its inner wall lined with arrow slits — bows firing from a room the party could not reach or even see into. Zanthar, on the shooters' accuracy: "That seems like a personal problem."
  • Finding the way in took three of them. Leoz worked the geometry first. Bai Jian sent Ren forward on scent; the hound rolled a natural 1, Bai Jian spent a hero point (10 → +10 house rule → 32), and Ren held out a paw at a blank stretch of wall — a gesture Bai Jian couldn't see ("you just hear him barking"). Bai Jian then made the same check on a plain 26 and spotted a half-moon of scratch marks where a hidden door swings — "I shouldn't have used the hero point."
  • He forced the door open onto three elite archer sentries and a mage for hire. Cyrathul mirrored through an arrow slit to flank, "I'll give like a nice toothy dragon smile," and opened with a crit.
  • The argument. It had a prologue: four minutes earlier Bai Jian had already conceded, "I think at this point we should just accept it. Just kill people." Then Zanthar: "Guess we're just killing everybody." Panacea: "These aren't good people." Bai Jian argued the other way anyway — "I mean, they're prison guards. They're just doing their job." Panacea shut that down: "No. They're torturing people in the basement. They knew they were torturing people in the basement. Don't act like they didn't." And Bai Jian rebutted himself: "they're accomplices. Silence is compliance." Three minutes later, still chewing: "I don't think at this point we're just killing innocent guards."
  • Two sentries died; the third dropped his shortsword, fell to his knees, and surrendered: "I only make two gold a week." Bai Jian, immediately: "Now I feel that."
  • Judge Leoz flipped a coin for him. The table made him actually roll it. It came up in the guard's favour. Bai Jian: "Well, it's your lucky day. You get to join your friends."
  • Panacea turned the surrender into an asset — "I want you to lead the way, please" — and Bai Jian took his ring of keys. Zanthar stripped the dead mage and finally got what he'd complained about all night: a thieves' toolkit and a seven-spell spellbook.
  • Session ends with the party holding the keys, a guide, and a building they have emptied — one floor below the people they came for.

"This bullet just ripped through this guy… in his side and comes out his other side." He closed with Raconteur's Reload — pistol twirl, "you're guilty," John Wick reload — and a Demoralize on an Artillerist that failed.

Round 2 — Panacea alone

  • Moonlight Ray hit Artillerist A for 12, killing him — "my Moonlight Ray went through right over the dead guy and exploded that guy" — then Trade Death for Life on Artillerist B, who critically failed the DC 22 Fortitude save for 18 and died.   • All three enemies dead before the party's second turn came around. Bai Jian never swung.
  • ⚠ Both Artillerists were Quickened for the entire encounter and never once used the extra action. That, plus one of them spending a turn reloading, is the real reason a three-enemy ambush produced only two attack rolls.

Round 1

Character Spotlight

Judge Leoz Balake · the Night Judge

  • The debut landed. Not "a gunslinger joins the party" but a fully-formed person: a well-loved, high-integrity magistrate, tough on crime, who never took a bribe — until somebody threw acid in his face. He's been a vigilante since, and that's how the League found him. He was supposed to be at the safehouse meeting; arriving late is the only reason he isn't in the Gyve with everyone else.
  • He speaks about himself in third person and ties his mechanics to his fiction. His first act was asking whether Leoz's knowledge of prisons, "from being who he is," could place the charges. His second was the best line of the night: "I've got a detonator," he says as he pulls out his pistol.
  • He hits like a truck. First round of play, ever: a critical hit for 42 through the tower sniper — narrated as the bullet going in one side and out the other. He closed with a full Raconteur's Reload flourish: the pistol twirl, the steely glare, "you're guilty," and a John Wick reload. The Demoralize itself failed. Nobody cared.
  • He is the party's conscience in a way none of the others are. In the torture chamber, before a single die was rolled, he muttered "this is injustice." In the evidence locker: "This seems to be the evidence locker. Evidence of injustice." And turning the party toward the stairs: "No time to waste. Justice waits for no one." The Night Judge names what he's looking at — and Bai Jian, hearing that last one, said "I like the sound of that." One session in, the new man is supplying the party's watchwords.
  • And he owns the moral weight of the ending. When the last guard surrendered and said he only makes two gold a week, Leoz didn't argue for mercy or execution — he flipped a coin. The table made him roll it for real. It came up in the guard's favour. That is a judge who has stopped believing in his own judgment, and it is a hell of a thing to establish in one session.
  • He is already contributing tactically, not just atmospherically. He offered to take point with a silenced shot loaded; flagged that a barracks room could be locked from outside before the party walked past it; was the first to work the geometry of the arrow-slit corridor before Bai Jian sent the dog; and checked the penalty for shooting into melee rather than assuming it.
  • Also, gamely, a new player learning Foundry in public — targeting, macros, the party stash — and cracking jokes about it. On critically failing initiative: "He's slow. He's new. He's got to get with the rhythm."

Panacea · the One Who Asks the Hard Question

  • She ran the session's planning and she ran its conscience. While everyone else argued about who got to hold the bomb, Panacea asked: "And while we're planning this, are we also planning on what we're going to do with the people that we free?" — and when the table drifted again, "this is why we should be discussing it. We need a good plan."
  • She took the recap hero point unprompted ("I'll do it if nobody else wants to") and delivered a genuinely accurate three-minute account of Session 17 from memory.
  • Natural 20 on Society to place the diversionary charges across the city, and the GM handed her the credit in narration: "Panacea really seems like she knows what she's doing… almost like she's done this before." Her Society check also turned up the torture chamber before they ever went down there.
  • She is still, at Session 18, the table's first-time roleplayer — she says so out loud at session zero — and she is running the party's logistics, its healing, and its ethics.
  • The moral spine of the ending is hers. When Bai Jian made the case that the prison guards were just doing their job, Panacea didn't hedge: "No. They're torturing people in the basement. They knew they were torturing people in the basement. Don't act like they didn't." She had just come up from that basement.
  • And she is kind in the small places. She shouted Milani's recognition phrase into a courtyard that turned out to be empty, on the off-chance. She checked whether the freed torture victim needed healing. She told the released prisoners the way out and warned them off the basement. She asked the surrendering guard to lead them, with a "please." And when she couldn't remember her own recruits' names, it played as a woman rattled by what she'd just seen, hunting for people she'd personally signed up.
  • Two kills in the wall breach, both narrated with relish — a Moonlight Ray that went "right over the dead guy," and Serrafina's ray bouncing off a skull and beheading its owner.

Zanthar Melodune III · the Do-Gooder Who Kept Score

  • He has the session's clearest arc, and it is a great one. At session zero: "a gnome that's particularly small… he's just looking for, trying to be better, trying to do good things for the world. He's a do-gooder."
  • Then he looked up at the guard tower and said "I think this is a we-don't-care-if-they-die situation" — and that one sentence became the party's rules of engagement for the whole night, replacing six sessions of scrupulous non-lethal doctrine. Nobody voted. Nobody argued.
  • Then he hero-pointed a Blazing Bolt to finish the sniper, sending it down the hole Leoz's bullet had already made, "Robin Hood style."
  • Then, two acts later, he swung his maul at the interrogator deliberately non-lethally and was disappointed it wasn't enough: "I was hoping I'd knock him out." The do-gooder is still in there.
  • Then, walking into the last corridor, "We're gonna kill them all!" Then he burst a sentry's head "like a watermelon" on a second consecutive critical Blazing Bolt: "That's it. I'm a killer machine."
  • And then, minutes later, he was the one who said it out loud: "Guess we're just killing everybody." He opened the door on this and he's the first to notice where it went.
  • He was also the party's key that opened the prison. Zanthar picked lock after lock with the Skeleton Key all night while wryly noting the gap in his kit: "I really need to get a thief's toolkit, because I've got the skill, but not the…" The session's last act paid it off: he stripped the dead Mage for Hire and walked away with a thieves' toolkit and a seven-spell spellbook.
  • Comedy throughout: claiming the biggest bomb in the pit because "that sounds like fun"; refusing to be first through any door ("I go down real quick"); and when Foundry disconnected him mid-turn and his token ran back upstairs, "I'm so embarrassed I ran away."

Cyrathul · the Dragon Who Set the Terms

  • Half his session was played by the GM — Snowgeen arrived at the break — and the GM's version of him still charged a guard tower alone, because Bai Jian vouched for it: "He's brave. He runs in all the time." Mirror Cloak to the tower top, jaws, critical hit, 30. His first words on taking the character back: "Seems to be working pretty well."
  • He supplied the principle that made the prison break stick. Freeing prisoners for cover was already in Jenverick's S17 plan — Zanthar's addition to it. But the plan assumed Hellbreakers. When the second cell held a dozen strangers and Zanthar shrugged that they were "some criminals maybe," Cyrathul answered the actual question: "Yeah, but it's what the current Chelaxian regime views as criminals, so I don't know if I trust their judgment on this matter." Then, to the cell: "Yes, we're freeing everyone in this prison." A tactical diversion became a moral position, and it was his.
  • He ended the stampede with a feat he'd been carrying for nothing. "I do have a skill feat called No Cause for Alarm… I can't think of another situation which could use it." He hero-pointed it, roared the corridor down, and a crushing mob turned into a queue saying "excuse me, can I squeeze through right here?"
  • His Exploit Vulnerability lane carried both fights he was present for — a failed check on the interrogator that fell back to personal antithesis, then a clean success on the bodyguard returning no weakness and a spent question on its weakest save: Will. He also brought a second implement, Regalia, whose fear aura Bai Jian immediately learned to time his turn around.
  • He is warm with the rescued, and he is the one who does the unlocking. Cyrathul personally unshackled both prisoners; Sarevial fell to his knees sobbing as the second manacle came off, and Cyrathul told him "Yes, it'll be all right. We have a handle on things now. You can come with us." Before that he asked the harder question first — drawing a finger across his own throat to ask whether Sarevial had given anything up. To Osney, who gave up nothing: "As expected."
  • Dry about everything else. On robbing the evidence locker: "it's just a lost and found. It's been there too long." On mirroring through an arrow slit into a room of archers: "I'll give like a nice toothy dragon smile."
  • And he gets the session's last word, agreeing with Bai Jian without a flicker of protest: "We're the bad guys."

Bai Jian · the One Who Wouldn't Let It Be Easy

  • He ate the session. A 36-damage arquebus crit before initiative had gone once. A 27-damage crit and a 25-damage follow-up in the basement, the second carrying 2d8 mental because the thing that cut him had already made him afraid of it. A failed Reflex against the stampede for 21 more. He is the frontline of one, and every encounter opened on him.
  • His best mechanical play was patience, not damage. Frightened in the torture chamber, he delayed his turn to slot in behind Cyrathul's so the dragon's brand-new Regalia aura would strip the condition at the end of it. He read a teammate's new class feature and rebuilt his action order around it inside one round — and it turned an ordinary hit into the crit that killed the bodyguard.
  • He is the party's problem-solver at the seams, and most of it is unglamorous. Failed Force Open at DC 27, then noticed the Skeleton Key doubles as a bonused thieves' toolkit and handed the job to Zanthar. Spent a hero point on Ren's Perception, found the hidden door on his own plain roll a moment later, and grumbled "I shouldn't have used the hero point." In the stampede he never got a turn at all and still did four things: caught a roll card the GM had misfired to the players, corrected the table's Disarm rules read, prompted Panacea's Recall Knowledge, and called the group heal afterward.
  • He knows exactly what he is. On stealth: "You look at Bai Jian, heavy armor, big ass sword, and a dog. Yeah, no. You need someone to guide him." Follow the Expert, every time.
  • Two kills, both narrated as teamwork with his dog — the bodyguard opened up from a desk-jump while Ren tore at his leg, and a 40-damage critical hit that one-shot the Mage for Hire ("He dares to cast a spell"). He also gave up his own healing for Ren and then took it back: "Actually, do it on me. Don't do it on Ren… I'm going to steal it from my dog."
  • The session's real weight sits on him. "We're here to disrupt the party. You gotta stop this madness" to the torturer. "We won't abandon our comrades" to Sarevial. And when Leoz called the march upstairs with "justice waits for no one," it was Bai Jian who took the line and endorsed it: "I like the sound of that."
  • And he is the only one who refused to let the killing become comfortable. Four minutes before the famous exchange he had already given up once — "I think at this point we should just accept it. Just kill people." Then he argued the other way anyway: "they're prison guards, they're just doing their job." Then he rebutted himself — "they're accomplices. Silence is compliance." And then, three minutes later, still not settled: "I don't think at this point we're just killing innocent guards." That is not indecision. That is resignation, devil's advocacy, self-rebuttal, and a conclusion, in that order, with nobody prompting any of it.
  • When the surrendering guard mentioned two gold a week his answer was "now I feel that," and when the coin came up mercy: "it's your lucky day." He closes the night on "I guess we're the bad guys now" — the only person at the table who spent the whole session trying to hold both halves of it at once.

Ren · the Boarhound Who Pointed the Way

  • Tumbled Through into a flanking position on the interrogator, ate two greatclub swings from the bodyguard and dodged both ("Ren is just way too nimble for that large club"), and tore a sentry's femoral artery out.
  • He pointed the way to the hidden door. Sent forward on scent into a corridor with no visible entrance, he rolled a natural 1, got a hero point spent on him, came back a 32 — and stood holding out a paw at a blank stretch of wall that his handler couldn't see him pointing at. Ren aimed him; Bai Jian found it.
  • Reliable saves all night: successes against Blood and Fear, the stampede crush, and Grease. His one blemish is the Bombard, where he was the only PC or companion who merely succeeded instead of critically succeeding — which is precisely why he was the only one who took damage from it.
