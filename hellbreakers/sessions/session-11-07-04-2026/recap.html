Session Summary

No combat for the first two hours — and nobody minded. The party woke inside the Elidir safehouse they'd reached at midnight, and met the people they'd crossed a locked-down capital to find: Alceo and Phoebe Demetrias, the Hellbreakers' co-founders, now burned, identified, and handing the whole Elidir chapter off to the PCs. The job comes down to one meeting: a Druman merchant named Quoster wants proof the League is worth backing, and he'll take that meeting at the New Year's Ball in a week. Get in, close the deal. First, though — three errands: forged invitations (from a red-skinned tax clerk who can't be seen helping them), gala costumes (the ball is devil-themed, of course), and a Lazy Bones pendant that's the recognition token for Quoster.

So the session became a day of walking a festival-day capital and doing the small, decent things that build an uprising. They scooped a legless dwarf out of the path of a runaway carriage. They talked a devilishly enthusiastic four-foot tailor into disguises — Bai Jian as a Chelaxian Hellknight paralictor, Panacea in a blood-dripping devil gown, Zanthar as a little Asmodeus, Cyrathul as a literal chain devil (with wings that matched his own), and Ren in devil warhound barding. They conned a nervous Nephilim clerk into a private office and walked out with the invitations. They calmed a runaway Daeodon a cruel handler had loosed at a pack of noble brats. Everywhere they went the city was a little kinder than it should be — "I hear there's an uprising going on" — and the Uprising points piled up.

The pendant was the hitch. At the Love Sting Printing House — a risqué-novel press whose owner writes Phoebe's favorite books — a fire-throwing goblin and a smoke-shrouded caster were beating the elf owner and torching the shop over "Hellbreaker material." The party put both down non-lethally and bucketed out the fire with barrels of printer's ink, but the pendant was already gone: stolen mid-fight, a blue ribbon left in its place. Zanthar recognized the calling card — the nascent thieves' guild that fronts as an orphanage, the "Old House of the Found Family," run by a figure called the father. A night of recon, a morning report to the Demetriases, and the party went in the back door to get the pendant back. The two "orphans" in the kitchen turned out to be genuinely dangerous rogues — no weaknesses, hard to pin, and terrified, screaming that their home was under siege. The party took them alive anyway, but one spat that "the father will have your throat" and wouldn't talk. Heading upstairs to find the father, they tripped a spring-loaded net and heard the twang of crossbows. Session ends at the top of the stairs, staring down a surprise volley.

Timeline

Act I · Faction / Roleplay · The Safehouse & the Founders

Handing Off a City

  • Sarevial got the party the last ten blocks to a nondescript mansion near midnight and went to wake the house. They were led into a chapel to meet Alceo and Phoebe Demetrias, the Hellbreakers' co-founders — barefoot, half-dressed, roused from bed. "Your exploits have reigned throughout the South."
  • The situation: a month of stirring discontent, but Hedvind cracked down on the protests, used his army to frame the League as rioters, and identified the founders. They're handing Elidir operations to the party.
  • The mission: close the deal with a Druman merchant, Quoster — supply-for-trade-rights — who now wants proof and will take the meeting at the New Year's Ball, a week out. The party negotiates in the founders' place.
  • Three errands: forged invitations from Oramos (a Hellspawn Nephilim tax clerk who can't be seen helping); costumes from Rasumili Brightgate at Dress to Impress; and the Lazy Bones pendant at the Love Sting Printing House — the recognition token for Quoster.
  • Alceo laid four keys on the table and warned the city is one bad fight from full lockdown. Taking stock, the party realized what they were actually carrying from last session: a dead Toady's body in the spacious pouch — the constable Adiutor was only knocked out and left behind at Isger's Circle to wake up. Food, wine, and the first real beds in weeks.
Act II · Roleplay · A Player Departs

Goodbye, Sammy

  • Over breakfast, Sarevial quietly pulled Sammy aside; she thanked the group and left on "a different mission." (Out of character: Sammy's player stepped away from the campaign so as not to keep taking a seat while missing sessions.) Bai Jian: "Bye, Sammy… see you again."
  • The DM recapped the new composition — wizard, fighter, witch-medic, and "the dragon fighter" (Cyrathul). "We'll do pretty good."
Act III · Roleplay / Social · Small Kindnesses on Fire Day

Good Deeds and Devil Costumes

  • The streets were packed for solstice ("fire day") — coffee, fresh pastries, last-minute wine, the tower of Isger's Fist looming over a changing of the guard.
  • The runaway carriage: a legless dwarf slipped on the ice, a prosthetic leg popping off, right in the path of a speeding carriage. Bai Jian sprinted and scooped him clear (Athletics); Panacea retrieved and returned his fallen leg. "I hear there's an uprising going on… everyone is being so much kinder." +30 XP and an Uprising point.
  • Dress to Impress: Rasumili Brightgate, a four-foot tailor bursting with enthusiasm, fitted the party for the devil-themed ball — Bai Jian as a Chelaxian Hellknight paralictor (sword worn openly, solving the "full plate at a ball" problem), Panacea in a deep-red gown with ragged, blood-dripping papier-mâché wings, Zanthar as a little Asmodeus, and Cyrathul as a chain devil whose red wings matched his real ones.
  • Ren was fitted in devil warhound barding (a horned dog-mask was tried first and rejected). Come back in six days (the 30th) for the finished costumes.
Act IV · Social · The Invitations

Getting Oramos Alone

  • Across the bridge to the Trade House at the docks — barrels crashing, taxmen collecting, a merchant loudly refusing a tax hike and storming out ("the last time I'm bringing cargo to Elidir").
  • At the front desk sat Oramos — a tall, very-red-skinned man. Bai Jian architected the cover (pose as anxious newcomers with private tax questions so Oramos could step away without looking like he was helping anyone), then handed the pitch to Cyrathul (Diplomacy) and spent a hero point to land it.
  • Behind the closed door, Panacea gave the sign — "We were sent by Phoebe" — and Oramos laid out four forged invitations. "It's wonderful to be a friend of the Rose… and I assume you'll never mention this to anyone." +40 XP.
  • Panacea, on getting through a whole scene without a fight: "It's just weird to not be fighting."
Act V · Roleplay · The Daeodon

A Boar, a Bully, and a Blessed Solstice

  • Past a Gorumite veterans' hospital, finely-dressed noble teens pelted a laborer's enormous Daeodon (a giant boar hauling a cart) with rocks and dung — and the handler deliberately loosed the beast at the kids when it enraged, cackling.
  • Bai Jian shouted at the handler (Intimidation, +0 — no dice), then ran to put himself between the boar and the children. The boar raked its tusks up his leg.
  • Panacea appealed to the handler's holiday good nature (Diplomacy, no luck), then cast Calm. The boar succeeded its Will save (incapacitation → unaffected), stayed enraged, and gored Bai Jian; only the handler was pacified. (The DM narrated the boar calming, then re-read the roll and corrected it.)
  • Cyrathul talked the dazed handler into reclaiming his beast (Diplomacy, chosen over Intimidate so as not to break the Calm); the boar recognized its master and settled. The teens fled; the handler tossed four silver at the party's feet — "now we're even" — and slunk off.
Act VI · Combat · The Love Sting Printing House

Fire, Ink, and a Held Blow

  • A bruised elf crumpled in the snow outside a smoking shop — "They're just harmless entertainment!" Inside, a goblin juggled fire and torched the place ("Hellbreaker material won't be tolerated"), with a smoke-shrouded caster, Odvolos, deeper in.
  • Panacea tried to defuse it first — moving to Calm the goblin — but the visible spellcasting blew it ("We got company!"), forcing the fight. Mindful of lockdown, the party fought to subdue.
  • Zanthar Fear'd the goblin (Frightened 1); Bai Jian's Slam Down tripped it prone; Cyrathul finished it with a non-lethal tail-whip — "a somersault flop… this huge tail comes around and knocks the goblin out."
  • Odvolos — out of spell range — closed and Demoralized Bai Jian (Frightened 1). Bai Jian crit him with a Snagging Strike for 51, off-guard — then deliberately held back the killing blow. Zanthar KO'd Odvolos with a non-lethal Hand of the Apprentice through the smoke.
  • The fire was the second enemy: the party stayed in initiative and bucketed printer's ink from the shop's barrels — Bai Jian, Zanthar, and Panacea (Rousing Splash) — five feet of flame at a time, until it was out. +2 Uprising points.
  • The grateful elf handed over disguise face-paints, a rare book, and romance novels for Phoebe — and announced the next title: "Breaking Hell for Her," starring the party. Then the catch: the pendant was already stolen, a blue ribbon in its place.
Act VII · Investigation · The Blue Ribbon

The Children's Charity

  • Zanthar recognized the calling card (a crit knowledge check): the nascent thieves' guild styling itself the "Children's Charity," fronting as the "Old House of the Found Family" orphanage — and he knew exactly where it was. "I had a criminal background. I wouldn't mind knowing personally." Bai Jian: "I'll pretend I didn't hear that."
  • Zanthar dumped buckets of ink on the two knocked-out attackers for the guard to collect — "they're going to be blue for a really long time."
  • Recon: the party staked out the orphanage into the night. Zanthar (in his element) clocked the comings and goings and confirmed a back entrance.
  • Next morning, over Sarevial's meat pies, they briefed the Demetriases: the "orphans" are adults now — highly trained thieves the father adopted; and since they left the ribbon on purpose, they may well sell the pendant back.
Act VIII · Combat · The Old House of the Found Family

Intruders

  • The party went for the unlocked back door. Bai Jian pulled Cyrathul up front — "we need our talker." The kitchen held two rogues mid-snack; they dropped their food and screamed "Intruders!" — playing the party, accurately, as home invaders.
  • These were no pushovers: Mobility, Deny Advantage (immune to off-guard), no weaknesses — Cyrathul's Exploit came up empty ("quite some rogues"). One arrow tagged Bai Jian (5); then both drew short swords and cut Cyrathul up in melee (~34); Panacea healed him 22.
  • Zanthar's Blazing Bolt (15, then a crit 34) set a rogue's back ablaze. Bai Jian Snagging-Struck (15), grappled, and dropped the rogue with a hero-pointed non-lethal Reactive Strike for 43 when they tried to draw a weapon in reach.
  • The second rogue fell to Zanthar's non-lethal Hand of the Apprentice (18) and Bai Jian's finishing strike (Ren's Support + Panacea's Guidance = +15 to hit; flat of the blade, 13). Both captured alive.
  • A revived captive refused to break — "the father will have your throat" — and Zanthar clubbed him back out ("He's got a brain trauma. Seems to already have it."). Heading upstairs, Zanthar tripped a spring-loaded net trap (Reflex — evaded), then crossbow clicks: the guild gets a surprise round. Session ends at the top of the stairs.

Character Spotlight

Bai Jian · the Blade That Pulls Its Punches

  • In a city one loud fight from lockdown, Bai Jian ran the whole session on non-lethal discipline — Slam-Down-tripping the arsonist goblin so it could be put out gently, crit-Snagging Odvolos for a monstrous 51 and then deliberately holding the follow-up rather than kill, and closing the orphanage with a hero-pointed non-lethal Reactive Strike (43) and a flat-of-the-blade finisher. "We'll knock them out, and then we'll see what happens."
  • He's the party's designated everyman, and he knows it — handed the talking, he punts ("Actually, why am I doing the talking? You guys do the talking. I gave up"), pulling Cyrathul up as "our talker" every time. But the plan is often his: the tax-office ruse to peel Oramos away was Bai Jian's design, handed to Cyrathul to deliver.
  • His agency shows in the doing: he scooped a legless dwarf out from under a carriage, put himself between the Daeodon and a pack of kids and ate a tusk for it, and ran the whole action-economy engine — commanding Ren for blocks and Support, tracking his own conditions, reading Mobility and Deny Advantage off the rogues in real time.
  • And he still lands the button: as the crossbows came up on the stairs, he ribbed Zanthar for ending up on point — "You were saying I should be the one going forward, and now you're the one…" — while the gnome took the volley.

Cyrathul · Our Talker (with a Tail)

  • Snowgeen was out, so the DM ran Cyrathul — third-person, and mostly quiet — but the dragon still owned two lanes the party can't fill. He's the face: they routed every hard conversation through him, and he delivered, hero-pointing the Diplomacy that peeled Oramos away for the invitations and talking the dazed Daeodon handler down. "We need our talker," Bai Jian said at the orphanage door, and meant it.
  • He's the party's non-lethal closer. With the goblin arsonist prone, Cyrathul strode in and "somersault-flopped," whipping his huge tail around to knock it out cold — exactly the kind of subdue this lockdown mission wants.
  • He read the orphanage rogues with Exploit Mortal Weakness (and honestly reported they had none — "quite some rogues"), took the brunt of their short-sword melee so squishier members didn't have to, and got patched up by Panacea and bandaged by Zanthar after.
  • His costume beat was pure Cyrathul: fitted as a chain devil whose false red wings lined up perfectly over his real dragon wings — a monster wearing a costume of itself.

Panacea · The Healer, Not the Shit Starter

  • Told to open the printing-house fight, she balked in the most in-character way possible: "I don't like being the first one. I'm the healer, not the shit starter." — and delayed her initiative to sit behind Bai Jian where her job is. That's the whole character in two sentences.
  • Her agency is de-escalation. She's the reason the Daeodon scene didn't end in a slaughtered animal or trampled kids — first appealing to the handler's decency, then Calming the situation, then wishing the sheepish handler a "blessed solstice" on his way out. She returned the legless dwarf's fallen leg without being asked, and drove the "keep them alive so we can question them" logic through both fights.
  • There's a dry streak under the conscience, too: told how readily Zanthar knew the thieves'-guild card, she just shrugged — "Aren't we all criminals?" — and she's as much the quiet planner (steering the party back to the founders for intel) as the healer.
  • Mechanically the anchor — Bless, Guidance, a clutch 22-point Heal on a bleeding Cyrathul, Rousing-Splash fire suppression — but the spotlight is that she plays a healer who'd rather solve the fight than start it. "We are far from stealthy, this group."

Zanthar Melodune III · The Rebel With a Rap Sheet

  • Scott's gnome quietly ran the utility belt — the party's artillery and its brain: a Blazing Bolt crit (34) that set a rogue on fire, Phase Bolt through a solid wall, a Fear on the arsonist goblin, and two clean non-lethal Hand of the Apprentice KOs — "a non-lethal spell," every time, mission-discipline intact.
  • He cracked the mystery: a crit knowledge check IDed the blue ribbon as the nascent thieves' guild and pinpointed the orphanage, and he ran the successful recon that found the back door.
  • The character beat was the reveal. Recognizing a thieves'-guild card a little too readily, he shrugged: "I had a criminal background. I wouldn't mind knowing personally" — earning Bai Jian's deadpan "I'll pretend I didn't hear that." He clubbed the uncooperative captive back out ("He's got a brain trauma. Seems to already have it.") and dumped ink on the downed arsonists to mark them for the guard.
  • And right after Panacea declared the party hopeless at stealth, the perfect two-word rebuttal: "I can be stealthy." Then he rounded the orphanage's upstairs corner first, straight into the guild's crossbows — "I'm drawing fire for y'all." Utility, comedy, a criminal past he's only half-hiding, and the guy who takes the first volley.
