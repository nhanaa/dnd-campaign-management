Heath

This was the end of the climb. After a quick resupply at Talmandor's Bounty — level-10 gear finally in reach, everyone loading up on moderate and greater potions (and, quietly, five Death Ward scrolls) — the party took the last stairs and walked into a floor that was one enormous treasure vault. It belonged to Viseldron the Fortune Dragon, a gem-bedecked young-adult emerald dragon whom Nizca and Deg had hired to guard the ascent. There was a real parley — Bael addressing the "distinguished gentleman," Bargrum offering to reinvest the hoard into renovations, Tivael reminding a sun-hating dragon about the merits of vitamin D — but Viseldron had been paid, and gold glimmers even under a blotted sky. What followed was a full dragon fight: a rank-5 Fireball opener that scorched the clustered party, four Animated Treasure Swarms, Disruptive Breath, Draconic Frenzy, a spell-stealing Capture Spell, and a treasure-shedding Share the Wealth. Tivael ran a bit the whole time — the tiny Dragonet challenging the "real" dragon to prove who was superior — while Ferrok ran Viseldron through under Bael's fire, and the party went swimming in the coins.

Then the pinnacle. The Shadow Beacon was already lit and screaming shadows into the sky, with Deg cavorting at its base and Nizca, the vampire lord of the Ebon Tower, waiting with a smile. Nizca's tragic monologue — thousands of years in stasis, an ocean between them and their living lover, the whole apocalypse a love letter — met the party's flat refusal, with Bargrum's answer the sharpest of the campaign. Then the gut-punch: Speckle, the pet homunculus the party had adopted, was revealed as Deg's spy all along and shattered into the Aeon Stone Swarm, which Raylta grieved even as it attacked her. Nizca opened with a Vampiric Exsanguination that crit-Tivael for 98 — survivable only because Bael had pre-cast party-wide Death Ward — and the finale became two problems at once: kill the bosses and disable the machine.

Tivael's control locked down both villains (Slow and Synesthesia on each), so the boss duo kept whiffing while Ferrok killed Deg and Bargrum dropped Nizca to zero — but Nizca used Spiteful Escape to fuse their soul into the beacon rather than die. The last rounds were a pure disable-the-device gauntlet under escalating void and cold, everyone rolling checks, until Raylta rolled the final 31 that destroyed the beacon and rent Nizca's soul apart with it. The shadows lifted like fog; the sun hit their skin for the first time in months. In the quiet after, a lovingly-painted portrait of Nizca's lover Lograsi tied the tragedy off, the party gave their send-offs — and six months later, letters sealed with a phoenix arrived. That is the end of Shades of Blood.

Timeline

Act I · Downtime · Shopping and the Last Stairs

Resupply, and a Vault of Gold

  • A quick hop back to Talmandor's Bounty before the top. The party is absurdly rich and finally shopping in the right bracket — Bael pushing everyone off lesser potions ("Moderates are only 50g, and greaters are only 400, so we're fine"), rune transfers and swaps traded around, level-10 items on the table.
  • The quietly decisive purchase: Bael buys five Death Ward scrolls (900 gp for the batch) "to buff ourselves before the final fight." It will save the whole party's lives an hour later.
  • Bargrum floats getting a tattoo (Raylta: "Anybody want to get a tattoo?"); the group divvies armor, scroll robes, and dragon-scale trophies.
  • They take the last stairs. The floor is quiet, the eastern wall crumbled into darkness, 40-foot ceilings — and then coins. A gargantuan hoard of gold, gems, statuettes, and art objects, deliberately arranged. Bargrum, sensing the scene: "Stand by for exposition."
Act II · Combat + Parley · Viseldron, the Fortune Dragon

The Superior Dragon

  • A booming voice — "Have you come to bask in the majesty of Viseldron?" — and an emerald dragon, its scales adorned with gems, coins, and rings worn like jewelry, looks the party over.
  • The parley. Bael, courtly: "This is your hoard, I presume? Distinguished gentleman?" Viseldron confirms Nizca and Deg are above — and that it has been paid to stop the party. Bargrum needles the pittance ("this fortune is all gonna go into renovations"; Viseldron: "My dear dwarf, that is what taxes are for"); Ferrok challenges ruling a sunless world ("No sun on your scales?"); Tivael pitches "vitamin D." Viseldron: "What does it matter the absence of the sun, when the glimmer of gold holds so much more sway." Then it animates its hoard.
  • Tivael runs the "superior dragon" bit all fight — the tiny Dragonet challenging the real one in melee ("I'm going to show him who's the superior dragon"), narrating his casts as a fellow dragon's condescension ("Well, hello, dragon. I am the superior dragon… I shall give you my present" — Synesthesia). That Synesthesia's Clumsy/Off-Guard set up Ferrok's 57-damage Ignition Spellstrike — Ferrok, out loud: "Because of clumsy" — the biggest single hit of the fight.
  • Viseldron opens with a rank-5 Fireball that scorches the whole clustered party (two 40s, turn one). Ferrok, deadpan: "Just a dragon, guys. We're fine." The dragon later casts a second Fireball, two Disruptive Breaths, and — the scary beat — a reaction Capture Spell that crit-succeeds to steal back a spent 5th-rank slot (it died before spending it).
  • Bargrum improbably trips the dragon (his Titan Wrestler makes a Tiny dwarf tripping a dragon stick), knocking Viseldron Prone and costing it an action; he Group-Taunts and aids with Commitment to Protection. Bael sets Thermal Nimbus, Demoralizes the dragon, and rounds up the treasure swarms to blast them together. Raylta keeps Ferrok alive ("because you get hit so much!") and sustains cold Interstellar Void to hold the dragon Fatigued.
  • Ground to single digits under Bael's Thermal Nimbus fire and Ferrok's blade, Viseldron falls to Ferrok's plain finishing strike — "you run the dragon through as it falls." Tivael's payoff: "Take that, stupid dragon. Just because you're bigger than me doesn't mean you're better." The party dives into the hoard — Ferrok narrating Bargrum "swimming through it like Scrooge McDuck," Raylta "showering in coins."
Act III · RP · The Pinnacle — Nizca & Deg

A Love Letter Written in Shadow

  • The top of the tower, spires reaching into the Netherworld. The Shadow Beacon — brass, wire, black crystal, motes of raw magic — is already active, shrieking shadow into the sky. Deg cavorts at its base; Nizca turns, a rare smile on their somber face.
  • Nizca's monologue: "Well, if it isn't the ones who have been ransacking my tower… We've already completed the Shadow Beacon, and this time it's perfect… The shadows of the Netherworld will pour into the sky, blotting out the sun for an eternity… Surrender and I'll allow you to leave with your lives."
  • The refusal. Tivael: "We will stop you here today." Bargrum lands the party's answer: "There are people in this world whose lives burn too brightly to be overshadowed by your love. We'll put a stop to you." Nizca, stung — "What would you know of my love, dwarf?" — and tells the tragedy plainly: thousands of years alone in stasis, an ocean now between them and the lover who first showed them the Netherworld's magic, whom they still sense across the sea. Bael: "Then after we take care of you, maybe we'll find them." Nizca: "All the more reason I shall gut you here and now."
  • The Speckle betrayal. Deg cackles — he's had eyes on the party the whole climb: "Had eyes on the back of my head. Or in the back of yours, I should say." Speckle, the party's adopted three-eyed homunculus pet (originally Deg's creation), glows and shatters into the Aeon Stone Swarm. Raylta, immediately: "See — I told you we should have killed it. No, it's not true!"
  • Ferrok dedicates the fight to the fallen: "Remember, everybody, we're doing this for Clinch and Nihkos" — the original party that TPK'd in the Bleeding Palace.
Act IV · Combat · The Shadow Beacon

Kill the Villains, Race the Machine

  • Raylta pre-empts with Oracular Warning (party init + temp HP) — everyone standing on Bael's pre-cast party-wide Death Ward. Nizca opens with Vampiric Exsanguination, a void cone that crit-fails Tivael for 98 — downgraded to a fail and cut to 39 by the Death Ward. It chunks Bargrum (39), Ferrok (39), and Raylta (14). The scare sets the tone.
  • Tivael locks down both bosses at once: Slow on both Deg and Nizca (halving their actions), Synesthesia on both (Clumsy 3 — and, decisively, everything Concealed from them), Dirge for Frightened, Evangelize for Stupefied. That concealment is why Nizca keeps whiffing its Channel Smites on DC-5 flat checks — enough that the DM openly went "salty." Only Bon Mot bounced (Nizca's high Will).
  • Ferrok kills Deg — a Sure Strike + Ignition crit chain (56/64/50) softened the Slowed, Off-Guard Deg ("he's not out yet"), and a plain strike finished him a round later. "With Deg dead, the energies of the beacon start to go haywire."
  • Deg activates the Shadow Beacon as a free action → magical darkness floods the arena and two Shadow Discharge hazards spawn (Fort-save void/cold AoE that intensifies each time a boss falls). The DM reframes the win: the device must be disabled — Crafting, Thievery, or any magic skill, a 2-action check, from anywhere on the map.
  • The party grinds Nizca under focus fire and Bargrum's taunts; Nizca self-heals ~15/round and lands Channel Smite / Shadow Blast (a later escalated discharge crits Tivael for 68, healed immediately), but Bargrum's Shielding-Taunt strike drops Nizca to 0. Nizca uses Spiteful Escape — evaporating into smoke and fusing their soul into the beacon rather than dying. The discharges intensify again.
  • The disable gauntlet. Both discharges need three successes each — Charge A: Bargrum → Bael → Ferrok completes it; Charge B: Tivael (self-Guidance + Arcana) → Bael → Raylta's finish. After a long miss streak ("maybe I'm too stupid to disable it"), Raylta rolls a Guidance-boosted 31 — success. The device combusts in black flame: "the last thing you see is the skull-like face of Nizca screaming as their soul is rent apart with the destruction of the Shadow Beacon."
Act V · RP · Sunrise, Send-offs, and a Phoenix

The First Sun in Months

  • The shadows lift like dissipating fog. Sunlight hits their skin for the first time in months. Bael: "I miss this." Ferrok: "This is what we were fighting for." Bael, seconds later: "nothing is like the sun… I'm going back inside. This is terrible." — Raylta drags Bargrum out into it ("You grumpy old man, come over here").
  • The Lograsi painting. Among Nizca's belongings, a portrait of their lover rendered with obsessive care. The reveal — Lograsi is a figure from Raylta's player's other campaign (Revenge of the Runelords), where that party killed him. His last words there: "I'll be with you soon, Nizca." The tragedy completes itself; the table sits with the bittersweet.
  • The send-offs (each PC, in the months after): Bael — learning his new powers, hunting down his favorite potion seller, enjoying being rich, taking it easy. Bargrum — a copy of the Lograsi sketch to the Pathfinder Society, a report to the Eagle Knights, some graves to visit. Ferrok — drinks to celebrate, then, gladiator by trade, takes up whatever fights come next. Tivael — bard and researcher, he writes a song about the journey and travels to spread it. Raylta — reports to the Bountiful Venture, object-reads the whole Ebon Tower's story before the loot is confiscated, says her farewells, and stays.
  • Raylta is not continuing to the next AP — a new character joins for Ruby Phoenix.
  • The hook. Legend spreads of the heroes who kept the sun from being extinguished. Six months later, letters arrive sealed in wax stamped with a phoenix. "And that is the end of Shades of Blood, everyone. Thank you for playing."

Combat Encounters

Encounter 1 · Viseldron the Fortune Dragon + 4 Animated Treasure Swarms · 5 PCs

Setup

Initiative (Pax's tracker): Tivael → Viseldron → Ferrok → Treasure Swarm → Bael → Bargrum → Treasure Swarm → Raylta → Treasure Swarm → Treasure Swarm. Viseldron — a young-adult emerald Fortune Dragon (spellcaster) hired to guard the ascent: rank-5 Fireball, Shield, Disruptive Breath (force cone), Draconic Frenzy (two claws + tail), Share the Wealth (sheds its treasure in a 30-ft bludgeoning burst), and a reaction Capture Spell (counteract a party spell + regain a slot). Four Animated Treasure Swarms — Blade Storm (piercing/slashing in-space) and Trash the Place (bludgeoning burst).

The fight

  • Round 1. Tivael crit-lands Lingering Composition (4 rounds), Rallying Anthem, and quaffs a Potion of Quickness (Bargrum drinks one too). A swarm delays to act after the dragon; Viseldron opens with a rank-5 Fireball into the bunched party (Tivael 20 / Raylta 35 / Bargrum 40 / Ferrok 40 / Bael 30) and Shields. Swarms Stride in and Blade Storm.
  • Mid. The party focuses the swarms one at a time (Ferrok's Ignition/Shielding Strikes + Bael's Flying-Flame roundup do the bulk). Tivael's Synesthesia lands Clumsy/Off-Guard that tees up Ferrok's 57-damage Ignition Spellstrike ("Because of clumsy") — the fight's biggest hit. Bargrum trips the dragon (Titan Wrestler — Prone, forced Stand), Group-Taunts, aids Commitment to Protection; Bael Demoralizes it; Raylta sustains cold Interstellar Void for Fatigued. The dragon adds a second Fireball, two Disruptive Breaths, and a Capture Spell crit-success that steals back a 5th-rank slot (never spent).
  • Late. Viseldron uses Share the Wealth (treasure spray) and Draconic Frenzy (a claw catches Bael). Ground to single digits under Bael's Thermal Nimbus fire + Ferrok's damage, it falls to Ferrok's plain finishing strike.

Outcome

Dragon and all four swarms destroyed (swarm kills: Bargrum ×1, Ferrok ×1, Bael ×2; dragon: Ferrok). No PC dropped — the two 40-damage Fireballs and relentless Blade Storm chip were the real strain. Loot: the entire Fortune Dragon hoard.

Encounter 2 · Nizca & Deg at the Shadow Beacon · 5 PCs · finale

Setup

Initiative (Pax's tracker): Tivael → Bargrum → Raylta → Bael → Nizca → Ferrok → Deg → Aeon Stone Swarm. (Two Shadow Discharge hazards roll in when Deg activates the beacon and act a round later.) Nizca — Strigoi vampire (they/them): Vampiric Exsanguination (12d6 void cone), Harm, Channel Smite, Shadow Blast, Fast Healing ~15/round, and Spiteful Escape (at 0 HP, fuses into the beacon instead of dying). Deg — Dero inventor: energy-expulsor gauntlet + cannon, Overclocked Blast (8d8+2 line), and the free-action beacon activation. The Aeon Stone Swarm is Speckle, transformed. The Shadow Beacon is the true win condition — disable it before it completes.

The fight

  • Death Ward saves the finale. Nizca's opening Vampiric Exsanguination crit-fails Tivael for 98 — but Bael's pre-cast party-wide Death Ward upgrades it to a fail and cuts it to 39 (Bargrum/Ferrok also 39, Raylta saved for 14). Bargrum, stacked to "resistance 20 from death": "money well spent."
  • Tivael's two-boss lockdown — Slow on both Deg and Nizca, Synesthesia on both (Clumsy 3 + concealment), Dirge (Frightened), Evangelize (Stupefied Nizca). The concealment forces Nizca into DC-5 flat checks it keeps failing → missed Channel Smites (the DM: "this fight's making me salty"). Only Bon Mot bounced.
  • Ferrok kills Deg (crit chain 56/64/50 softened; plain ~24 strike finished). Deg activates the beacon → two Shadow Discharge hazards; the objective becomes the disable checks. Bargrum's Shielding-Taunt strike drops Nizca to 0 → Spiteful Escape into the beacon; discharges escalate (+1d6, +2 DC).
  • The disable gauntlet. Two discharges, three successes each — Charge A: Bargrum (1/3) → Bael (2/3) → Ferrok completes. Charge B: Tivael (1/3, self-Guidance + Arcana) → Bael (2/3) → Raylta's Guidance-boosted 31 finishes it and the whole device. Bael disabled on both charges; Raylta lands the last roll of the campaign.

Outcome

Shadow Beacon destroyed; Nizca's fused soul rent apart with it; Deg dead; the Speckle-swarm cleared. The shadow pall dissolves and the sun returns. Never a true near-TPK — the two-healer economy, Bael's Death Ward, and Bargrum's Intercept kept every PC off the floor; the real threat was the clock, and Tivael's control is what beat it. End of Shades of Blood.

Character Spotlights

Tivael · the Superior Dragon ★

  • The thematic payoff of the whole campaign. Faced with an actual dragon many times his size, the tiny Dragonet ran the bit end-to-end — the melee challenge ("I'm going to show him who's the superior dragon"), the condescending casts ("I am the superior dragon… I shall give you my present"), and the line over its corpse ("Take that, stupid dragon. Just because you're bigger than me doesn't mean you're better").
  • The control engine that decided the finale — not flavor. In the dragon fight his Synesthesia set up Ferrok's 57-damage Ignition Spellstrike ("Because of clumsy"). At the beacon he locked down both bosses at once — Slow on both Deg and Nizca, Synesthesia on both (Clumsy 3 + everything Concealed from them), Dirge, Evangelize.
  • His control is why the DM went "salty." Synesthesia's concealment made Nizca whiff its Channel Smites on DC-5 flat checks — so his cheeky "you failed all my big spells" was a victory lap, not an apology (the boss kept failing the saves against his spells). Only Bon Mot bounced, off Nizca's high Will.
  • Ate the two scariest hits of the night — the 98-damage Vampiric Exsanguination (a crit-fail) and a later 68 discharge crit — surviving the first only because of Bael's pre-cast Death Ward, then kept casting.
  • His send-off is pure Tivael — bard and researcher, he writes a song about the journey and travels to spread it. His finale reflection: "My first finished AP, I guess." He carries on into Ruby Phoenix.

Bargrum · the Load-Bearing Heart

  • The wall, as always — group/Shielding Taunt across both fights, Intercept + Energy Interceptor eating cold and fire for Raylta and Bael, Defensive Advance, Juggernaut Charge, and self-aware guardian comedy ("Sorry if I'm taking everybody else's hits").
  • Improbably tripped a dragon — his Titan Wrestler made it stick, knocking Viseldron Prone and costing it an action. A Tiny dwarf, putting a Fortune Dragon on the floor.
  • Delivered the party's answer to the villain — "There are people in this world whose lives burn too brightly to be overshadowed by your love. We'll put a stop to you." Meeting Nizca's tragedy with a principled refusal rather than a taunt; the sharpest line of the confrontation.
  • The blow that ended Nizca — his Shielding-Taunt strike dropped Nizca to zero (triggering Spiteful Escape), and he opened one of the two beacon charges. His send-off fits the Eagle Knight: a copy of the Lograsi sketch to the Pathfinder Society, a report home, and some graves to visit.

Ferrok · the Finisher of Record

  • Closed the campaign the way he ran it — the deadpan comic who quietly kills everything. "Just a dragon, guys. We're fine" opened the Viseldron fight; his straight sword-strike put the dragon down.
  • The damage that mattered on the clock — self-Haste, Ignition Spellstrikes into a Slowed, Synesthesia'd Deg (his crit chain softened Deg; a plain strike finished him), a Reactive-Strike crit, Force Fang, Emergency Targe, Fire Shield, and repeated Ignition Spellstrikes into Nizca — holding through a cursed miss streak with a flicker of sympathy for the doomed villains ("I feel kind of bad for them now").
  • Best comedy image of the night — Bargrum "swimming through it like Scrooge McDuck" — plus the war-dead dedication ("we're doing this for Clinch and Nihkos") and the quiet beat when the sun returned: "This is what we were fighting for."
  • His send-off is on-brand for the gladiator — drinks to celebrate, then takes up whatever fights come next. He continues into Ruby Phoenix (excited for the new Magus rules).

Bael · the Showman with the Sun in His Hand

  • Opened the last fights and closed a lot of them — the dragon parley's opening line ("This is your hoard, I presume? Distinguished gentleman?"), Thermal Nimbus set to protect and chip, the Flying-Flame roundup on the treasure swarms, and Solar Detonation into the beacon fight ("Since you want to blot out the sun so bad, I guess you want a taste of it").
  • His quiet MVP play was foresight — he spent 900 gold on five Death Ward scrolls and dosed the entire party before the beacon fight, the single reason Tivael and the front line survived Nizca's void alpha strikes (Bargrum: "money well spent").
  • A genuine second healer — Treat Wounds on Tivael, greater healer's gloves on Bargrum, potions passed around ("I'll extend my hand and ask if you want a drink") — and he disabled on both beacon charges, completing one and aiding Raylta's finish.
  • Best comic register at the table — the moment the sun returned he basked, then flipped instantly to "nothing is like the sun… I'm going back inside. This is terrible," needling Bargrum as a "grumpy old man." His send-off: new powers, his favorite potion seller, and being rich.

Raylta · the Heart Who Buried Her Pet

  • Ran the heal economy through both grueling fights — rank 1-4 Heals, Nudge, Quickened-Casting, Cloak of Light as an anti-vampire aura against Nizca, potion patches, Vital Beacon high-fives — while carrying the session's emotional through-line.
  • Grieved Speckle in real time. The pet she adopted turned out to be Deg's spy and became the Aeon Stone Swarm; she mourned it while it tried to kill her ("But they were my friends! We bonded… I learned from the best… He would have wanted this") — a tragicomic beat only she could carry.
  • Sharp tactics under it all ("Wait, Nizca's a vampire, right? So I can use Cloak of Light so they can't get near me") and pure healer comedy — pre-targeting Ferrok "because you get hit so much!", cutting a potion-happy Bargrum off ("you're so addicted, we're cutting you off").
  • Landed the last blow of the campaign. After a run of misses and a declared "too stupid to disable it," she rolled the 31 that destroyed the Shadow Beacon and ended Nizca — the literal final act of Shades of Blood, hers.
  • The campaign's quiet goodbye. Her epilogue — report to the Bountiful Venture, object-read the tower's story before the loot is confiscated, say her farewells, and stay — sends her off as the one player stepping away. A hell of a note to leave on.

What Tivael is thinking about

  • Bael's Death Ward scrolls were the difference. The 98-damage Exsanguination was a crit-fail that would have dropped me; the pre-cast Death Ward upgraded it to a fail and cut it to 39. When a boss telegraphs a void/death alpha strike, buy the scrolls in advance.
  • Clustering nearly cost us again — Viseldron's Fireball caught the whole party bunched up (same lesson as Jubilant's screams and the Sacristan bleed). Spread out against AoE bosses.
  • My control lane won the beacon fight. Synesthesia is the MVP vs a single boss — Clumsy 3 is nice, but the concealment rider is what made Nizca miss (everything is Concealed from the target, so it flat-checks to hit anyone, and Nizca kept failing). Slow landed on both bosses (action denial). Only Bon Mot bounced (Nizca's high Will) — skip it vs high-Will enemies and lead Synesthesia/Slow.
  • The win condition wasn't damage — it was the disable check. When the objective is an object, pivot the whole party to it early rather than tunnel-visioning the boss (the DM literally warned us about boss tunnel vision).
