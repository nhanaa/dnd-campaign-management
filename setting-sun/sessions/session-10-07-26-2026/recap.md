## Session Summary

The team finished the temple and then found out what they had actually signed up for.

Two rooms remained. The garden was a nest of enormous tick swarms — a fight nobody enjoyed, in which Sovael learned the swarms' one weakness was *area and splash damage* and then had to sit on it, because his own party was standing inside the area. The shrine held a smiling old man carved in stone, a heap of treasure, and the **Phoenix Necklace**. Ferrok called it before anyone moved: *"Statues are always trying to murder us."* Sattva respectfully approached anyway, and the statue stood up and drew its staff.

The **Old Man Statue** was the hardest thing the party has fought since Kannitri — AC 37, a fifteen-foot reach, a +31 to hit, and a staff that critted for 86 and then 70. It took nearly twenty minutes of real time and a lot of missing. Sattva's bow went ice-cold for the entire encounter (*"they just kind of plink off"*), Sovael's two rank-5 Thunderstrikes were both saved against, and Clutch spent the fight alternately grabbed and putting a fifteen-foot statue on the ground with critical Trips. **Seva landed the killing blow** with a Deathseeker strike, and the thing went out the way it was built to — a burst of divine light that turned out to be **vitality** damage, harmless to everyone in the room who wasn't undead. Behind it: a spacious pouch, a jade serpent, a ring of swimming, fifteen hundred gold in gems, and the necklace the tournament required. **Level 12.**

Then Ingdani sat them down and explained the qualifying round — the whole thing, in one go, and it is a genuinely different game from a dungeon crawl. Ten silver feathers by the end of day three. Three days on a hex map with a clock that advances ten minutes at a time. Challenges you cannot refuse. Wagers of one to three feathers. Zero feathers means you go home. There are things on this island that will kill you and you are expected to run from them. And the Phoenix Necklace means nobody dies in a tournament bout — but if someone dies to the *wildlife*, bringing them back costs a feather, and the price goes up every time. (Full text below — Pax asked for it as a reference section.)

Day one began at six in the morning with a knock at the door. The **Ahmoza sisters** — twin monks, polite to the point of ceremony — challenged for a single feather at the party's own base and were humbled in two rounds; Ferrok crit Manyala for 130 in a single turn and Sattva put an arrow through her temple. They forfeited with grace. *"I admit, I thought my sister and I could take on anything together."* One hex east, the very first flat check of the day came up hot and produced the **Lightning Callers**, three archers who spent the entire fight kiting backwards and raining 26-damage volleys on the whole party at once. They also forfeited. Feather five.

Reconnoitering the same hex turned up the reason it's called Danger Island: **three gargantuan praying mantises**, twenty-foot reach, speed 50, and a habit of grabbing people and throwing them thirty feet into the air. Round one went badly enough that the party openly discussed running. Then Clutch rolled two natural 20s back to back — *"that was a tide turner"* — and the fight turned into a grind the party won. One hundred platinum, 320 XP, and one hex of a very large island explored.

## ⭐ The Rules of the Qualifying Round

*Delivered by Ingdani at the party's base immediately after the level-up break. Reproduced here as a reference. All quotes are the DM.*

### At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | Hold **10 Silver Feathers** at the end of Day 3 |
| **Starting feathers** | 3 (from the temple) |
| **Duration** | 3 days |
| **Active hours** | 6:00 AM – 10:00 PM (challenges and travel allowed) |
| **Inactive hours** | 10:00 PM – 6:00 AM (no travel, no challenges; shopping only) |
| **Wager per match** | 1 to 3 feathers, negotiable |
| **Losing all feathers** | **Immediate disqualification.** No recovery |
| **Excess feathers** | Anything past 10 is confiscated for a cash prize |
| **Clock** | Advances **10 minutes** per Traverse, Reconnoiter, fight, Treat Wounds cycle, **or Refocus** |
| **Encounter check** | **DC 17 flat check** on every Traverse → you meet another team |
| **XP** | Experience, not milestone. Everyone starts Level 12 at 0 XP |

### The feather economy

> "In order to qualify for the Ruby Phoenix Tournament proper, you must collect 10 Silver Feathers. You have been given three to start. Over the course of the next three days, each of the qualifying teams is free to travel Bonmou. Teams may challenge each other, wagering their Silver Feathers. You may wager from one to three Silver Feathers per match.
>
> Should you reach ten Silver Feathers and hold on to ten Silver Feathers by the end of day three of the qualifying round, you will qualify for the tournament. **If at any point you fall to zero feathers, you will be disqualified and escorted off the island.**
>
> …should you reach 10 Silver Feathers from an early point, it does not guarantee your qualification — for having ten feathers does not exempt you from being challenged by other teams. You might attain ten, but you can still lose feathers at that point. Any feathers you collect past your tenth will be confiscated, but you will be given a cash prize for doing so."

Asked whether hitting zero is instant: **"If you reach zero feathers at any point, you have lost your place in the tournament."**
Ferrok: *"Don't bet all our feathers at once."* — DM: *"I would advise against it."*

### Challenges — you cannot refuse

> "The challenged team must reciprocate the challenge, or automatically forfeit their feathers."

**Sovael**: *"Wait, so we can't refuse a challenge?"* — **"You cannot."**

The two anti-abuse guardrails:
- **You may only fight the same team once per day.** Different teams, as many as you can survive. *"Functionally, it is to prevent a team from being defeated and then being challenged again by the same team."*
  > ⚠️ The DM's first pass on this was *"a team may only engage in combat once per day"* — which would have capped the party at **one fight a day**. **Sovael's follow-up question is what produced the correct rule.** Worth remembering which version is live.
- A team facing back-to-back challenges gets **time to recuperate** between them.
- **A team down to its last single feather may refuse** any challenge.

> "Basically assume a fight's gonna happen if you encounter a team. Because everybody wants your feathers."

### Active and inactive hours

> "Active hours will be from 6am to 10pm each day. During this time, you are free to wander Bonmou, seeking out and challenging other teams or exploring the island at your leisure.
>
> During inactive hours, wandering the island is forbidden, and teams may not challenge one another. In the event you are outside of your home base during inactive hours, we will escort you promptly back."

Functionally: if 10 PM lands while you're out, your day ends where you stand and you're walked home.

### The stone markets

> "This is the stone market. There are several like it on Goka, though outside of this one, you'll need to discover them for yourselves. The stone markets on Bonmou connect directly to the endless market in Goka, and you can use the stone markets to purchase equipment as you see fit.
>
> Should you run into another team at the stone market, you may challenge them, or they may challenge you. But you must allow each team to perform what business they seek to do at the markets before the challenge is initiated."

**Mechanically** — the market counts as a **level 20 settlement**:

| Rarity | Max item level |
|---|---|
| Common | **20** |
| Uncommon | **15** |
| Rare | **10** |

Shopping is available **during inactive hours**, and teams met at the market during inactive hours **cannot be challenged**.

### Dangers and wonders

> "Along Bonmou, there will be plenty of dangers, per the moniker of the island. You will find other teams, of course, but you will also find terrifying and powerful beasts. **You will also find wonders on Bonmou, but I will not spoil the dangers or wonders.**
>
> I will say that where the dangers are concerned, vanquishing certain creatures on Bonmou will also net you gold and platinum, which of course can be spent at the stone market. **Use the island as a chance to grow stronger.**"

*The "wonders" are an unelaborated hook — the party has not found one yet.*

### Exploration — the modified Hexploration rules

> "This chapter will use a modified version of the Hexploration rules… Two actions can be taken in each hex. **Traverse**, which moves you from one hex to another. And **Reconnoiter**, in which you explore a hex. Both activities take 10 minutes, as each hex is only half a mile across.
>
> When traversing from one hex to another, you will roll a **DC 17 flat check**. If you meet the DC, you will encounter another team, and a challenge might be ensued.
>
> In reconnoitering, you might find secrets hidden in that hex — dangerous creatures, treasure, or other items. You won't know until you Reconnoiter."

- **Traversal is adjacency-only** — one hex at a time, no skipping across the map.
- **Reconnoitering is not a roll.** *"You just say you're doing so, and then I'll let you find it."*
- **This is not a dungeon crawl** — rest back to back as often as you like, but every Treat Wounds cycle costs 10 minutes.
- Longer rests (an hour, eight hours) are available and cost a correspondingly massive chunk of the travel day.
- **You will not cover the island.** *"Depending on how well you do… you might only see two-thirds or even a half of it."*

### Fights you are meant to lose

> "Especially at level 12, there are things on the island you cannot beat. There are going to be fights you have no hope of winning and that you have to run from. But, of course, this is by design, and you are accumulating experience… some fights that are impossible now might be very possible on future days.
>
> …It's best that you judge carefully whether you want to surrender. Because, yes, surrendering means you do forfeit some feathers if you're outmatched, but it also means you've got stamina for future fights. If you're guaranteed to lose and fight to the bitter end, well, that is valorous, but it also means you've blown through the majority of your resources for the day."

**The escape mechanic.** Sovael asked whether Recall Knowledge could tell them in advance that something was too dangerous. The DM's ruling was not that RK fails, but that **you don't get the choice** — *"You're going to get into a fight if you encounter something too dangerous right away. Everything that could fight you, you're going to have some combat against."* What you get instead is a way out:

> "If **everybody** on their turn declares that they are contributing to running away, the party can **automatically escape** from a non-tournament fight."

Confirmed later in play: **this still works while a party member is grabbed.**

### The Phoenix Necklace — nobody dies in a bout

> "Fights to the death are prohibited in the Ruby Phoenix Tournament, as is helping yourselves to other combatants' equipment. The Ruby Phoenix Necklace that you have helps prevent this.
>
> **While doing a tournament fight, you cannot die.** Anything that would kill you just reduces you to unconscious. This includes things with death effects. So say you get crit by a disintegrate and reduced to zero HP — you're just rendered unconscious.
>

### What Sovael is thinking about

- **Talent Envy is the best thing he took at 12, and it's free.** It rides on the Lingering Composition Performance check he was already making on round one of every fight — so it is a **free-action Demoralize every single combat, no action cost, no Cursebound cost**. It crit on its debut. This should be the new round-one default and it should target whatever the martials are about to swing at.
