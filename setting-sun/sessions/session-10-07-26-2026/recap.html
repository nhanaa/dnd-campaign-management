Session Summary

The team finished the temple and then found out what they had actually signed up for.

Two rooms remained. The garden was a nest of enormous tick swarms — a fight nobody enjoyed, in which Sovael learned the swarms' one weakness was area and splash damage and then had to sit on it, because his own party was standing inside the area. The shrine held a smiling old man carved in stone, a heap of treasure, and the Phoenix Necklace. Ferrok called it before anyone moved: "Statues are always trying to murder us." Sattva respectfully approached anyway, and the statue stood up and drew its staff.

The Old Man Statue was the hardest thing the party has fought since Kannitri — AC 37, a fifteen-foot reach, a +31 to hit, and a staff that critted for 86 and then 70. Sattva's bow went ice-cold for the entire encounter ("they just kind of plink off"), Sovael's two rank-5 Thunderstrikes were both saved against, and Clutch spent the fight alternately grabbed and putting a fifteen-foot statue on the ground with critical Trips. Seva landed the killing blow — on her first session, on the hardest thing in the temple. Behind it: a spacious pouch, a jade serpent, a ring of swimming, fifteen hundred gold in gems, and the necklace the tournament required. Level 12.

Then Ingdani sat them down and explained the qualifying round — the whole thing, in one go, and it is a genuinely different game from a dungeon crawl. Ten silver feathers by the end of day three. Three days on a hex map with a clock that advances ten minutes at a time. Challenges you cannot refuse. Wagers of one to three feathers. Zero feathers means you go home. There are things on this island that will kill you and you are expected to run from them. And the Phoenix Necklace means nobody dies in a tournament bout — but if someone dies to the wildlife, bringing them back costs a feather, and the price goes up every time.

Day one began at six in the morning with a knock at the door. The Ahmoza sisters — twin monks, polite to the point of ceremony — challenged for a single feather at the party's own base and were humbled in two rounds. They forfeited with grace. One hex east, the very first flat check of the day came up hot and produced the Lightning Callers, three archers who spent the entire fight kiting backwards and raining volleys on the whole party at once. They forfeited too. Feather five.

Reconnoitering the same hex turned up the reason it's called Danger Island: three gargantuan praying mantises, twenty-foot reach, speed 50, and a habit of grabbing people and throwing them thirty feet into the air. Round one went badly enough that the party openly discussed running. Then Clutch rolled two natural 20s back to back — "that was a tide turner" — and the fight turned into a grind the party won. One hundred platinum, 320 XP, and exactly one hex of a very large island explored.

Act VII · Combat · Reconnoiter · Three Deadly Mantises

Why they call it Danger Island

  • "Time to learn why they call it Danger Island… as you watch as not one, not two, but three of the biggest praying mantises you've ever seen come stealthily out of the underbrush." Gargantuan.
  • Sattva rolled a natural 20 on Recall Knowledge (Nature), critically succeeding on three of the four tiers of DC: best save Fortitude, worst save Will, 20-foot reach with the legs and 10 with the mandibles, and a 40-foot vertical leap. His summary: "So anything within 20 feet is danger zone."
  • Round one was a disaster. Mantis B opened on Seva with Sudden Strike, critted for 84, and Clutch ate 77 intercepting — then Intercepted the follow-up too. Mantis A ran 50 feet to the back line to bite Sattva.
  • The party seriously discussed running, and the escape rule was checked live. Ferrok: "I'm wondering if this is not one we should retreat from." Sovael: "The plus 28, I think, is a sign there."   • Clutch's whole Wrestler kit was switched off by size. "These are gargantuan, right? They're four by four? So none of my athletics skills will work." He spent the fight on Taunt, Defensive Advance, Intercept and raw claws — and out-damaged everyone.
  • Both Ferrok and Clutch got Flung thirty feet into the air. Ferrok made the save. Clutch critically failed it — 30 bludgeoning and prone — and erased every point of the damage with Cat Fall. Sattva narrated the landing: "You parry at the last second."
  • Ferrok's escape from a critical Restrain: "Ferrok is going to flex his muscles and try to escape." — "Whoa, what a flex!" And after a mantis whiffed all three strikes: "I Matrix Dodged the whole thing." DM: "Bulls are natural predators for mantises."
  • Aftermath, over three dead gargantuan insects: Sattva: "Anybody up for breakfast?" Seva: "It's cooked fried mantis leg?" … "Yes, actually." This became a five-minute tangent on whether crabs are technically insects.
  • Ingdani paid out: "There are no feathers awarded for surviving such creatures, but defeating them will yield coins… this will be the smallest payment you'll receive for such a feat." — 100 platinum. And 320 XP, awarded only because Sattva stopped the DM to ask about it.

⭐ The Rules of the Qualifying Round

Delivered by Ingdani at the party's base immediately after the level-up break. Reproduced here as a reference.

The feather economy

"In order to qualify for the Ruby Phoenix Tournament proper, you must collect 10 Silver Feathers. You have been given three to start. Over the course of the next three days, each of the qualifying teams is free to travel Bonmou. Teams may challenge each other, wagering their Silver Feathers. You may wager from one to three Silver Feathers per match.

Should you reach ten Silver Feathers and hold on to ten Silver Feathers by the end of day three of the qualifying round, you will qualify for the tournament. If at any point you fall to zero feathers, you will be disqualified and escorted off the island.

…should you reach 10 Silver Feathers from an early point, it does not guarantee your qualification — for having ten feathers does not exempt you from being challenged by other teams. Any feathers you collect past your tenth will be confiscated, but you will be given a cash prize for doing so."

Asked whether hitting zero is instant: "If you reach zero feathers at any point, you have lost your place in the tournament." Ferrok: "Don't bet all our feathers at once." — DM: "I would advise against it."

Challenges — you cannot refuse

"The challenged team must reciprocate the challenge, or automatically forfeit their feathers."

Sovael: "Wait, so we can't refuse a challenge?" — "You cannot."

  • You may only fight the same team once per day. Different teams, as many as you can survive. "Functionally, it is to prevent a team from being defeated and then being challenged again by the same team."
  • A team facing back-to-back challenges gets time to recuperate between them.
  • A team down to its last single feather may refuse any challenge.
⚠️ The DM's first pass on this rule was "a team may only engage in combat once per day" — which would have capped the party at one fight a day. Sovael's follow-up question is what produced the correct rule. Worth remembering which version is live.
"Basically assume a fight's gonna happen if you encounter a team. Because everybody wants your feathers."

Active and inactive hours

"Active hours will be from 6am to 10pm each day. During this time, you are free to wander Bonmou, seeking out and challenging other teams or exploring the island at your leisure.

During inactive hours, wandering the island is forbidden, and teams may not challenge one another. In the event you are outside of your home base during inactive hours, we will escort you promptly back."

Functionally: if 10 PM lands while you're out, your day ends where you stand and you're walked home.

The stone markets

"This is the stone market. There are several like it on Goka, though outside of this one, you'll need to discover them for yourselves. The stone markets on Bonmou connect directly to the endless market in Goka, and you can use the stone markets to purchase equipment as you see fit.

Should you run into another team at the stone market, you may challenge them, or they may challenge you. But you must allow each team to perform what business they seek to do at the markets before the challenge is initiated."

Mechanically the market counts as a level 20 settlement:

Shopping is available during inactive hours, and teams met at the market during inactive hours cannot be challenged.

Dangers and wonders

"Along Bonmou, there will be plenty of dangers, per the moniker of the island. You will find other teams, of course, but you will also find terrifying and powerful beasts. You will also find wonders on Bonmou, but I will not spoil the dangers or wonders.

I will say that where the dangers are concerned, vanquishing certain creatures on Bonmou will also net you gold and platinum, which of course can be spent at the stone market. Use the island as a chance to grow stronger."

The "wonders" are an unelaborated hook — the party has not found one yet.

Exploration — the modified Hexploration rules

"Two actions can be taken in each hex. Traverse, which moves you from one hex to another. And Reconnoiter, in which you explore a hex. Both activities take 10 minutes, as each hex is only half a mile across.

When traversing from one hex to another, you will roll a DC 17 flat check. If you meet the DC, you will encounter another team, and a challenge might be ensued.

In reconnoitering, you might find secrets hidden in that hex — dangerous creatures, treasure, or other items. You won't know until you Reconnoiter."
  • Traversal is adjacency-only — one hex at a time, no skipping across the map.
  • Reconnoitering is not a roll. "You just say you're doing so, and then I'll let you find it."
  • This is not a dungeon crawl — rest back to back as often as you like, but every Treat Wounds cycle costs 10 minutes.
  • You will not cover the island. "Depending on how well you do… you might only see two-thirds or even a half of it."

Fights you are meant to lose

"Especially at level 12, there are things on the island you cannot beat. There are going to be fights you have no hope of winning and that you have to run from. But, of course, this is by design, and you are accumulating experience… some fights that are impossible now might be very possible on future days.

…It's best that you judge carefully whether you want to surrender. Because, yes, surrendering means you do forfeit some feathers if you're outmatched, but it also means you've got stamina for future fights. If you're guaranteed to lose and fight to the bitter end, well, that is valorous, but it also means you've blown through the majority of your resources for the day."

The escape mechanic. Sovael asked whether Recall Knowledge could tell them in advance that something was too dangerous. The ruling was not that RK fails, but that you don't get the choice — "Everything that could fight you, you're going to have some combat against." What you get instead is a way out:

"If everybody on their turn declares that they are contributing to running away, the party can automatically escape from a non-tournament fight."

Confirmed later in play: this still works while a party member is grabbed.

The Phoenix Necklace — nobody dies in a bout

"Fights to the death are prohibited in the Ruby Phoenix Tournament, as is helping yourselves to other combatants' equipment. The Ruby Phoenix Necklace that you have helps prevent this.

While doing a tournament fight, you cannot die. Anything that would kill you just reduces you to unconscious. This includes things with death effects. So say you get crit by a disintegrate and reduced to zero HP, you're just rendered unconscious.

This only applies to tournament fights though, not wildlife encounters."
"On Danger Island your Phoenix Necklace has a further use. You can permanently eliminate one phoenix feather, burning it away. Doing so will resurrect an ally who has died on Bonmou… It's also not infinite, and draws more energy each time. Each subsequent resurrection you do while on Bonmou takes an additional feather. So the first resurrection costs one feather, the second costs two, and so forth."

Guides and enforcers

"I will be with you every step of the way. However, I have been instructed not to interfere in matters where wildlife is concerned, unless you forfeit the tournament. Should you find yourselves in mortal peril, I have been instructed to stand by and watch you die, should you not relinquish your place in the tournament.

However, there are enforcers on the island and more guides like myself. If you find it necessary and offer your forfeit, then we will rush to your aid — but only in that instance.

There are guides like me, one for each team, and there are enforcers across the island, those meant to both enforce the tournament rules and offer a further layer of challenge. They may challenge you as well, requiring that you ante up feathers. Defeating tournament enforcers, or succeeding at whatever challenge they put before you, does not win you feathers, but it does win you gold and platinum.

All of this is meant to ensure that only the best of the best reach the tournament proper."

Wildlife payouts

"There are no feathers awarded for surviving such creatures, but defeating them will yield coins… The amount of coinage you receive is dependent not on how dangerous the creature itself is but how many you have brought down."

Three Deadly Mantises → 100 platinum, described as "the smallest payment you'll receive for such a feat."

Rules confirmed in play

  • Pre-buffing is banned. Exploration actions (Hunt Prey) are fine; Haste is not.
  • A bout ends the moment a team forfeits, typically declared at the top of a round. Both bouts this session ended that way.
  • Wagers are negotiable before the bout begins.
  • There is no handout. Seva asked. "There is not a solid list, no. But if anyone has any questions at any time, you can ask me."
