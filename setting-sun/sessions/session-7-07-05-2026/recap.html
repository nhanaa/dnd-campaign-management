This was the long climb — six fights up the last habitable floors of the Ebon Tower, the architecture itself curdling into the Netherworld as they rose (the honest Azlanti stone giving way to black-mist glass, every cast shadow deepening, every lamp dimming). It opened quietly: cleanup from the velstrac massacre below, one more room of half-broken captives staged for rescue, a reading nook where they could look out through the tower wall and see the world beneath them swallowed in supernatural dark. Then the fights started and mostly didn't stop.

The floor's guardians were a rogue's gallery of "not again" enemies. First, two winged vampire-Strix — Okoa and Rukh — dropped screeching into a sea-battered reception hall; Ferrok cracked Okoa in half and Bael's fire ran down the fleeing Rukh. Then a hall of paintings breathed out five Greater Shadows that spent the fight literally stealing the party's shadows (Ferrok's, Raylta's, Bael's, and Bargrum's all went — only Tivael's "evasive dragon" kept his), until Tivael answered with a spell he'd been itching to cast — a three-action Spiritual Torrent cone, "Kaboom" — that vaporized one shadow and softened two more, and Raylta punched the one that stole her shadow to death with Holy Light. A statue of the vampire god Nin split in two and lunged as a pair of Stone Bulwarks; a wall-mural of gems tore itself down as three Aeon Stone Swarms and became the worst grind of the night — every swarm exploded when it died, so the party "kept losing when they won."

Between the gem swarm and the finish, the session's one real conversation: the party found Anareth Zorlan, the engineer who'd seemingly absconded from the Warden's floor — revealed here to have been captured by Degg and forced to build spare parts. He handed them blueprints for safely dismantling the Shadow Beacon (and a key past a paralysis trap), plus the reveal that landed: the beacon is a rushed prototype. Degg and Nizca skipped the tests, so only the surrounding seas are shrouded so far — but given time it will tie all of Golarion to the Netherworld, permanently. Two more fights closed the floor: a pair of masochist velstrac Sacristans who begged to be hurt (and whom Bargrum met with the session's best bars), and finally a river of blood that congealed into four self-healing Bulbous Blood Oozes. The party ended battered, rich, and one short floor from the top of the world.

Timeline

Act I · Explore · Cleanup and the Climb

Freeing Captives, Reading the Dark

  • The session picked up right after the velstrac boss floor (Jubilant, last session). A rough start on hero-point-fueled healing rolls (Bael: "failed again — good start") gave way to methodical cleanup: one more room of captives — not as broken as the mirror-hall victims — staged with food, water, and directions for later evacuation.
  • Exploring the last rooms of the floor: a small torture chamber (Tivael, deadpan: "This is a torture chamber."), coffin rooms already emptied (the vampires the party killed), and a Nin shrine/office full of pre-Earthfall prison records. Loot: a Spiritual Warhorn (Bael took it), and a legend-boosting relic that needed a sanctified wielder — nobody qualified.
  • A reading nook with windows let them look out the tower wall: below, the world was twisted and obscured by supernatural darkness; above, everything vanished into shadow. The higher they climbed, the more the Netherworld bled through — the Azlanti stone literally replaced by clear glass full of dense inky mist.
  • Bargrum, ever the quartermaster, appraised a rug ("It really does tie the room together, you know?") and floated the post-campaign dream: "If we build an adventuring team house after all this, we're going to be set." Ferrok: "Manacles hanging from everything."
Act II · Combat · Okoa & Rukh, the Vampire-Strix

Two Winged Shadows in a Sea-Battered Hall

  • In a salt-encrusted reception hall slick with sea spray near the ocean surface, a screech echoed and two shadow-clad figures dropped in on enormous wings — shortbows, claws, burning red glares, no words. Bargrum tried anyway ("Let's talk about this."); they answered with weapons.
  • Tivael's Recall Knowledge named them: Strix — winged humanoids native to Golarion — but infused with Netherworld energy and turned into undead vampires. High physical resistance; even Raylta's adamantine barely bit. Both self-healed ~10 HP a turn.
  • Okoa was the melee flyer — claws, a Vicious Flurry, and a Strafing Strike that turned her into a winged shade and carved the party line. Rukh was the caster — Synesthesia on Bargrum (Clumsy 3 / Dazzled) and a rank-4 Grim Tendrils void-line that bled most of the party.
  • Bargrum anchored: a Juggernaut Charge that wrapped the silver monkey's fist around a Strix's neck to drag it, Group/Shielding Taunts, and two Intercepts in one round ("you learn to block things with your face as well as your chest"). His clutch play was a Commitment to Protection call-out — "Watch out for her claws, Ferrok" — reading the danger and handing Ferrok a +1 AC edge (Raylta: "That call-out was clutch.").
  • Ferrok killed Okoa with an Ignition Spellstrike run — "that guy's dead. Dead, dead." / Raylta: "Why are you so strong?" Bael's Flying Flame finished Rukh — "that is enough to break her down."
  • Tivael controlled with a rank-3 Fear (both frightened), Befuddle, and Frostbite; Raylta chipped with Vitality Lash (vitality vs. undead) and kept Bael topped with Nudge; Bael used Flinging Updraft and blasts. Afterward, a callback to last session's spared poppet ("We did a good thing, you guys.").
Act III · Combat · The Painting Shadows

Five Greater Shadows, and the Theft of Shadows

  • A U-shaped hall lined with paintings; a staircase ascended at the center. As the party entered, five Greater Shadows peeled out of the paintings. Ferrok: "Painting monsters. I pull out my eraser." (DM: "I think that would just make the eraser very dirty.")
  • Incorporeal — resistant to everything but force, ghost touch, and vitality — and armed with Steal Shadow: every solid hit enfeebled a PC by ripping their shadow off. They stole Ferrok's ("I look so weird walking around with no shadows"), Raylta's ("How dare you? It's mine!"), Bael's, and Bargrum's — only Tivael, the self-styled "evasive dragon," kept his.
  • Tivael ran the fight. First a spell he'd wanted to cast for ages — Wall of Mirrors — which both punished anything that struck it and became difficult terrain that bogged the incorporeal shadows down. Then, once the party clocked that vitality was the lever (Bael called it first — "I think vitality might hurt them"), Tivael dropped a three-action Spiritual Torrent vitality cone. "Kaboom." It destroyed Greater Shadow B (84 damage) and gutted two others.
  • Bael opened with his Crescent Aeon Stone's Moonbeam — a 100-foot silver line ("Big Beam Attack! His Crescent Stone" / Raylta, dry: "It's so lame.") — then cleared A, C, and D with Flying Flame and blasts, plus a literal fire-fist punch from his new handwraps.
  • Raylta took her revenge personally: Holy Light killed the shadow that stole her shadow — "I just punch him with a holy light. Where's your holy light?" Ferrok's Force Fang and Ignition Spellstrikes chipped through the incorporeal resistance; Bargrum reactive-struck and taunted.
  • Out of combat, Bargrum found the perfect reference: "Has anybody seen the old, old Peter Pan? Bargrum's gonna take his shadow and nail it back on."
Act IV · Explore · The Scrying Circle & the Golden Goose

Two Temptations, Both Declined

  • A room held a heavily-warded scrying circle. Ferrok RK'd it — a scrying tool, but trapped; a botched attempt to use it deals "pretty intense mental damage." Tempting to spy on the floors above, but nobody was skilled enough to risk it. Left alone.
  • A lounge cabinet held a cursed golden goose — feed it the bloody heart of a non-evil creature and wait, and it lays a gold egg. The catch spooked them. Raylta, gleefully: "I kind of want to see if it's the 12th egg or not. I love gambling." Ferrok: "It's freaking cursed." They left it.
  • A magically locked door (detect magic flagged it) got noted for later — it would turn out to be the Warden's old chambers, and Anareth would hand over the key.
Act V · Combat · The Stone Bulwarks

The Statue of Nin, Split in Two

  • A bare lounge held a single statue of the vampiric god Nin (Bael: "He just looks like a dude." / Ferrok: "We are all set to start a vampire cult after this." / Raylta: "We're trying to destroy the cult."). As they stepped in, the statue split in two and both halves lunged — a pair of Stone Bulwarks.
  • Mindless, with heavy physical resistance bypassed only by adamantine. Ferrok ran the party's signature combo — Incendiary Fog placed over the enemies, then Bael's Thermal Nimbus ignited it into a fireball — though one Bulwark crit-saved the blast (a soul-crushing nat-20 that shrugged off the fire; only its twin took the brunt).
  • Bargrum anchored: double Shielding Taunts pinning both statues (forcing a string of taunt-misses) and a 38-damage Intercept eaten for Raylta.
  • Raylta's adamantine Needle Darts crit shattered one Bulwark ("Off the board!"); Tivael's Electric Arc finished the other, with Ferrok crowing "No mercy — all crits!" over the lucky streak.
  • The statue left behind a copy of The Immaculate Solution (Nin's scripture — the party had sold their first copy). Raylta: "Are we spreading the religion accidentally?"
  • The northern library held a dozen glowing hovering notes; Raylta's Occultism cracked them: schematics for the Shadow Beacon. Confirmed — the device is still a prototype, which is why only this region is shadowed rather than all of Golarion. Bael, unbothered: "If there isn't a flaw, we'll make one."
Act VI · Combat · The Aeon Stone Swarms

"We're Not Rocks, We're Gems"

  • An office's east wall was a mural of tiny polished stones. As they searched, the mural tore itself off the wall and became three Aeon Stone Swarms — "we're not rocks, we're gems" (Ferrok, theorizing: "they crave our blood. That's all I can think.").
  • The ugliest grind of the session. The swarms flew into the party's squares and hammered them with Battering Orbit AoE and launched-stone strikes — and each swarm, when killed, detonated with Stone Shatter (a 30-foot burst that caught allies too). Raylta ate a 56 (DM: "a headshot and a nutshot simultaneously"): "Why are we still losing when we win?"
  • The three swarms split one kill apiece: Bargrum (Silver Monkey's Fist) → A, Bael (crit blast) → B, and Tivael — after switching to hit their weaker Fortitude save ("these guys keep saving my spells, so I'll do a 40 instead") — closed the last with a Frostbite.
  • Bael's Four Winds scattered the party out of the swarms, then his signature Brace turn: "Instead of dodging, I just Brace, and everything that flies into my face I catch in my hand and burn." Ferrok ground away through a genuinely cursed streak — Enfeebled 2 the whole fight from the Greater Shadows — while Bargrum interposed his body over Raylta to eat AoE ("he just dives and rolls and blocks both hits") until his Intercepts ran dry.
  • The room's Aeon Stones became loot — a black pearl (+4 vs. mental / reflects, to Bargrum), a delaying stone (Ferrok), and a stasis stone — and the party's absurd wealth became a running bit (Bael: "I only have moderates. Look at all the money I'm carrying around." / Raylta: "I didn't know we were made out of money.").
Act VII · RP · Anareth Zorlan & the Beacon Blueprints

The Captured Engineer, and the Way to Break the Sun-Killer

  • In a converted workshop, the party found Anareth Zorlan — the same engineer they'd tried to free two floors down (the one who seemingly absconded back in S4). The reframe: he hadn't run off — Degg's people captured him after the party left and put him to work on spare parts for the beacon. Ferrok, deadpan: "There — you listen to us this time and actually run."
  • Asked to help, he refused to fight ("I'm no warrior. Or mage. I'm an inventor.") — but offered the campaign's most important handoff. The engine is unstable: Degg and Nizca rushed the activation and skipped tests, which is the only reason the shadow stopped at the surrounding seas. Given time, "Galarian will become tied to the Netherworld. Permanently."
  • He gave them blueprints to safely dismantle the beacon (ten minutes of study → mechanical circumstance bonuses to next session's disable checks) with a blunt warning: fail the disable and "big boom" — a mass release of Netherworld energy. He also slid them the enchanted key to the Warden's old chambers (it disables a paralysis trap), then took the cleared stairs out. Raylta: "See? I knew you weren't such a bad guy."
  • The Strix journal (Okoa and Rukh's room) filled in the rest: the two were exiled Strix who joined Nizca hoping their own Netherworld ties would buy them belonging. It also confirmed the upper tower's backstory — when the beacon first darkened the sky, it drew the velstracs in; Nizca struck an uneasy alliance with them, but once he and Degg got consumed with repairs up top, the velstracs turned on everyone below — captives and vampires alike.
Act VIII · Combat · The Sacristans

The Choir That Wanted to Be Hurt

  • Behind the Warden's key: a chamber preserved since Earthfall, full of luxury and red embroidery — occupied by two velstrac Sacristans still grieving Jubilant. "You brought an end to her. Please show us how." They were masochists — they wanted to be hit — which flipped the fight's whole emotional register.
  • Tivael controlled hard: a four-round Dirge of Doom, a Laughing Fit that stuck a Sacristan unable to use reactions ("No reaction strike for you, bitch."), a second Laughing Fit sustained, Evangelize (Stupefied), Demoralize, Telekinetic Projectile, and Electric Arc. He paid for standing too close — a 56-damage hit and stacked persistent bleed he never once saved against ("I'm just going to bleed to death").
  • Bargrum got the session's sharpest exchange. As he deliberately Intercepted hit after hit — and Shield-Blocked a Sacristan's nat-20 crit — a Sacristan admired him: "I was unfamiliar with your game. You've my respect." Bargrum, spitting blood: "We're nothing alike" — and, on the same theme: "You're taking hits for the love of the pain. I'm shielding crits for the love of the game." Raylta nailed it: "He's right — he's not one of you! … I'm a fucking freak."
  • Tivael's Electric Arc killed Sacristan B (the DM: "B had exactly nine hit points — that is enough"). The last Sacristan came down to Raylta, who did not want the kill — "I hope I miss. I hope I miss." — because giving a masochist his death felt like giving him what he wanted. Her Needle Darts connected anyway, and with cruel irony crit: "the darts just shatter the glass behind him as he tumbles out the window into the ocean."
Act IX · Combat · The Bulbous Blood Oozes (finale)

A River of Blood, and the Wall That Wouldn't Fall

  • Clearing the last rooms (an extravagant bath, a fine bedroom — "this is where the rich people who own the prison operate"), the party turned back toward the stairs and a massive river of blood poured down and congealed into four Bulbous Blood Oozes.
  • Mindless, immune to crits, high HP, and armed with Siphon Vitality — a 15-foot pulse that damaged everyone nearby and healed the ooze (Raylta, exasperated: "Man, they get healed."). Another grind, and another where the party defaulted to damage before Recall Knowledge (their reflex saves turned out to be the weak point — learned late).
  • Bargrum was the immovable object — Group/Shielding Taunts holding three oozes at once, Shield Blocks, and Intercepts soaking Siphon Vitality until the party begged him to stop ("Bargrum, no, you can't!" / Bargrum: "I can take it."). The DM's verdict: "If you ever GM with a Guardian in the party, the best way to damage the Guardian is to attack literally everybody else."
  • Tivael's Electric Arc did the quiet closing work — finishing two of the four oozes on their weak reflex — while Ferrok re-ran the Incendiary Fog / Nimbus combo with Bael and put the final blade on the last ooze. Bael flung Raylta clear on a gust and crit a Battle Medicine (40) on Bargrum. Raylta's rank-5 Divine Immolation ("That's a divine fireball") aimed at their weak reflex — but both targets shrugged it off on a nat-19 and nat-20 ("very unfortunate"), the exact luck that defined the grind.
  • The last ooze fell around the 3h50m mark, and the DM called it there. Next week: the top of the tower, and the end of Shades of Blood.

Combat Encounters

Encounter 1 · Okoa & Rukh (vampire-Strix) · 5 PCs

Setup

Initiative (Pax's tracker): Bargrum → Bael → Raylta → Ferrok → Tivael → Rukh → Okoa. Two winged Strix infused with Netherworld energy and turned to undead vampires — high physical resistance (adamantine only partly bypassed; silver/force/vitality are the levers), both self-heal ~10 HP/turn. Okoa — melee flyer: claws, Vicious Flurry, Strafing Strike (semi-incorporeal line dash). Rukh — caster: Synesthesia (Clumsy 3 / Dazzled on Bargrum), rank-4 Grim Tendrils (void line + persistent bleed).

The fight

  • The party won initiative decisively (the Strix rolled a 1 and a 3). Bargrum opened Defensive Advance, discovered the deep physical resistance, and set up Group/Shielding Taunts, a Juggernaut Charge drag, and two Intercepts in one round.
  • Tivael RK'd them (Strix / undead vampires), stuck a Stupefied on one, then a rank-3 Fear (both frightened), Befuddle, and Frostbite. Bael used Flinging Updraft (wind-jump on Ferrok), blasts, and Flying Flame. Raylta worked Vitality Lash, Divine Lance, and a Nudge for Bael.
  • Ferrok killed Okoa — an Ignition Spellstrike run that near-maxed ("Dead, dead."). Bael's Flying Flame finished Rukh ("that is enough to break her down"). Bargrum's Commitment-to-Protection call-out fed Ferrok a +1 AC bonus vs. the Strix (Raylta: "clutch").

Outcome

Both Strix down; the party callbacked the spared poppet ("we did a good thing"). Loot: +2 handwraps of mighty blows, +1 striking astral shortbow, +1 resilient leather armor, +1 resilient scroll robes, scroll of Grim Tendrils.

Encounter 2 · Five Greater Shadows · 5 PCs

Setup

Initiative (Pax's tracker): Bargrum → Ferrok → Tivael → Raylta → Greater Shadow B → Greater Shadow A → Bael → Greater Shadow D → Greater Shadow C → Greater Shadow E. Greater Shadows ×5 (A–E), incorporeal (resist all but force / ghost touch / vitality), manifesting from the paintings. Steal Shadow — solid hits enfeeble by tearing off the target's shadow (Ferrok, Raylta, Bael, and Bargrum all lost theirs; only Tivael kept his).

The fight

  • Tivael opened with Wall of Mirrors (damages attackers + difficult terrain vs. the incorporeal shadows), then a three-action Spiritual Torrent vitality cone that destroyed Greater Shadow B (84) and softened C and E.
  • Bael led with the Crescent Aeon Stone Moonbeam (silver line) and cleared shadows with Flying Flame and blasts (finishing A, C, and D) plus a fire-fist punch from his new handwraps. Raylta — after Bael flagged vitality as the lever — killed Greater Shadow E with Holy Light (the one that had stolen her shadow).
  • Ferrok chipped with Force Fang and Ignition Spellstrikes against heavy incorporeal resistance; Bargrum reactive-struck and taunted.

Outcome

All five destroyed; kill split Tivael (B), Bael (A/C/D), Raylta (E). Loot: none carried; the paintings themselves were worth some coin.

Encounter 4 · Three Aeon Stone Swarms · 5 PCs

Initiative (Pax's tracker): Aeon Stone Swarm A → Bael → Aeon Stone Swarm B → Tivael → Ferrok → Aeon Stone Swarm C → Raylta → Bargrum. Aeon Stone Swarms ×3 — the office's gem mural come alive. Encounter 5 · Two Sacristans (velstracs) · 5 PCs

Setup

Initiative (Pax's tracker): Tivael → Bael → Bargrum → Sacristan B → Sacristan A → Ferrok → Raylta. Sacristans ×2 — blindfolded velstrac casters (Chilling Darkness cold rays, barbed-chain strikes + persistent bleed, rank-3 Fear). Masochists — they invite being hit; silver stops their Fast Healing.

Both Sacristans dead; the masochists never got the satisfaction they wanted. Loot: none carried (velstracs).

Encounter 6 · Four Bulbous Blood Oozes · 5 PCs · finale

Setup

Initiative (Pax's tracker): Bulbous Blood Ooze D → Bargrum → Ferrok → Bael → BBO A → BBO C → Raylta → Tivael → BBO B. Bulbous Blood Oozes ×4 — a river of blood congealed. Mindless, immune to crits, high HP, weak reflex (found late), with Siphon Vitality (15-foot pulse that damages the party and heals the ooze) and Pseudopod strikes.

The fight

  • Bargrum held three oozes at a time on Group/Shielding Taunts and soaked Siphon Vitality until the party protested ("Bargrum, no, you can't!" / Bargrum: "I can take it.").
  • Tivael's Electric Arc finished two of the four oozes on their weak reflex (plus Flurry of Claws). Ferrok re-ran the Incendiary Fog / Nimbus combo with Bael, added Force Fang and Ignition Spellstrikes, and put the final blade on the last ooze.

Outcome

The last ooze fell and the session ended out of combat. No level-up — the party stayed at level 10, one short floor from Nizca. Loot: none noted.

Character Spotlights

Tivael · the Spell He'd Been Saving ★

  • The single most satisfying moment of the night — against the incorporeal Greater Shadows he finally uncorked Wall of Mirrors (a spell he flagged out loud as one he'd "been itching to do"), then a three-action Spiritual Torrent cone ("Kaboom") that erased one shadow and cracked two more open.
  • The party's most reliable engine across the grinds — the Recall Knowledge anchor (he ID'd the Strix as Netherworld-infused vampires) and the dependable AoE (Flurry of Claws + Electric Arc, every single fight).
  • The finisher more often than a glance suggests — dropped the last Aeon Stone Swarm with a Frostbite (after switching to its weaker save), closed two of the four Blood Oozes with Electric Arc, and killed Sacristan B on the nose (exactly 9 HP left).
  • A full control board vs. the Sacristans — a four-round Dirge, a Laughing Fit that locked a velstrac out of its reactions ("No reaction strike for you, bitch"), Evangelize, and Demoralize.
  • His one running cost was self-inflicted geometry — clustered too close to the Sacristans, ate a 56 and a stack of persistent bleed, and never once saved, which he turned into the session's best self-deprecating bit ("I literally bled to death every time"). Also the loot pragmatist: "Money is money."

Bargrum · "I Can Do This All Day"

  • Everywhere at once — the double-Intercept discovery in the Strix fight ("you learn to block things with your face as well as your chest"), the monkey's-fist-around-the-neck drag, the body-block Interpose that ate Aeon-swarm AoE meant for Raylta, and enough taunt-and-soak that the DM conceded "the best way to damage the Guardian is to attack literally everybody else."
  • The session's sharpest exchange — against the masochist Sacristan who offered him "my respect" for taking hits on purpose, Bargrum drew the exact line between a tank and a fanatic: "We're nothing alike" and, better, "You're taking hits for the love of the pain. I'm shielding crits for the love of the game."
  • The dry heart out of combat — the rug that "really ties the room together," the dream of a fully-decorated adventuring-team house, and the Peter Pan fix for everyone's stolen shadows ("take his shadow and nail it back on").
  • Clutch reads — his Commitment to Protection call-out in the Strix fight was, by Raylta's own word, "clutch," and he ended the Aeon-swarm grind having cracked a swarm himself with the silver fist.

Ferrok · the Hammer

  • The kill leader — combat thesis stated outright ("when you have a hammer, everything is a nail. So Ignition Spellstrike"), and backed it: cracked Okoa the vampire-Strix in half ("Dead, dead."), ran the Incendiary Fog setups Bael detonated in the Bulwark and ooze fights, and put the final blade on the last Blood Ooze while bleeding profusely.
  • Held through a cursed streak — Enfeebled 2 the whole Aeon-swarm fight ("you're killing me"), he stayed the finisher of record anyway.
  • Carried the dungeon-crawl humor — theorizing that denied gems "crave our blood," proposing the party "start a vampire cult after this" in front of a Nin idol, and mocking the beacon apocalypse ("won't be able to tan anymore").
  • Best line of the night — reacting to a Greater Shadow stealing his shadow with pure minotaur exasperation: "I'm already weak, you fool. I look so weird walking around with no shadows."
  • Threw the velstracs' ethos back at them — "It hurts so good." — and cut the oozes to the bone: "If it bleeds, we can kill it."

Bael · Big Beam, Bigger Wallet
