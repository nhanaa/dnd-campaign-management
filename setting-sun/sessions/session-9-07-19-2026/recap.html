Session Summary

A new adventure path, a new party, and a new island. Six months after the Ebon Tower, phoenix-sealed invitations gathered five strangers onto a magically propelled ship bound for the Isle of Bonmou — the once-a-decade Ruby Phoenix tournament, hosted this year by Hao Jin herself, returned from a death everyone had assumed was permanent. Thirty-two hand-picked teams. Only eight advance. And this particular team is the wildcard: not a veteran adventuring company but a set of individual fighters Hao Jin assembled because they were each worthy and each teamless.

The day began with a handshake and ended with a temple full of dead things. A rival team — Tino's Toughest — flagged the party down on the beach and asked for a friendly spar, and it turned into a genuine brawl: three rounds, both sides trading knockdowns, Alastor and Clutch on the sand for the visitors and Takatorra flattened by Ferrok at the bell. Partway through, Alastor's player took a bad run of dice, went down, and left the call, Foundry, and the DM's Discord permanently. The remaining four shrugged, agreed the crabs got him, and carried on as a party of four.

Their assigned base turned out to be a derelict Temple of Irori — which meant something particular to Savatt, an Irorian pilgrim who spent the session treating the dungeon as a desecrated holy site rather than a loot pile. "Make it safe for habitation" meant clearing it room by room: two Goliath Spiders whose venom put Ferrok on the floor, four incorporeal ghost monks, a catoblepas rotting in the atrium pond, six sand-elemental monks that sheathed themselves in electricity-resistant glass, a one-shot sutra trap, and finally Kannitri — the grief-stricken ghost of an Irorian nun who mistook the party for the raiders who massacred her temple centuries ago.

Six encounters in one session, three silver feathers recovered, the Phoenix Necklace still missing, and a level promised for next week.

The Session

Act I · Roleplay · Session Zero and the Voyage

Five strangers on a boat

  • Cody framed the campaign: ~20 sessions through the end of 2026, Book 1 extended and Book 2 shortened, and a one-line pitch — "This is the Shonen Anime Tournament campaign." Expect combat to dominate every session.
  • The premise: Hao Jin returned to Goka, resumed control of her own tournament, cut the field to 32 hand-selected teams, and replaced the opening bracket with a three-day free-for-all on the Isle of Bonmou — nicknamed Danger Island. Top eight advance.
  • Alastor introduced himself as a finalist from last decade's tournament — middle-aged now, estranged from his old team, given one more shot with strangers. "He's reached that ripe old middle age."
  • Ferrok is from Absalom and fights in the Irorium; he was travelling to Tian Xia to train early for this very tournament when the Ebon Tower sidetracked him.
  • Savatt is a pilgrim and wanderer from Janja — unfamous, long-lived, a follower of Irori, reading his invitation as a sign pointing toward enlightenment.
  • Sovael is Tivael's cousin, a devotee of Hao Jin who came to meet her and to find the cousin who promised to be here. His verdict on Tivael: "Not as skilled as me, of course." Ferrok's counter: "He did run from a dragon this one time."
  • Clutch's cousin Clinch competed ten years ago on a separate team. Both lost. Clutch has spent the decade since tearing through legal and underground fight circuits — and he is here to compete and to find him.
  • The first dark note: Ferrok recognised Clutch's face, couldn't place it, then landed on it — "the only Kholo I've seen was a dead one in Ebon Tower." Clutch let it pass without comment.
Act II · Social · Landfall and the Ceremony

Welcome to Danger Island

  • Shifu Shou Noh, emissary of the tournament and guardian of Hao Jin's legacy, welcomed the 32 teams from the docks as the waves themselves seemed to pause.
  • The task: each team is assigned a site somewhere on the island, occupied by something dangerous. Clear it, claim the Phoenix Necklace and three silver feathers hidden there, then acquire seven more over three days.
  • Ingdani, a Tian human woman in her mid-thirties, introduced herself as the team's handler — and was very clear about her limits: "Should you get yourselves in over your heads, my orders are to stand back and, if necessary, watch you all perish."
  • Tino's Toughest approached on the beach: Tino Tong (Tian monk, Irori's symbol tattooed on his chest), Takatorra (a near-seven-foot Tengu, they/them), Ji-yook (a Kitsune fighting bare-clawed), and Yabin the Just (a Nagaji caster).
  • Savatt made the first real connection — bowing to Tino as a fellow Irorian: "I see you're a follower as well."
  • Yabin singled out Ferrok as an Ebon Tower survivor and asked to discuss the Netherworld later — a scholar's interest in the last campaign.
  • Clutch opened negotiations by tilting his muzzle to the sky and growling "I must break you" at a man who had just offered a friendly warm-up. Sovael, immediately: "Maybe not that." Takatorra, delighted: "That a threat or a promise, big guy?"
Act III · Combat · The Temple of Irori

Make it safe for habitation

  • Ingdani led the party inland to a temple wrapped in enormous webs, a fully grown triceratops cocooned and writhing against the northern wall. Clutch, dryly: "Anything that keeps a triceratops as a snack is probably going to be pretty big."
  • She smiled, leapt several dozen feet backward into the treeline, and called: "Welcome to your base. Your first task is to make it safe for habitation." Two Goliath Spiders descended.
  • Inside, paintings of a faceless man undergoing trials — wading a river, balancing atop a tree, meditating in a storm. The figure was Irori. The tournament had quartered Savatt in a ruined temple to his own god.
  • The training courtyard held a 30-foot pagoda covered in hundreds of carved names — a memorial for all who served the temple, not only its battle dead — and four Irorian ghost monks rose from the sand.
  • Savatt asked to clean the temple rather than strip it, and pressed the DM on why an Irori site would be haunted — establishing that there had been violence here, and that the scattered weapons marked a battle rather than a ritual.
  • The dormitory gave up a holy rune, a Ring of Climbing, a Wand of Cleanse Affliction, and ~400 gp in opals — and the session's best running joke, Clutch turning the ring down on behalf of his dead cousin.
  • The mess hall was searched because Clutch insisted people always drop things while eating. He was right: a +2 Striking Astral Hook Sword, Greater Holy Prayer Beads, a fire-resistance potion, and a vial of endless water.
  • The atrium pond — dark, fouled water around a stone platform — opened its eyes. Sovael: "It's the weirdest temple I've ever been to."
Act IV · Mystery · Feathers and the Broken Rebus

"I am sorry. We could not stop them."

  • First silver feather recovered from the sand chamber, after six sand-elemental monks rose out of the coloured dunes to defend it.
  • A prayer chamber of candles, bells, and singing bowls held the second — and a one-shot sutra hazard: a spectral monk appeared, recited a sutra, filled the room with kinetic energy, and vanished.
  • Savatt recited the sutra back at it: "One must know the teachings of Irori." The DM confirmed a complex hazard with a single trigger — "Nothing happens. That was the threat."
  • A chamber with a eucalyptus tree growing up through a ceiling window, an old skeleton slumped at its base, and the third feather among the bark scraps.
  • Beside the skeleton sat Kannitri, the ghost of an Irorian monk woman, dazed and staring past them. She stood and said: "I am sorry. We could not stop them."
  • Asked what happened, she named them: "The broken Rebus. They came, they showed no mercy." Then her eyes cleared into rage — "They've come back!" — and she attacked.
  • Savatt tried to spare her, delaying his turn to check whether she would respond to his Irorian holy symbol — "maybe we can resolve this without destroying her." The game gave him nothing.
  • She was destroyed, fading "with this horribly sad expression on her face, this guilt-ridden look of anguish" — and the DM noted her presence is still there. She's gone, but not completely.

  • Yabin retreated and answered with a rank-6 Arrow Salvo — Sovael 27, Clutch 21, Ferrok 27. Clutch shield-blocked.
  • Ji-yook used Skirmish Strike to step-and-strike, clawing Clutch and then Ferrok. Clutch Intercepted the blow meant for Ferrok and offered him a free 10-ft reposition; Ferrok declined to stay on the offensive.
  • Tino charged Alastor with a Flurry of Blows using Inner Upheaval, choosing force — both strikes hit and combined for 34 and 38.
  • Savatt ran to Alastor, laid a hand on his back to "align your chi" and healed him 36, then Hunt Preyed Takatorra.
  • Alastor entered Clinging Shadows Stance and spent the encounter's only hero point on a Combat Grab that landed and held Tino, dealing Shadow Grasp damage (19).
  • Ferrok opened with Sure Strike into an Ignition Spellstrike on Ji-yook — she Nimble Dodged and it missed by a hair. "Now Ferrok's sad."
  • Clutch ran Defensive Advance for 28 into Ji-yook — and Tino answered with Retributive Strike, the Irori tattoo flaring on his own chest, granting Ji-yook resistance 11 and cutting the hit to 16. Clutch followed with a MAP claw strike and grabbed Ji-yook, 6 shock splashing onto Takatorra.

Round 2

  • Sovael cast a three-action rank-6 Blazing Bolt — Tino 36, Takatorra 40, third ray at Ji-yook wide — and triggered Foretell Harm as a free action, accepting Cursebound and 1 persistent fire per round from here on. Tino took 12 more at the top of his turn.
  • Yabin dropped a Fireball on four of the party. Clutch Intercepted for Ferrok; Ferrok used his cloak reaction for +1 to the save and fire resistance 5, taking 23.
  • Ji-yook, still grabbed, feinted Ferrok successfully and hit him with a Foxfire Blast that would have critted him off-guard for 43 — Emergency Targe downgraded it to a normal hit.
  • Tino hit Alastor with a second Inner Upheaval for 105 and dropped him. The DM apologised on the spot: "I didn't mean to pick on you that hard, I'm sorry."
  • Alastor's player left the call here. Savatt covered the gap — a Hunted Shot at Takatorra for 27 and 13, then hands on Ferrok for 45.
  • Ferrok answered with a Force Fang and an Ignition Spellstrike on Takatorra for 76 — the biggest single hit of the fight, plus persistent fire. "Takatorra gets rocked by that one."
  • Clutch used Group Taunt via Shielding Taunt on Ji-yook, Tino, and Takatorra, then a MAP Combat Grab to maintain his hold.

Round 3

  • Sovael used Elf Step to disengage — prompting Ferrok's "You don't look like an elf" — weighed a rank-6 breath weapon, and stood down when the DM warned this was only the first fight of the day, settling for Electric Arc.
  • Yabin cast Phantasmal Killer on Clutch. Clutch failed — damage and frightened 2 — but his Black Pearl Aeon Stone reflected a copy back on a +30 counteract, and Yabin failed his own spell, taking 27 and frightened 2. The stone survived its flat check. A 2,000 gp consumable, spent to make an enemy caster share his condition.
  • Tino flanked and dropped Clutch with 64 — "a liver punch that sends you to your knees."
  • Savatt put his bow away and punched Takatorra "right in the beak" for 42, then kept punching once he learned Clutch would be revived at the bell.
  • Takatorra used Blade Barrage on Savatt — three strikes, two landing, inflicting persistent bleed and off-guard.
  • Ferrok closed the spar by putting Takatorra on the sand, then landed a second swing on Ji-yook.
  • Ingdani raised her hands: "That is quite enough. Well fought, everyone."

Aftermath — handshakes all round; Ferrok helped Takatorra up, Savatt helped Clutch. Ingdani noted this had been suggested as a sparring match "and yet, it seems martial artists just can't help themselves." Unprompted, the DM told Ferrok that each of Tino's Toughest struck him as as powerful as Nizca.

  • Savatt opened by Hunt Preying Spider A and casting Gravity Weapon — the campaign's first focus spell — then landing both arrows of a Hunted Shot (31, 27). Sovael, baffled: "Wait, how do you do two hits?" Savatt: "It's Legolas. Just Ranger things."
  • Ferrok became the venom sponge immediately — bites for 15, 20, 24, 22, 34, stacking slowed 1 → slowed 2. He burned the fight's only hero point on a save, and at one point simply drank a potion while prone: "That's all I can do."

  • Ferrok went down after a 76-damage bite — unconscious, dying 3, still saving against venom. He raised the idea of his delaying Aeon Stone and the DM talked him out of it; he never used it. What saved him was Sovael's rank-3 Heal. "I'm alive."
  • Clutch took a 72-damage nat-20 bite and chose to eat it rather than break his shield, then waved off Savatt's offer to block for him: "Cody never goes for me. He goes for you all to hit." The DM agreed — "that is the best way to hit a guardian."
  • Savatt doubled as second healer — healer's gloves on Ferrok (27, 30, 45), Guidance on Sovael, and Elf Step to pull out of reach. He and Clutch also caught that Shielding Taunt and Doctor's Visitation are both flourishes and can't be combined.
  • Goliath Spider A died between Clutch's Snagging Strike crit ("Single digits") and Savatt's follow-up — the DM never announced the kill, so credit is unresolved.
  • Ferrok killed Goliath Spider B from the floor — a Battle Medicine on Clutch, then a swing from prone that critted: "you just spear this thing from underneath, with this spray of flames and ichor, it dies."

Aftermath — Ferrok stood up and vomited poison, then asked Savatt whether he could be healed by exploding, "I knew a guy that did this once, and it really worked well." Savatt, deadpan: "I could try blocking your meridian so your chi explodes, but I don't think that's healthy."

The Synesthesia window, turn by turn

Four turns fell inside it. Ferrok spent his entire turn on Haste and Arcane Cascade — "that's all my actions" — and made no attack at all. Clutch taunted, ran Defensive Advance, swung and missed. Kannitri's own three-action One Inch Punch missed — the DM said outright it was because she was clumsy. Savatt wasted one action on a redundant Recall Knowledge the DM shut down, but then landed both Hunted Shot arrows for 13 and 17.

Three of the four turns used the window. Ferrok's was the one that didn't.

  • Sovael's Whispers of Weakness identified Fortitude as her weakest save, and he immediately followed with Spirit Blast — 16d6 spirit, basic Fortitude, DC 31. The sequencing was his sharpest play of the fight; the dice were not. She succeeded on the save and he rolled 34 on 16d6 (average 56), so it landed for only 17.
  • Ferrok opened his real offense with a Blink Charge teleport-strike (22), then cycled Spellstrikes including a Disintegrate Spellstrike that missed. He finished the night five attacks missed and out of hero points: "I'm not having luck. Unfortunate rolls today."
  • Clutch tried repeatedly to trip her and failed, then went back for more on principle: "Must go for Redemption." His Astral rune let him Combat Grab a ghost. He correctly declined one Intercept after working out that moving would break the grab — but made another, eating 32 for Savatt, and took 60 more before the end.
  • Corrupting Gaze stupefied Savatt for a full minute, breaking his spellcasting for the rest of the fight — flat check after flat check failing to cast Shield. He stayed on his bow and kept hitting, including a nat 20 for 28. "I keep trying to pray, but I fumble my words every time."
  • Sovael kept the party standing — Ferrok took 101 through a 7-point shield block, and Sovael answered with a rank-5 Heal for 67. His Demoralize failed. Late on he burned Cursebound once more, then landed a Telekinetic Projectile for 16: "Hey, finally."
  • Ferrok stepped forward to flank and swung for the last 20. She faded with "this horribly sad expression on her face, this guilt-ridden look of anguish."

Aftermath — the DM noted her presence is still in the room. "She's gone, but not completely." The skeleton at the tree's base remains unexamined.

Character Spotlight

Sovael · the Burning Pearl

The team's only healer, only buffer, only debuffer, only counteract, and its Recall Knowledge lane — running all of it at once, all session, while on fire.

  • He took the burn five times. Every Whispers of Weakness costs a Cursebound stack and a point of persistent fire per round; he paid it in five of six fights, plus a sixth stack from Foretell Harm in the spar. A Tiny dragonet hovering over the scrum, setting himself alight to tell everyone else where to hit.
  • He is the reason the ghost monk fight was clean — RK established worst-save Fortitude, he got a ruling that the monks had no reactions, repositioned freely on it, and dropped a Divine Wrath (Fortitude, spirit, enemies-only) that destroyed Monk C outright. Identify, match the save, pick the ally-safe burst.
  • His best mechanical read was recognising a dead end — Whispers revealed Will on the spider, which meant his Shadow Signet (Fort/Reflex only) was useless. He took the +2 status bonus instead and landed Fire Ray anyway.
  • He converted a self-inflicted burn straight into a kill on the sand monks — Cursebound purely for the attack bonus, then a crit: "Double that. Shatters in your glass."
  • He kept Ferrok alive twice — a rank-3 Heal off the floor mid-spider-fight, and a rank-5 Heal for 67 after Ferrok ate 101 through a shield block.
  • In character he is a delight and slightly a menace — a devotee who came to meet Hao Jin, a cousin who introduced Tivael to the table purely to rank himself above him, a tiny dragon explaining Elf Step with "I had a lot of elf best friends growing up," and the designated straight man to Clutch: "Maybe not that."

Ferrok the Unyielding · the Load-Bearing Minotaur

The only survivor of the old campaign, and the party's lightning rod exactly as he always was — and the session's kill leader.

  • Everything in the temple went for him: the spiders put him unconscious and dying, the catoblepas critted him, the sand monks blinded him, Kannitri hit him for 101 through a shield block.
  • Kills: Takatorra in the spar, Goliath Spider B, Ghost Monks A and B, the catoblepas, at least one sand monk while blind, and Kannitri.
  • He kept fighting from the floor — unconscious and dying 3 from venom, he came back up and killed Spider B with a crit from prone.
  • He fought an entire stretch blind, eating flat checks on every swing, connecting anyway, then casting Glass Shield because "I have no idea what's going on around me."
  • His most knowingly reckless call paid off anyway — Chain Lightning into a room of monks whose armour triggers on electricity. He flagged the risk first, hit all six for 247 total, and handed the survivors resistance in the same breath. "I was just happy to use it."
  • He carries the campaign's memory — the only one who can speak to the Ebon Tower, the one Yabin wanted to interrogate about it, and the one who told Clutch, without quite realising, that the only Kholo he'd ever seen was a dead one in that tower.
  • He was the most gracious person at the table about Alastor's exit — "low rolls are part of the game, it happens" — and then wrote the eulogy anyway.

Savatt · the Pilgrim Come Home

A wanderer who is not famous and has never competed — and then the tournament quartered him in a ruined temple to his own god.

  • He greeted Tino as a fellow Irorian before a punch was thrown — a bow and "I see you're a follower as well" — the first real connection anyone on either team made.
  • He wanted to clean the temple, not loot it. He asked why an Irori site would be haunted, read the hundreds of names on the memorial pagoda looking for ones he knew, and asked whether monks had been massacred here.
  • He recited the sutra back at the trap: "One must know the teachings of Irori."
  • He tried to spare Kannitri — delaying his turn and spending an action checking whether she'd respond to his holy symbol, "maybe we can resolve this without destroying her." The game gave him nothing, and he had to help kill one of his own faith's dead.

What Sovael is thinking about

  • Synesthesia's success/failure split is about duration, not effects. This is the correction worth internalising: a success still applies Dazzled, Clumsy 3, and the concealment rider (everything Concealed to her, flat check DC 5) — it just lasts one round instead of one minute. The scroll wasn't wasted on a weak outcome; it bought a full-strength round. The lesson is about the clock, not the effect.
  • The window was mostly used, and the one miss was Ferrok's. Three of the four turns inside it did something: Clutch swung (missed), Kannitri's One Inch Punch missed because of the Clumsy, and Savatt landed both arrows for 13 and 17. Ferrok spent the entire round on Haste and Arcane Cascade and never attacked. That's the actual complaint — and it's a communication problem, not a Ferrok problem. When a consumable buys a window, say out loud what it bought and how long it lasts before the next person acts. This party has never fought together before and cannot yet read each other's setups.
  • Whispers of Weakness cost six Cursebound stacks this session (five Whispers + one Foretell Harm), each a point of persistent fire per round. It does not compete for the focus pool — it's a Cursebound action, not a focus spell — so the real constraint is HP and the fire-resistance suppression, not actions. At 151 HP that's affordable, but six is a lot more than it felt like in the moment. Refocusing between rooms is for Lingering Composition and Fire Ray; the Cursebound bill is separate and paid in blood.
  • Whispers is worth taking for the +2 alone. The sand monk kill proves it: no useful information, just the attack bonus, straight into a crit. That reframes it from an RK action to an attack buff that happens to also scout.
  • Divine Wrath is the best spell in the book for this party. Enemies-only means no friendly-fire discipline with four melee bodies in the room. It killed a ghost monk outright and needed no ally saves. Lead with it over Fireball once the party has closed.
  • Match the spell to the revealed save — and check the item actually helps. The spider taught this the hard way: Whispers said Will, and the Shadow Signet only converts to Fort or Reflex, so the Signet did nothing and the +2 status bonus was the real payload. Against Kannitri it went right: Fortitude revealed → Spirit Blast (Fortitude) one action later.
  • The sole-healer problem is real but survivable. Savatt is a genuine second healer (healer's gloves, Battle Medicine, Guidance) and Ferrok has Battle Medicine too — better coverage than the dynamics predicted. Ask whether Seva brings any.
  • Ferrok is the aggro magnet exactly as before — down once, 101 through a shield block, blinded, critted, poisoned. Beseech the Sphinx and Vital Beacon should default to him.
  • Being blinded by a sand monk crit was a warning. A Tiny caster hovering near the scrum still eats crits. Permanent flight and Elf Step exist; use them earlier.
