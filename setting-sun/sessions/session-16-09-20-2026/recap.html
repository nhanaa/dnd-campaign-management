Session Summary

The Nameless qualified. The session opened on the cliffhanger from S15 — a mob of eliminated fighters hammering on the Ruby Palace gates, screaming for Hao Jin to answer for the island she'd sent them to — and it ended a day later with the party three-for-three in the Ruby Phoenix exhibition, in front of the Empress of Goka. In between: ten silver feathers turned in, an infinite buffet, the final eight teams named, and Hao Jin raising every beast on Bonmou from the dead with a flick of her hand.

The gate mob called themselves the Fallen Moon — not a team but a collection of them, ten fighters deep, who turned on the Nameless the moment someone pointed out that "the gate opens for the feathers, nothing more" and "bet you they've got some on them." Clutch answered the ultimatum in kind — "I would say the same to you. If you value your life, turn away" — and then three more voices came out of the wind behind the party. Ran-to of the Lightkeepers ("Bunch of sore fucking losers. Didn't we beat half of you once already?"), Mafika Ayuwari of the Speakers to the Wind, and Tino of Tino's Toughest all fell in beside them. It was the first fight of the campaign the party did not have to win alone, and it showed: of the ten Fallen Moon, the allied NPCs put down six. Tino alone opened with a 130-damage Inner Upheaval flurry and killed three, which prompted Seva's dry "Wait, we're kind of getting shown up, team."

The kill list undersold what actually happened. Sovael did 739 of the fight's 2,430 damage — thirty percent, more than any allied NPC and more than the other four PCs combined. His opener was Courageous Anthem, Lingering Composition, a Talent Envy Demoralize off the same Performance check, then an eighth-rank Divine Armageddon across nine of the ten: six failures, two successes, and a critical failure that took the Sneak for 132. Six of the ten were at or below half health before most of those NPC kills landed. Ferrok answered with a Sure Strike Beheading Buzz Saw Spellstrike — a critical hit plus a doubled 48 on a natural-1 Reflex crit-fail, 176 of the swordsman's 270 hit points in one action — though Sovael caught that the decapitation was a second save, and the man survived it. Ferrok then ate a Telekinetic Bombardment, went prone, and dropped that same swordsman from the dirt: "Well, I've fought on the ground before."

Afterward came the part of the session that will be remembered longer than the fight. Sattva's Risky Surgery went catastrophically, repeatedly wrong outside the gates — ninety seconds of stabbing Ferrok and Sovael while trying to heal them. Ferrok: "Why did you just stab me?" Sattva: "Stop moving so much." The joke has a real mechanical spine: he failed three straight DC-40 Treat Wounds, drawing blood each time and healing nothing, then gave up and dropped to DC 30 — "Okay, fine. I'll stop using my experimental methods. I'll just massage you. Here, a deep tissue" — and immediately critically succeeded twice, for 56 and 51 across the party. Clutch delivered the verdict: "I don't think we needed the deep tissue massages."

At the gate stood a Tian woman in violet robes with vermilion hair, who apologised for the ambush — "I was about to step in and deal with the Fallen Moon myself, but at a point it seemed you were all enjoying yourselves" — collected ten silver feathers, and opened the Ruby Palace. Sovael did not play it cool. "I'm a big fan. Can I get an autograph?" — then realised he had nothing to sign, pulled out a spell scroll, and got Hao Jin's signature on it: "I'll treasure this scroll and I'll never cast it." Ferrok, the only one at the table who speaks Tien, answered her in it. Seva, a beat late: "Oh, it is her! Oh my gosh, I bow."

Inside: Ingdani's farewell after three days of guiding them, a suite with its own hot spring, and a few hours to decompress. Sattva took a sauna and stepped straight out into the snow to meditate. Ferrok worked on his forms and drafted a speech. Sovael worked on the song he owed Sattva from S13. Clutch produced a threadbare doll, sat facing a corner, and practised saying "I must break you." Then a feast that Cody described as "a scene out of Dragon Ball Z" — an infinite, self-replenishing buffet where the roast duck respawned the instant someone carried it off. Sovael, watching it: "This could have fed my whole village. Not that I had one, anyway."

Hao Jin named the final eight — the Arms of Balance, the Biting Roses (Grandfather Mantis included; Sovael: "Yep, that Mantis"), the Lightkeepers, the Speakers to the Wind, the Steps of the Sun, Tino's Toughest, Winter's Roar, and the Nameless last. The room turned and openly sized them up, because word had spread that these were the ones who killed Jaiban. Then she walked to a balcony, let vermilion flame run down her arm, and threw a phoenix out over the island — and every beast the teams had killed on Bonmou roared back to life. "Well then, dessert anyone?" Sovael went for dessert; Clutch went for both — "This means all the monsters have revived. And Clutch will turn to the party and say, let's go again."

The tournament proper is a double-elimination bracket — winners' and losers' — so a team must lose twice to go out. Before that, one more evening: an exhibition in front of the sponsors, including Nai Yan Fei, Empress of Goka, personal guest of the Ruby Phoenix. Four bouts per team, three against opponents of Hao Jin's choosing and one against another team. Clutch asked when they'd get to fight the dignitaries. Sattva had to explain: "They fight in a different way. They fight with words."

Bout one was the Empress's own personal guard — five Butterfly Blades, identically armed twin-blade duellists with Parry, feints, mobility strides and Reactive Strikes. It ran thirty-eight minutes and cost the party a body: the Blades swarmed Clutch and put him down. He had opened by howling into Animal Form for the crowd, which dropped his AC from 41 to 33 — eight points — and which he took anyway: "It looks cool, and it increases size and reach." Sattva landed a clean 100-damage crit; Seva sank her dagger into Clutch's enormous shadow, drew it out as a greatsword (Inscribe Shadow Pamor) and asked the dice "Please hit. I really want to impress people"; Sovael's rank-6 Divine Wrath finished one outright. Clutch got up on a Kip Up, killed another, then grappled a third and Whirling Threw him out of the ring purely for style. Cody: "Just so you know, it's not a ring out kind of thing." Seva: "We're here for flair." The fifth Blade was still standing at 77 hit points when the bout was called. Afterwards Sattva, having concealed an arrow in a high five for a Battle Medicine that failed and healed nothing: "I hope the Empress didn't see me stabbing you." Sovael: "I think she saw everything."

Bout two was a pair of Rune Giants, level 16, 330 HP each, AC 38, with 20-foot-reach greatswords and Invoke Rune — a 30-foot electricity cone that also charges the sword with 3d6 fire. Ferrok marked the moment with the speech of the session, bellowed to the crowd before initiative: "We are the Nameless, and though we don't have names yet, I am Ferrok the Unyielding. We have Seva the Shade, Clutch the Rampart, Sattva the Unerring, and Sovael the Cryptic. We will win this tournament and earn true names." Sovael, flatly: "I was never the cryptic, though. I'm always very transparent." Ferrok, unbothered: "He didn't get any feedback from you guys on those. He doesn't say."

The giants were immune to fire, which blanked Ferrok's Ignition Spellstrike and taxed the Flaming rune on every swing that landed; Sovael started to send a Fire Ray into one before Cody stopped him at the declaration. What decided it was a sixth-rank Roaring Applause that both giants failed on a natural 2 and a natural 3. The half that mattered most was the quiet half: rune giants carry a bonus reaction each turn usable only for Reactive Strike, and those reactions had already taken 85 HP off the party in round one alone. After the applause landed, they got none for the rest of the fight — across at least five repositions through 20-foot reach. The loud half was the manipulate rider: the giants had to clap on their own turns, which Sovael flagged for the table and then narrated — "Clap, clap, clap for the audience." That bought four provoked Strikes for 72, one of which was Seva's natural-20 crit that killed Rune Giant B on its own turn-start, before it ever acted. Cody: "No one's ever failed it until now is the thing." Clutch tripped and grappled both giants off Legendary Athletics, and Ferrok killed the other. Sovael finished on 16 of 188 hit points, having lost 114 in a single enemy turn — his Cursebound flames suppress his own fire resistance, so every point of the giants' Rune of Flames landed: "I'm still alive. I'm still standing. And I'm burning as well."

Bout three was a Clockwork Dragon and two Clockwork Choppers, and it was the hardest fight of the night by a distance — roughly 1,530 damage into the party across six rounds. The dragon had AC 42, 400 HP, a 120-foot fly speed, Draconic Frenzy, physical resistance 15 that only adamantine bypassed, and a 20d6 Breathe Oil cone it got back on a fixed two-round timer rather than a recharge roll. It fired that breath three times — 67, 79 and 76. The lever was electricity, weakness 15: Sovael's opening Chain Lightning arced Chopper A → Dragon → Chopper B for 33 / 51 / 33 and exposed it on the first action of the fight. Sattva then led the damage with 328 of the party's 1,000.

The fight was carried on the defensive end, and the single best number in the session is Clutch's: he absorbed 776 damage, and 552 of it was aimed at somebody else. Eight Intercept Attacks, three party-wide Taunts, four Battle Medicines, Paragon's Guard the whole way, and one of those Battle Medicines rerolled with his last hero point to stay upright. Sovael, by contrast, finished on 131 taken — because Clutch ate a 158-damage crit-failed breath and a 48 on his behalf. Before accepting one of those Intercepts, Sovael stopped to ask: "Do you have HP, brother?"

Ferrok was reduced to 0 by the third oil breath and sprang back up on It's Not Over at exactly 25 hit points, having spent the previous five minutes insisting to a sceptical table that he could come back to life ("No, seriously, I can. I have the feat for it"). Sattva went scroll-first — sixth, sixth and seventh-rank Heals out of his retrieval belt ("These are cheap at this point. We're living rich") — shot Chopper B's head off with a critical Hunted Shot, and closed by putting two arrows in the dragon's eyes for what Cody could only call "insane." Seva killed Chopper A and hero-pointed a Comet Charge Spellstrike for 57. Ferrok put the dragon down with a Live Wire Spellstrike for 82 into a construct sitting on 74.

A small, good moment near the end: Sovael cast Incarnate Skeletal Giant and Sattva asked whether it was Jaiban's skeleton. Seva: "Jaiban's not dead!" Sovael: "Oh yeah, it can't be Jaiban." The phoenix had put him back that morning.

Three bouts, three wins. Cody: "Exhibition number three, you are victorious." Seva: "Hopefully the last." Cody: "Well, no, that's four." Seva: "There's more?" — the fourth bout, team versus team, is still owed.

Character Spotlight

Sovael · the Flaming Pearl

  • Talked to his hero and completely lost his composure. Meeting Hao Jin — the immortal sorcerer whose tournament he has structured his entire life around, and whose fanaticism is literally his background — he opened with "I'm a big fan. Can I get an autograph?", realised he had nothing to sign, dug a spell scroll out of his belt, and got it signed: "I'll treasure this scroll and I'll never cast it." Seva bowed and Ferrok offered his chest, but Sovael is the one who treated her as a celebrity rather than a host.
  • Ran the fight-winning play of the exhibition. The sixth-rank Roaring Applause that both Rune Giants failed on a natural 2 and 3 shut off their bonus Reactive Strikes for three straight rounds — reactions that had cost the party 85 HP in round one — and the manipulate rider forced them to clap into four more Strikes, one of which killed Rune Giant B before it acted. "Clap, clap, clap for the audience."
  • Probed for weakness with damage. With no Recall Knowledge on the table, his opening Chain Lightning against three unknown constructs doubled as a diagnostic and found the electricity weakness on the first action of the fight.
  • Did 739 of the 2,430 damage at the gate — thirty percent, more than any allied NPC and more than the other four PCs combined — off a single eighth-rank Divine Armageddon that caught nine bodies before the melee closed.
  • Lost a rules argument gracefully. He cited Pathfinder Society errata on Foretell Harm riding an area spell and offered a link; Cody ruled against it. Sovael took it in one line — "Okay, you can run it that way, then" — retargeted and moved on without costing the table a minute.
  • Kept checking on people. Offered to burn a spell reducing Seva's Drained before the DM waived it for free; asked Clutch "Do you have HP, brother?" before accepting an Intercept that would have cost him nothing to take silently.
  • Comedy in a very dry register. At the infinite buffet: "This could have fed my whole village. Not that I had one, anyway." On the party's history of fleeing monsters: "It's called fighting smart." On Ferrok's improvised title for him: "I was never the cryptic, though. I'm always very transparent."
  • Paid the fire tax, and took the hit for it. He reached for a Fire Ray against a fire-immune Rune Giant and Cody stopped him at the declaration. He finished that fight on 16 of 188 hit points — 114 lost in one enemy turn, because Cursebound suppresses his own fire resistance. "I'm still alive. I'm still standing. And I'm burning as well."

Ferrok · the Unyielding

  • Gave the team its names. Seconds before two Rune Giants stepped out of a crimson portal, in front of the Empress of Goka, he stopped and made a speech naming every member of the Nameless and staking the team's identity on winning. Nobody asked him to — and he had told the table he was drafting it eighty-six minutes earlier, in the palace hot spring. Only the titles were improvised. When it landed flat, he committed harder: "He didn't get any feedback from you guys on those. He doesn't say."
  • The gladiator is not a costume. He rolled Performance for initiative with an audience bonus, asked "I assume I have an audience for this", upgraded to gleaming Dawnsilver plate for the exhibition, and picked Goring Charge and Comet Charge over better options on the honest grounds that they look cool. Between bouts, speculating about what Hao Jin would send out next — "Bring out the lions" — he landed the thesis: "I'm a gladiator. I build all my life up to this point."
  • Spent the session being right about his own feat. He told the table repeatedly, to open scepticism, that he could come back from the dead. "No, seriously, I can. I have the feat for it." Five minutes later the Clockwork Dragon's oil breath reduced him to 0 and It's Not Over put him straight back on his feet at 25 HP.
  • Top damage against the Butterfly Blades (~313) and the finisher on the two biggest single targets of the night — Rune Giant A and the Clockwork Dragon — plus a Fallen Moon swordsman he dropped from prone off a Beheading Buzz Saw Spellstrike that took 176 of the man's 270 hit points in one action.
  • Adapted off his signature fast. Ignition Spellstrike is his whole thesis and the Rune Giants were immune to fire. He swore once, said "Better to know now," and switched to Live Wire for the rest of the night — which is what killed Rune Giant A and, two hours later, the Clockwork Dragon.
  • The only one who speaks Tien. At the gate he answered the Ruby Phoenix herself in her own language, and it was Ferrok who told Mafika where the Nameless had come from — which is how the party learned their own story had travelled.
  • Best indignation of the night, after the Beheading Buzz Saw very nearly took a man's head off and the man hit him back: "That's rude. I just tried to cut his head off."

Clutch · the Rampart

  • Spoke first and set the tone. The mob at the gate told the party to hand over their feathers and walk away. Clutch turned it around without raising his voice: "I would say the same to you. If you value your life, turn away."
  • Absorbed 776 damage in a single fight against the Clockwork Dragon — and 395 more from the Butterfly Blades. 552 of that 776 was damage aimed at someone else; only 224 was ever pointed at him. Eight Intercepts, four Battle Medicines, and his last hero point spent on one of them — not to do something impressive, but to stay upright long enough to keep intercepting.
  • Chose flair over survivability on purpose. Howling into Animal Form for the crowd dropped his AC from 41 to 33 — eight points — in a fight where five duellists were already swarming him. It is the whole reason he absorbed 395 and went down. He kept it: "It looks cool, and it increases size and reach." Later he grappled a Butterfly Blade and Whirling Threw him out of the arena. Cody warned him it wasn't a ring-out. He did it anyway.
  • Tripped and grappled two level-16 Rune Giants off Legendary Athletics — a critical-success Trip and a natural-20 Combat Grab — and hero-pointed a natural 1 into a natural 19 to land the grab that pinned one until it died.
  • The doll. Given a luxury suite with a private hot spring and a few hours to decompress, Clutch produced a threadbare doll, sat facing a corner, and rehearsed: "I must break you. I must break you."
  • Genuinely funny about being a Kholo among diplomats. On the visiting sponsors: "When do we get to fight them?" … "Oh, I thought everyone was here to fight." On the dragon's resistance 15, which only adamantine cut through: "I got to shop wherever Wolverine shops."

What Sovael is thinking about

  • Roaring Applause is the single best spell he owns against a reaction-heavy opponent — but for the boring half, not the flashy one. The manipulate rider is the fun part and it bought 72 damage; the reaction denial bought more. The giants' bonus Reactive Strikes took 85 HP off the party in round one and then nothing at all for three rounds. Both halves are real and both are repeatable — this is the third time the combo has paid (the Duneshaker Solifugid ate 141 damage the same way in S14). Against the tournament teams, stripping reactions from up to ten enemies is worth more than any damage spell in the repertoire.
  • Whispers of Weakness cannot solve the fire problem. It returns weaknesses and the lowest save — never immunities — and he did cast it first against the Rune Giants; it could not have surfaced the immunity. The real lesson is narrower: against an unknown opponent, spend the cheap probe before the focus point. Chain Lightning found the constructs' electricity weakness on action one in bout 3. A cantrip or a small AoE is a better opening question than a 16d6 Fire Ray, because an Oracle of Flames whose entire signature is fire will reach for it by reflex — and two of the three exhibition opponents punished exactly that.
  • Energy Aegis paid for itself — on him alone. It is touch, 1 creature, so the break-time cast covered Sovael and nobody else. Rune Giant A's Invoke Rune rolled 47 electricity: Seva took the full 47, Sattva 23, and Sovael 42. Keep casting it before every bout — and note that protecting anyone else costs another minute-long casting each.
  • Divine Armageddon is the right opener against a crowd and the wrong one against a party. It caught nine bodies at the gate because the allied NPCs hadn't closed yet. In a four-on-five team bout there will be no such window — Divine Wrath stays the only enemies-only burst.
  • The exhibition is an audition, not a bracket. Hao Jin said outright that the people watching are potential patrons and that the bouts have no bearing on seeding. The value of a Whirling Throw out of the ring or a howl into Animal Form is real here in a way it will not be next week.
  • The party is running hot on consumables. Sattva burned three healing scrolls in the last fight alone and called them cheap. Seva's Lucky Number let her save a hero point in bout 1 — Clutch has one too, rolled at 9.
  • Clutch took 776 damage in one fight and 552 of it was aimed at somebody else. The Intercept umbrella is the only reason Sovael finished that fight on 131 instead of on the floor. Staying inside his reach is not optional any more; it is the position.
  • He still owes Sattva a song. Promised in S13 for the cave worm, worked on in the hot spring, not finished.
