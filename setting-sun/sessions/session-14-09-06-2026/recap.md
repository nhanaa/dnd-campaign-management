## Session Summary

**They walked in holding all ten feathers and lost one in the first eleven minutes.** The Nameless had spent S13 clawing to the qualifying number; S14 opened with a troupe stepping out of the trees, asking pleasantly how the day was treating them, and then taking a feather off them in two rounds. It was the party's first real thrashing in a long time, and nobody at the table pretended otherwise.

**The Biting Roses** were not a grudge match or a rival with history — they were four strangers who were simply better on the day. Yarrika Mulandez opened with **Phantasmal Calamity** into the whole party; Lantondo, a **Wyrwood**, followed with a fifth-rank Fireball and then a **Frigid Flurry that critically failed Ferrok for 93 out of 84 hit points**, putting him on the floor; and **Grandfather Mantis** pounced on Seva and **critically hit her twice in one turn** — 92 on a natural 14 that beat her AC by eleven, then 58 on a natural 20 — leaving her restrained, blinded and dying. Sovael's rank-5 Heal got her up once and Clutch's Battle Medicine got her up again. Then Grandfather Mantis came for Sovael, hit for 40, grabbed him, hit again for 43, and **picked him up and started eating him.** *"I'm sorry, guys. I'm sorry. It can't be."*

The arithmetic is the whole story: across roughly two rounds the party put **122 damage** into about 1,270 hit points of enemies and absorbed **792**. They never landed a single point on **Yarrika or Artus**, who finished the fight untouched at full health. They conceded. There is no single voice on the tape calling it — the concession sits inside one un-diarized crosstalk blob — so it reads as what it was: everyone arriving at the same conclusion at once.

Then the Biting Roses did something that reframed the whole loss. Yarrika called her mantis off — ***"Grandfather Mantis. That is not food. Let them go"*** — and **healed the party she had just beaten**, apologising for the cone. Artus said *"well fought."* And then, turning her prize over in her hand, she asked: ***"Wait, what did we want these for again?"*** Lantondo had to explain that ten feathers is how you enter the tournament. *"Oh, that's what we're doing! I thought you just liked them because they were shiny."* She skipped away with her team trailing after her. **Sovael**, watching her go: ***"I love her."*** **Sattva**: *"At least they're friendly."* Ninety seconds before any of this, unprompted, Sovael had warned her about something dangerous on the southern side of the island.

The post-mortem that followed is the best conversation of the night, and it's a genuine disagreement rather than a group shrug. Sovael argued the shape of the loss was positional — *"I really think, Clutch, you should stick more to your Seva and me at the beginning, because I feel like you charging in kind of made us a bit too exposed"* — and Clutch answered with the actual bind he was in: ***"If I stay back, the mage kills us. If I move forward, the mage kills us."*** Seva took her share (*"that's my bad… I was just, like, away, so I couldn't react"*), the DM called it *"a damned-if-you-do, damned-if-you-don't situation with how the initiative and casters lined up,"* and Sattva landed it: ***"double crits and failing all saves, which was just unlucky. So it's fine. You live and you learn."*** Ferrok, drily: ***"We haven't been soundly thrashed in a while, so…"***

**Then they went and won five straight.** Forty-five minutes later the **Trembling Meteor** handed the tenth feather back. That fight opened with the single biggest enemy action of the session — one Rain of Arrows roll of 45 that hit Clutch for 23 and **critically hit Sovael, Sattva and Seva for 62 each, 209 damage in one action** — and was then decided by one spell in reply. Sovael cast a Quickened **Incarnate Ancient Specter**, and the DM walked all four opponents through the save one at a time: *"Success — prone for the archer. Success — prone for the mage. Prone on a success is pretty good."* Every one of them made the save and every one of them went down anyway, because only a critical success avoids it. It cost the enemy team a full round of standing up and **broke the Meteor mage's turn outright** — *"they won't be able to do that, because that's three actions. They don't have the luxury if they're going to stand up."* Sovael then killed the Swordsman himself with a Shadow Signet Fire Ray; Ferrok's crit killed the Mage; and the Archer, left alone, forfeited.

**Under the Pale Sun** was the grudge match — the team from S11 — and Ferrok took it personally (*"this time it's personal"*) and accounted for **550 of the party's ~1,019 damage**. But the fight turned on a wall. Sovael put a **Wall of Wind** between the party and two archery specialists, announcing it as *"about to do something funny."* Arrows cannot cross it, and the DM confirmed it on the spot: *"anything going through that won't work."* Archer output fell from **157 damage before the wall to 44 after it** — helped enormously by Ferrok's Reactive Strikes, which cancelled two Rain of Arrows mid-declaration and killed Archer A in the middle of one. Both archers ended up **Grabbed, Off-Guard and Immobilized** by Clutch, and died there. Sovael killed Swordsman A with a rank-7 Electric Arc; Sattva hero-pointed a Fist that killed Swordsman B and knocked his helmet off. **Under the Pale Sun were eliminated from the tournament** — ten years out — and Sovael, when Ferrok jeered, said: *"Hey, let's be graceful."*

There was a lot of nonsense in between — a mid-afternoon dance party at **Umantar's shrine** where Sovael pointed out that he was *singing, not dancing*, two more **Taumatan shrines** (Nisaye and Ramijav), a stop at a stone market wired into Goka's Endless Market, and a sustained bit about the whole party getting **matching tattoos of Sovael** on their backs (*"He's a Pyro dragon. He's a shiny Pyro dragon"*). Day 2 closed at a carpenter's-shed team base, where **Clutch — who runs the party's coin — proposed selling the staff of illusions**, a decision that came back four hours later.

**Day 3 opened with the sky on fire.** A plume of red flame erupted from Mount Haminabu and twisted into a blazing phoenix, and **Hao Jin** spoke across the whole island: a palace on the peak, an evening with **Empress Nai Yan Fei**, and the terms — ***"simply bring me your necklace and ten silver feathers."*** They have until the end of the day to reach the top.

They did not get to contemplate it, because something ten feet tall, red-skinned and horned crashed down into the Temple of Irori. **Koto Zekora**, an Onidoshi and **head enforcer of Bonmou**, had been tasked by the Ruby Phoenix with weeding out the weak — and she wanted **three feathers** on a fight they were not allowed to refuse. *"If it makes you feel any better, my job is to beat the shit out of a few people today. You were just closest."* Sattva's answer was two words: ***"Do it."*** It was the session's hardest fight by a distance — **968 damage taken**, an Eclipse Burst opener that **all five PCs failed at DC 41**, two Reactive-Strike criticals that put 170 into Clutch, and a Falling Stars that dropped Sovael to Dying 1, prone and burning from his own Oracle curse. Sattva's healer's gloves brought him back for 30, and — still Wounded, still on fire — he asked the table *"Should I gamble, or should I heal?"* Ferrok said *"I say go for it."* His fifth-rank **Thunderstrike** hit for 31 and put her into single digits, and the free **Foretell Harm** off it set 10 more to land at the start of her turn — **which is what actually dropped her.** Foundry still shows her at 3 of 500 only because Cody waved the tick through rather than entering it. She never got that turn, and that was the whole point: her Eclipse Burst had already made **all five PCs fail a DC 41**, and a second one could have ended the party. She conceded, grinning: *"You all pass. A good match… Orders from the big boss lady — not allowed to kill anyone."* Then she flew off to go test somebody else.

The rest was a victory lap through two dens. A **Duneshaker Solifugid** — 500 hit points and Quickened — ate the best action-economy play of the night. Sovael's Lingering Composition Performance critically succeeded, firing **Talent Envy** for a free critical Demoralize, and he followed it with **Roaring Applause**, which the creature failed. Slowed 1 **cancelled its Quickened outright** — the MAP progression proves it, four Strikes in round 1 and exactly three in every round after — and because the applause carries the **manipulate** trait, the thing had to stop and clap on its own turn. In rounds three and four that produced **four provoked Reactive Strikes, two of them natural 20s, for 141 damage — 28% of the creature's health.** The DM enjoyed it enormously: *"this thing is just chomping, flailing at you, causing earthquakes — and the whole time, with its little pincers, it's going clap, clap, clap, clap."* Sovael's Fire Ray then took it from 53 hit points to 1, and Ferrok's Force Fang collected the last point. Sattva, of the giant camel spider: ***"It's friend-shaped. I can't shoot it."***

Last came a **Berberoka**, which Sattva's Recall Knowledge revealed was **terrified of crabs** — a weakness the party could not exploit, because they had sold the staff of illusions that morning. *"No, we shouldn't have sold the staff."* Ferrok did a Goring Charge *"doing his best impression of a crab walk"* anyway. Seva's Rooting rune critical **immobilized the giant for the entire fight**, which is why a boss with fifteen feet of reach stood still and punched. Clutch finished it with a Combat Grab Claw critical — 69, plus 7 from Sattva's shared Warden's Boon, into a creature with 75 left — and then threw the corpse back into its own pool. Among the remains: a single silver feather, and a team that had not fared as well.

**Ten feathers, confiscation-capped, going into the last day.** The mountain is marked on the map.

## Character Spotlight

### Sovael · the Flaming Pearl, who fights from the floor

- **He was kind to the people who were about to eat him.** Ninety seconds before the Biting Roses took a feather off him and their mantis picked him up, he warned Yarrika about the dangerous part of the island, unprompted and for nothing. Two hours later, when Ferrok jeered at Under the Pale Sun — a team that had just been eliminated for ten years — he said ***"Hey, let's be graceful."*** Same character, twice, in the two scenes where it cost him something.
- **He lost the first fight and immediately diagnosed why, out loud, to the person he thought caused it.** The post-mortem critique to Clutch — *"you should stick more to your Seva and me at the beginning… you charging in kind of made us a bit too exposed"* — was a positional argument, and he defended it when Clutch and the DM pushed back. Then he de-escalated it himself: *"This has been close. A lot of times."*
- **His three big control casts each broke a specific enemy plan, and he called all three in advance.** *"I'm about to do something funny by casting Wall of Wind"* against a team built on archery — their output fell from 157 to 44. The Quickened **Incarnate Ancient Specter** that put four opponents on the ground on *successful* saves and cost the Meteor mage its Howling Blizzard. And **Roaring Applause** into a Quickened boss, which cancelled its fourth action and left it clapping into four Reactive Strikes worth 141 damage. He'd tried to cast Wall of Wind a whole fight earlier and been one action short.
- **★ The Solifugid fight is the best two-spell sequence he has run in this campaign.** **Roaring Applause** forced the creature to clap on its own turn, which handed the party free Reactive Strikes — and then **True Target** (rank 7, one action) designated it for **all four allies at once**, so the first strike each of them made rolled twice and kept the better die. All four connected: Seva 16, Clutch 15, Sattva 19, and **Ferrok a natural 20 for 78 — on one of the Reactive Strikes the clapping had provoked.** One spell made the opening; the other guaranteed it landed.
- **★ His Synesthesia scroll bought one roll — his own finisher.** Koto *made* the save; the Clumsy 3 then made her **fail the basic Reflex save against his Thunderstrike**, 32 against DC 34 where 35 succeeds, **doubling the damage of the spell that ended the fight.** Its −3 to AC flipped nothing, but only because the party had lost flanking and she was carrying Clumsy without off-guard — the one attack that had both hit her at AC 35.
- **★ He has three kills this session and put a fourth enemy on one hit point.** A Shadow Signet **Fire Ray** killed the Trembling Meteor Swordsman by attacking Reflex instead of AC; a rank-7 **Electric Arc** killed Pale Sun Swordsman A on a critical failure; his **Fire Ray** took the 500-HP Solifugid from 53 to 1 for Ferrok to finish; and his **Thunderstrike + Foretell Harm** ended Koto Zekora at 3 hit points.
- **His buffs are measurably load-bearing — and he shares the credit.** Heroism on Ferrok converted three ordinary hits into criticals (76, 70, 74), though the log flags Clutch's off-guard as equally decisive on all three. His rank-4 **Heal scroll** on Ferrok the turn before Swordsman A's natural-20 Reactive Strike is the only reason a 110-damage hit was survivable.
- **★ He beat the head enforcer of Bonmou from the floor, and he did it by denying her a turn.** Falling Stars put him at Dying 1, prone and burning from his own curse. Sattva scraped him up for 30. He asked the table *"Should I gamble, or should I heal?"*, took Ferrok's *"I say go for it,"* and spent the turn on Thunderstrike for 31 — then the free **Foretell Harm** off it, 10 more at the start of her turn, **which is what actually dropped her.** The read behind it is the real credit: her opening **Eclipse Burst had already made all five PCs fail a DC 41**, and the party could not have absorbed a second one. He didn't just finish her — he made sure she never acted again.
- **He is unusually honest about not wanting to be in fights.** *"Oh, God. I just don't want to fight ever."* — and then he spends every fight sustaining a spell that keeps a monster clapping. The comedy is dry: joining the shrine dance party and immediately establishing jurisdiction (***"Wait, I'm singing, not dancing"***), and the map completionism (*"My OCD, though. I still want to cover the whole map at least"*).

- **The loss was a positional problem before it was a dice problem.** Sattva is right that the double crits decided it.
