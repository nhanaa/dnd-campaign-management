Session Summary

They walked in holding all ten feathers and lost one in the first eleven minutes. The Nameless had spent S13 clawing to the qualifying number; S14 opened with a troupe stepping out of the trees, asking pleasantly how the day was treating them, and then taking a feather off them in two rounds. It was the party's first real thrashing in a long time, and nobody at the table pretended otherwise.

The Biting Roses were not a grudge match or a rival with history — they were four strangers who were simply better on the day. Yarrika Mulandez opened with Phantasmal Calamity into the whole party; Lantondo, a Wyrwood, followed with a fifth-rank Fireball and then a Frigid Flurry that critically failed Ferrok for 93 out of 84 hit points, putting him on the floor; and Grandfather Mantis pounced on Seva and critically hit her twice in one turn — 92 on a natural 14 that beat her AC by eleven, then 58 on a natural 20 — leaving her restrained, blinded and dying. Sovael's rank-5 Heal got her up once and Clutch's Battle Medicine got her up again. Then Grandfather Mantis came for Sovael, hit for 40, grabbed him, hit again for 43, and picked him up and started eating him. "I'm sorry, guys. I'm sorry. It can't be."

The arithmetic is the whole story: across roughly two rounds the party put 122 damage into about 1,270 hit points of enemies and absorbed 792. They never landed a single point on Yarrika or Artus, who finished the fight untouched at full health. They conceded. There is no single voice on the tape calling it — the concession sits inside one un-diarized crosstalk blob — so it reads as what it was: everyone arriving at the same conclusion at once.

Then the Biting Roses did something that reframed the whole loss. Yarrika called her mantis off — "Grandfather Mantis. That is not food. Let them go" — and healed the party she had just beaten, apologising for the cone. Artus said "well fought." And then, turning her prize over in her hand, she asked: "Wait, what did we want these for again?" Lantondo had to explain that ten feathers is how you enter the tournament. "Oh, that's what we're doing! I thought you just liked them because they were shiny." She skipped away with her team trailing after her. Sovael, watching her go: "I love her." Sattva: "At least they're friendly." Ninety seconds before any of this, unprompted, Sovael had warned her about something dangerous on the southern side of the island.

The post-mortem that followed is the best conversation of the night, and it's a genuine disagreement rather than a group shrug. Sovael argued the shape of the loss was positional — "I really think, Clutch, you should stick more to your Seva and me at the beginning, because I feel like you charging in kind of made us a bit too exposed" — and Clutch answered with the actual bind he was in: "If I stay back, the mage kills us. If I move forward, the mage kills us." Seva took her share ("that's my bad… I was just, like, away, so I couldn't react"), the DM called it "a damned-if-you-do, damned-if-you-don't situation with how the initiative and casters lined up," and Sattva landed it: "double crits and failing all saves, which was just unlucky. So it's fine. You live and you learn." Ferrok, drily: "We haven't been soundly thrashed in a while, so…"

Then they went and won five straight. Forty-five minutes later the Trembling Meteor handed the tenth feather back. That fight opened with the single biggest enemy action of the session — one Rain of Arrows roll of 45 that hit Clutch for 23 and critically hit Sovael, Sattva and Seva for 62 each, 209 damage in one action — and was then decided by one spell in reply. Sovael cast a Quickened Incarnate Ancient Specter, and the DM walked all four opponents through the save one at a time: "Success — prone for the archer. Success — prone for the mage. Prone on a success is pretty good." Every one of them made the save and every one of them went down anyway, because only a critical success avoids it. It cost the enemy team a full round of standing up and broke the Meteor mage's turn outright. Sovael then killed the Swordsman himself with a Shadow Signet Fire Ray; Ferrok's crit killed the Mage; and the Archer, left alone, forfeited.

Under the Pale Sun was the grudge match — the team from S11 — and Ferrok took it personally ("this time it's personal") and accounted for 550 of the party's ~1,019 damage. But the fight turned on a wall. Sovael put a Wall of Wind between the party and two archery specialists, announcing it as "about to do something funny." Arrows cannot cross it, and the DM confirmed it on the spot: "anything going through that won't work." Archer output fell from 157 damage before the wall to 44 after it — helped enormously by Ferrok's Reactive Strikes, which cancelled two Rain of Arrows mid-declaration and killed Archer A in the middle of one. Both archers ended up Grabbed, Off-Guard and Immobilized by Clutch, and died there. Sovael killed Swordsman A with a rank-7 Electric Arc; Sattva hero-pointed a Fist that killed Swordsman B and knocked his helmet off. Under the Pale Sun were eliminated from the tournament — ten years out — and Sovael, when Ferrok jeered, said: "Hey, let's be graceful."

There was a lot of nonsense in between — a mid-afternoon dance party at Umantar's shrine where Sovael pointed out that he was singing, not dancing, two more Taumatan shrines (Nisaye and Ramijav), a stop at a stone market wired into Goka's Endless Market, and a sustained bit about the whole party getting matching tattoos of Sovael on their backs ("He's a Pyro dragon. He's a shiny Pyro dragon"). Day 2 closed at a carpenter's-shed team base, where Clutch — who runs the party's coin — proposed selling the staff of illusions, a decision that came back four hours later.

Day 3 opened with the sky on fire. A plume of red flame erupted from Mount Haminabu and twisted into a blazing phoenix, and Hao Jin spoke across the whole island: a palace on the peak, an evening with Empress Nai Yan Fei, and the terms — "simply bring me your necklace and ten silver feathers." They have until the end of the day to reach the top.

They did not get to contemplate it, because something ten feet tall, red-skinned and horned crashed down into the Temple of Irori. Koto Zekora, an Onidoshi and head enforcer of Bonmou, had been tasked by the Ruby Phoenix with weeding out the weak — and she wanted three feathers on a fight they were not allowed to refuse. "If it makes you feel any better, my job is to beat the shit out of a few people today. You were just closest." Sattva's answer was two words: "Do it." It was the session's hardest fight by a distance — 968 damage taken, an Eclipse Burst opener that all five PCs failed at DC 41, two Reactive-Strike criticals that put 170 into Clutch, and a Falling Stars that dropped Sovael to Dying 1, prone and burning from his own Oracle curse. Sattva's healer's gloves brought him back for 30, and — still Wounded, still on fire — he asked the table "Should I gamble, or should I heal?" Ferrok said "I say go for it." His fifth-rank Thunderstrike hit for 31 and put her into single digits, and the free Foretell Harm off it set 10 more to land at the start of her turn — which is what actually dropped her. Foundry still shows her at 3 of 500 only because Cody waved the tick through rather than entering it. She never got that turn, and that was the point: her Eclipse Burst had already made all five PCs fail a DC 41, and a second one could have ended the party. She conceded, grinning: "You all pass. A good match… Orders from the big boss lady — not allowed to kill anyone." Then she flew off to go test somebody else.

The rest was a victory lap through two dens. A Duneshaker Solifugid — 500 hit points and Quickened — ate the best action-economy play of the night. Sovael's Lingering Composition Performance critically succeeded, firing Talent Envy for a free critical Demoralize, and he followed it with Roaring Applause, which the creature failed. Slowed 1 cancelled its Quickened outright — the MAP progression proves it, four Strikes in round 1 and exactly three in every round after — and because the applause carries the manipulate trait, the thing had to stop and clap on its own turn. In rounds three and four that produced four provoked Reactive Strikes, two of them natural 20s, for 141 damage — 28% of the creature's health. The DM enjoyed it enormously: "this thing is just chomping, flailing at you, causing earthquakes — and the whole time, with its little pincers, it's going clap, clap, clap, clap." Sovael's Fire Ray then took it from 53 hit points to 1, and Ferrok's Force Fang collected the last point. Sattva, of the giant camel spider: "It's friend-shaped. I can't shoot it."

Last came a Berberoka, which Sattva's Recall Knowledge revealed was terrified of crabs — a weakness the party could not exploit, because they had sold the staff of illusions that morning. "No, we shouldn't have sold the staff." Ferrok did a Goring Charge "doing his best impression of a crab walk" anyway. Seva's Rooting rune critical immobilized the giant for the entire fight, which is why a boss with fifteen feet of reach stood still and punched. Clutch finished it with a Combat Grab Claw critical — 69, plus 7 from Sattva's shared Warden's Boon, into a creature with 75 left — and then threw the corpse back into its own pool. Among the remains: a single silver feather, and a team that had not fared as well.

Ten feathers, confiscation-capped, going into the last day. The mountain is marked on the map.

Character Spotlight

Sovael · the Flaming Pearl, who fights from the floor

  • He was kind to the people who were about to eat him. Ninety seconds before the Biting Roses took a feather off him and their mantis picked him up, he warned Yarrika about the dangerous part of the island — unprompted, and for nothing. Two hours later, when Ferrok jeered at Under the Pale Sun, a team that had just been eliminated for ten years, he said "Hey, let's be graceful." Same character, twice, in the two scenes where it cost him something.
  • He lost the first fight and immediately diagnosed why, out loud, to the person he thought caused it. The critique to Clutch was a positional argument, and he defended it when Clutch and the DM pushed back — then de-escalated it himself: "This has been close. A lot of times."
  • His three big control casts each broke a specific enemy plan, and he called all three in advance. "I'm about to do something funny by casting Wall of Wind" against a team built on archery — output 157 → 44. The Quickened Incarnate Ancient Specter that put four opponents on the ground on successful saves and cancelled the Meteor mage's turn. And Roaring Applause into a Quickened boss, killing its fourth action and leaving it clapping into four Reactive Strikes worth 141 damage. He'd tried the wall a whole fight earlier and been one action short.
  • ★ The Solifugid fight is the best two-spell sequence he has run in this campaign. Roaring Applause forced the creature to clap on its own turn, handing the party free Reactive Strikes — then True Target designated it for all four allies at once, so each one's first strike rolled twice and kept the better die. All four connected: Seva 16, Clutch 15, Sattva 19, and Ferrok a natural 20 for 78 — on one of the Reactive Strikes the clapping had provoked.
  • ★ His Synesthesia scroll bought one roll — his own finisher. Koto made the save; the Clumsy 3 then made her fail the basic Reflex save against his Thunderstrike (32 vs DC 34; 35 succeeds), doubling the damage of the spell that ended the fight.
  • ★ He has three kills this session and put a fourth enemy on one hit point. A Shadow Signet Fire Ray killed the Trembling Meteor Swordsman by attacking Reflex instead of AC. A rank-7 Electric Arc killed Pale Sun Swordsman A on a critical failure. His Fire Ray took the 500-HP Solifugid from 53 to 1 for Ferrok to finish. And his Thunderstrike + Foretell Harm ended Koto Zekora at 3 hit points.
  • His buffs are measurably load-bearing — and he shares the credit. Heroism on Ferrok converted three ordinary hits into criticals (76, 70, 74), though the log flags Clutch's off-guard as equally decisive on all three. His rank-4 Heal scroll on Ferrok the turn before a 110-damage natural 20 is the only reason that hit was survivable.
  • ★ He beat the head enforcer of Bonmou from the floor, and he did it by denying her a turn. Dying 1, prone, burning from his own curse; Sattva scraped him up for 30. He asked "Should I gamble, or should I heal?", took Ferrok's go-ahead, and spent the turn on Thunderstrike for 31 — then the free Foretell Harm off it, 10 more at the start of her turn, which is what actually dropped her. The read behind it is the real credit: her opening Eclipse Burst had already made all five PCs fail a DC 41, and the party could not have absorbed a second. He didn't just finish her — he made sure she never acted again.
  • He is unusually honest about not wanting to be in fights — "Oh, God. I just don't want to fight ever" — and then spends every fight sustaining a spell that keeps a monster clapping. The comedy is dry: "Wait, I'm singing, not dancing", and the map completionism ("My OCD, though. I still want to cover the whole map at least").

Clutch · the quartermaster who ends fights with his body

  • He runs the party's money and makes the calls that cost it. He distributes the coin, and he proposed selling the staff of illusions — "We want to sell the staff of phantasms?" — the decision the session's best running gag hangs on four hours later. He also set the party's stake against Koto Zekora: "We want to win this one, right? Yeah, it's three feathers."
  • He gave the best answer of the post-mortem, and it was a defense of a decision he'd already been blamed for. "If I stay back, the mage kills us. If I move forward, the mage kills us." No excuse, no counter-accusation — just the shape of the trap. The DM independently agreed with him.
  • His damage numbers understate him badly, because most of his work is subtraction. 296 absorbed from the Solifugid — more than half the party's intake. 277 from Koto Zekora across two Reactive-Strike criticals. 80 intercepted off Seva against the Trembling Meteor, and twice more against the Berberoka. Seva took literally zero from the Solifugid because he stood in front of all of it.
  • He plays his Strikes for other people. Snagging Strike is something he runs for the casters; his Taunt generated the off-guard the log flags as essential to his own killing critical; and his grapples generated the off-guard that made Ferrok's three Heroism crits possible.
  • He ends fights with his body rather than his damage. He ran the length of a beach at the Trembling Meteor's last survivor until they forfeited. He killed the Berberoka with a Combat Grab critical and threw the corpse back into its own pool. Offered a choice earlier: "Do we want to beat on this creature, or should I throw it into the ocean? Let's throw it."
  • He is funny in a completely deadpan register. After Koto landed a reactive-strike critical: "Couldn't be me." Asked whether he had stretchy powers: "Got long teeth." Honest about his ceilings: "I'm not legendary in Athletics, so I still can't grab these Gargantuan creatures" — which is why Sattva made him big.

Ferrok the Unyielding · the grudge, personally

  • He carried the Pale Sun fight outright — 550 of ~1,019 damage — and told everyone why beforehand: "This time it's personal." Five criticals, three of them made criticals by Sovael's Heroism and Clutch's grapples. His Reactive Strikes cancelled two Rain of Arrows mid-declaration, one of them killing Archer A in the middle of the action — at least as much of the archers' collapse as the wall itself.
  • He was top damage against the Solifugid at 143 and collected the kill on Sovael's setup with the most casual possible description: "Ferrok will just lazily wave his sword and send a phantom blade at it." His +16 critical Spellstrike killed the Trembling Meteor Mage.
  • He also went down in the first fight and the party nearly lost him. Frigid Flurry critically failed him for 93 out of 84 hit points. He came back at 10/196, took a natural-20 Reactive Strike for 110 in the next fight, and kept walking at people.
  • He names the team to strangers — "We at the Nameless accept" — and he's the one who wants the collection completed: "I want the achievement for all the shrines too. I'm worried I missed one." It was Ferrok who talked the party into finishing the hexcrawl before the mountain.
  • He is the party's tonal thermostat. After the loss: "We haven't been soundly thrashed in a while, so…" After Koto: "Crit or be critted, is what it was." After the spider: "None of us got eaten that time. That's cool." When Koto crashed in at dawn: "Well, so much for sleeping in."
  • He asks the questions that create work for the DM, on purpose. "I'm going to ask each one of them their names now." → DM: "Why would you do that to me?" He also floated grinding an over-level boss down over ten hours of kiting, because "he surely can't Treat Wounds — he's a monster."
  • He commits to a bit physically. Told the Berberoka fears crabs and the party can't make one, he did a Goring Charge "doing his best impression of a crab walk."

Sattva · the one who takes the loss well and the spider personally

  • He defused the post-mortem without dismissing it. "I think it's more of a case of double crits and failing all saves, which was just unlucky. So it's fine. You live and you learn." Two people had just been arguing about positioning; he pointed at the dice, and the conversation ended there.
  • ★ He tried to avoid the fight, and then took the loss with total grace. "I'm fine with walking away" before it started; "At least they're friendly" after it ended, agreeing with Sovael's "I love her" about the woman who had just taken a feather off them.
  • He answered Koto Zekora's unrefusable challenge with two words. "Do it." Koto: "That's the attitude." He then top-damaged that fight at 181, including a natural-20 bow critical for 96.
  • He killed Pale Sun Swordsman B with a hero-pointed punch and knocked the man's helmet off doing it. "I will punch Swordsman B in the face." → "It punched his helmet off."
  • He is the party's force multiplier as much as its healer. Warden's Boon shared to whoever is about to connect — it added the 7 that finished the Berberoka. The Enlarge on Clutch so he could wrestle a giant. The taxi service: "I pick you up — here comes the taxi." And the Recall Knowledge that found the Berberoka's fear of crabs.
  • He revived Sovael off the floor against Koto Zekora, and ran an almost unbroken chain of critical Treat Wounds between every fight in the session.
  • The line of the session, delivered with total sincerity about a 500-HP gargantuan camel spider: "It's friend-shaped. I can't shoot it."

Seva · the table's rules conscience

  • ★ She twice read a rule out loud that cost her, when nobody would have known. Mid-heal, she looked up and volunteered that healer's gloves carry the manipulate trait — costing herself the action. Later, against Koto Zekora, she ruled the one-activity reaction limit for the whole table: "It's one activity — you can't take two reactions for one activity." That call is what let Clutch spend his hero point correctly. Nobody asked her to make either.
  • She took her share of the loss unprompted, and it was the most technical self-assessment at the table. "That's my bad, because there's a reason why I'm staying close to Clutch — I can, as a reaction, follow him in. I was just, like, away, so I couldn't react." Nobody had accused her of anything.
  • She was the Trembling Meteor fight's top damage dealer at 264, on three Deathseeker criticals, and she reads targets before she swings: "I also have something for my Deathseeker, since her Will is low." Against the Berberoka her Rooting rune critical immobilized a fifteen-foot-reach giant for the whole fight — the single reason that boss stood still.
  • She plays position deliberately. She delayed twice against the Berberoka on Sovael's suggestion, purely to get inside the Courageous Anthem. She asked the question that produced the session's only ancestry lore, and she was the player actually engaging with the shrines.
  • She spent her last hero point on someone else. After surviving a 158-damage round on 18 hit points: "I'll reroll my last hero point. We need to keep Clutch up." She cheered when he survived a hit he shouldn't have: "But you're still up! Oh my god."
  • She had the worst opening fight of anyone and refused to be sidelined. Two critical hits in one turn left her restrained, blinded and dying; she went down again to the Frigid Flurry. Dropped, she first said "Do not get me back up" — then reversed herself within seconds: "No, no — go, give it a try, give me a turn." And after eating a 70-damage Disintegrate: "I'm tanking."

What Sovael is thinking about

  • The loss was a positional problem before it was a dice problem. Sattva is right that the double crits decided it. Sovael is also right that he spent his one good turn healing instead of controlling, and that he only had to because the front line was too far forward to intercept. Both things are true. The hard number: the party never touched Yarrika or Artus at all.
  • His whole value is denial, and denial needs the actions to deploy. Every fight the party won, it won because an enemy plan stopped working: the Meteor mage couldn't cast, the Pale Sun archers couldn't shoot, the Solifugid lost its fourth action and had to applaud, Koto never got another turn. The fight they lost is the one where he never got a control spell off — healing on turn one, grabbed by turn two.
  • Denying a turn beats dealing damage, and the Koto fight proved it. Ten points of Foretell Harm were worth more than any Strike on the board, because they removed a DC 41 area save from a party that had already failed one five-for-five.
  • Quickened Casting on a rank-7 spell is not RAW. It worked at this table and it decided the Trembling Meteor fight. Worth knowing before building around it again.
  • Level 15 for Master's focus. Ryu agreed 14 is where full casters feel furthest behind martials.
  • Ten feathers is a defensive position, not a win. Every remaining team fight can only lose ground — the eleventh feather is worth 100 platinum and nothing else. The party chose to finish the hexcrawl anyway.
  • Heroism is underused. "I love Heroism. I should cast that some more, actually." One 10-minute buff on the party's damage dealer bought three criticals in a single fight — with Clutch's grapples doing half the work.
