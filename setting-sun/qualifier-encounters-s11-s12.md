# Ruby Phoenix Qualifier — Encounter Shapes, Sessions 11–12

*Fists of the Ruby Phoenix, Book 1: "Despair on Danger Island." Bonmu, the pre-qualifying round.*
*Compiled from the Foundry roll logs (v2 export + recovered S11 log), the session transcripts, and the Book 1 PDF.*

## Reading this document

Each fight is given three ways: **what it is** (creatures + difficulty + stat block), and **how Ryu piloted it** — the actual turn-by-turn control, reconstructed from target selection and ability sequencing in the logs.

Two framing facts up front:

- The book is written for **four** players; the Nameless are **five** (a ~25–30% baseline uplift before any change).
- The chapter assumes **level 12 on Day 1, 13–14 on Day 2.** The party was 12 through S11 and 13 from the first fight of S12 — **on the intended curve.** The difficulty is the tuning, not a level deficit.

**On defenses — a correction worth stating plainly:** Ryu does **not** relax enemy defenses, and mostly does the opposite. The book already writes several of these teams as **weak** NPCs ("weak ki adepts," "weak mage of many styles," "weak weapon masters"). So Golarion's Finest at AC 31 is the *book's own* number, not a relaxation — and for the Glowing Embers and Under the Pale Sun he ran the **base** (CR13) statblock, *stripping* the book's weak template and making them tougher. The base chassis under all of these (Bestiary 6) is CREATURE 13. His pattern is: often remove the book's weakening, inflate solo-boss HP, add a body or a level, and rebuild offensive payloads — while leaving the **level field** untouched, which is why Foundry's XP math keeps under-reporting the real difficulty.

**On saves — the DM's own stated rule.** Mid-fight in S11, Ryu volunteered his philosophy verbatim: *"one thing I typically don't do is I typically don't give things resilience or, like, the improved thing, because I feel like that targets spellcasters in particular on the GM side. So 90% of the time, if I'm getting a crit success, it's just because I roll high."* So the save increases that do appear come from **templates** (Elite = +2 across the board) rather than bolted-on anti-caster tech — with one outlier, the Grave Spinosaurus's +6 Fort, which is a genuine defensive rebuild.

House rules: **group-escape** (whole party agrees + a round → flee any non-tournament fight; not in the book), **10-minute clock ticks** (book: 30-min), active hours **6 AM–10 PM**.

# The one pattern that runs through everything: he hunts the backline

Before the fight-by-fight, the meta-read, because it's the answer to "how does he run them": **almost every creature Ryu pilots goes for the squishiest reachable target, and the smart teams coordinate to delete a caster.** The logs are unambiguous:

- **S11, no Dust of Disappearance yet:** Sovael was critically hit by the Pale Sun swordsman (+14), the Nightmares (+15), and the Last Breath (three crits, +10/+11/+10 as the whole team ganged him). Every intelligent team in S11 converged on him.
- **Solo monsters grab-and-eat the soft target:** the Grave Spinosaurus Swallowed Seva; a Tyrannosaurus Grabbed→Swallowed **Sovael**; both bypassed the front line to do it.
- **S12 finale, WITH the Dust:** Golarion's Finest assigned an adept to hunt Sovael and crit him for 72 — and then, the instant he vanished at 16:57, **every attack on Sovael stops for the rest of the fight** (provable in the log: zero targets after 16:57). The team pivoted to Sattva and ground him for four rounds. That single moment is the clearest evidence both of the hunting behavior *and* of the counter working.

This is the mechanical spine of the "uphill battle" feeling: the tuning taxes casters (HP/saves/encounter frequency), and the piloting targets them.

# Session 11 — "The Lord of the Island" (Day 1 morning, level 12)

## E1 · Five Dromornis — *Den, WIN (200 pp)*

| | Book (CR10) | As run |
|---|---|---|
| Count | 4 | **5** |
| HP / AC | 150 / 28 | ~175 / 27–28 |
| Attack | beak +22, talon +20 | +24 |
| Beak / Talon | 2d12+10+1d8 / 2d8+10+1d4 | 2d12+12+1d8 / 2d8+12+1d6 bleed |

**Signature:** *Rushing Attack* (Stride + Strike). **Difficulty:** ~Moderate–Severe for 5.
**How he piloted them:** distributed pressure, not focus fire. Round 1 each bird picked a *different* PC (B→Seva, D→Sovael, C→Sattva, E→Ferrok, A→Clutch) and opened beak-and-talon. As the fight went, **B found Sovael and critted him for +13** — the pack's one concentration was onto the caster the moment a bird had the angle. Otherwise a swarm spreading bleed across the whole party.

## E2 · Under the Pale Sun — *Team bout, FORFEIT (−1 feather)*

*Retroactively winnable — the counters (Wall of Wind vs the archers, Roaring Applause vs the swordsmen) existed and weren't reached for in time.*

| | Book (CR12, **weak**) | As run (CR13, base) |
|---|---|---|
| Swordsmen | *dervishes*, scimitar +25, 2d6+8 | katana **+29**, Swift Blow, Dueling Expertise +2d10, Demoralize |
| Archers | weak archery specialists | Per +26, longbow **+28**, **Rain of Arrows** |

**Difficulty:** ~240 XP vs level 12 = **≈120% of Extreme.** Weak template stripped, weapons upgraded.
**How he piloted them:** a coordinated ranged-saturation + melee-assassination machine. **Swordsman B opened the entire fight by critting Seva (+15) on turn one** — immediate alpha on a squishy. The archers then threw **Rain of Arrows every round** (one roll vs every AC in a burst — party-wide chip that ignores focus). Meanwhile the swordsmen crit through the backline: A crit Ferrok (+11), B **Demoralized Clutch and then Swift-Blow-crit him (+10)** using the fear to set up, A crit Sovael (+14). The shape: archers keep the whole party bleeding while the swordsmen delete one target at a time, debuff-first. The crit rate (four crits in the fight) is the +4-to-hit tuning showing.

## E3 · Jaiban, the Lord of the Island — *Boss, GROUP ESCAPE*

| | Book (CR15) | As run |
|---|---|---|
| AC / Per | 36 / +23 | **~41 / +34** |
| Jaws / Tail | +31 3d12+14 / +31 3d6+14 | **+37 4d12+24 / +35 4d10+23** |
| Quickened | — | **permanent** |

**How he piloted them:** as a force of nature meant to be fled. It **opened with Quaking Stomp → Earthquake** (party-wide AoE, DC 41), then Jaws into Clutch and a **Tail crit on Ferrok for 90.** Permanent quickened meant it acted more than the party could answer. Two rounds of that and the group-escape rule was the correct and only play.

## E4 · The Lightkeepers — *Team bout, FORFEIT (−1 feather). Genuinely unwinnable.*

Book: a **Day 3, no-stakes exhibition** (`EXTREME 14`). Ryu moved it to Day 1 and wagered feathers.

| Member (all **CREATURE 14**) | Book | As run |
|---|---|---|
| Syu Tak-nwa (witch) | DC **35**, attack **+27** | **DC 39, +31** |
| Shino Hakusa (monk) | fist +30, Per +22 | +30, Per +22 — **exact** |
| Blue Viper (poisoner) | shortsword +27, Per +28 | attacks +30/+31 |
| Ran-to (grappler) | gauntlet +30, **330 HP** | +32 |

**Difficulty:** four CR14 vs level 12 = **320 XP, ≈160% of Extreme.**
**How he piloted them:** the book's scripted roles, executed precisely. **Blue Viper opened by crit-bombing Ferrok (+13)** and coating him in Cerulean Scourge poison (stage 1, 31 damage on the failed save), harrying from range. **Shino tumbled past the front line (Tumble Through crit) to reach Sovael and pressure-point him** — the monk's written job is "rush spellcasters and reduce their spellcasting." **Syu landed Synesthesia on Clutch (DC 39 — Clutch's 36 was a *success* against book DC 35, a failure at 39)**, Clumsy 3 + Dazzled for a minute, gutting the tank. **Ran-to interposed and Whirlwind-Tossed Clutch as a weapon.** Every member did exactly their book role, and the +4 on Syu's DC is what turned the fight-deciding save from a make into a break. ~111 dealt vs ~316 taken; the party bought the antidote for a feather.

## E5 · Caustic Monitor — *Den, WIN (300 pp)*

Book: **Elite Caustic Monitor, CR14** — run ~as written.
**Signature:** quickened Stride, Jaws + Improved Grab + Fort venom, Caustic Spray (cone, DC 36 Reflex).
**How he piloted them:** a clean tank-and-spank that the party's tank *won*. It **dumped everything into Clutch** — Jaws crit (+12) turn one, Caustic Spray cone twice, then Jaws and three Talon strikes, all into Clutch. The Guardian's taunt held it and his AC turned the talons into three straight crit-misses. A boss played honestly, defeated by the tank doing its job.

## E6 · The Nightmares — *Team bout, WIN via forfeit (+1 feather). Run EASIER than the book.*

| | Book (CR13) | As run (CR12, weak) |
|---|---|---|
| Count / mounts | 3 / **war horses, mounted** | **4 / dismounted** |
| Weapon | halberd +27, reach 10, 2d10+9, *fight to KO* | wakizashi +25, **forfeited** |

**How he piloted them:** rogues, not lancers — **feint for off-guard, then converge on the backline.** They focus-fired **Sattva and Sovael** all fight: B crit Sattva (+10), A crit Sattva (+10), and **A crit Sovael (+15)** then hit him again. Nightmare C feinted (Deception) to set up sneak attack. Even the softest team of the day pointed itself at the casters — and Ryu let them yield when the book says they never do.

## E7 · The Last Breath — *Team bout, WIN via forfeit (+1 feather)*

Book: a **Day 3 comedy chase** (`LOW 14`), *"a way of running the clock."* Ryu made it a real Day 1 fight.
**How he piloted them:** AoE openers into a caster gang-tackle. The **Monk opened with Qi Blast (11d6 force cone) twice**; the **Mage threw Disintegrate twice**; then the whole team piled onto **Sovael** — Swordsman crit him (+10), Monk hit-hit-crit (+11) and later crit again (+10). The book's joke pirates became a coordinated assassination of the bard, with a rank-6 Disintegrate in the mix.

# Session 12 — "The Nameless" (Day 1 midday → Day 2, level 13)

3–0 in team fights, no feathers lost. **Foundry's own threat ratings: three Extreme, three Severe.**

## E1 · Grave Spinosaurus — *Den, WIN (300 pp) — threat: SEVERE*

| | Book (CR15) | As run |
|---|---|---|
| HP / AC | 280 / **30** | **400 / 35** |
| Fort | +23 | **+29** (+6 — the one genuine save rebuild) |
| Jaws | 3d12+15 | 3d12+20, +31, reach 20 |

**Signature:** Grab, Pouncing Pin, **Swallow Whole** (3d12+12, Rupture 36).
**How he piloted them:** isolate-and-eat the softest target. It **Tripped Clutch (crit) to open**, then immediately pivoted off the tank onto **Seva** — Jaws, Grapple, and back-to-back **Swallow Whole** attempts. It spent the fight trying to swallow the squishy rogue rather than trade with the front line. ("*Who's ready for Swallow Hole?*" — the table's running joke by this point.)

## E2 · Hana's Hundreds — *Troop, WIN via forfeit (feathers 5 → 6) — threat: SEVERE*

| | Book (CR15 troop) | As run |
|---|---|---|
| Level / HP | 15 / 270 | **16 Elite / 420** |
| Weakness | area 20 / splash 10 | area 10–20 / splash 10 |

**How he piloted them:** a trample troop — but it barely got to act. Sovael banked a hero-pointed Diplomacy initiative to drop Eclipse Burst into the splash weakness *before* the troop could move; **376 damage in one round** and it **forfeited at the top of round 2 having used a single ability.** Its intended shape (Run Them Over into the party) never came online.

## E3 · Two Tyrannosaurus Imperators — *Den, WIN (400 pp) — threat: SEVERE*

| | Book (CR14, one) | As run (two) |
|---|---|---|
| HP / attack | 260 / +28 | **~350 / +30** |
| Jaws / Tail Swipe | 3d12+12 / 4d8+12 DC 34 | 3d12+**17** / 4d8+**17** DC 35 |

**How he piloted them:** two grapple-engines, each running a Grab chain, splitting the party. **B locked onto Ferrok** (the front magus) — repeated Jaws, then **Grab (crit) → Fling.** **A hunted the backline** — Foot and Jaws into Sattva across R3, then in R4 reached **Sovael and ran the full combo: Jaws hit → Grab (crit) → Swallow Whole (crit)** — it ate the caster. The Tail Swipe cone (prone-on-fail) was the AoE setup; the grapple-swallow was the finisher, aimed at whoever was softest in reach.

## E4 · Black Scorpion — *Den, WIN (600 pp) — threat: EXTREME*

| | Book (CR15) | As run (~CR16) |
|---|---|---|
| HP / AC | 275 / 38 | **395 / 39** |
| Attack / Fort | +30 / +29 | **+32 / +31** (clean Elite except AC) |

**Signature:** venom DC 38, Greater Constrict (2d12+14, DC 38 Fort).
**How he piloted them:** a reach-30 poison sprinkler. Rather than focus, it **spread Stinger strikes across all five PCs** to stack venom as widely as possible — Seva, Clutch, Sattva, Ferrok, and around again — critting the two squishies it could (Sattva +11, Seva +14). The reach meant it never had to move into threat; it stung the whole party from the middle. Wide venom application over focus-fire.

## E5 · The Glowing Embers ×5 — *Team bout, WIN (feathers 6 → 7, opponents eliminated) — threat: EXTREME*

| | Book (4 × **weak** mage, CR12) | As run (5 × base mage, CR13) |
|---|---|---|
| Count / level | 4 / weak | **5 / full** |
| Saves | — | Fort +24 / Ref +21 / Will +26 |
| **Fatigue rule** | clumsy 1 + enfeebled 1 from round 4 | **NEVER APPLIED** (base AC stayed 32 all 5 rounds; no −1 on any of their 8 attack rolls) |

**How he piloted them:** stand-off arcane artillery. They opened at range with **Disintegrate (multiple), Chain Lightning, Lightning Bolt, and Chromatic Ray**, targeting Ferrok, Sattva, and Seva — only dropping to longsword when a PC closed. A pure caster team throwing big single-target and line/AoE damage from the back. The book's self-weakening clause was forgotten, so they fought at full strength the whole way — if anything, *harder* than written.

## E6 · Brarkor & Golarion's Finest ×7 — *Team bout, WIN (feathers 7 → 8) — threat: EXTREME. Hardest fight of the campaign.*

All eight share one **Ki Adept** chassis. Seven at **book-weak** (CR12, AC 31), plus **Brarkor**, an invented **Elite** (CR14, AC 35) with zero mentions in the book.

| | Book weak ki adept | As run |
|---|---|---|
| Fist | 2d6+11 (~18) | **3d6+13 + 1d6 spirit + 2d6 force** (~34; ~45 with Punch Down) |
| Added abilities | — | **Inner Upheaval** (+2d6 force), **Qi Rush**, **Harmonize Self** |
| Mercy rule | surrender <50 HP; team yields if 5 down | **declined — fought 8 rounds to the last man** |

**How he piloted them — the masterclass:** a coordinated **Trip → Punch Down flurry** engine with assigned targets.
- **The core loop:** an adept Trips a target prone (Athletics +24), which enables **Punch Down (+2d10 vs prone)** for the follow-up Fists. G opened by crit-Tripping Ferrok; A, D, and C ran the same play all fight.
- **Assigned caster-hunter:** adept **D was pointed at Sovael** — Trip Sovael, Fist Sovael, and **the crit on Sovael for 72** (16:56:36). Then the pivot that proves everything: Sovael dropped the pre-held **Dust of Disappearance at ~16:57, and no enemy targets him again for the rest of the fight** — D's next three swings are crit-misses into the empty space, then the whole team abandons him.
- **The pivot target:** with Sovael gone, the team converged on **Sattva** for rounds 3–7 — A, G, C, and F all piling Trip+Fist onto him, keeping him prone and pounding (G alone crit him three times).
- **The tank as a sink:** Clutch's **Shielding Taunt** dragged a huge volume of swings onto AC 38 + Sturdy Shield + Benediction, where Brarkor and the adepts crit-missed repeatedly — wasted enemy actions by design.

That's the whole philosophy in one fight: **weaken them just enough that the party still lands hits, double the offense, remove the surrender valve, and pilot with real focus-fire discipline** — Trip the target, gang the caster, and let the tank soak the overflow.

# The pattern across both sessions

| Lever | What Ryu does |
|---|---|
| **Level field** | left untouched → Foundry under-reports the difficulty |
| **Solo-boss HP** | +35 to +65% (Spinosaurus, T-Rex, Scorpion, Jaiban, Hana) |
| **Team fights** | strips the book's *weak* template and/or adds a body/level (Embers, Pale Sun, Finest+Brarkor) |
| **Offensive payload** | rebuilt, bolted-on abilities (Jaiban, Finest, T-Rex) |
| **Defenses** | **not relaxed** — soft ACs are the book's own weak-NPC values; base statblocks are often *tougher* |
| **Saves** | template-driven (Elite +2); he deliberately avoids resilient/improved-evasion "because it targets spellcasters." Exception: Spinosaurus Fort +6 |
| **Mercy/surrender rules** | declined (Finest no surrender, Embers no fatigue) |
| **Run by the book** | genuinely sometimes (Caustic Monitor, Shino Hakusa, Ahmoza); Nightmares run *easier* |

**Piloting signature:** hunt the backline. Solo monsters grab-and-swallow the squishy; smart teams feint/Trip to enable their big hits and focus-fire the caster; the tank's taunt is treated as a sink to be avoided, not fed. The one fight where the caster answered this (Dust of Disappearance, S12 finale) is visibly the fight the pattern broke.

**Run in the party's favor:** the group-escape rule (the only reason Jaiban didn't kill anyone), the finer 10-minute clock, four-plus encounters left at or below book, the Nightmares run soft, and a *stated* fairness line about not stacking anti-caster save tech.

**The net read:** a hard, deliberate, system-literate DM who treats a well-built five-person party as license to raise the ceiling — and who pilots with genuine focus-fire discipline aimed at the squishiest target. The levers he leans on (HP, encounter frequency, focus-firing the backline) are precisely the ones a save-based caster feels most; the lever he won't touch (dedicated anti-caster save boosts) is one he's consciously ruled out. Both halves of that are true, and both are on the record.
