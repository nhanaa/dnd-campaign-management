Session Summary

The Lord in Iron is dead. Gorum, god of war and battle, was slain by the mantis god Achaekek during the Godsrain — and when a god dies, the afterlife he kept dies with him. The Clashing Shore, the endless proving ground where the faithful of Gorum were promised they would test their mettle forever, vanished "like smoke and ash, scattered to the cosmic winds."

Six dead warriors were left standing on the palm of Gorum's severed gauntlet. Every one of them had already died once — Keegru bled out in a bar fight, Jassik was murdered in his sleep for being a greedy pillaging warlord, Graz was a gladiator who challenged his way from the bottom to the top until he could no longer bear the consequences of his own arrogance. What they were offered on that iron palm was not resurrection. It was five fights, ten minutes apart, escalating from trivial to extreme, with Cody's framing hanging over it: "The chances that your characters will not survive to the end of the gauntlet is possible."

They survived all five. Not one PC died. The party killed eighteen enemies across eleven stat blocks, dealt roughly 9,500 points of damage, and burned three party-wide safety nets and most of a healer's spell list. The Sword Master of Gorum had to be reduced to zero four separate times before he stayed down — but it was the High Cleric who actually put people on the floor.

The shape of the day was set in the first ninety seconds of the first fight and never really changed: Graz's Battle Cry Demoralize at initiative, Tam's Dread Striker converting that fear into off-guard, and Perfect Finisher rolling two dice for every swing that mattered. By the end Graz was saying it out loud about his own build — "I think the Perfect Finisher is just — I don't know. It's crazy" — while Cody measured it from the other side of the screen: "There was a point where you took down like all 600 of the champion's HP in two rounds."

The Gauntlet

Prelude · 13:06 – 13:29 · The Iron Palm

Six dead warriors, one dead god

  • Cody lays out the format: five encounters, ten minutes between each, increasing difficulty, no guarantee of survival.
  • The ruling that shapes the whole day: "Hero points don't come back between fights. You've got three for the whole five fights."
  • Graz pre-casts Foresight (1 hour), Fly, Heroism and See the Unseen, and drinks three Greater Resistance potions — fire, cold and electricity.
  • Keegru pre-casts Sacred Covenant on all six PCs — the first time each of them hits 0, they return at 1 HP with 50 temporary. It is the single most load-bearing decision of the session.
  • Character introductions establish the theme without anyone planning it: every backstory ends in a death.
Act I · Combat · The Magma Worms · 13:33 – 14:04

Budgeted trivial. Ate two party members.

  • Three level-18 Magma Worms erupt from drops of blood on the sand. Budgeted at exactly Trivial for a six-player party — 60 XP on the nose.
  • Graz opens with a critical hit before any enemy acts. Battle Cry Demoralize crits at initiative, Rogue's Surprise Attack lands off-guard, and AC 40 becomes an effective 36 before the first die hits the table.
  • Worm B dies without ever taking a turn, despite rolling 48 initiative.
  • Shuyinlo is swallowed whole in round 1 and fights from inside a stomach for four PC turns.
  • Keegru's Roaring Applause hits a natural 20 on the save. The worm keeps its reactions — and four minutes later uses Fast Swallow to eat Chthon'ar'ax too.
  • Chthon'ar'ax bites his way out through Rupture 36. Cody: "As this enormous dragon just bursts its way out."
  • Cody apologises for his own dice three separate times. He rolled 16+ on seven of eleven enemy d20s.
  • Graz takes all three kills and zero damage.
Act II · Combat · Two Dragons and a Rider · 14:08 – 14:38

The Reveler was a mirror of Graz. It got one attack.

  • A Wyrmwraith, a Delight Dragon, and a Reveler mounted on the dragon — a Braggart Swashbuckler built as Graz's opposite number, with Perform for Panache, Confident Finisher and a starknife.
  • Chthon'ar'ax grapples the Wyrmwraith and it critically fails its Escape — 33 against DC 48. Grabbed, immobilized, off-guard and frightened until Tam deletes it.
  • Graz deals 238 damage in two actions to the Reveler, including a natural-20 finisher for 174.
  • The Reveler gets one attack all encounter and rolls a natural 1.
  • Both dragon breath weapons target Graz. He critically succeeds both, on nat 12s.
  • Jassik fails both Will saves on nat 7s, spends a round Confused swinging at his own healer, and finishes having dealt zero damage.
  • Cody, on the enemy dice: "Ooh, double nat 1s. Makes sense. I had to pay for the worms."
  • Graz takes 11 damage all fight — from Shuyinlo's own cold rune.
Act III · Combat · The Battlemage and the Lich Legion · 14:42 – 15:09

The fight that inverted the party

  • A Battlemage backed by the Lich Legion — three troops, AC 41, ~350 HP each across four segments.
  • The troops broke the party's damage hierarchy in half. Weakness 15 to area damage per instance, per troop; resistance 15 against single-target physical Strikes; immune to bleed; and, by Cody's ruling, impossible to Demoralize.
  • That single ruling switched off Battle Cry, Threatening Approach, Antagonize, Dazzling Display and Aura of Despair simultaneously. Graz: "Well, here comes the shit part for me."
  • Keegru, the healer, was the top damage dealer at 528 — ahead of both dedicated strikers. His 7th-rank Fireball collected the area weakness on two troops from one roll: 66 + 117 = 183 damage, killing Lich Legion B outright.
  • Shuyinlo etches Germantria, Rune of Partnership onto Graz — a damage-share that splits everything Graz takes onto himself. "I look forward to getting the shit kicked out of me."
  • He also builds a portal engine — Tear Rift, Define the Canvas — and never gets to use it. The fight ends one initiative slot before his round-3 turn.
  • Graz deletes the Battlemage in a single turn: Tumble Through (critical success by +16, granting panache and off-guard) into two critical hits for 162 and 149.
  • Chthon'ar'ax chases the teleporting Battlemage with a Long Jump that crits by +32 — and leaves the party standing in four Falling Stars bursts. Cody: "who left you all to get nuked. What a jerk."
  • Tam's Aid ends the encounter, converting Chthon'ar'ax's Miss-by-1 into the kill on the last troop.
Act IV · Combat · The Adamant Sentinels and the Norn · 15:27 – 16:17

Fifty minutes. The longest fight of the day.

  • Four Adamant Sentinels at AC 45 / ~345 HP (book: 42 / 255) — resistant to physical, immune to mental, and pointedly not immune to fire. Cody, the moment Graz opens: "Unfortunately, they are mindless."
  • The Norn at AC 47 / ~650 HP (book: 46 / 375), with cold-iron-gated regeneration nobody could turn off.
  • She opens with rank-10 Tear the Veil, DC 47 Fortitude. Jassik, Chthon'ar'ax and Tam fail; Graz and Keegru succeed; Shuyinlo critically succeeds. Then she spends Shift Fate to reverse Keegru's success into a failure for 48 damage.
  • Three Vent Flames cones back to back — ~323 fire damage in one round. Cody, narrating the party's afternoon in two words: "Getting crispy."
  • Phantasmagoria confuses Chthon'ar'ax and Shuyinlo into friendly fire. Round 2 is the low point of the session.
  • Jassik's Reactive Strike disrupts the Norn's Snip Thread. Cody flags the manipulate trait himself; Jassik crits for 102; her 125-point void death effect — which she had three of — never resolves once all fight.
  • Jassik kills the Norn on a swing Foundry had scored as a miss, after Cody worked out the software wasn't applying her prone off-guard.
  • Graz takes three of the five knockdowns, all three set up by a Tumble Through, including a natural-20 finisher for 136.
  • Sentinel A silently fell out of the Foundry initiative tracker for rounds 2 and 3. Graz spotted it and told Cody at 16:07:18. A quarter of the enemy melee skipped two rounds — part of why nobody dropped in the hardest fight so far.
Act V · Combat · The Champions of Gorum · 16:20 – 17:19

617 damage before an enemy acted — and it still took fifty-six minutes

  • Three champions: Sword Master (AC 47→48, ~600 HP), High Cleric (AC 47), Monk (AC 46, Perception DC ~51).
  • Graz's Battle Cry Demoralize crits at initiative → Frightened 2 → the Sword Master is off-guard to Tam's Dread Striker before anyone has taken a turn.
  • Tam deals 316 (a 148 crit plus a 168 amped Imaginary Weapon). Graz follows with 301 across two Perfect Finisher crits. 617 damage in two consecutive PC turns.
  • Gorum's Major Boon — heal to half, become Doomed 1 — was three charges shared between all three champions. The party believed it belonged to the Sword Master alone and focused him. He ate all three. The other two got none and died on their first drop. The wrong premise produced the right play.
  • Graz's Hide fails against the Monk — a 49 beat the Sword Master's DC 46 and lost to the Monk's ~51. The Monk walks over and opens a two-round beating.
  • Graz's Dazzled rider erases a critical Crashing Slam on a failed DC 5 flat check. Cody: "All right, because Dazzled."
  • Dazzling Display: three Demoralizes, three critical successes — one via Graz's only hero point of the day, one on a natural 20, with Exemplary Finisher stripping the immunity to make the third legal.
  • Shuyinlo moves Germantria off Graz. Graz eats 151 and 111 unsplit over the next two turns.
  • Graz kills the Sword Master with a 181-damage Perfect Finisher crit. Jassik kills the High Cleric. Graz kills the Monk on a nat 20 chained into a second crit.
  • Tam takes zero damage the entire encounter — the observation that drove Cody's own postmortem.

Character Spotlight

Graz · the Gladiator Who Never Stopped Climbing

  • Dealt roughly 4,100 damage and took ten of the enemy kills — all three Magma Worms, the Delight Dragon, the Battlemage, three Adamant Sentinels, the Sword Master and the Monk of Gorum.
  • Missed one attack in four hours. Forty strikes, twenty-three critical hits, zero critical misses, against ACs climbing from 40 to 48.
  • The loop: Battle Cry Demoralize at initiative → Threatening Approach or Dazzling Display to hold Frightened 2 → Exemplary Finisher wiping the immunity so it can happen again → Perfect Finisher rolling two dice on every swing → Illimitable Finisher chaining a second finisher off the regained panache.
  • His debuffs were the party's damage. The Sword Master's 617-point collapse runs entirely through one free action: his Battle Cry crit made the target off-guard to Tam before anyone had taken a turn.
  • Six Tumble Throughs, six critical successes, against Reflex DCs up to 47 — his answer whenever fear stopped working, and the setup for four of his kills.
  • He got shut out twice and adapted both times. The Sentinels were immune to mental; the Lich Legion couldn't be Demoralized at all. "Well, here comes the shit part for me." Then he found the floor of the kit — "I still gain my panache, even if it's immune" — and used Demoralize as a battery instead of a debuff.
  • Went to 0 exactly once, on a 39-point ray, after his damage-share rune had been moved to someone else. Never went unconscious, and never stopped swinging.

Tam al'Thor · Always Aiding

  • 17 attacks, 17 hits, 10 crits — he did not miss once all session.
  • Took zero damage in encounters 1, 2 and 5, and ~174 across the entire gauntlet. A 120-foot sniper the champions never found an answer to — the observation that drove Cody's own postmortem.
  • "Always." When Cody asked whether he was aiding, that was the answer, and the table adopted it as a standing ruling. One Aid in encounter 2 turned Graz's finisher into a 143-damage crit; another ended encounter 3 outright.
  • His round-one volley in the finale — a 148 crit plus a 168 amped Imaginary Weapon — is half of the 617 damage that broke the Sword Master before he ever acted.
  • Killed the Wyrmwraith outright with an Eldritch Shot crit, and set up the Norn's death by knocking her prone with Transform Ammunition.
  • Opened the session unable to shoot — a level-20 gunslinger with no bolts in his inventory. Cody had to hand him ammunition.

Chthon'ar'ax · Dragon All the Way Down

  • The wall. Absorbed ~675 in encounter 4 — 45% of everything the enemy dealt that fight — and 577 more in the finale. Roughly 1,700 across the gauntlet, about 39% of everything the party took.
  • Hit 0 HP three times in the finale and came back every time: Sacred Covenant, a silent Orc Ferocity nobody noticed, and a genuine unconsciousness that took a 10th-rank Heal to reverse. Finished at Wounded 3.
  • Two grapples decided two fights. The Wyrmwraith critically failed its Escape at 33 vs DC 48 and never got going; the Reveler was pinned with its mount already gone and never acted again.
  • Ate his way out of a Magma Worm. "All I've got are unarmed strikes, so — you know what, let's eat from the inside out." 82 damage against Rupture 36. Then, considering it: "This feels like one of those videos of frogs that try to eat each other."
  • His build's one real counter arrived in encounter 4: a grappler against four physically-resistant constructs. Grapple, Crushing Grab resisted to zero, a critically successful Whirling Throw resisted to zero. He shrugged and kept tanking.
  • Dropped by a Reactive Strike while trying to Battle Medicine himself inside the Sword Master's reach — the most human mistake of the night, made at Wounded 2.

Jassik · The Man Who Cancelled the Death Effect

  • Killed the Norn and the High Cleric — two of the gauntlet's three hardest targets — on a night when his dice were genuinely awful: three critical misses and six misses in the finale alone.
  • The single highest-leverage action of the session was his. A Reactive Strike on the Norn's Snip Thread, crit for 102, and her 125-point void death effect — which she had three of — never resolved once in the entire fight.
  • Killed the Norn on a swing Foundry had scored as a miss, after Cody worked out the software wasn't applying her prone off-guard.
  • Killed the High Cleric knowing exactly what it would cost — he confirmed the retaliation damage before swinging, took the shot anyway, and Inviolable duly hit him for 9.
  • Encounter 2 was a disaster for him — nat 7 on both dragon saves, slowed out of round 1, confused through round 2 swinging at his own healer, zero damage dealt. He said the honest thing: "I feel bad for just swinging away, but I don't really have a whole lot else to do here." Two fights later he cancelled a death spell and killed a Norn.
  • Bought five potions for five combats and drank one in every single fight, exactly as planned.

Keegru · The Reason Everyone Walked Out

  • Healed roughly 900 HP mid-combat — 491 in encounter 4 alone — on top of four between-fight Garden of Healing pulses that reset the entire party each break.
  • In encounter 3 he was the party's top damage dealer at 528, ahead of both dedicated strikers. His 7th-rank Fireball collected the troops' area weakness twice on one roll — 183 damage, killing Lich Legion B outright.
  • Sacred Covenant is the reason this was a no-death session. Pre-cast on all six PCs. It fired twice in the finale and both times prevented an unconsciousness outright.
  • Brought Chthon'ar'ax back from actually unconscious with a 10th-rank Heal through Echoing Channel, then topped him up with a second Battle Medicine that only exists because of the Medic archetype.
  • Did all of it while being the most-targeted PC in encounter 4, and taking a 126-point Stand Still crit plus a 163-point flurry in the finale — the Monk hunting him "for healing reasons."
  • His one bad roll changed a fight. Roaring Applause at DC 47 Will; the worm rolled a natural 20. A plain success would have denied it reactions, and four minutes later it used Fast Swallow to eat Chthon'ar'ax.

Shuyinlo Gallalva · The Man Who Volunteered to Be Hit

  • Etched Germantria, Rune of Partnership onto Graz and then spent the session eating half his damage — announced cheerfully, in advance: "I look forward to getting the shit kicked out of me."
  • Swallowed whole by a Magma Worm in round one of the session and simply kept working — four PC turns inside a stomach, throwing Engraving Strikes and invoking two runes at once from within.
  • His build and his backstory are the same object: an elf of ancient Thassilon whose signature rune is Aiuen, The Elf-Gate Key — the Aiudara gates he was born among, turned into one Stride per turn that ignores distance. "Distance be damned."
  • Saved Graz's life with Warping Pull on the called-shot Overwhelming Blow — teleporting him and granting resistance on the one attack Cody had announced would be a maximum-damage critical.
  • His four-tradition Annihilating Compound Invocation was the most elaborate thing anyone attempted all night — and netted 45 damage into a 345 HP construct while consuming the rune that was actually the right answer. The healing invocation a round later, 137 HP plus a group reposition, is what the feat is for.
  • Also killed the Reveler with an En-diacritic cold emanation that caught three of his own party. He knew: "It makes it a 15-foot emanation, so it does catch some of us, but it should be fine."

Pax's Notes — Graz's Journal

Private notes, observations, and things to think about. Pax's notes only — not shared with the rest of the party.

What Graz did this session

  • Opened every fight the same way and it worked every time: Battle Cry Demoralize at initiative, free action, before anyone moved. Critically succeeded on it in the finale and that one roll produced 617 damage in two turns.
  • Killed ten enemies across five fights.
  • Missed one attack all day. Forty swings, twenty-three crits.
  • Tumble Through six times, six critical successes. The fallback whenever fear was illegal — against the mindless Sentinels it was the entire off-guard engine.
  • Passed all thirteen saving throws, ten of them critically.
  • Spent one hero point, on a Dazzling Display reroll against the High Cleric. Turned a failure into a critical success.
  • Hit 0 hit points once, and was caught by Sacred Covenant before going down.

Things Graz knows that the party does not

  • The Foresight reaction can be pointed at the enemy, not just at himself. He spent the whole gauntlet using it to reroll his own saves. Its other half makes an attacker roll twice and take the lower — against a called-shot Overwhelming Blow that would have been the better use, and nobody at the table knew it was on the menu.
  • Moving Germantria off him is what got him killed. He then ate 151 and 111 unsplit and went to zero on a 39-point ray. The rune was worth more than it looked.
  • His Hide is not universal. A 49 beat the Sword Master's DC 46 and lost to the Monk's ~51. Legendary Sneak didn't fail — the Monk was simply out of its reach, and walked over and opened a two-round beating because of it.
  • The Dazzled rider on his finisher erased a critical Crashing Slam. Not the frightened, not the AC — a DC 5 flat check. The best defensive moment of his night came from a condition he applies as an afterthought.
  • Exemplary Finisher is doing more work than the damage feats. Without it, Demoralize is once per enemy per ten minutes and the whole loop dies after one turn.

What Graz is thinking about

  • Fortitude is still the hole, and it never got properly tested. Three saves, cleared by +6, +4 and +2 — all successes, none upgraded, because that axis has no clause. Reflex and Will each converted five saves into critical successes on the same nights.
  • Tam is the multiplier. Frightened 2 from Battle Cry turns straight into Dread Striker off-guard, and Tam's standing Aid turns normal hits into crits. Two of Graz's biggest turns exist because Tam was doing something on someone else's initiative.
  • Fear got switched off twice, by two different mechanisms, and the fallback held both times. He still dealt 454 and 988 in those two fights.
  • Against troops the whole striker chassis is the wrong tool. Resistance 15 to single-target physical, immune to bleed, four HP segments, and a footprint that moves when a segment dies — a finisher literally removed the square he was standing next to. Keegru's Fireballs out-damaged him.
  • Opportune Backstab earned its slot. It killed three enemies outright, including one where giving up the hidden position was the explicit cost and it was still correct.
  • Three actions is rarely three attacks. The finisher trait shuts off attacks afterwards; the third action was always setup, Hide, or Shield.

Open questions for next session

  • Cody is tuning it up and specifically wants an answer to ranged characters. What does that do to a melee reach build that relies on being adjacent to apply fear?
  • Is there a way to raise Fortitude that isn't blocked by Greater Resilient's item bonus and Heroism's status bonus? Nothing found before this session; nothing tested during it.
  • Is it worth asking Shuyinlo not to move Germantria off? It's the only thing that actually dropped Graz.
  • The Monk of Gorum's Perception was roughly 51. What tier of enemy makes Legendary Sneak stop working entirely, and how common is that at this level?
