# Servant War (working title, no real name yet)

A PF2e homebrew idea: teams fight to the death across an island for a grand prize, each with a legendary Servant at their side. It could start as a one-shot or short adventure and grow into a campaign.

**Status:** early idea, as of 2026-11-02. Five Toe Cove comes first. "Decided" means Pax chose it. "Proposed" means it came up in discussion and he hasn't chosen it.

## The pitch

Think Fate/stay night's Holy Grail War crossed with the tournament of Fists of the Ruby Phoenix, except it's life or death and only one team can win the prize.

## Decided

**Setting**
- The war takes place across an island or similar big territory, not a confined city.
- The threats are monsters and the other teams.
- Teams also face survival and sustain challenges.
- A big territory also copes better with varied player numbers.

**Teams**
- The party is one team and stays together.
- This differs from Fate/stay night: each team has several people plus one powerful Servant as a follower.

**The seven Servants**
- Prep seven Servants, one for each Fate class: Saber, Archer, Lancer, Rider, Caster, Assassin and Berserker.
- Each one is a fixed hero, not a pick from a pool, so unique abilities can be built around that hero's actual history.
- NPC teams are prepped first, without their Servants in mind.
- The party rolls a d7 for its Servant (or a d8, rerolling an 8). As with summoning in Fate, nothing is guaranteed.
- The other six Servants are then distributed among the NPC teams, so no prepped Servant goes unused.

**Session zero**
- The campaign starts with a session zero to explain the homebrew rules and roll for the Servant.
- The roll happens before players finalize their characters, so they can build around the result.

**Servant rules (first draft, not yet playtested)**

| | Not commanded | Commanded |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | Nothing | The commanding PC spends 1 action on their turn |
| **Actions** | 2 | 3 |
| **When it acts** | End of the round | Right then, on the commander's turn |
| **What it can do** | Basic things only | Everything, including its strong abilities |
| **Who runs it** | The GM, as an NPC, with player input | The commanding player |

- Only one PC can command it each round, so the party has to decide who needs it most.
- If commanded, it doesn't act again at the end of the round.
- Strong abilities are available **only** when a PC spends the action.
- A strong Servant is fine. It's run as a strong companion.

For comparison, the normal PF2e minion rule gives 2 actions only when commanded, and almost nothing otherwise. A similar house rule ran in Orion's Korvosa game, where Ishani had one action a round, plus two if a PC Commanded him.

**Why this design**
- The GM doesn't mind running an NPC. The real worry is spotlight.
- Basic uncommanded actions don't take the spotlight, and the big moments always belong to a player.
- It creates combos, because the Servant acts on the commander's turn:
  - A PC can buff up or Demoralize, then command the Servant to attack.
  - A caster Servant can debuff a target, then the commander attacks it.

## Proposed, not decided

- **Command Seals:** three per war, shared by the team, never refilled. Uses could include forcing an order, teleporting the Servant to you, or a big burst of power. A Servant might refuse an order against its nature unless a Seal is spent.
- **Secret identities:** Servants hide their true names. Scouting and Recall Knowledge reveal who they are, which exposes a real weakness. The party's Servant has one too, and rivals can dig it up.
- **Mana as the survival layer:** the Servant draws on the team. Holding territory, leylines or food keeps it strong, and running low weakens it. This would tie survival and combat together.
- **Rival teams:** recurring teams with personalities, truces and betrayals, so their deaths matter. A team whose Servant dies is still alive, desperate and dangerous.
- **Enemy tell:** rivals follow the same rule, so an enemy Master spending an action on a Command warns the party that a big move is coming.
- **Party tension:** an "only one wish" prize could slowly crack the party itself.
- **Island pressure:** weather, monsters and scarce food push enemies into each other.
- **Build method:** build Servants as creatures using PF2e's monster-building numbers, not as full PC class builds.
- **Levelling:** a shared table of numbers by level, with signature abilities unlocking over time, so seven stat blocks don't need rebuilding at every level. The big ability could tie into the secret identity reveal.
- **Legends as clues and weaknesses:** a hero's history gives away who they are and how to beat them. Achilles could have high resistances that a discovered weak spot bypasses. Cú Chulainn's taboos could let a rival beat him with a trap at a feast rather than in a fight.
- **Session zero practice fight:** a short fight with the rolled Servant, so everyone feels the command rule before finishing their builds.
- **Mismatched pairings:** because teams are written without a Servant in mind, the roll can create drama, such as a pacifist healer with Berserker or a schemer with Saber.

**First pass at the classes (proposed)**

| Class | PF2e inspiration | Uncommanded (basic) | Commanded (strong) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Saber** | Fighter/champion | Strike, Stride, protect the Master | Big sword finisher |
| **Archer** | Ranger (Hunt Prey) | Ranged Strike, reposition | Barrage or a single huge shot |
| **Lancer** | Reach fighter (Reactive Strike) | Strike with reach, hold a line | Lethal thrust or long-range charge |
| **Rider** | Cavalier | Move, Strike | Mounted charge through a line of enemies |
| **Caster** | Wizard | Cantrip-level magic | Big spells or terrain control |
| **Assassin** | Assassin archetype (Mark for Death) | Sneak, Strike | Mark and kill |
| **Berserker** | Barbarian | Attacks the nearest target | Devastating rampage |

Possible hooks from Fate's class traits:
- **Archer:** Independent Action could allow one strong ability per fight without a command.
- **Caster:** Territory Creation fits the island, since it gets stronger on ground it holds.
- **Berserker:** Mad Enhancement could make it hit hardest but be the least controllable and the hungriest for mana.
- **Assassin:** Mark for Death costs 3 actions, so that version could only be used on a commanded turn. It probably needs to hit harder than the archetype's version.

## Open questions

1. Whose legends are the seven heroes? Claude's suggestion is real-world myth, because players can guess identities from clues they half-know, with summoning from beyond the world as the in-world reason. Golarion legends and original legends both mean teaching each story before a reveal can land. This also decides whether the game is set in Golarion (question 8). Merlin and Morgan can't both be Caster.
2. Does the Servant get a reaction? Suggested: yes, but only a signature one, like a guardian protecting its Master.
3. What happens at 0 HP? It could fade for the fight, cost mana to bring back, or die for good.
4. What can the Servant do outside combat, like scouting, talking or carrying out orders? This matters a lot on a survival island.
5. How strong should it be? Suggested: level-appropriate, and strong through unique abilities rather than raw numbers.
6. What's the prize, why does this war exist, and who runs it?
7. How long is the game: one-shot, short adventure or campaign? Seven Servants implies seven teams, the party plus six NPC teams.
8. Is this Golarion or an original setting?

## To test

- **Action math:** a third action on a Strike takes a heavy penalty in PF2e, so the extra action is mostly worth it if strong abilities cost 2 or 3 actions. A 2-action signature move plus a move or shield is a good default to try.
- **Balance:** a strong ally makes normal encounters too easy, and both sides having one is harder to tune. Run one sample fight on paper.
- **Two jobs:** every Servant has to be a fun ally for players and a dangerous enemy under the GM. Test each one both ways.
- **Random fit:** every Servant has to be good for any party. Watch for overlap, like a melee-heavy party with Saber, and for Assassin being weak in open fights. A mismatch can be good story, as long as it's still fun to play.
