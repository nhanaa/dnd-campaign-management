Session Summary

Book Two's road finally has a map on it. The party finishes picking over Baolen's chamber and finds that the nine alcoves are a gallery of Runelord landmarks — the Black Squares of Ungevik, Sekhnavali, the Storval Stairs, Rimeskull, the Dome Hive, the Sunken Queen — and, in the fourth alcove, a flawless green gem that is not a gem. The Emerald Eye is a living blueprint of the Emerald Chambers, and studying it that night in Absalom gives up the one thing nobody had: where the entrance is. The Velashu Uplands, a sprawl of grassland in the Saga Lands, three days out on Kandirvek's back.

Between the temple and the road, two pieces of housekeeping. Paya runs the sell-off — the kukri, the tridents, the shield, the Staff of Arcane Might, the Bands of Force, the Potency Crystal all go out the door for 4,788 gp each — and Sevek finally claims the greater flaming runestone that has been sitting in the stash since Quoroc's vault. And Kandirvek makes an introduction: an old ally of his, a mythic one, a gnome who looks like a kindly grandfather until you notice the blood-red left eye. Kreebnock floats a few feet off the ground, bows without landing, and says he's looking forward to ending this threat so he can get back to wandering.

The Uplands are not empty. Three armies are camped around the dais where seven enormous carved Runelord heads circle in the air: Velashu Horselords under Nood Helarsin, a Shoanti tribe under Roaring Storm, and — the reason everyone is nervous — an army flying the flag of Runelord Belimarius, led by an Azlanti man with violet eyes calling himself General Lograsi. Paya calls the approach ("trying to stop Doomsday by starting a war seems counterproductive") and suggests the white flag; Aureys runs the parley, and over an honest accounting of what they've come for, wins a truce. Lograsi is delighted: the heroes can deal with the heads, and "all the better for us."

The heads are the Trial of Runes. Mithtirith presents the party's mythic power as proof of a right to rule, and then six of the seven Runelords ask a question each. The party answers by playing to each sin, workshopping every one out loud, and lands five of six. Only Alderpash rejects them: the table's answer was to ally with the weaker enemy and betray them later, and Cody ruled the question had already named both as enemies. Then the seventh head — Xanderghul's — takes a long pause and refuses the game entirely. "Nothing you say will suffice, intruders. The only answer I'll accept is your death." His head splits like an eggshell and a peacock phoenix unfolds out of it; the five satisfied heads crash to the ground, and only Alderpash stays airborne, eyes lighting with runic fire.

That five-out-of-six is the whole fight. The Rune Sentries are a complex hazard that gets one action per surviving head — six heads would have meant six Lightning Arcs a round, roughly 190 points of electricity the party never had to eat. They faced one. It is still an extreme-threat fight: the phoenix opens with a 102-damage Reactive Strike crit that puts Paya on 69 before round one ends, then drops Phantasmagoria on everyone and Confuses Sevek and Mithtirith — Sevek puts 38 into Paya's back, and Mithtirith swings a staff in melee for the first time in fifteen levels and rolls a natural 1. Aureys does 188 of the phoenix's 400 herself, moves her astral rune onto her fan mid-fight to beat its incorporeal resistance, and finishes it with a keen claw crit. She takes zero damage all fight. The sentry itself is an object — hardness 28, immune to crits — and simply cannot be killed fast; Mithtirith disables it on his third legendary Arcana attempt, at DC 45, with 10 HP left on the thing.

Below, the Emerald Chambers: infinite branching corridors that swallow people for a lifetime, navigable only by the Eye. A day in, five Silent Devotees come around a corner at a sprint and make no sound at all — pale, eyeless things with emerald pits for sockets, wearing a ten-foot aura that suppresses hearing and speech, teleporting thirty feet a turn without provoking, and dealing sneak-attack damage to anyone who is merely bleeding. Cody ran five PL+0 bodies at 325 HP each: 200 XP, the exact Extreme threshold for a five-player table, and forty-eight minutes of real time.

Another day's walk, a full rest, and then an emerald door bearing the seven-pointed Star of Thassilon. Through it: the Crypt of Runes itself — an eighty-foot circular chamber, seven doors around a seven-pointed star, six ajar and the seventh sealed in glowing runes. And one occupant. Czaresia, a rune giant with the rune of pride carved over and over into her skin. Six hundred hit points, a twenty-foot reach, and a Wide Swing that rolls once and applies to two people. Sevek Trips her, Taunts her twice, and takes 188 — but the Taunt does not keep her off the casters: in round three she walks past everyone to Wide Swing Kreebnock and Mithtirith, eating the off-guard to do it. Paya answers with two natural 20s. And in round four, with the giant at 27 hit points, Aureys puts both halves of an Earth to Heavens Strike through her.

Six doors stand open. The seventh is still sealed.

The Session, Act by Act

Act I · Exploration · The Nine Alcoves

A gallery of everything the Runelords built

  • The party finishes searching Baolen's chamber under the Isle of Xin. The pocket alcoves are murals, each depicting a Runelord landmark; Cody waives the checks — "there's no reason you wouldn't be able to identify these."
  • Counterclockwise from the south: the Black Squares of Ungevik (Zutha's necromantic engine, still under the sea off Varisia); Sekhnavali, the Hanging Monastery, greatest temple to the peacock spirits; the Storval Stairs and the colossi flanking them (Karzoug); and the Emerald Chambers themselves — built as a memorial for First King Xin and a tomb for the Runelords who followed.
  • Aureys searches the Emerald Chambers alcove and turns up a single flawless green gem: the Emerald Eye. Peering into it shows shifting passageways — a live map. Aureys: "It will be a centerpiece of my hoard." Paya: "Yes, but first it'll be very useful in our travels." Sevek: "It can be both."
  • Mithtirith insists on seeing the rest — "I'll indulge my obsession with the Runelords" — and gets five more: the obelisk forest of Mundatei; the Shining Queen (today the Sunken Queen), Sorshen's pyramid in the eastern Mushfens, whose carving Cody notes bears a striking resemblance to Xin-Eurythnia; Rimeskull, whose skull-carved mountainside once bore First King Xin's face; the Dome Hive and its legions of bureaucrats; and Azaradne's Gardens, grown by the third Runelord of Sloth from seeds that never needed tending.
  • Up and out. Kandirvek is circling above the isle. Aureys holds up the gem. "Plus, I like flying on your back."
Act II · Downtime & RP · Absalom, and a New Ally

The sell-off, and a floating grandfather

  • Kandirvek: "I took the liberty of contacting an old ally of mine… They've mythic power like yourselves." Aureys: "They're more than welcome to join our little band." Paya: "Doom-driven heroes should work together."
  • The sell-off, Paya running it as loot master: the +2 greater striking keen kukri, three +2 greater striking shock tridents, the Major Sturdy Shield, the Greater Staff of Arcane Might (nobody can invest a second staff), the second Greater Bands of Force, and the Major Potency Crystal ("unless you have an Investigator, they're generally just very sad"). Kept: the Scroll of Teleport, the scroll case, the countering charm — and the Emerald Eye. 4,788 gp each.
  • The greater flaming runestone finally lands. Sevek: "I will take the greater flaming, though, if you guys don't mind." The S7 stash argument is over in one sentence. The Runic Skullcap goes to Mithtirith; Paya keeps the Paradigm Cube — "if you spend a mythic point on it, you just get to choose, and I do like that."
  • Kreebnock's introduction. An elderly gnomish man, orange and green hair, kindly-grandfather face ruined by a scarlet blood-red left eye (the right is bright amber). He floats the entire conversation and never touches the ground. "You can call me Kreebnock, or Kree, if you prefer. I've been called both." Aureys, after he mentions his mother's preference: "Alright, let's not offend the mama."
  • Aureys introduces herself as a very small dragon in slightly oversized robes: "I am Aureys. Hello. You may have heard of me." Kreebnock has: "Kandirvek did mention you and your companion." Paya removes his helmet to reveal a pineapple head, explains he's a fruit leshy, and shakes hands: "Hi, I'm Paya. Nice to meet you."
  • Asked whether he's coming out of retirement, Kreebnock: "Oh, no retirement. Just wandering. My kind of retirement."
  • Mithtirith's Staff of Healing won't cast above 3rd rank. A few minutes of Cody-and-Paya debugging finds it: the staff was set to three charges instead of eight. "Oh, that's an easy fix."
  • Studying the Emerald Eye overnight gives up the entrance: the Velashu Uplands, hundreds of miles of rolling grassland home to Shoanti tribes and Velashu Horselords. Three days on dragonback. "Once you're in the Crypt of Runes, it may take some time before you emerge again." Paya buys bottled sunlight.
Act III · Social · Three Armies

A truce between people who intend to kill each other

  • The destination is a stone dais on the highest hill for miles, with seven enormous carved heads circling in the air above it — Xirie, Xanderghul, and others, each a perfect and eerily lifelike likeness. Around the hill: three full-sized armies at a standoff.
  • Velashu Horselords; a Shoanti tribe; and an army in steel and enchanted gear flying the flag of Runelord Belimarius — "prior to the Risen of the Trinity Star and Queen Sorshen herself, the only surviving Runelord." Mithtirith asks about Belimarius' history with Sorshen: they have never gotten along, Sorshen was the more powerful by a wide margin, and with Sorshen out of the picture Belimarius may be feeling confident.
  • Mithtirith also asks the sharpest question of the scene, about the scenery: "the description of the carved Runelord heads makes it sound like they're iron stones revolving around something. Is that the vibe it's giving off?" Cody: "It's not dissimilar magic, but they're too large to be iron stones."
  • Paya says the obvious thing out loud — "trying to stop Doomsday by starting a war seems counterproductive" — and it's Paya who proposes the white flag and the neutral ground. Aureys agrees and takes the talking.
  • One rider from each army. Nood Helarsin of the Horselords, mid-thirties, battle-scarred, auburn. Roaring Storm of the Shoanti, deep scowl, bird tattoos across her neck and scalp. And from Belimarius' army, an Azlanti man with a well-groomed beard, black leather armor, and almost-glowing violet eyes: General Lograsi.
  • Introductions all round. Aureys: "my companions and I are heroes of these lands. You may have heard of me." Paya: "Hi, I'm Paya. We're here to stop Doom." Sevek: "I'm just a guy that's trying to do good." Kreebnock bows without landing. Mithtirith: "I am Mithtirith, Nethys's humble servant."
  • Lograsi senses the mythic power on them and presses the real question twice: why are you here? Aureys gives the honest answer, and then the dragon answer — "there is probably wonderful, delicious stuff, and maybe a few undead treats inside. Snacks, if you will." Nood: "A dragon, no matter the dragon, is still a dragon, I suppose."
  • Lograsi turns it into a truce: "My lady Belimarius has no care for the Runelord of Pride… I say we allow them the chance to at least deal with those heads, yes? All the better for us." Roaring Storm had already promised him an arrow in the throat and never audibly agrees to anything. Nood nods — to the party, not to Lograsi: "If you've come for that area, by all means. But know, we will be watching."
  • Aureys closes it: "And if we do not return, you may well have to deal with this yourselves." Lograsi: "Confident, aren't we?" Aureys: "I am a dragon after all."
  • Kandirvek stays topside — Thassilonian magic is unreliable down there, he has contacts to work, and he'll make sure nobody follows. His parting read: "I expect once you descend down into the Emerald Chambers, they're primed to start killing one another." Aureys, unbothered: "if you have to kill a few to keep the rest in line, yeah, you do what you gotta do, guy."
Act IV · Puzzle & RP · The Trial of Runes

Six questions from six dead tyrants

  • The seven heads descend as the party sets foot on the dais — each eight feet tall and nearly as wide — and speak in one merged voice: "You seek entrance to the Emerald Chambers, the final resting place of First King Xin. To begin the trial, show us, the Lords of Thassilon, proof of your right to rule through your command of rune magic."
  • Mithtirith's Recall Knowledge reads the assignment: cast a spell of rune magic, or present an item inscribed with it. Cody rules it narrative, not mechanical. Mithtirith presents his own mythic power and his study of Thassilon; the sentries' eyes flash and the heads begin to circle. The party's working theory is his too: play to each Runelord's sin.
  • Kaliphesta, Gluttony — "What separates the living, the mindless dead, the dead that think, and that which moves without spirit?" Mithtirith burns Scholarly Recollection on a riddle rather than a fight. → Aureys: "For what they hunger." ✓
  • Envy — "How does a ruler ensure the loyalty of those who serve?" → Kreebnock, cold, with no table discussion at all: "By serving those beneath him." ✓
  • Greed — "What fills your coffers more quickly, and why: truth or lies?" The table splits — Mithtirith: "I feel like this is a philosophical difference in the party" — and Paya notes he wouldn't lie himself but has watched Aureys do very well at it. → Aureys: "Lies. Because through schemes and deception, you are able to fill your coffer as quickly as possible." ✓
  • Xirie, Sloth — "What is the shortest path to greatness?" Aureys offers "letting it come to you"; Paya offers "sacrifice"; Sevek synthesises and delivers it: "Step on the path that others have laid for yourself, so you don't have to build a path yourself." ✓
  • Alderpash, Wrath — "You have two enemies. One camps at your borders but could be crushed easily. The other poses no immediate threat but is more formidable. How do you proceed?" ✗ The one wrong answer, and it was the whole table's. Aureys opened with "destroy them both"; Kreebnock proposed the mechanism ("play one against the other, and then strike when they're both weakened"); Sevek shaped it — and then raised the exact objection Cody would later rule on: "I think in this question it says exclusively to enemies." Paya and Mithtirith both endorsed the plan; Aureys added the betrayal; Sevek gave the final version anyway. Cody, after: "The word 'enemies' dictates they're already both against you." The party had the right read in the room and answered past it.
  • Sorshen, Lust — "Which of my works will be best remembered?" Aureys names the city, Paya supplies the name, and Aureys delivers it: "Your mighty city of Xin-Eurythnia." ✓
  • Five of six. It's Paya who names the miss afterward, and then immediately lets it go: "I will take one out of six. That's pretty good."
  • The seventh head is Xanderghul's, and it does not ask a question. A long, uncomfortable pause. His head cracks open like an eggshell; an enormous spectral peacock phoenix unfolds out of the iridescent light. Five heads crash to the ground. Alderpash's remains airborne, glowing with runic energy. Kreebnock, on the phoenix: "Cool. I want one of those."
Act V · Combat · The Peacock Phoenix

The difficulty dial nobody knew they were turning

  • See Combat Encounters below for the round-by-round. The short version: the trial answers were the fight's difficulty dial. Cody, mid-fight: "Imagine this fight if you had gotten them all wrong." Paya: "Maybe if you had gotten three wrong even, it would be very hard."
  • The phoenix opens with a 102-damage Reactive Strike crit into Paya, then Phantasmagoria for 63 across the party — Confusing Sevek and Mithtirith, both of whom spent resources on the save and failed anyway.
  • Aureys does 188 of the phoenix's 400, moves her astral rune onto her fan mid-fight to beat incorporeal resistance, and kills it with a keen claw crit. She takes zero damage in six rounds.
  • The Rune Sentry is an object — hardness 28, immune to crits and precision — and the party discovers that the hard way over two rounds. Mithtirith ends it with a legendary Arcana check at DC 45, on his third attempt, with 10 HP left on the thing.
  • After the sentry falls, a staircase manifests in a glyph of runes beneath the dais. Aureys pre-buffs with Bolster Flesh and Summon Mythic Power, reminds the table to do the same, and goes down first looking for traps.
Act VI · Exploration & Combat · Into the Emerald Chambers

A maze that kills people slowly, and five things that do it fast

  • "Tall, sprawling hallways that seem to go on forever. A maze of passageways that spiral out into infinity." Magnificent locked doors, catacombs and tombs that could hold riches, all of it passed by. Cody's line for the place: "It is not an uncommon story to hear of one who somehow finds the Emerald Chambers, dying of old age well before they reach their target plunder." Only the Emerald Eye makes it navigable.
  • Not far in: "You don't hear them. You never hear them." Five Silent Devotees — skin ice pale, eyes pits of emerald green, barefoot, leaving no sound or mark on the tile — sprinting at the party in total silence. Mithtirith, on the art: "Looks like overcompensation to me."
  • The fight runs forty-eight minutes of real time and six rounds. Loot: five +2 greater striking wounding frost longswords. Sevek, watching the party total climb: "I still need some time to get accustomed to the big numbers."
  • A day's travel and a full rest inside the Chambers. Then the destination: a magnificent emerald door inscribed with the seven-sided Star of Thassilon. Aureys pre-buffs at the threshold — Bolster Flesh, then Hollow Robes for invisibility.
  • Through the door, a flash of prismatic light, and the party is somewhere entirely new: the Crypt of Runes. An 80-foot circular chamber, ceiling twice that. A seven-pointed star set into the floor, its tips aimed at seven doors. Six stand ajar; the seventh, directly north, glows with runes. A statue of a long-haired man holding a staff stands on a plinth at the center, facing a mural on the southern wall.
Act VII · Mythic Boss · Czaresia

The guardian of the Crypt of Runes

  • One occupant: a towering female rune giant with burning red eyes and a mace "so large it may as well be a scepter, even by her standards." On every inch of exposed skin, the rune of pride, inscribed over and over.
  • "What is this? Who are you? And how have you all come here?" Kreebnock, deadpan: "Well, it all started with a dream." Paya, flatly honest: "Heroes of Golarion, here to stop Xanderghul."
  • "Is that so? Your honesty would be charming if it wasn't so foolish. I am Czaresia, servant of Runelord Xanderghul and guardian of the Crypt of Runes. I don't know how you all found your way here, but in doing so, you've forfeited your lives."
  • Four rounds, 600 hit points, and a Wide Swing that rolls once and applies to two people. She never got a fourth turn.
  • Aureys kills her with an Earth to Heavens Strike, both halves connecting. Cody calls the session on the description: "the dragonet carves a giant hole through her torso, and there is a torrent of blood that cascades down onto the stones as she falls dead."
  • Cody switches the backdrop to Book Two's art and immediately notes he can't use it as a landing page: "it's a spoiler, you know."

The Peacock Phoenix and Rune Sentry Alderpash

Rune SentriesComplex hazard, run exactly to book: AC 43, hardness 28, 110 HP, immune to crits and precision, disable DC 45 Arcana (legendary)

The setup that decided it. The hazard gets one action per surviving head, each spent on Lightning Arc (3d12+19, DC 42 basic Reflex, random target). Five heads had crashed on correct answers, so the party faced one arc a round instead of six — roughly 190 points of electricity a round they never had to eat. The healing clause is smaller than it sounds and still real: unused arcs restore 20 HP each to the phoenix, and with six sentries against five PCs exactly one would have gone begging — 20 HP a round.

  • Aureys critically fails a Recall Knowledge; Cody gives her the disable method anyway. She Hides in the open on Legendary Sneak.
  • Phoenix flies down and opens Radiant Beam — 77 force — on Sevek and Paya. Sevek spends a mythic point to save at mythic proficiency (33 through); Paya eats all 77.
  • Paya leaps in on Needle in the Gods' Eyes, makes her Radiant Panoply save by exactly zero, and takes a Reactive Strike crit for 102 mental on the way in — 69 of 248 before round one is over. One halberd hit lands for 42.

  • Sevek fuses Reflective Ripple and Whirlwind, Flurries two Trips into the phoenix's Reflex DC 46 and misses by 4 and by 1, and Taunts instead.

Sevek burns his Ascended Celestial Will reroll and still fails; Mithtirith spends a mythic point for mythic proficiency and still fails. Both take 63 and both are Confused.

  • Kreebnock's first Unleash Psyche fires an amped Daze — 46 rolled, halved, 8 through resistance.
  • Sevek, confused, rolls Paya, phoenix, Aureys — 38 into Paya's back, 45 into the phoenix, and a miss on Aureys by 1.
  • Mithtirith, confused, Frostbites Kreebnock for 12 and then swings a staff in melee for the first time in fifteen levels — natural 1. Both shake the condition at end of turn.

Round 3

  • Sevek's Inner Upheaval Flurry misses three times, the last a natural 1. He moves to Group Taunt the phoenix and the sentry together — Cody stops him before any action is spent: "You can't taunt the sentry because the sentry is not a creature. It is a hazard." He Taunts the phoenix instead.
  • Mithtirith three-action Heals for 42 across four PCs.

Round 4 — the phoenix dies on turn two

  • Alderpash finally picks someone else (Paya, 30).
  • Aureys steps to line up an Aether Beam across both the phoenix and the sentry — force, chosen specifically to beat incorporeal resistance — and it does nothing to either: the phoenix critically succeeds, and the sentry's halved 26 dies against hardness 28. Then the fan: natural 19 on a keen rune, critical hit, 90 rolled, 70 through — and the phoenix only had 28 left. "It explodes into iridescent light and is gone." +2 mythic points to Aureys, +1 to everyone else.
  • Phoenix total: 400 damage exactly. Aureys 188, Paya 133, Sevek 45 (all of it thrown while Confused), Kreebnock 34.
  • The rest of the round is the party discovering what an object is. Kreebnock's Blinding Foam rolls a natural 20 — the hazard is immune to critical hits, but Cody doubles it anyway, and that ruling is the only reason 16 gets past hardness. Sevek's Electric Arc does literally nothing, and the Jolt Coil plan dies with it: "it's not really going to help against object hardness, because it's against all damage." Mithtirith's first Arcana: 43 vs DC 45.

Rounds 5–6 — the object

  • For two rounds almost nothing clears hardness 28. Aureys hits it once for 21, absorbed. Sevek hits it once for 33, absorbed. Both of them stop swinging and start Aiding, and that turns out to be the correct read — Sevek's +3 is the entire margin on Paya's round-6 hit (44 vs AC 43).
  • What actually lands: Paya's two Vicious Swings (22, 18) and Kreebnock's amped Imaginary Weapon — "a comically large hammer" — for 43 through hardness, taking it from 54 to 11.
  • Sevek rolls a natural 20 and gets nothing: "it's also immune to crits because it is an object… it only takes the persistent fire at the end of that" — and the persistent fire couldn't beat hardness either. Kreebnock's persistent acid never got a point through; both ticks read "takes no damage."
  • The arc finds Aureys (natural 20, nothing) and then Sevek (48, through a second Prevailing Position).
  • Resolution: not a kill, a disable. Aureys tries first — "this is my job, and I don't care if I have to lie to do it" — Trained Arcana, mythic reroll, 42. Mithtirith goes three times at legendary +29, mythic rerolls on the last two: 43, 43, 46. "Ironically, when it had 10 HP left, that is when you get the success."

Party floors: Paya 69/248 in round 1, Sevek 53/248 in round 3. Aureys took nothing at all.

Encounter 2 · 6 rounds · 15:24–16:11 · 48 minutes of real time

Five Silent Devotees

ScalingBook lvl 14, AC 36, 255 HP. Run at AC 39, 325 HP, attack +33, all saves +3, longsword 3d8+19, Cruel Sneak 3d6 — PL−2 turned into PL+0
Difficulty100 XP (Moderate) as written · 200 XP (Extreme) as run — the exact Extreme threshold for a five-PC party
InitiativePaya 51 · Aureys 48 · C 46 · A 46 · E 41 · B 40 · D 37 · Kreebnock 37 (mythic Prescience) · Sevek 32 · Mithtirith 32
Damage dealtAureys ~584 · Paya ~544 · Sevek ~283 · Kreebnock ~214 — 1,625, exactly five full bars

What they do. Transdimensional Step — a 30-foot teleport with no move, concentrate, or manipulate traits, so it never provokes. Pall of Silence — a 10-foot aura blocking auditory actions and any spell without the subtle trait. Cruel Sneak Attack — +3d6 against anyone off-guard or taking persistent bleed, and their wounding longswords apply bleed on every hit. The loop is self-sustaining: one landed hit makes every later hit a sneak attack, and it ran on Paya, Sevek, and Kreebnock all fight.

Rounds 1–2 — the backline gets teleported into, and B dies

  • Paya opens with Needle in the Gods' Eyes (47 into B). Aureys critically Feints B and puts 126 into it on a natural-20 claw.

Five right answers meant one Lightning Arc a round instead of six — roughly 190 points of electricity a round the party never had to eat — plus denying the phoenix 20 HP a round from arcs with no target. Cody said the quiet part: "Imagine this fight if you had gotten them all wrong." Expect more encounters built this way in this book.
  • Flaming is dead weight twice over right now. Hardness applies to the whole damage packet, not per type, so Electric Arc did zero to the sentry and the flaming rider did nothing either. Then Czaresia was immune to fire and ate the rest of it. The staff has been a stick with a +3 on it for two of three fights.
  • Taunt does not work on hazards. The Rune Sentry was an object; Group Taunt was stopped before an action was spent. Long-Distance Taunt's 120 feet was never the problem. In a dungeon built out of Thassilonian complex hazards, that limitation will come up again.
  • Taunt did not keep Czaresia off the casters. This is the correction worth writing down. With Taunt live in round three, she strode past the frontline and Wide Swung Kreebnock and Mithtirith for 55 and 45, accepting the −1 and the off-guard to do it. Cody said it out loud: "This'll just keep going at the two spellcasters here." What Taunt actually bought was the −1 and the off-guard, and the off-guard is what fed the flanking math — Aureys' 45 Opportune Backstab and two hit-by-exactly-zero strikes.
  • Czaresia has two Reactive Strikes a turn, and Sevek confirmed it the expensive way. Assume every rune giant down here does.

What Sevek is thinking about

  • The new gnome is the softest body the party has ever fielded, and Bodyguard is still assigned to Mithtirith. Kreebnock has 184 HP and AC 37/38, floated the whole session, and still bottomed at 22% — the lowest anyone reached. Mithtirith solved his own exposure problem last book by leaving the ground; Kreebnock is already off the ground and it didn't help. Reassign Bodyguard, or accept that the Taunt lane no longer covers the backline at all.
  • The tank math needs a harder look than "it held." Six Taunts across three fights bought a phoenix that missed and a rune giant that walked past him anyway. Prevailing Position was the reaction that actually paid — it erased a devotee crit worth ~39 and downgraded a giant's crit to a normal hit. The mitigation is in the reactions, not the aggro.
  • The Trial was a skill check the character couldn't roll. Int 10, untrained Arcana, no Thassilon Lore. He answered two questions anyway because this table workshops out loud, and got one wrong. Playing to the sin was the right frame; reading a question's literal terms is the part he missed. The Chambers are a Thassilonian test, and this book is built out of them.
  • Kreebnock is a control-and-buff engine, not a striker, and that raises Sevek's floor. Haste on five bodies, two anthems up every round, amped Dazes, and the single best hit anyone landed on the Rune Sentry. Courageous Anthem was live on several of the few attacks Sevek connected with. The party's action economy just went up by a third.
  • Paya and Aureys are carrying the damage and it is not close — roughly 2,000 of the session's ~2,700. That's fine, but it prices the next class choices toward more control and more mitigation, not toward chasing damage the dice aren't giving.

Third Path halves damage on a failed save of that type; it should have been 31. That's 32 HP he shouldn't have lost, two rounds before he bottomed at 21%.
