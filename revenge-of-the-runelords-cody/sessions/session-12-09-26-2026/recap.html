Session Summary

The session opens with homework. Aethusa — more lucid now, still in no shape to fight — piles a table in the party's rented Absalom quarters with books and scrolls, and Cody runs a three-day research subsystem on Xanderghul's Immortality. It is the party's weakest arena and everyone says so: Mithtirith ("Oh, Mithtirith is so excited, which means the rolls will be low") burns three Mythic Points on it, Sevek declares himself "definitely not book smart" and brute-forces it on Perception — his day-one 50 is what crosses the first threshold — and after one failed reroll Cody observes that the table has "found the party's weakness. Books." They cap it out anyway. What they learn: Aethusa and the Risen Runelords are Xanderghul's soul cages, and because Aethusa is a living person rather than something woven from dream-stuff, Xanderghul must have made a duplicate of her in the Dreamlands to use her as a cage. Kill the duplicate and his link to her is cut. Her real body can be the focus for a planar teleport straight to where the copy is hiding, and the last of the research turns up a scroll of Interplanar Teleport. Meanwhile the allies the party called in are paying off: a Doomsday Door was shut in old Xin-Edasseril (the Risen tried to open it on Belimarius' turf and "she didn't take too kindly to it"), and the doors at Windsong Abbey and Sekhnavali are secured. The real Aethusa's send-off: "Burn her for all I care. Just don't take too much pleasure in it."

Mithtirith casts the teleport, and the party lands on the edge of Cyrusian — a tidy village of a few dozen houses in a forest clearing, ringed by a wall of black trees, where every inhabitant looks human and looks at the party like they have never seen a pineapple or a dragon before. Aureys runs the investigation from the front: the barkeep Thomas explains that the village's "Lord Mayor" — a blonde woman — flies in every sunset to collect tribute of mushrooms and produce, and in exchange the nightmares in the forest stay away. Kreebnock sketches her and Thomas confirms it. The old Lord Mayor turns out to be Simon Cradle, a bow-tied bookkeeper from the Boston Library who dreams his way to Cyrusian between shifts to work on a typewriter — and who figures out, unprompted, that the party is here physically rather than dreaming. He mentions that the new Lord Mayor rides "a big dragon… all green and purple," and Sevek calls it before anyone else: "Sounds exactly like a Mirage Dragon." Aureys' Dragon Lore confirms it's an ancient one. Aureys asks Simon to get the villagers into their basements, and then — as soon as he leaves — steals his typewriter ("Gonna be a centerpiece of my hoard").

At sunset Dream Aethusa arrives on the dragon's back with a rune-engraved bec de corbin. Aureys tries to talk; Dream Aethusa calls it "sickening optimism" and draws steel. The fight runs an hour and a quarter of table time with a break in the middle. She opens with Cataclysm and a quickened Eclipse Burst in the same turn, and the dragon's Hallucinatory Breath confuses Paya and Aureys — Aureys spends her confused turn hitting Sevek ("I'm just taking a beating") until Mithtirith's Restorative Channel counteracts Paya's before the fighter can do the same to him. The turn of the fight is Kreebnock's Falling Sky, which grounds the dragon; after the break, Aureys' Earth to Heavens Strike stupefies it, Paya's Nightmare rune stacks Frightened on top, and Paya kills it with back-to-back crits. Kreebnock's Weight of the World stuns Aethusa, on the heels of a Bon Mot. Sevek lays into her with Herald's Weapon (87 in two hits), Bear Hugs her into a grab, and when she swings at him, Rippling Spin trips her — and she burns a Mythic Point to shake off prone. Paya finishes her.

She doesn't die cleanly. She tears apart into tricolored light and becomes the Trinity Star — a comet with three tails, a hazard that blinds on a bad Fortitude save and dives through the party for 10d6 to 20d6 force. You can't hurt it; you have to pull its tails off. Sevek yanks the first with Athletics, Mithtirith takes the second on Arcana (with Sevek's Guidance lifting a 44 to the 45 he needed), and Paya siphons the last away with a Nature check of 50.

In the quiet afterward, a woman's voice: "Find me. I'm here. Leng. The Amaranthine Oubliette. Come find me, please. It's Lirallaru. I can help you." Cody confirms at the table that Lirallaru is "the person you met at the beginning of the campaign" — met "in our dreams" — and Paya adds, uncontradicted, "She gave us our mythic power." Simon returns, forgives the typewriter ("it was my old one anyway"), trades Aureys stories and a gold coin for paper-money trivia, and explains the trick: because the party is in the Dreamlands physically, they can dream their way to someone they know. They pitch the Dream Hunter's Lodge they took off Dream Aethusa, sleep, and roll for it. Only one of five succeeds: Sevek, on Religion. They wake on the purple-gray plains of Leng, under an iron spindle-prison hanging hundreds of feet above an inverted-pyramid pit, and Lirallaru's voice again: "I am at the top. Watch out for the Bittersweet Sisters."

The gate opens for them as they fly up — Aureys: "I think I should be worried that they're inviting us in" — and inside, three Elder Shantaks uncoil. That fight belongs to Sevek. He opens by critically Tripping one and Taunting all three; when that one stands, Stand Still crits on a natural 20 for 82 and blinds it, and the blind makes its follow-up critical hit on Paya miss its flat check outright. Rippling Spin crit-trips another. Then, with Cody having just announced that "Paya's not the main attack roll culprit. It's Sevek this game": Inner Upheaval, Flurry, four attacks, four hits — 269 damage in one turn, taking the last Shantak from 303 to 34. Paya, watching: "Didn't move, hit with all four attacks from a monk. That's crazy." Aureys kills two of them, Paya finishes the third.

A door creaks in the chamber's southern outbuilding, and a very tall hooded figure with the head of a horse, glowing yellow eyes, and a cage over its face steps out: "Come here. We have much to talk about. I know who you've come to seek. And I can tell you what's waiting for you."
