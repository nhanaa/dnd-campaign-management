Session Summary

Book One ends the way it started: with a question about who was really behind the fall of Xin-Eurythnia, and an answer nobody wanted. The party follows the glowing footprints east through the buried city and immediately runs into the people who live there — four omen dragons, kin to the mirage dragons the party shot out of the sky over the Kodars last book. Aureys handles it exactly as Aureys handles everything, by announcing herself as "the greatest of the dragons of this world" and then telling the absolute truth about killing their relatives. It very nearly goes badly. It doesn't, because she refuses to lie about it and Sevek says the plain thing — "It was self-defense" — and Shephodian, the dragons' spokesman, looks at her and says "I see in your eyes you don't deceive." The dragons let them pass, and hand over the two facts that structure the rest of the session: the Warshard of Gorum is no longer in this city, and a tall blonde woman in black traveling robes came here weeks ago, walked east, and never came back out.

The road east costs them. A cave worm erupts out of the stonework beside the woman's abandoned campsite and opens by critting Paya for 82; three enormous adamantine-toothed shulns burrow up mid-fight and attack the worm, briefly becoming Aureys' beloved allies ("I do consider them my allies at this point") right up until the worm dies and they turn on the party ("No longer my friends"). The camp itself is the real prize: journals belonging to Aethusa, a modern wizard who took the name of the Runelord of Gluttony, full of dreams sent by a cloaked, smoke-like figure — the same one from the apocalypse dream that gave the party their mythic power — promising her the power in Xanderghul's hidden Runewell of Pride. She tracked the Trinity Star here exactly as the party did. Her final entry is about a dream of meeting Xanderghul, being promised secret knowledge, and waking too early. Then the entries stop.

Past a wall of five pridespawn sentinels in peacock robes stands a silver quicksilver portal, and in its reflection Runelord Xanderghul himself steps forward and sneers — the Mirror of Glory, a mythic hazard that spends the next twenty-five minutes showing each of them a different face of Xanderghul's contempt (Cruelty, Jealousy, Worship, Disappointment) while they claw six successes out of it. Aureys lands four of them; Paya, made Jealous, swings his halberd at his own cleric and gets what the DM calls "the worst turn in the history of Paya."

The walk through the portal is the payoff. Four mythic future-selves step out of the quicksilver and narrate what happened here: Xanderghul's ghost flowed into the sleeping Aethusa through her own wards; he looted the tomb of Emperor Jinn for Runelord bones and had her raise Gimmel and the rest of the Risen from them; he then abandoned her body for fresh flesh in a chamber of four immense steel vats; and he intends to open a doomsday door, ride the Oliphaunt of Jandelay out of it, and ascend. In the last image, Xanderghul and Queen Sorshen are locked in battle, whisked through the doors an instant before they slam shut and are buried — the first sign in two sessions that Sorshen was not simply swallowed by the sinkhole.

Beyond the portal: a garden of peacocks and roses under a false noon sky, ten thirty-foot statues of Xanderghul looking down in imperious disdain, a throne backed in gemstone tail feathers — and the Shattered Runewell of Pride, its fragments frozen in midair around a churning magenta beam pouring out of a hole in reality. Two Risen Runelords of Wrath, Angothane and Thybidos, are waiting. Thybidos gives away the whole book in one sentence — "when our Lord Xanderghul cast his judgment on Sorshen and her pathetic stolen city" — Angothane monologues for twelve seconds, and Sevek answers with two words. Seventy minutes later both Runelords have exploded into light, the last runes on the Runewell have gone dark, and the party is level 15.

The Session

Act I · Social · The Omen Dragons

Four dragons, owed an honest answer

  • The party heals at the crater and follows the glowing footprints east. Half an hour in, four omen dragons — green and violet — circle down and land around them in the street.
  • Their spokesman is Shephodian, with Ithariel, Nobukis, and Yvinsbrel. They have studied these ruins for months. His question is polite and pointed: had the party met any mirage dragons out in the Kodars?
  • Aureys answers honestly — they were ridden by the Risen, they attacked the refugee column, the party defended itself. Pressed on what became of them: "Well, as you see that we still stand, I can tell you that they no longer fly." Snarls go up all around the party.
  • Shephodian: "I see in your eyes you don't deceive." Aureys doubles down rather than softening — "I could tell you a lie, but you would know that it was a lie" — and offers friendship instead. Sevek adds the flat version: "It was self-defense. We didn't intend to cause any harm."
  • Mithtirith argues for telling them everything about the Risen riding the mirage dragons, and does. The dragons stand down: "Though I hold no love of you and your friends for what has happened to our kin, we also understand that is the nature of the business." Aureys' secret sense-motive check confirms they mean it.
  • Two revelations. This city is the Trinity Star's landing site — but the Warshard of Gorum has vanished from it, and they cannot say where. And a woman came weeks before: human, tall, black traveling robes, staff, blonde hair, "Thassilonian, we assumed." She descended eastward and never re-emerged; when they tried to hail her she seemed to vanish into thin air.
  • Their parting warning: powerful creatures haunt these upper Darklands, and more Agents of the Risen, who "will not take so kindly to intruders as we." Aureys closes in the one register she rarely uses: "We are prepared to defend ourselves. We of course seek no harm, but we feel we must proceed."
Act II · Combat · The Cave Worm and the Shulns

An apex predator, and three more that showed up uninvited

  • Approaching a scattered campsite, the ground shakes far too suddenly for an earthquake and an enormous worm breaks through the stonework.
  • Mid-fight, three shulns — mole-like Darklands predators with adamantine teeth and claws — burrow up out of fresh pits and start savagely swarming the worm as practiced pack hunters. Cody, drily: "That is fair, but they are also flanking with one another."
  • For one glorious stretch they are, in Aureys' words, allies. They contribute 175 of the 522 damage the worm takes. Then it dies and they look around for the next thing.
  • Full round-by-round in Combat Encounters below.
Act III · Exploration · Aethusa's Camp

The journals of a woman who got here first

  • Aureys searches the camp (Perception 43 with Thorough Search), Sevek and Mithtirith Seeking alongside. It was abandoned in a hurry weeks ago, mid-work: workstations set up, journals on the tables, satchels unpacked.
  • The journals are signed Aethusa — the name of a Runelord of Gluttony, but the writing makes clear this is a modern woman who took the name, as magical pedigrees in the Saga Lands sometimes do.
  • They record dreams of a cloaked figure telling her about power to be claimed from Xanderghul's hidden Runewell of Pride — and the party recognizes the description. It is the same smoke-like being from the dream at the very beginning of the adventure, the one that showed them the apocalypse. Aethusa believed the dreams came from her own past life as the Runelord of Gluttony.
  • She worked out that the Trinity Star struck the Throne of the Troll King. She found the illusion over the shaft — and in her own words, the guardians expected her arrival and the trolls did not stand in her way. The party had to kill Gavergash and four warleaders to reach the same shaft.
  • The final entry: a dream of meeting Runelord Xanderghul and being promised some secret knowledge, only to wake too early to hear it. She wanted the dream again. She made camp for the first time. There are no further entries.
  • Loot: ~300 gp of incense, a Scroll of Teleport, and a Wand of Widening (Phantasmal Calamity) — claimed by Mithtirith, the party's only arcane caster.
  • The party takes a long rest in the dead woman's camp. Sevek argues for it ("I say we risk it… because we don't know what will await us"). Mithtirith admits he's "low on healing," stores a contingency Heal for dropping below zero, and has two Energy Aegis — Sevek breaks the tie in Aureys' favor ("she's a bit more frail").
  • Aureys spends the evening deliberately frightening her own party with Majestic Presence and Frightening Power so they're immune to it for the rest of the day: "Feel my power. You might be shaking a little bit at first, but it'll wear off."
  • Sevek tries to farm a mythic point with a pre-bedtime Long Jump. Cody rules it has to be a jump that would actually be useful; Paya supplies the reasoning — "it's fulfilling your mythic calling, not just jumping up a wall." (He collects it properly two hours later, mid-finale.)
Act IV · Combat · The Sentinels at the Gate

Five pridespawn in peacock robes

  • Half an hour deeper, the trail ends at a palace buried to its outer wall. In the gate hangs a rippling silver portal like a vertical sheet of quicksilver, its reflections distorted and shuddering — and what's reflected inside looks glorious.
  • Guarding it: five sinspawn in resplendent green peacock robes with enchanted bec de corbins. Mithtirith identifies them as pridespawn — resistant to mental damage, with high bonuses on saves against mental effects, which raises the bar on Aureys' entire fear suite.
  • Aureys scouts ahead and rolls Stealth for initiative; Sentinel A spots her anyway. Full breakdown in Combat Encounters.
  • Afterward: five +2 greater striking shock bec de corbins and five +2 resilient raiment leather armors. Cody explains the haul unprompted — he upgrades enemy gear to match creature level as a matter of course, because "they're a level 19 creature and they've got a level 12 weapon. It's like, what the hell?"
  • Mithtirith's new Peer Past the Hedge lets him Detect Magic and Search at once. The archway is carved with peacocks whose tail feathers curve up to meet a glowing keystone set with a colorful cubic gemstone. A stray sound cue makes the portal sound like water, so Sevek starts casting Water Breathing on the whole party — and finishes it even after the table works out that it isn't water at all. Eight hours, no cost, why not.
Act V · Hazard · The Mirror of Glory

Xanderghul steps into the reflection

  • As Mithtirith moves to identify the portal, a resplendent figure steps into the reflection. "For who has spent any amount of time in the Saga Lands could fail to recognize Runelord Xanderghul? First and last Runelord of Pride, and greatest among the Runelords." He sneers, and everyone feels awe and dread.
  • The opening Will save drops Paya, Mithtirith, and Aureys prone and Stupefied 1 for 24 hours. Mithtirith and Aureys buy their way out with mythic rerolls; Paya keeps the condition and stands up the hard way.
  • Disabling it takes six successes at Arcana (disrupt the enchantments), Thievery (disable the runes), or Intimidation (stand up to the sheer arrogance in the reflection) — and you have to stand where the mirror can reflect you to try. Each success darkens one face of the glowing cube and permanently strips one of the mirror's actions.
  • The reward: the Paradigm Cube, a rare mythic illusion item — one color per day, with a flat check you can skip by spending a mythic point.
  • Aureys, who landed the disable, takes +2 mythic points; everyone else +1. Paya then runs Unusual Treatment across the party — "okay, this is when I reveal to you my fun feat" — clearing the Clumsy, Enfeebled, and Stupefied the mirror stapled on for 24 hours.
Act VI · Lore · Four Visions in the Quicksilver

The party's own mythic future-selves explain the whole book

  • Walking the path inside the portal, images race by like the prophecies on Kandirvek's wings — then four solidify into the party's mythic future selves, each stepping out to narrate one piece of what happened here.
  • Paya's vision — the possession. "Runelord Xanderghul returns to the universe and takes the graciously offered body of a minor wizard named Aethusa." She flies down into the ruined city, examines the crater, takes copious notes, makes camp, erects magical defenses, and sleeps. A ghostly Xanderghul descends, passes through her wards as if they weren't there, and flows into her. Her body wakes up laughing, eyes shimmering.
  • Sevek's vision — where the Risen come from. "Runelord Xanderghul lays claim to the tomb of his beloved friend, the late Emperor Jinn." Aethusa walks a crypt collecting the bones and burial raiments of long-dead Runelords, then performs a ritual with the Risen over a pile of dusty bones — and Runelord Gimmel rises out of them. The Risen are manufactured, from real Runelord remains.
  • Aureys' vision — the second body. The Risen carry a human figure wrapped in vibrant cloth into a room where Aethusa lies unconscious amid magical apparatuses and four immense steel vats. Xanderghul's ghost rises out of her and flows into the wrapped body; it tears free of the shroud; the Risen clothe and adorn him in his traditional robes and armaments. Aethusa, still unconscious, is carried away. He blesses each attendant with a touch to the brow and leads them out.
  • Mithtirith's vision — the doomsday door. "Runelord Xanderghul opens a doomsday door to start the process of attaining his well-deserved godhood and lay waste to the undeserving wretches of the Saga Lands." He raises his arms before an enormous door; the earth shakes; the Oliphaunt of Jandelay surges out and slays the Risen in attendance. Xanderghul climbs onto its back. The cavern opens above and a cascade of sunlight and rubble buries the door — and in the last instant the party glimpses Xanderghul and Queen Sorshen locked in battle, whisked away through the doors alongside the elephant, the moment before they slam shut.
Act VII · Combat · The Garden and the Shattered Runewell

Book One's finale

  • The portal opens on an opulent garden: lush plants, hundreds of brilliant flowers, iridescent peacocks strutting about and softly squawking, a night sky overhead but noonday light on the lawn. Twin rows of five thirty-foot statues of Xanderghul look down in imperious disdain over rose bushes in full bloom. On a raised stone platform, four more statues surround a throne whose back is gemstones arranged as peacock tail feathers.
  • At the far end: the Shattered Runewell of Pride — a stone well whose fragments hang suspended in midair as though frozen a split second after its explosion, with a churning beam of magenta and violet light shining up out of a hole in reality, blooming vapors that make the garden flicker where they touch it. This is Kandirvek's fourth vision, arrived at last.
  • Waiting: two pridespawn and two Risen Runelords of Wrath — Angothane and Thybidos.
  • Thybidos: "Foolish pawns. You should have just laid down and accepted your fates when our Lord Xanderghul cast his judgment on Sorshen and her pathetic stolen city." — the confession that Xanderghul destroyed Xin-Eurythnia.
  • Angothane: "You, who have dared to thwart his plans and to have stolen his valor and place in history, will perish in agony as he ascends to walk among the gods and takes his vengeance upon your pitiful doomed world."
  • Paya: "And we will stop him by any means at our disposal. So let's get going." — Sevek: "You talk too much." — Mithtirith: "I mean, honestly, I come from a long line of people thwarting Runelord plans, so welcome."
  • Eight rounds and seventy minutes later, both Runelords have exploded into light and the Runewell's final runes have gone dark. "That brings us to the end of Book One of Revenge of the Runelords, everyone. Congratulations. You all reach level 15."
  • The throne's gemstones — and whatever else is in the garden — wait until next week.

Jaws is 3d10+25 with Deadly 2d10; Cave Worm Venom is Fortitude DC 39 for 5d6→6d6 poison and Enfeebled 2. The shulns have adamantine fangs and claws, resist all physical except adamantine and bludgeoning, and resist poison heavily.

Round 1 — the opening broadside

  • Aureys' Battle Cry Demoralize misses by 8 on a rolled 2.
  • The worm strides in and Thrashes: a Jaws crit on Paya for 82, a Stinger into Sevek for 42, a body strike into Aureys for 35 — "Oh, it hits everything." Only one body strike lands, because Mithtirith is standing 25 feet back outside its reach, a position he does not leave all fight.
  • Sevek fails the venom save even after a mythic-point reroll that comes up nat 4 — 13 poison and Enfeebled 2.
  • Mithtirith opens with a two-action Fast Channel Heal (23 to the front rank) and critically succeeds a Nature Recall Knowledge: best save Fortitude by far, Reflex and Will tied for worst, no weaknesses, and it can swallow you whole.
  • Aureys' Majestic Presence is saved — which still means Frightened 1 — her Feint sticks, and she banks Mithtirith's Heal with an Absorb Magic reaction to go Overflowing. Both strikes miss.
  • Sevek enters Reflective Ripple Stance, casts Guidance, and closes with a Flurry-of-Maneuvers critical Trip (45 vs Reflex DC 35) plus 6 stance damage. The worm ends the round Frightened 1, Prone, Off-Guard — and its attack bonuses and venom DC drop a point for all of round 2.

Round 2 — the Trip lock starts paying

  • The ground shakes again, not from the worm: three shulns surge out of fresh pits and attack it, formation-perfect. Aureys, delighted: "I do consider them my allies at this point."
  • The worm spends its first action standing and eats three reactions on the way up — Paya's Reactive Strike for 30, Sevek's Stand Still for 31, and an Opportune Backstab from Aureys that crit-misses.
  • Then it Thrashes: Jaws crit on Aureys for 76, Stinger into Paya for 39, a body strike that finally misses Sevek. Paya crit-saves the venom; Sevek fails again for 14.
  • Aureys lands a 31 with the fan (stapling Enfeebled 1 on with her Debilitation) and threads an Aether Beam past the moles into the worm for 19.
  • Sevek burns Become Destiny (free action, second mythic point) to shed the poison and the Enfeebled with it, Trips the worm again on the second attempt, switches into Whirlwind Stance for AC 39, and hands Guidance to Paya. The shulns pile on for 86.

Round 3 — the worm dies standing up

  • It stands again — Cody, drily: "not to be a one-trick pony, but this is working for it" — and eats another 69 in reactions (Sevek's Stand Still 25, Paya's unarmed "pineapple punch" 10, Aureys' Opportune Backstab 34) before it can act.
  • Then it Thrashes everything in reach: 44 into Sevek — a hit rather than a crit only because of Whirlwind Stance ("Ooh, not a crit because I switched stands") — a crit into Paya for 76 reduced to 62 by Deflecting Wave, 34 into Shuln A, and a 60-point crit into Shuln B.
  • Mithtirith lands a 66-point Heal on Paya from outside the worm's reach.
  • Aureys critically Feints (46 vs DC 35), making the worm off-guard to everyone's melee — and the finish is immediate: Sevek opens Inner Upheaval and crits for 84 plus persistent fire, and Aureys' Opportune Backstab crits for 88. The worm "crashes into the side of a building before slumping down."
  • Kill: Aureys, on Sevek's crit and her own Feint. Total logged damage into the worm: 522 — of which the shulns contributed 175.

Round 4 — the shulns turn

  • Aureys, betrayed: "No longer my friends."
  • Shuln B strides in and gets shredded on the approach: Paya's Reactive Strike crits for 74 and blinds it with the Brilliant rune, and Sevek's Stand Still crits for 62. Blinded, it has to flat-check to find Paya at all.
  • Paya opens his account at last: a Spear Dancer crit for 70 kills Shuln B — "Told you it'd be glorious" — seven minutes after calling the shot.
  • Mithtirith's Lightning Storm catches A and C; Aureys' Majestic Presence frightens both survivors; Sevek's Electric Arc double-fails them for 18 apiece before a flurry crit puts 83 into Shuln A.

Round 5 — the finish

  • Paya's Vicious Swing crits Shuln C for 94 applied (104 rolled, through resistance), with an Opportune Backstab from Aureys on top.
  • Mithtirith sustains Lightning Storm into a nat-1 critical failure on Shuln C for 34, then Battle-Medicines Sevek for 27 on a critical success.
  • Aureys' Frightening Power fails both survivors into Frightened 2, and her Overwhelming Combination kills Shuln C for 117.
  • Sevek closes with an Electric Arc for 25, then a flurry that rolled five consecutive 1s for a minimum-damage 42 — exactly enough. "Between those two hits, the shulns will be brought down." Kill: Sevek, on the worst damage roll of the session.
  • Paya's verdict on the untouched cleric: "Smart cleric kept his distance. Can't fault him for that."

Encounter 3 · The Mirror of Glory · complex mythic hazard · 4 rounds
InitiativePaya 44 · Aureys 41 · Mithtirith 38 · Mirror 35 · Sevek 35
Disable6 successes — Arcana, Thievery, or Intimidation, DC 37
Success ledgerAureys 4 · Mithtirith 2
Mythic points9 spent by the party
RewardParadigm Cube · +2 MP Aureys, +1 all

Opening effect: Will DC 37 or drop prone and Stupefied 1 for 24 hours. Routine: one 10d6 mental attack (basic Will DC 37) per remaining action, each aimed at a DM-chosen target — usually whoever is actively disabling it — followed by a d6 for which face of Xanderghul's contempt the victim sees: Cruelty (Enfeebled 1 / 24h), Disappointment (Clumsy 1 / 24h), Jealousy (Confused 1 round), Worship (Stunned 3). Each success permanently strips an action, so the mirror attacked four PCs in round 1, two in round 2, and one in round 3.

Round 1 — six successes needed, and the mirror hits everyone

  • Opening save: Sevek crit-saves. Paya, Mithtirith, and Aureys all fail. Mithtirith and Aureys mythic-reroll out; Paya keeps Stupefied 1 rather than spend, and starts prone.
  • Paya stands, Recall-Knowledges the mirror (Arcana disrupts the enchantments, Thievery disables the runes), strides into reflection range, and relays it.
  • Aureys channels Draconic Essence, fails her Thievery, rewrites fate with a mythic point — success 1 of 6. Mithtirith takes success 2.
  • Sevek Recall-Knowledges and turns up the third option — Intimidation — then spends the rest of his turn on Guidance and Bless Ally for Mithtirith.
  • The mirror closes the round by spending all four remaining actions, one per PC: Sevek and Mithtirith critically succeed and take nothing — Mithtirith's crit-save has Sevek's Guidance on the die — Paya takes 17, and Aureys critically fails, spending Draconic Salvation to upgrade it to a plain failure for 35 and rolling Cruelty — Enfeebled 1 for 24 hours.

Round 2 — Paya pays twice for nothing

  • Paya, having eaten the opening Stupefied rather than spend, now burns two mythic points for nothing — one on the round-1 Will save, one on a Thievery that still comes up 34 — then takes a spite swing at the mirror and misses: "Wow, not a great turn, guys."
  • Aureys lands success 3 on another mythic reroll; Mithtirith takes success 4 on his.
  • Sevek, holding his last point, uses only one of his three actions — Prepare to Aid Aureys with Intimidation, crit-succeeding for +2.

Round 3 — the worst turn in the history of Paya

  • The mirror's two remaining actions hit both of the party members who had been disabling it.
  • Mithtirith rolls Worship — Xanderghul is the one true god, and you must pray for absolution — Stunned 3, his entire turn gone.
  • Paya rolls Jealousy — Confused for a round, attacking randomly between Mithtirith and Sevek. He crit-misses the cleric, hits Sevek for 32, and misses again. "Guys, I want you to know I don't mean this at all, but I might enjoy it a little."

The Runewell's routine fires three waves a round: Iridescent Fire (3d10+6 mental plus Will DC 37 or slowed), Black Fire (3d10+6 void plus 2d6 persistent acid), and Crimson Fire (3d10+6 slashing plus 2d6 bleed). By the last round the disable had cost it an action and only two waves came out.

Round 1 — both Runelords open with the same spell

  • Aureys' opening Demoralize critically fails on a rolled 1.
  • Both Runelords cast Explosive Barrage — 39 and 52 fire-and-sonic across the whole party.
  • Sevek Trips at Thybidos twice and misses both, going prone on the critical failure and Kipping Up for free.
  • The Runewell opens its account: Iridescent Fire into Sevek for 29 and a failed save (slowed), Black Fire into Paya for 24 plus persistent acid.

Rounds 2–3 — the Wall of Force gambit

  • Cody warns the table unprompted: "They've got Mythic Ferocity. They have way more HP than anything else… I try to warn folks about boss tunnel vision. I've had a lot of PCs nearly die because the minions are liquidating them while they're attacking the boss with full health." The party takes the note and switches to the sentinels.
  • Mithtirith asks whether the Runewell's fires travel or simply appear, is told they travel, and spends three actions on the Wand of Wall of Force across the garden.
  • Angothane Recognizes the Spell and Disintegrates it — destroying it automatically, and burning his whole turn to do it: one action standing out of Sevek's Trip (eating a 21-point Stand Still on the way up), the other two on the Disintegrate. Mithtirith: "I just had to see if they actually bothered to get that skill feat in."
  • Thybidos drops a 7th-rank Frigid Flurry (67) — Aureys crit-saves off legendary Reflex, Sevek pays a mythic point for his, Mithtirith eats 62 — then eats Sevek's Stand Still on the way out.
  • Paya's reactive-strike crit staples Stupefied onto Thybidos via the Nightmare rune. It decides the fight three rounds before anyone notices.

Rounds 2–4 — clearing the minions

  • Sevek Taunts a sentinel, then flurries Sentinel B and blinds it with the Phantasmal Doorknob.
  • Paya Vicious-Swings Sentinel A; Aureys' Opportune Backstab kills Sentinel A.
  • Sentinel B, blind and burning, throws three attacks at Sevek — one of them a critical hit that the blindness erases on the flat check — and then dies to the persistent fire from Sevek's staff.
  • Both Runelords settle into a Howling Blizzard rotation from range. Paya, on the template: "It looks like Godzilla, but it's legally distinct."

Rounds 5–7 — running down the casters

  • The Runelords spend the fight teleporting away — Blink Charge, Translocate, Quickened Casting into Translocate — and the party spends it closing the gap.
  • Sevek burns Ascend's 130-foot daily burst to cross the garden after Thybidos, discovers he still can't reach him, and salvages the turn with a 120-foot Taunt.
  • Paya activates Godspeed and starts chaining Sprinter Stance, quickened Strides, and Sudden Charge — "like a pineapple with my head cut off." Aureys Translocates through a Puff of Smoke.
  • At the end of round 6, Angothane's own persistent bleed and fire — Aureys' riders — tick him to zero, and he burns every mythic point he has on Mythic Ferocity, recovers half his HP, sheds prone, and comes back Wounded 1.
  • Thybidos got no such option. Paya's Stupefied disrupted his casting three separate times, and every one of them cost a mythic point to fix — the third failed anyway and left him stranded in the open. When he hit zero he had nothing left to spend.
  • Sevek drops Herald's Weapon and the Inner Upheaval he'd held all fight into Thybidos — a Flurry that crits and then hits again for 133 combined, blinding him on the way. Two turns later Aureys' Opportune Backstab crits for 86 and Thybidos "explodes into light and is no more" (+2 MP to Aureys).
  • Angothane holds out a round longer before Paya's MAP−10 strike puts the halberd through his chest — "he too explodes into light" (+2 MP to Paya).

Rounds 7–8 — the Runewell alone

  • Mithtirith Scholarly-Recollects the well and turns up three skills — Arcana, Occultism, or Crafting — needing three successes. He opens with a mythic-rerolled success (1 of 3).
  • Sevek, untrained in Arcana and Crafting, goes for Occultism — and reaches for Guidance first, only for the table to work out he's still immune to it from before they walked through the portal. So he rolls raw: 35, mythic point, 39 against a DC of 40. He loses Book One's last check by exactly the +1 he tried to cast.
  • Two turns later Aureys rolls the same 39 in Arcana and succeeds — because Arcana was DC 37 and Occultism was DC 40 (2 of 3). She then Aids Mithtirith.
  • Paya burns a mythic point on Arcana too and still comes up short at 33, then spends his last action putting 53 HP back into Sevek.
  • The Runewell keeps firing throughout — a Black Fire crit for 48 plus doubled persistent acid, on a natural 20 that Sevek's Prevailing Position could not undo.
  • Then Mithtirith lands the third: "the final runes on the Runewell of Pride vanish." (+2 MP to Mithtirith, +1 to everyone else.)
  • Aureys turns to him: "I think that made sense to me, given your god."

Aureys went four-for-four on Opportune Backstab this fight, all four critical hits — 340 of her 383 damage came off a reaction.

Character Spotlight

Sevek · the Plain Answer

  • Two words closed Book One's villain monologue. Angothane spent twelve seconds promising the party would "perish in agony as he ascends to walk among the gods," and Sevek said "You talk too much." It is the entire character in one line — a man who does not perform, does not bluff, and is not impressed.
  • His register carried the session's tensest scene. Facing four omen dragons who had just learned the party killed their kin, with Aureys mid-boast, he supplied the flat truth instead of the flourish: "It was self-defense. We didn't intend to cause any harm." Ashava's anathema against misleading anyone isn't a note on his sheet — it is why the negotiation worked, because Shephodian could see he wasn't being lied to.
  • The Trip lock ground down a Darklands apex predator. He opened the cave worm fight with a Flurry-of-Maneuvers critical Trip and then never stopped: the worm burned its actions standing up over and over while the party unloaded, until Cody said it out loud — "not to be a one-trick pony, but this is working." Same engine on the sentinels, same engine on Angothane.
  • He is the reason the healer lived. When a sentinel got past the front rank and crit Mithtirith for 69, Sevek spent his reaction on Life Connection and took the whole 74 himself. In the finale he spent Prevailing Position three times with honest, mixed results — one Iridescent Fire that hit by exactly 0 turned into a clean miss at AC 41, one Reflex save the base roll would have passed anyway, and one thrown at a natural 20 that no amount of AC undoes. That last one is the 48 he ate.
  • The big numbers were saved and spent well: a 128 double-crit on Sentinel E, an 84 crit that set up the cave worm's death, an 83-point flurry crit into Shuln A, and in the finale Herald's Weapon plus the Inner Upheaval he'd held all fight — a Flurry that crit and then hit again for 133 combined into Thybidos, blinding him. That was the softening that let Aureys take the kill. He also killed Shuln A off five consecutive 1s for 42 damage, and burned Sentinel B to death with his staff's persistent fire.
  • The mythic-point joke paid off two hours later. Before bed he tried to farm a point with a pointless Long Jump and got shut down. In the middle of the Book One finale, needing to cross the garden anyway, he Long Jumped, critically succeeded, and collected the point — "that's also keeping me at the point into my calling." Told the rule once, applied it correctly under pressure.
  • The support lane never went quiet: Guidance handed out all session for nothing, Guidance and Bless Ally on Mithtirith in the mirror — which is exactly why Mithtirith critically saved the first routine and took 0 of 35 — plus crit-succeeded Aids and Water Breathing on the whole party because the portal sounded like water.
  • The quiet register is the whole character. He broke the Energy Aegis tie for Aureys without being asked twice ("she's a bit more frail"), made the call to rest in a dead woman's camp, handed the Paradigm Cube straight to someone else the moment he saw it, and was first to act on the boss-tunnel-vision warning. He also closed the book on a pun: "Papaya resembles that."

Paya · the Setup and the Punchline

  • He called his shot and then made it. Seven minutes into whiffing everything the cave worm fight had to offer: "One of these days I'll hit on my turn, and it'll be glorious." Seven minutes later, a Spear Dancer step-and-strike put down the first shuln, and: "Told you it'd be glorious."
  • The party's plainest sincere voice, again. Two Risen Runelords finished monologuing about godhood and a doomed world, and Paya answered for the party without any bit attached: "And we will stop him by any means at our disposal. So let's get going." He also framed the book's stakes back at the camp — "especially if we're following some of Xanderghul's secrets, I'd like a fully equipped cleric."
  • He killed a Risen Runelord — and set up the other one. Angothane burned every remaining mythic point on Mythic Ferocity to come back with half his HP, and Paya ran him down anyway on Godspeed and a Sudden Charge. Meanwhile his round-2 reactive crit stapled Stupefied onto Thybidos via the Nightmare rune, which drained all three of that Runelord's mythic points on disrupted castings — which is why Thybidos had nothing left to resurrect with.
  • Then the mirror got him. Jealousy left him Confused, rolling randomly between Mithtirith and Sevek, and he took it with grace: "Guys, I want you to know I don't mean this at all, but I might enjoy it a little." He crit-missed the cleric, hit Sevek, missed again — Cody: "I think that's still the worst turn in the history of Paya."
  • The healing lane is now most of what he does, and he's excellent at it: Fresh Produce, Dash of Herbs, Ocean's Balm, Torrent in the Blood, Deflecting Wave as mitigation, Doctor's Visitation to reach Mithtirith mid-fight for 66, and the reveal of Unusual Treatment to scrub the mirror's 24-hour conditions off the entire party at once. He also ran Foundry support all night — the Battle Medicine macro, Translocate's unoccupied-space rule, why deafened doesn't stop a non-auditory spell.
  • The bits stayed load-bearing: seasoning the party so the monsters find them delicious, "my lobster remains perfectly buttery," "it looks like Godzilla, but it's legally distinct," "a pineapple can dream," "like a pineapple with my head cut off," "Love from Sandpoint, friend" called after a Runelord who burned his last mythic point on an escape and went nowhere, and the best read of the night on Anna's pronunciation: "Angothane is going to be devastated when he hears you pronounce his name."

Aureys Hollow · the Honest Braggart

  • She talked the party past four hostile dragons by refusing to lie to them. Confronted by omen dragons asking what became of their mirage-dragon kin, she announced herself "greatest of the dragons of this world," admitted the killings outright, and when Shephodian said "I see in your eyes you don't deceive," answered "I do not. I could tell you a lie, but you would know that it was a lie because you see my greatness. But we do not have to be enemies. We could be friends." She ran the entire negotiation from there — the sense-motive read, the ask about safe passage, and the questions that got the party both the missing Warshard and Aethusa's description.
  • In Xanderghul's city of Pride, she out-prided him on the record: "No one could be more prideful than me." The funniest line of the session, and also why the Mirror of Glory was always going to be her fight.
  • She won that fight. Six successes, and Aureys landed four — two Thievery, one Intimidation staring down Xanderghul's own arrogance, and the final one, a nat 20, that dimmed the cube. She paid for it: four mythic points spent, one clawed back mid-fight with Summon Mythic Power, and even with the +2 for the disable she came out a point down — Cody turned down her cheeky ask for a Thief's Calling refund on the 20.
  • Four kills, all on the same reaction. Her Opportune Backstab finished the cave worm, two pridespawn sentinels, and — after Sevek's 133-point flurry blinded him — Risen Runelord Thybidos. A fifth sentinel died to the persistent bleed and fire her fan had left on it, and Angothane's first death came the same way. Her ceiling was the highest number anyone rolled all session: a double-crit Overwhelming Combination for 149.
  • The best piece of tactical housekeeping at the table was hers: before the long rest she deliberately hit her own party with Majestic Presence and Frightening Power so they'd be immune to it all day. "Feel my power. You might be shaking a little bit at first, but it'll wear off."
  • She also adopted three enormous mole-rats mid-combat purely on the strength of them attacking someone else — "I do consider them my allies at this point" — and took the betrayal personally: "I thought we were friends." And she closed Book One exactly where the character lives: "Aureys is going to be, like, hitting a candy store. Grabbing at stuff. The gems on the throne. Everything."

Mithtirith · the Long Line

  • He got the last word on the Book One villain speech and it was the best line of the night. One second after Sevek's "You talk too much," to two Risen Runelords promising the end of the world: "I mean, honestly, I come from a long line of people thwarting Runelord plans, so welcome."
  • He landed the hit that ended Book One. With both Runelords dead and only the Shattered Runewell left, he opened the disable with a mythic-rerolled success and then closed it — "the final runes on the Runewell of Pride vanish" — for +2 mythic points. He'd also landed two of the six Mirror of Glory successes earlier.
  • The knowledge lane ran the whole session: the cave worm (best save Fortitude, Reflex and Will tied worst, can swallow you whole), the shulns (adamantine, resistant to everything physical except adamantine and bludgeoning), the pridespawn (resistant to mental with big bonuses on mental saves — which raised the bar on Aureys' fear suite without closing the lane), and Scholarly Recollection on the Runewell to find the three skills that could shut it down.
  • The Hedge Mage experiment got its first real test and passed on points. He claimed Aethusa's Wand of Widening, then in the finale asked exactly the right question — do the Runewell's fires travel, or just appear on us? — and on being told they travel, dropped a Wall of Force across the garden. Angothane immediately Disintegrated it, costing the Runelord his entire turn, and Mithtirith's response was pure deadpan: "I just had to see if they actually bothered to get that skill feat in."
  • The enemy pattern held: everything still hunts him. He ate a 66 crit and a 74 crit from the sentinels (the second intercepted by Sevek), and took more damage than anyone in the finale. His answer was to stop being reachable — flying twenty feet straight up out of a sentinel's reach, waving down at it, and asking "Is my free action to give him the middle finger salute?" When Sevek looked up — "Hello there. Why are you up there?" — the reply was pure Mithtirith: "Because you're the next most wounded on the field."
  • The dry commentary never let up. On being Stunned 3 in the same round Paya swung at him twice and missed: "Was Mithtirith stunned three by the mirror, or by the fact that Paya missed twice?" On his own status: "as your sole magic user, I have to tell you, I am low on healing." On being able to make the Runewell checks from the back line: "That's the best news I've heard all day." And after the Runewell went dark, Aureys turned to him and said the kindest thing anyone said all session — "I think that made sense to me, given your god."
