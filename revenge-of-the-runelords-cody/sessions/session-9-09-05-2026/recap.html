Session Summary

The Crypt of Runes is a lock, and the party spends the session picking it. Seven doors ring a circular hall with a seven-pointed star set into the floor; six stand ajar, and the seventh — north, to the Crypt of Pride, where the Risen are — is sealed under all seven Runelord sigils stacked on top of one another. Mithtirith reads the enchantment and finds the exploit: the runes hold each other up, so the party doesn't have to strip all seven. Just enough to make the whole thing collapse. Nobody knows what “enough” means until they try.

What follows is four crypts and three very different answers. Wrath answers with violence — Alaznist's screaming spectral torso yanks the party onto a phantom battlefield where three mountains of welded corpses are already closing in. Sloth answers with a joke: a daunting staircase spiraling hundreds of feet into darkness that goes precisely nowhere, a false wall Aureys finds by refusing to believe Sloth would build stairs, and seven Runelords who cannot be bothered to fight and take their own door-rune down when Mithtirith points out that letting the party do the work is the lazy option. Lust answers with Queen Sorshen herself, haggard and reaching through from somewhere else with a warning she doesn't get to finish — “Find me in Jandelay, and I can—” — before gray smoke swallows her and three burning, elephant-masked things step out of it. And Greed answers with a negotiation, because Karzoug is a businessman: he'll pull his rune off the door, but he wants paying, and words don't count.

The party skips Envy. Aureys scouts the hall of ten statues and finds it rigged to strip magic items off anyone who walks through — disabling it means ten separate checks with a hazard waiting on any failure. Sevek does the math out loud — “we can do another room, we don't have to go down this” — and the table follows him. It's the correct call and it costs nothing, which is a sentence that has rarely applied to this dungeon.

Four runes down, the north door dims and dies. The party sleeps at the foot of Xin's statue, walks into the Crypt of Pride, and finds a hall eighty feet wide with three Pridespawn cultists in it and a man in gold and green robes admiring the frescoes, Aeon stones set into his forehead. Risen Runelord Haphrama — Greed — turns around, decides the how doesn't matter, and orders them killed. It goes badly for his cultists and fine for him: two die, Paya goes down under a polearm, and Haphrama — who lands nothing all fight, both of his big spells critically saved by every target — simply casts Translocate and leaves. The last cultist goes with him, to warn the rest.

The Session, Act by Act

Act I · Exploration · The Lock

Seven doors and a statue

  • Cleanup from Czaresia: a +2 Greater Resilient Explorer's clothing and a +3 Greater Striking Flaming Frost Shock mace — the mace gargantuan, the clothing rune-giant-sized and needing its rune transferred — plus a mythic pair of Beloved's Bracelets.
  • Aureys' immediate regret: “if only Aureys had taken as her 16th level feat Dragon's Hoard, where she could do that, apparently.”
  • The hall: 80 feet across, ceiling twice that, a seven-pointed star in the floor pointing at seven doors, and a statue of a long-haired man with a staff facing a mural on the south wall. Both turn out to be Xin — and the mural holds nothing else. Aureys searches the statue for a hidden stairwell; there isn't one.
  • Mithtirith identifies the north door. He tries Thassilonian Lore first and Cody steers him to Arcana: all seven runes overlaid and circulating, each a barrier, no teleport or bypass. His key finding — the session's operative fact — is that the more runes you remove, the more unstable the enchantment gets. The party doesn't need all seven. Nobody knows the number.
  • Cody maps the compass: N pride, NE envy, E gluttony, SE greed, SW sloth, W lust, NW wrath. Order doesn't matter. Paya votes clockwise “because it's appealing to my brain.”
  • Sevek and Aureys frame the choice as a damage-type question — she'd rather take “fiery boom spells” because she can dodge them; he says flatly he'd rather not face anything that hits his mind. Four minutes of circling later, Kreebnock ends it: “any door is as good as another until we try one.” They start with Wrath.
Act II · Combat · Wrath and the Field of Ruin

Alaznist screams, and the floor becomes a battlefield

  • Aureys scouts and finds five mausoleums — Alaznist, Alderpash, Angathane, Ziren, Thibodos — doors shattered, contents gone, runes drained. A spiritual presence remains, and she flies back to warn the party rather than push: “I'm going to fly back stealthily to my friendly neighborhood cleric.”
  • The party moves in anyway. Alaznist's undead upper torso fills the chamber screaming, other faces rise with her, and the scream carries everyone somewhere else: a bloody battlefield under a lightning-shot sunset, five pristine white mausoleums on a distant hill, and three titanic masses of congealed corpses and armor rising out of the ground.
  • Three Warsworn. Five rounds. Sevek two kills, Paya one. Full breakdown below.
  • Back in the crypt, Alaznist's ghost screams one word — “Xanderghul!” — then “You will pay for this,” and shatters into fire along with the rest of the Runelords of Wrath.
  • Every grave is already empty. Mithtirith: “Does this officially make us grave robbers?” Aureys: “I've always been a grave robber, you know.” Paya dates it precisely: “I imagine at some point during Rusthenge at level 2, we became grave robbers.”
  • The Rune of Wrath is gone from the north door.
Act III · Negotiation · Sloth and the Stairs That Go Nowhere

The laziest crypt in Thassilon

  • The Sloth crypt opens on “a daunting flight of stairs spiral[ing] upward hundreds of feet before disappearing into the darkness.” Aureys: “Hope you got your cardio in, boys.” Sevek: “Yeah, don't worry. Sevek has a lot of stamina.”
  • Mithtirith: “They're magicians and they couldn't put it in an elevator?” — which sets off the running gag, because Sevek doesn't know what one is. “An elevator? What is that?” … “Yeah, I'm from backwater town, so I'm not really familiar with all those technologies.”
  • Aureys refuses to climb. Her reasoning is character, not caution: “stairs just doesn't seem like a slothful thing to do.” A thorough search turns up a false wall running south. The staircase is a pure troll — it climbs to nothing and dumps you back on the floor, and people at this table have fallen for it before.
  • Behind the wall: seven more looted mausoleums and the seven Runelords of Sloth — Ziriae, Illithrius, Azaradne, Zalilat, Krenleth, Iravina, Kroon — floating lazily overhead, too bored to attack. Zalilat: “Can you not see? You've been beaten to the theft. That fiend Xanderghul's already made off with everything.”
  • Mithtirith wins the crypt with one sentence: “But wouldn't you want someone to do the work for you to get your revenge?” Paya closes it — “You already have an axe to grind with Xanderghul. We could take a hit on him for you.” The Runelords agree, remove the Sloth rune themselves, and dismiss the party: “Now off with you.”
  • The Rune of Sloth is gone. No fight, no resources spent.
Act IV · Mystery · Lust and Sorshen's Warning

“Find me in Jandelay, and I can—”

  • A long pillared hall, murals of carnal pleasures on both walls. Aureys: “so it's just porn.” Cody: “Look, she's the runelord of lust, not the runelord of chastity.” Sevek announces he “is going to ignore all these worldly desires. He's a monk.” Kreebnock, one beat later, “takes notes as he goes along.”
  • From the western mural, pieces detach and assemble into Queen Sorshen — ethereal, haggard, “as though she's been through some kind of ordeal.” Recognition dawns when she sees them.
  • “At last I've reached you. Look, as you already know, Xanderghul has returned, and I believe he intends to destroy Golarion, starting with the Saga Lands. Find me in Jandelay, and I can—”
  • Gray smoke cuts her off. Through it: a ruined cityscape, crumbling buildings and broken boulevards. And out of the smoke and ash step three humanoid figures dripping oil, flames licking their sides, each wearing a mask shaped like an elephant's skull, broken tusks and all.
  • Three Jandelay Tolokands — the longest fight of the session. Full breakdown below.
  • Afterward, Mithtirith brackets the deduction: he asks the question (“do you think that means that Sorshen's dead?”), Paya notes she looked tired, Aureys points at the destination, and Mithtirith closes it — “there's no mausoleums here, so… So she never died.”
  • The Rune of Lust is gone. Three down; Cody says the enchantment is “really struggling to hold it together” and one more should do it.
Act V · Exploration · Envy, Scouted and Refused

Ten statues, ten chances to fail

  • Nine statues of Runelords in finery holding magical implements, five north and four south, with a tenth — Belimarius, holding an ornate halberd — standing apart at the east end. The lineage: Napht, Tannaris, Ivamura, Jura, Chasarda, Esedria, Zar, Dissimilia, Faradni, and Belimarius.
  • Aureys walks the hall and her trap check comes back bad: the whole corridor is a magic-item-stealing hazard, triggering the moment anyone goes further. Disabling it means Arcana, Athletics, Thievery, or Dispel Magic against a 9th-rank effect — on all ten statues, with any failure setting the hazard off.
  • Aureys, delighted anyway: “These are thieves, so they're kind of my kind of people.”
  • Sevek calls it off: “I say we need to do that ten times. We can do another room. We don't have to go down this.” Mithtirith pushes back honestly — it's checks, not something attacking us — and Paya lands it: “Just 10 chances of failure is just not the best.” Kreebnock buries it: “then the fight at the end of the statues is probably not going to go well.”
  • Mithtirith circles back thirty seconds later, after the party has already chosen another door: “I'm, I'm, I'm upset. Hold on a second. I'm obsessing on this. So the trap steals your magic items, right?… What if you don't walk in with any magic items?” Sevek points out their weapons are magic items.
  • They go to Greed instead. The hazard is still there, untriggered, if they ever need it.
Act VI · Negotiation · Greed and the Karzoug Bargain

“Then what price will you pay in return for our help?”

  • Seven mausoleums, doors open, coins and gems spilling across the floor. A brass plaque in Azlanti — Mithtirith is the only one who reads it — says Abundance Everlasting, and lists the Runelords of Greed: Kaldurne, Fethrir, Gimel, Lignia, Mazerana, Aethusa, and Haphrama. Mithtirith: “I'm not sure I believe this.”
  • Aureys reaches for a coin and has to make a Fortitude save. She succeeds, and her build upgrades it to a critical success — which is the only reason she gets a flash of herself “as a somewhat tacky obsidian statue” instead of becoming one. The hoard is an illusion. Kreebnock, immediately: “Don't touch the treasure!”
  • Mithtirith, dryly: “I just shake my head and look at Paya and go, whatever happened to greed is good.”
  • Paya works out where to look — the east side is “the non-treasure area” — and three PCs search it. Sevek finds the hidden door, beating two better-trained searchers on the die. Behind it: more looted mausoleums and one circular tomb inscribed Karzoug.
  • Spectral Runelords boil out screaming “Thieves!” and “The wealth! Gone! All gone!” — and are cut off by Karzoug: “That is quite enough. These troublesome heroes aren't worthy of our ire. They are not the thieves. This we all know.”
  • Beat one — Kreebnock opens it. A Diplomacy Request under his Cloak of Repute (Greater). The offer is considered, but “words ring hollow where wealth is concerned” — it lowered the asking price without closing. Nothing after this happens without it.
  • Beat two — Aureys holds it open. She offers 10% of everything recovered from the despoiled tombs and rolls well. Then Gimel steps forward: “A promising start. And yet, for all promises you have made, no material has been produced.” Karzoug tests her: “Surely a dragon such as yourself has an eye for what is valuable.” Her answer: “I do indeed, which is why I, like you, do not bargain away my wealth happily. But I'm sure we have something that perhaps we can part with.”
  • Beat three — Paya closes it. He produces the Paradigm Cube from his stash — a mythic item he'd claimed the session before — without hesitating. Cody: it wouldn't be worth enough, but because it's mythic, its monetary value is doubled for the negotiation. Karzoug turns it over twice: “This will suffice.”
  • The Rune of Greed is gone. The prismatic light on the north door fluctuates and dies. Aureys checks it for traps anyway (“You can never be too careful”), then: “Never make a deal with a dragon. I don't know if you ever heard that one.”
Act VII · Combat · The Crypt of Pride

Haphrama, and the boss who leaves

  • Kreebnock calls the rest after Mithtirith flagged he was “out of my healing font spells… I think I have about one more fight in me” and Kreebnock had volunteered his own Soothes to cover the gap. A full 8-hour rest at the foot of Xin's statue. Paya: “Bedrolls and candlelight.”
  • Beyond the door: a hall 80 feet wide and nearly twice as long, 50-foot ceiling, twin rows of marble columns, two stairwells down on the west wall and a passage east, a giant-scale fresco on the north wall.
  • Three Pridespawn cultists with bec de corbins — “easily the most powerful among their number you've encountered thus far” — and, at the back, a man in opulent gold and green robes admiring the frescoes, Aeon stones embedded in his forehead. Risen Runelord Haphrama, of Greed.
  • “How— No, how doesn't matter. However you got down here, it seems I'm to make sure you leave in pieces. Get them, now! Leave none alive.”
  • Haphrama escapes. After a polearm strike at Sevek that comes up a natural 1, he declines to reposition and casts Translocate. Cody: “He has gone from sight. And he is out of here.” Aureys: “No one said that, you know, Runelords fell off the turnip truck yesterday. There's some smart cookies.”
  • Two cultists dead; the last leaves with the Runelord. Mithtirith gets the closing line: “The Runelord has his pride.”
  • Aureys initiates the looting after Cody had already moved to wrap-up — the only reason the party got the Pridespawn gear at all.
  • Cody notes this chapter is the longest in the entire AP, and there is much more of the Crypt of Runes to come.

Round 1 — the taunt pays immediately

  • Aureys opens with a Demoralize on C that fails, then Hides.
  • Warsworn B strides in and Corpse Waves Sevek for 56; he rolls a natural critical success on the Energy Drain save (46 vs DC 35).
  • Sevek enters Whirlwind + Reflective Ripple Stance, crit-Trips B on a natural 20 (53 vs Reflex DC 37, by +16), hits for 35, and Group Taunts all three at 120 feet.
  • Warsworn C — mis-clicked as B in Foundry, which Sevek caught and Cody corrected mid-swing — reaches across the battlefield with its 100-foot weapon aura to crit Mithtirith for 78 and hit Kreebnock for 43.
  • Because C swung at Sevek's allies while taunted, Taunt's rider left it off-guard — and Kreebnock's Murder of Crows critically hits by exactly +10 on the reduced AC, for 68 and Blinded. Remove either Taunt or his own Courageous Anthem and it's an ordinary 34.
  • Paya answers with Needle in the Gods' Eyes: a hit for 28 and a natural-20 crit for 86.
  • Warsworn A strides in, flanks Sevek with C, and crits — Cody calls it explicitly, “that is going to crit because taunt penalty does not apply to you” — and Sevek spends Prevailing Position to take 40 as a normal hit instead, giving up his stance to do it, then eats 37 more on the follow-up.
  • Mithtirith Recalls Knowledge — undead, best save Fortitude, worst Reflex — then Heals Sevek for 69.

Round 2 — Aureys opens the off-guard floodgate

  • Aureys Grovels C into a Feint; Distracting Feint hands the off-guard to the whole party, and her Overwhelming Combination lands a claw crit for 163 — the largest single hit of the fight.
  • B stands and crits Sevek for 72.
  • Sevek deliberately Strides into reach to test the Reactive Strike — nothing fires, and Cody simply explains the rule: the reaction rides the Corpse Wave, reach 5.
  • C answers with a Corpse Wave for 43, and then Sevek crits C for 78 and destroys it (natural 17 — the log flags Aureys' Distracting Feint as essential to the crit).
  • Kreebnock Unleashes Psyche and drops a 2-action Force Rain on B for 48; Paya adds 78 across two hits before a natural 1.
  • Warsworn A crits Sevek again — second Prevailing Position, second stance surrendered, 40 taken as a normal hit — and its follow-up Corpse Wave comes up a critical miss into his AC 43.
  • Mithtirith's Divine Immolation catches A and B for 12 and 25.

Round 3 — the two near-deaths

  • Aureys crit-Feints B, lands Majestic Presence for frightened 1, then crits it for 152, pushing it to frightened 2 on her Greater Fearsome rune — and Terrifying Mien holds it there.
  • Cody points out B is no longer taunted and redirects it off Sevek onto Aureys for 38: “if they were taunted, they were just going to go for you.”
  • Kreebnock casts Imaginary Weapon while adjacent to A — and finds the Reactive Strike the hard way, taking a Corpse Wave crit for 86.
  • Paya Battle-Medicines Sevek for 69, re-grips, and kills B with a Spear Dancer crit for 74.
  • Warsworn A turns back on Kreebnock, hits for 52, and puts him on 3 HP.
  • On the next Strike Paya spends a Mythic Point on Unbelievable Interception — Strides twice across the battlefield, becomes the target using the enemy's original roll against AC 38, and eats 41. “It will still hit. My AC is only 38.” The third Strike stays on Kreebnock and critically misses.
  • Mithtirith's 8th-rank Heal brings Kreebnock back to 105.

Rounds 4–5 — grinding down the last one

  • Aureys crit-Grovels A and whiffs both Strikes. Sevek burns Inner Upheaval, lands one for 37, and gambles a MAP −10 into a critical miss (“Sometimes I want to gamble too”). Paya crits for 92 and adds 44.
  • A crits Aureys for 76 — the only Energy Drain save the party fails all fight — then misses twice and burns down under persistent fire and vitality.
  • Round 5: Aureys crits A for 95 and Aether Beams it for 26. Sevek's Electric Arc chips 13; both Flurry strikes miss. Cody: “And this one is surviving.”
  • Then Kreebnock casts amped Message and hands Sevek a reaction Strike — and against A's stripped AC (frightened 2 and off-guard, 35 instead of 39) it comes up a crit for 82. A had 68 HP; a normal hit would not have finished it. Warsworn A destroyed.

The Desiccate barrage

  • All three opened by striding, free-Hasting, and casting Desiccate (rank 8, 10d10 void, basic Fort DC 40, unlimited targets) — three in round one alone, nine across the fight. Paya, a leshy: “It's a bad day to be a leshy, friends.” Afterward: “Eight desiccates that I couldn't do better than succeed on.”
  • Mithtirith's Recall Knowledge came up a natural 2 — “You have absolutely no idea what these creatures are” — so the party fought the entire thing blind.
  • Mithtirith answered the first one with mythic. Vindictive Thoughts: spend a Mythic Point, save at mythic proficiency, throw 32 mental back at the caster. Tolokand A rolled a natural 20 and took nothing — but Cody's response set the terms: “that's only one out of nine between them all.”
  • Aureys' Draconic Salvation, twice — a self-only Fortune reaction, both used on herself, turning critical failures into successes that her build upgrades to critical successes. She is the reason the party's most fragile save target took 55 damage all fight.
  • Kreebnock nearly went down and Paya found the out. Staring at 118 damage: “I don't have a mythic point to keep myself alive, so I'm going down, guys.” Paya remembered Liralarue's Glimpse — a free once-weekly reroll everyone has and nobody had spent — and talked him through it. Natural 18. Twenty-nine damage. He stayed up.

The healer goes down and gets back up

  • A 62-point Desiccate dropped Mithtirith from 36 to 0; his pre-set Contingency Heal fired automatically while he was dying 1, unconscious and prone. Foundry rolled it at rank 7 and Cody corrected it up to rank 8 — “just roll 1d10 plus 8 and add it to that” — for 110 total.
  • Earlier he had taken 93 from a critical failure that was a natural 1 on a mythic-proficiency reroll — the biggest single hit anyone ate all session.
  • Paya's Divert Destiny (first use). A Desiccate would have dropped him even after a mythic reroll, so he spent a Mythic Point and stood back up at 26 HP. He then healed himself off the floor mid-combat: Doctor's Visitation into a DC 40 critical Battle Medicine for 87.

Control, and the Earthquake that never landed

  • Sevek fused stances on action one, then Group Taunted all three, three times across the fight. A critical Rippling Spin Trip on B (49 vs Reflex DC 37) forced it to spend an action standing, which Paya punished with a Reactive Strike for 47.
  • Paya then took a second Reactive Strike — he has Tactical Reflexes, a bonus reaction each turn usable only for Reactive Strike — on the Desiccate that followed and, as ever, failed to disrupt it: “I'll see if I can interrupt it. OK, great. I cannot.”
  • Sevek also crit B for 84 off Inner Upheaval and crit A for 94 on his one Electric Arc + Jolt Coil round.
  • Kreebnock taxed their resources rather than their HP. With Friends like These cost Tolokand A its second Mythic Point and left B Sickened 1. Scintillating Safeguard twice — resistance 10, then 11, against the void damage of one incoming Desiccate each — which carried Mithtirith and Paya through two of the nine. Paya: “I take one degree less.” And late, a critical Telekinetic Projectile for 54.
  • Paya's Nightmare rune killed the Earthquake. His Reactive-Strike crit stapled Stupefied 1 on C; C then failed the DC 6 flat check to cast Earthquake, burned a Mythic Point to reroll, and rolled a natural 1. Paya: “Not today. Nightmares of Sandpoint save us in this case.”

Kills

  • Paya killed B first — Needle in the Gods' Eyes, natural 18 crit for 72.
  • Paya killed A second — Needle in the Gods' Eyes, natural 17 and natural 20, an actual double crit, 74 then 86.
  • Sevek killed C — Flurry of Blows landed one of two, and then he gambled the third Strike at MAP −10: “he's still up. So I'm just gonna gamble with a map timing” — and hit by exactly zero for 43.
  • Afterward most of the party was back at maximum Mythic Points from the three kill refunds. Paya: “That's a mythic fight through and through.”

Round 1 — not going for the boss

  • Aureys' opening Demoralize on A is a natural 1. She flies wide and hits the cultists with Majestic Presence — their mental saves are absurd, so they succeed, but Frightened 1 sticks anyway — then swift-sneaks behind a pillar.
  • Two cultists stride wide, which Paya reads correctly: “they seem to be making room for an area of effect, so I will not play into that.” He spends a Mythic Point on Godspeed, Sudden Charges a cultist for 38, and deliberately does not go for the boss.
  • Cody approves out loud and tells the story of a party that tunnel-visioned a boss and got nearly wiped by two minions casting Breathe Fire.
  • Cultist C answers by striding in and putting 92 into Paya in one turn.
  • Sevek fuses his stances, misses, then Trips C prone — the quietest roll of the fight and the one everything else hangs on.
  • Haphrama strides up and casts a 7th-rank Haste on his own side. Paya: “Fully expected another desiccate just because of the rules of comedic timing.”
  • Mithtirith identifies the Pridespawn — best save Fortitude, worst Will, huge mental bonus, +1 to saves vs magic — then drops Arrow Salvo on three of them for 22 each. That 22 is the only damage Haphrama takes all fight.
  • Kreebnock Hastes Sevek and Aureys and leaves himself out; Sevek was five feet past the full spread.

Round 2 — Paya goes down, Sevek takes the first cultist

  • Aureys uses her hasted action to fly onto Sevek's shoulder, Grovels, Feints, switches her fan's rune to holy (“go with holy just because they seem bad”), and opens Overwhelming Combination — fan and claw — critting the prone C for 65 and stapling on Enfeebled, Frightened 2 and a bleed.
  • Cultist A puts four Strikes into Paya, including a natural-20 crit for 80; the fourth, at MAP −10, would have killed him, so he burns Divert Destiny and stands back up at 26 HP.
  • He spends his last Mythic Point on Divide and Conquer, striking A and C at mythic proficiency — both hit, so both take Sickened 2 — and Aureys' Opportune Backstab crits C for 79 off his hit, her biggest swing of the night.
  • Paya goes down. His follow-up triggers C's Hallucinatory Backlash; he makes the Will save and takes 18, leaving him on 8. Then C, still prone, hits him for exactly that. The damage is piercing, so Deflecting Wave can't answer it: “I am out of mythic points, so I will just be dying one.”
  • Sevek kills Pridespawn C — C spends its last action standing up, eats a Stand Still for 34, and then takes a 94-point crit on the first swing of a Flurry of Blows. Not a spiked die: a natural 14 against an AC the party had dragged from 37 down to 33. He repositions and Group Taunts Haphrama, A and B at 120 feet.
  • Haphrama's turn is the whole boss in miniature. He strides and drops Arctic Rift — 120-foot line, 12d8 cold, DC 40 Fortitude — on three PCs. Sevek rerolls a natural 10 into a 44, Aureys makes 40, Kreebnock rolls a natural 20: three critical successes, zero damage. He follows with quickened Telekinetic Bombardment, DC 40 Reflex — and Sevek crit-succeeds that one on a natural 20. Paya: “A 20 will do it, even if it's not your path to perfection. Perfection sometimes finds you.”
  • Mithtirith goes invisible, and Kreebnock does the clutch work: Unleash Psyche, Soothe on the downed Paya for 64, then Restore the Mind for another 34 — floor to 98 HP in one turn. Sevek: “Hey, we've got two healers. Let's go.”

Rounds 3–4 — the boss leaves

What Sevek is thinking about

  • Taunt did not absorb the Tolokand fight, and that's the real lesson. Nine party-wide Desiccates and 467 rolled void damage do not care about aggro; only five hand Strikes were even Taunt-eligible. What Taunt actually bought was the −1 and the off-guard — and the off-guard is what let Paya crit four times and Aureys crit three. Against a caster group, Taunt is a to-hit debuff and a flanking enabler, not a damage sink. The mitigation lane there is Prevailing Position and standing further apart.
  • The reach-100 Animated Weapon is the counter-case. In round one of the Warsworn fight, a taunted Warsworn reached across the entire battlefield to crit Mithtirith for 78 anyway. Taunt's penalty applied; the distance did not matter. Positioning cannot solve a weapon that ignores it.
  • Check the Taunt automation before next session. Foundry is putting the −1 on attacks aimed at Sevek, which is backwards — and it cost him two logged crits' worth of evidence that Prevailing Position is doing its job. Get it fixed; the reaction looks weaker in the log than it is.
  • Five kills is not the shape of this character, and the reason matters. Every crit landed against an AC that Aureys had already pulled down four to six points with Feints, Grovels and frightened stacks. He is standing where the debuffs have already landed — which is exactly where a Guardian should be, but it means his kill count is a party statistic, not a personal one.
  • The party is running two healers now, and it changed the calculus on pushing into the Crypt of Pride. Mithtirith flagged he had one fight left; Kreebnock volunteered his Soothes and then picked Paya up off the floor in a single turn.
  • Bodyguard is still assigned to Mithtirith — who solved his own exposure again this session by going invisible, and who still ate 388 from AoE that no reaction could have stopped. Worth revisiting who that reaction should be on.
