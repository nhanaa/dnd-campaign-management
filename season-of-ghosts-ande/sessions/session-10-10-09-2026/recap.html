Session Summary

The session opened on the fight S9 left hanging. The Dalgyal Gwishin in the bachelor’s yard went straight for Iryoku, as the research had said it would, and spent two rounds headbutting him while the rest of the party took it apart. Kaoru’s Spellstrike left it “barely still in existence” and Kaelen finished it with the first strike of a Flurry of Blows. Zhen’s verdict on Iryoku’s evening: “I guess it’s not always good to be charismatic.” The town gained a point of Hope and a point of Northridge reputation.

One more week of downtime closed the fall research. Mu-Jin ran his own investigations and Zhen took the Great Willow on Nature and critically succeeded, and the GM announced the final research threshold. With it came a ritual, Open the Wall of Ghosts, and the news that everything left to learn about the curse lies beyond that wall, at the ruins of the Tan Sugi Monastery. Then everyone leveled to 5. Zhen and Kaoru both came out of it with Fireball, Mu-Jin learned Fear, and Iryoku’s fire junction now gives enemies near him a weakness to his fire. The ritual can only be cast under a crescent or new moon (weeks 7, 8, 11 or 12), and it was week 7. Iryoku wanted to wait and keep preparing the town. Mu-Jin, Kaoru, Zhen and Kaelen wanted to go, and the table went four to one. Zhen led the ritual as primary caster on Religion, with Mu-Jin, Kaoru and Iryoku as his secondaries, and it worked.

The Wall of Ghosts let them in and closed behind them. Inside was a fog-bound forest with no wind and no animal sound. Iryoku recognized the ground as the monastery’s site, and Mu-Jin read the trees: this woodland is nearly a thousand years younger than it should be, from before the monastery existed. At the end of the trail, on a slab of stone, lay a woman with her chest torn open and fog pouring out of the wound. Mu-Jin named her on a natural 20: Tan Sui Jing, the heroine who defeated a fiend here a thousand years ago. The word carved into the stone under her read Kugaptee. Her arm pointed north, to a mound of fallen trees and a talking giant vulture that told them to leave or be added to its meals.

That fight is the one Pax asked to have remembered, and the log bears it out. Zhen rolled a natural 20 on initiative and opened with the spell he had learned an hour earlier. One Fireball: the vulture critically failed for 50, two of the three Cacodaemons died outright, and the third was left on 10 hit points. Kaoru rolled a natural 20 too, walked up and cast his own. The vulture got exactly one turn. Kaelen killed it, Iryoku picked off the last Cacodaemon, and the whole thing was over inside a single round. Then the vulture’s corpse burst into razor-winged red butterflies, and in its talon was a human heart, whispering “save me.”

The party already meant to carry the heart back to the body. Mu-Jin picked it up, and the forest went dark. Something enormous began roaring and walking toward them through the trees, and the trees themselves started clawing at the party. Nobody could see it and nobody could identify it. Mu-Jin stopped trying and made the call: everyone back to the slab, now. It became a relay. Kaoru took the heart, Mu-Jin’s Loose Time’s Arrow gave him the extra stride, and Kaoru ran a hundred feet and put the heart back in Tan Sui Jing’s chest. The unseen thing howled and fled. Her body sank into the stone, a sugi tree grew a hundred feet out of it and drove the fog away, and the party was standing on the Pilgrim’s Path again. The GM’s reveal afterward: it was a hazard, and nobody could realistically have spotted it.

Character Spotlight

Zhen Stillness · the Primary Caster

  • He led the ritual. With no social skills to offer as a secondary, he took the primary seat on Religion at Iryoku’s suggestion and made the DC 24 check with room to spare. Earlier the same session his Great Willow research came up a critical success as the last research threshold fell.
  • He asked what waiting would buy before he voted. Three votes were already in. Zhen asked the GM whether there was anything concrete to gain and whether Willowshore felt ready for winter, got “you can never be too prepared,” and voted to go.
  • The Fireball. First turn of the vulture fight, a natural 20 on initiative, and a spell he’d had for an hour: “Well, no time like the present. Let’s debut the new spell.” It took 106 of the 149 hit points the enemy side had, and killed two of the four outright. When Kaoru walked up to do the same: “Hell yeah, join the gang.” And on the turn order: “the two caster with AoE spell go first. … That’s a nice recipe.”
  • He stayed in the fiction when the table didn’t. When the Back to the Future jokes started inside the wall, his answer was Zhen’s: “I wouldn’t know what you’re talking about, but… I feel like a dimension sounds more likely.” Then the practical elder: “Let’s be careful,” and at the slab, “Let’s watch our backs and sides.”
  • The rules resource, lightly. He told Kaoru that Spellstrike counts as two attacks for the multiple attack penalty (the GM confirmed it from the text), offered Mu-Jin Slow and Roaring Applause when he asked for crowd control, suggested a flank to Kaelen, prompted the Recall Knowledge on the Gwishin, and when Kaelen was out of focus points and the table was weighing Electric Arc against a ghost: “Probably better to just bonk it, honestly.”
  • Dry about all of it. “I guess it’s not always good to be charismatic.” On leveling: “This is the level where I stop having fun as a martial.” When the vulture bit Iryoku: “Not against my friend, no.”
  • In the dark, he delayed his turn so Mu-Jin’s free Recall Knowledge could go first, and ran when the call came. At the slab: “So, we’re here already. So, might as well do something productive.” He swapped apparitions and cast Garden of Healing, which caught Mu-Jin and Weaver.

Mu-Jin · the One Who Named the Dead

  • He named the dead woman. A natural 20 identified Tan Sui Jing, and he was the one who could read the word under her body: Kugaptee. His first reaction was that the story they’d been told might be wrong: “There might be more to the story than we think, folks.”
  • He dated the forest, on a critical success, and delivered it in character, pointing at the trees: a growth pattern from a thousand years ago, no animals, no wind. Then the best theory of the night: “I’m not sure if we went back in time or if we just went into a dimension trying to recreate a moment in time.”
  • He drove the vote. He was for going from the first minute and said so, but when Kaoru asked what the town still needed, he set out the case for waiting as carefully as the case for going, counted the town’s points against the minimums, and put it to the table. He voted first and didn’t hide it. He also dug into the ritual rules (order of checks, how many secondaries, whether townsfolk could help) until the plan was sound.
  • He called the retreat. With the party blind and something huge approaching, he tried a free Recall Knowledge, tried talking to the heart, and then committed: “My best suggestion is for everyone to get the fuck out of here, [run] back to Tan Sui Jing, and try to see if we can put the heart back in there.” He cast Light to show the path, asked for a faster runner to take the heart off him, and cast Loose Time’s Arrow to speed the relay.
  • Still funny. “Rituals are a bitch.” The Cacodaemons were “the ones that look like a demonic Pac-Man.” And the horror-movie line as the footsteps got closer: “I think this is the point where we’re all supposed to run the fuck away from whatever’s approaching us.”

Kaoru · the One Who Carried the Heart

  • He ended it. He offered to take the heart because he was standing next to Mu-Jin, ran a hundred feet in one turn under Loose Time’s Arrow, and on his next turn said “I’m going to put the heart back in.” The night’s whole mystery resolved on that action.
  • He matched Zhen spell for spell. Natural 20 on initiative, second in the order, and: “Kaoru’s gonna come up here, and Kaoru is going to repeat. Or in other words, I’m going to copy your step.” His Fireball took the vulture to 15 hit points and the last Cacodaemon to 1.
  • The Spellstrike that broke the Gwishin. After opening that fight with plain strikes (“I am going to actually do it the easy way”), he came back in round two with Spellstrike and a melee Ignition and left it “barely still in existence.”
  • He asked the question that opened the vote up: “How much do we have to prepare the village?” Mu-Jin’s whole both-sides breakdown was the answer to it. Kaoru then voted to go, because it would only get worse left alone.
  • Level-up choices made for the party. He dropped Aklo for Halfling the moment he heard it was the one gap in the party’s languages. On Fireball: “I’m going to run fireball through a sword Spellstrike.”
  • The sharp read and the old catchphrase. On whose heart it was: “It was ripped out by an animal, so nine out of ten odds…” On the place itself, “it feels like Ravenloft.” And as the ritual checks went around, “Keep on sucking until you do succeed” was back. His Diplomacy was one of the three secondary checks, from the man who had just said “I’m not that good at running my mouth.”

Kaelen Ashford · the Pilgrim at Tan Sugi

  • This was his pilgrimage. Kaelen came to Willowshore to honor the Sangpotshi faith at the temple up the Pilgrim’s Path, and tonight the party finally went up it. The GM guessed his vote for him (he wants to see the monastery, “because of your religion”), and he confirmed it: “because we don’t know what happened there.” That question stayed with him all night. Over the vulture’s corpse: “But what happened here? That’s my question.”
  • He answered the monster. When the vulture told the party to leave or be eaten, Kaelen was the one who talked back: “Oh, why? Why should we leave?” At the slab, told the dead woman was the grandmaster of his order: “I will put the demon back where it belongs.” And looking at the whole clearing: “I think we gotta exorcise the demon in this area.”
  • Both killing blows. He finished the Dalgyal Gwishin with the first strike of a Flurry after finding he had no focus point left for Inner Upheaval, and he killed the vulture with Inner Upheaval and a hero-pointed strike that turned a miss by 1 into a hit.
  • Generous at the table. “Mu-Jin is really good at knowledge check.” When Mu-Jin proposed running the heart back, “That sounds appropriate.” At the break he said he was having fun.
  • Level 5: Clever Improviser over Incredible Initiative, because he’d rather be able to try everything, plus Stealth. He is now the party’s only expert in Perception.
  • In the dark he couldn’t see at all (the GM: “You’re human”), was frightened for most of it, and still thought to ready a Flurry “if anything come attack me out of nowhere.” “Let’s adventure forth. … Before something eats us.”

Iryoku-Yasha · the One Who Read the Fine Print

  • He was the one vote against, and he made the case properly. “I’m concerned about our ability to continue preparing the town once we open that door. We know there’s some shit on the other side.” He asked the GM whether conditions had been changing week to week (not yet), stated his vote plainly, and took his place in the ritual once the table had decided.
  • He built the ritual plan. He found the minimum-secondaries rule, worked out that Zhen should be primary so Mu-Jin’s Deception wasn’t wasted, and called the DCs.
  • He caught the distinction everyone had blurred: the Wall of Ghosts is not what’s sealing the town. He recognized the monastery’s ground inside the wall, read out the nindoru line at the slab, and was the one to ask whether the heart was Tan Sui Jing’s.
  • The Gwishin’s target. It attacked nobody else. He Shield Blocked a headbutt in round two, explained to the table why it wanted him, and answered with a blast.
  • Level 5 toys, and the line of the level-up. A fire junction that gives enemies near him weakness to his fire, a stance that gives allies resistance, and when the GM said nothing would go wrong in this forest: “I’ll just regrow it, I got wood too.”
  • Loot under fire. On his first turn in the dark, with the forest roaring, he ran to the mound and took what was in it. He also killed the last Cacodaemon and critically succeeded against the vulture’s disease.

Open Threads

  • What did putting the heart back do? A sugi tree grew from Tan Sui Jing’s body, and the research line Iryoku read speaks of someone reincarnating into the towering tree the monastery was built around. Whether the party watched that happen, caused it, or repaired it is open. So is whether the Wall of Ghosts is still there. The party came out near it.
  • The unseen giant fled; it wasn’t destroyed. It howled “in pain and frustration.” Her wound was made by a large clawed hand, and the word under her was Kugaptee.
  • Kugaptee is a nindoru, and a deity you can worship (the GM, in passing). The vulture’s Death Burst is described as a blast of nindoru butterflies.
  • Who was Tan Sui Jing to the monastery? Mu-Jin called her its founding grandmaster. The GM said only that she was a heroine who fought at the site, and the monastery was founded by Zhi Hui in 7020 IC, around an ancient sugi tree.
  • Why could the vulture talk? It kept her heart and had daemons with it. Mu-Jin couldn’t learn what makes it different from an ordinary giant vulture.
  • The whisper at the slab. “Find the seed” is Kaelen’s hearing of it; the GM’s pasted text is lost with the chat.
  • The Survival check everyone critically failed. Something at the slab was trackable and the party doesn’t know what.
  • The monastery itself. The research said the answers are at the Tan Sugi ruins. The party went through the wall and found a forest from before the monastery existed. They haven’t seen the ruins yet.
  • The ritual window: weeks 7, 8, 11 and 12. It is week 7. Iryoku’s concern about leaving the town half-prepared still stands, and the rest of fall downtime is still to play.
  • Is S9’s “ritual that might allow passage through the fog wall” the same ritual? It looks like it was this one, described loosely. If so, there is still no known way through the barrier sealing the town.
  • Carried forward: Candora’s suspicion of You So-Jin; Mama Bao’s grandfather; Yami the bakeneko; the Consecrate ledger (Mu-Jin); who else is an “old soul”; the missing governor; Mugirou’s truce; Shinzo; the wrong-gods lantern secret (S2); “blood will rise”; the Great Willow’s promise and missing friend; the Noppera-Bo Hood, still never used; Weaver’s hunting-spider venom (S7); the lumber camp storeroom’s hidden trap door; Mu-Jin’s Desna adoption.

What Zhen is thinking about

  • Fireball on the first turn works. High initiative, enemies bunched and far away, nobody in melee yet. Kaoru has it too. Two Fireballs before the enemy moved left the vulture on 15 and one Cacodaemon on 1, and Kaelen and Iryoku finished both in the same round.
  • Iryoku’s fire weakness only helps Iryoku. It applies to enemies, and only against his own fire impulses. It does nothing for Fireball. His stance does give allies near him fire resistance 5.
  • Earth’s Bile is 30 feet. The opening Fireball was thrown from much farther back, and the follow-up didn’t reach even after a Stride.
  • Hero points ran out before the roars started. One went on the Survival check at the slab, which failed anyway.
  • The heart said “save me,” and the answer was to put it back. The thing hunting it was never seen.
  • The Wall of Ghosts is not what seals the town. Opening one says nothing about the other.
