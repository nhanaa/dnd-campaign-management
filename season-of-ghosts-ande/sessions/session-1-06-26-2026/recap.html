Session Summary

The five abductees of this year's Reenactment Festival wake at dawn in a forest clearing — but on the east side of Willowshore, not the west where they were carried, and no one has come to ransom them with breakfast. Uncle Choe Chung-hu, the miller who leads the festival, never arrived. As they free themselves from the straw mats and start home down a game trail, two giant centipedes attack — and the insects speak, hissing "meat, flesh, bones" before Iryoku burns one in half and Kaelen flattens the other. Recall Knowledge only deepens the wrong: these are ordinary giant centipedes, yet they have a human's intelligence and were speaking plain Tian. Something is influencing the animals.

The walk home turns the morning into a horror story. Fog rolls in unnaturally fast; an old nursery rhyme surfaces in Kaoru's memory about a lazy lamplighter, a blood moon, and the ghosts that come when the lamp goes dark. At the Spider Gate, the town's beloved stone guardian "Ugly Cute" is simply gone — its lantern broken on the ground, deep furrows showing the horse-sized statue walked off east on its own. At the Eastern Watchtower they find four corpses: two town guards dead of viper venom, and two jinkin (blue-grey gremlin-fey) cut down by the guards' blades. A surviving guard, frightened in her tower, recounts that the jinkin came from the south after midnight with trained serpents. Then night falls early, the moon turns blood-red, and rain of real blood coats everything before reverting to water — the party only shrugs it off thanks to a generous hand of hero points.

After gathering cold iron, ghost charges, and gear from their homes, the party reaches Dawnstep Bridge and finds the worst confirmed: the Eternal Lantern is dark, and a flesh-eating Buso lounges on a throne of stolen furniture, swilling stolen liquor while jinkin caper around it. The party opens with buffs — Mu-Jin's Forbidding Ward and familiar trick, Zhen finally drawing the wrapped nodachi he has carried unsheathed for fifty years — and the fight is over almost before it begins: the jinkin flee, and a crit from Kaelen's Inner Upheaval drops the drunken Buso in the first round. The loot includes the three ancient copper coins whose blessing kept the Lantern lit — blessing now stripped away. The party shelters the night at the lime-warded Matsuki Estate and, by the next foggy dawn, has a plan: tour three shrines, re-bless the coins, and relight the Lantern before whatever has taken downtown comes for the rest of Willowshore.

Timeline

Act I · Mystery · The Wrong Clearing

No Ransom at Dawn

  • The five festival "abductees" wake blindfolded in straw mats in a clearing east of town — the wrong side; they were carried west the night before.
  • The sun is already up and Choe Chung-hu has not come with the ransom breakfast — an abnormality that worries Old Zhen, who has lived here his whole life.
  • They free themselves easily (no check needed). Mu-Jin casts Know the Way to find the game trail south toward Willowshore — silver thread spooling from his fingers as he stitches the spell into the air.
  • Zhen, unbothered: "Well, let's just wait for a bit more. Unless you children are impatient and hungry."
Act II · Combat · The Talking Centipedes

"Meat. Flesh. Bones."

  • Two giant centipedes burst from the trees and attack. As they bite, their hissing forms words: "Meat. Flesh. Bones. Fingers. Hair. Skin. Eyes."
  • Quick, decisive fight (see Combat). Iryoku crit-burned one in half; Kaelen's Flurry of Blows finished the other. Iryoku took 3 damage, Kaoru 1; no one was poisoned.
  • Recall Knowledge (Mu-Jin, Occultism): ordinary giant centipedes — but with +0 INT (average human intelligence) and able to speak Common/Tian, both wildly abnormal for vermin that normally flee daylight.
  • Working theory (Mu-Jin, with Zhen and Kaoru): the animals are possessed or influenced by evil spirits. Zhen, dryly: "I do have a lot of spirits around me, but none of them is evil, so I wouldn't know."
  • Zhen casts Garden of Healing out of combat to top everyone off — apologizing that he forgot his healer's toolkit ("old age").
  • Above-table character introductions on the walk back (see Spotlights): Iryoku the ancient town spirit, Mu-Jin the lifelong local, Kaoru the bookish kitsune, Kaelen the newcomer pilgrim, Zhen the elder-healer with a blade he never draws.
Act III · Exploration · The Road Home Turns Wrong

The Fog and the Rhyme

  • Ten minutes out of the clearing, an unnatural fog floods the trees. Mu-Jin re-casts Know the Way to keep their bearing as they cross the Douyu River onto the Riverside Road into town.
  • A childhood nursery rhyme surfaces in Kaoru's memory: "Lazy Ado took a break and did not light the lamp that day… Hazy mist and moon of blood, all because he just gave up." — implying the Eternal Lantern may have gone out.
  • Detect Magic (Mu-Jin, as an exploration activity) lights up the whole area. Iryoku: "Dude, it's lit up like a Christmas tree." Magic is everywhere.
Act IV · Mystery · The Spider Gate & the Watchtower

Four Bodies and a Missing Guardian

  • At the Spider Gate, the horse-sized stone guardian "Ugly Cute" is missing. Its lantern lies broken on the ground (no oil). No chisel marks, but large furrows show the statue animated and walked east. Kaoru greets it before realizing it's gone: "Hey there, little guy!"
  • At the Eastern Watchtower (~1 mile on): the smell of blood and four corpses — two town guards (dead of poison) and two jinkin (tiny blue-grey gremlin-fey) slain by sword. Two dead vipers nearby.
  • RK (Zhen): the humanoids are jinkin — fey/gremlin, weak to cold iron. A second RK yields nothing more. Medicine (Zhen): the guards died of viper venom; the snakes are ordinary.
  • Theory firms up: Iryoku seeds it — "something is influencing the animals to attack" — and Mu-Jin develops it: jinkin are fey known for enchantment, so they likely beguiled the animals. Possibly the front of a larger invasion.
  • A surviving guard (the table nicknames her "Ha-hire" — unnamed in canon) hails them, frightened. Eight Practices challenge: each PC names one of the town's anti-ghost tenets; collectively they pass.
  • Her account: Choe Chung-hu's crew carried the actors west, then returned without them; after midnight, jinkin came from the south with trained serpents; the other two guards killed them but died to the venom.
  • Comedy beat: Zhen moves to comfort her with a pat on the shoulder — a direct Eight-Practices taboo. The table catches it and rolls it back (the old elder would never). She gives the party flares/oil, a ladder, and a utility item.
Act V · Mystery · Blood Moon

The Rain That Was Real

  • Night falls early (the DM runs time at 1 real-second = 1 in-game-minute). The fog clears into a blood-red moon.
  • Rain of actual blood coats everything for about a minute before reverting to plain water.
  • Everyone makes the Will save — hero points plus the table's "under-10-becomes-10" heroic-reroll rule make it a clean sweep; no one is affected.
  • Zhen confirms by touch that the blood was real, not illusion. The DM hands out a hero point and officially welcomes the table to Season of Ghosts over the soundtrack. (Break.)
Act VI · Combat · The Eternal Lantern

The Buso on the Throne

  • The party loots their homes for gear (cold iron, ghost charges, snares, potions) and reaches Dawnstep Bridge.
  • The Eternal Lantern — an immobile 5-foot stone lamp — is dark. A large Buso (a flesh-eating humanoid) sits on a throne of stolen furniture, drinking from a brewing pot, while jinkin entertain and taunt it. Blood smears mark a recent fight.
  • RK (party): Buso — humanoid, "a simmering desire to consume the flesh of others," AC 18, no weaknesses.
  • Buffs, then the fight (see Combat). The jinkin flee the instant combat starts; only the drunk, sickened Buso fights, and it falls in round one. Kaelen's Inner Upheaval crit lands the kill.
  • Loot from the throne: a kukri + studded leather (Iryoku), a silver scepter, a jewelry case, and — crucially — three ancient copper coins. Detect Magic confirms the coins' blessing is gone; these are the very coins that kept the Eternal Lantern lit. Iryoku's Ignition can't relight the Lantern — the block is magical, not fuel.
  • The quest: carry each copper coin to a shrine and use the Invoke Shrine Blessing activity (Religion / deity-lore check, easy DC) to re-bless it. Success heals 1d8+8 and grants Guidance 1/24h; crit-fail destroys the coin and stupefies you.
Act VII · Social · Refuge & the Plan

The Walls of the Matsuki Estate

  • The party hustles to the Matsuki Estate (lime-warded walls, the town's safest building). Old Matsuki Shou admits them after another Eight Practices challenge. South Willowshore's townsfolk shelter here; others are at the Thrice-Blessed Inn with Mother Otis. Ha-hire's husband is confirmed safe.
  • They rest the night. Reward: +1 reputation with both Northridge and South Bank for clearing Dawnstep Bridge.
  • Next foggy dawn, they recount everything to Old Matsuki and set the day's plan: bless the three coins at three shrines (Abadar next door → a shrine at Mother's Coil → the Kofusachi shrine at the Trade Office, where Granny Hu is), then relight the Lantern.
  • Matsuki warns: light the Lantern before touching downtown (north of the river), which is in shambles and "particularly infested with monsters."

Character Spotlight

Zhen Stillness · the Blade Still Wrapped

  • The session's quiet emotional weight. For most of it Zhen is exactly who the town thinks he is — the gentle, unhurried elder-healer who calls grown adventurers "you children" and frets that the festival organizers were careless.
  • The investigation's backbone. It's Zhen who identifies the jinkin and their cold-iron weakness, reads the guards' viper-venom deaths off a Medicine check, and quietly Garden-of-Healings everyone back to full.
  • The line that is the whole character: to the dead-eyed guard — "I do have a lot of spirits around me, but none of them is evil, so I wouldn't know." Kind, a little uncanny, exactly the man on the sheet.
  • His one near-misstep (going to pat a frightened woman on the shoulder, an Eight-Practices taboo) is corrected in-fiction precisely because Zhen of all people would know better.
  • The beat the whole character is built around: facing the Buso, Zhen draws the wrapped nodachi for the first time — fifty years bound in cloth and prayer paper — strides in on his reach, and lands the blade.
  • Closed the night, OOC, asking the DM to make combat harder.

Iryoku-Yasha · the Town That Walks

  • The deepest root in Willowshore. An ancient Yaksha nature spirit who has "been in the town since its founding," who manifested to protect the people who came to trade with the monks, and who quietly resents the town's slide toward something "larger and more civilized."
  • The party's driest voice: opening the Buso fight with a flat "Well, I say we fight them, guys," calling the detect-magic chaos "lit up like a Christmas tree," and announcing looting as "my favorite part of this game" a half-second after the boss hits the dirt.
  • Carried the centipede fight: Flying Flame, then a weapon-infusion fire blast crit that cut one "like a fire halberd, straight through the body."
  • Seeded the session's working theory — "something is influencing the animals to attack" — which Mu-Jin then developed into the fey-enchantment read.
  • The party Face by build, and the one who quietly handles the bookkeeping (reputation tracking, who can use which martial gear).

Mu-Jin · the One Who Reads the Threads

  • If anyone "ran" this session it was Mu-Jin — the lifelong Willowshorer who actually knows the town, its NPCs, its shrines, and its monsters, and who drove nearly every investigative beat.
  • The session's most striking image, narrating his Spinner of Threads casting: "silver thread comes from his fingers, making a symbol, as if he's stitching the very magic into the air."
  • The knowledge engine: the Occultism RK that cracks the talking-centipede mystery ("even from 50 feet away, I very clearly heard them speaking Tian"), the Detect Magic that exposes the saturated battlefield, and the theory-builder who lands on possession/enchantment by fey.
  • The planner who turns a pile of clues into an actual to-do list ("a tour of the village — a blessing for each coin").
  • Rich RP: a full backstory (elven adventurer father Mubong, late herbalist mother Xion, his spider familiar Weaver, mentors You So-Jin and the doctor Dami) and small grace notes like stroking Weaver's head as he reasons aloud.

Kaoru · the Boy with the Note

  • The quiet one, and the party's soft-hearted heart. His introduction is the session's saddest thread: a kitsune of about 21, grown up in Willowshore making mischief, whose mother disappeared when he was ~15 and father when he was ~17, and who now spends his days in libraries trying to decipher the note his mother left behind.
  • His town-rooted childhood pays off when the fog rolls in and it's Kaoru who dredges up the old local nursery rhyme — "Lazy Ado took a break and did not light the lamp that day…" — the clue that first tells the party the Eternal Lantern may have gone dark.
  • His tenderness shows when he greets the (now-missing) stone guardian like an old friend — "Hey there, little guy!" — a half-beat before the horror of its absence lands.
  • A genre-savvy streak: calling the blindfolded-abductee opening "that one intro to Skyrim," and floating that the centipedes are "genetically modified… or brought back from the dead."
  • The party's cleanest mechanical signature: stepping up to the Buso and calling "Actually, I'm going to Spellstrike."

Kaelen Ashford · the Open Door

  • The outsider who chose to belong. A clean-shaven, bald Human Monk, he came to Willowshore on pilgrimage to honor the Sangpotshi faith at the temple up the Pilgrim's Path — and then, with no real stake in the place, volunteered for the town's festival and threw in with strangers to defend it.
  • That open-handedness is his throughline: when the exhausted party needs somewhere to weather the night, it's Kaelen who offers up his own (tiny) shack without a second thought — "No, it'll be fine."
  • Sneaky-good comic timing, the dry newcomer's-eye view of a town's weird rituals: deadpanning "I didn't realize we were being kidnapped" mid-combat, and answering "whose house do we crash at?" with a wordless "I look at the magus."
  • Carried real weight in both fights despite battling the worst laptop in the room (the good-natured "this laptop is smoking some crack" saga): the Flurry of Blows that finishes the second centipede, and the Inner Upheaval Ki-spell crit that kills the Buso — "First crit of the first one!"

The Morning After

The blood moon is gone. So is the blood. But when the party steps out of the lime-warded walls of the Matsuki Estate at sunrise, the dense fog has rolled back in, thicker than before — and somewhere across Dawnstep Bridge, the Eternal Lantern still stands dark over a town that has always, for its entire history, kept a light burning.

  • Healing economy is comfortable: Garden of Healing out of combat + unrestricted refocus + heal-to-full rests. No reason to hoard Heal spells.
