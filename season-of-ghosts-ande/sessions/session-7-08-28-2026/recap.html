Session Summary

Four PCs, no Kineticist, and a stockade full of faceless things. The party picked up exactly where Session 6 left off — standing over three dead noppera-bo and one live one — and the first thing they did was try to make the prisoner talk. It gave them nothing. “I’m only a lonely guard. What would I know why we attacked Willowshore?” Mu-Jin pressed, threatened, and finally rolled Sense Motive: the thing genuinely did not care whether it lived. “I have nothing to say to people from Willowshore.” Mu-Jin turned to the one person at the table carrying a sword — “Old Zhen, can you take care of it?” Zhen, whose entire life is an argument for restraint, agreed on a condition (“I mean, I can, if you don’t want to”), then pushed it straight back (“you can also do it yourself”), and when Mu-Jin asked for a kneeling, sword-to-the-neck execution because “this is kind of a medieval Japan period,” Zhen refused the theatre: “We’re not in medieval Japan.” Mu-Jin closed it himself — “We kill the guy” — and the GM’s entire narration was four words: “Essentially, he’s dead.” Who actually swung was never established. This was the same prisoner Zhen had knocked out nonlethally in Session 6 specifically so the party could question him.

Then the night became a methodical, building-by-building sack of the camp. Cackling from a western island drew them across the water, where four “heroic adventurers” were beating a kappa half to death and cheerfully invited the party to join in. Zhen tried to talk them off it; Kaoru, who had called it before anyone, watched the disguises drop and said “Told you. Disguise.” The party killed all four, and the battered kappa paid them back with the single most useful thing they learned all night: there is friction between two of the noppera-bo leaders, and their names are Zoudou and Mugirou. The camp is not one enemy. It’s two.

The southern building held two noppera-bo occultists — red-robed, masked, one of them wearing a human face it was still trying to press into place. One of them reached for Kaoru’s face and nearly took it; he burned a hero point on the Fortitude save and made it by exactly one. The other spent the fight slamming the door in the party’s face until Zhen dropped Earth’s Bile through the gap for exactly enough damage to kill it, then ran down the survivor and finished it with a hero-pointed natural 20 through the nodachi. Behind a curtain at the back of that room was a human being at death’s door: Kum Soon-Chong, the man who runs the Willowshore stables — the same stables this party rebuilt for him. Zhen healed him, and he asked for the only thing he wanted. “I know I asked for your help with the stables — but they took my son. Please, can you save them?” The party nearly walked out before hearing the rest, and the rest was the session: the son, Meng-Soon, was taken northwest, to the end of a trail; he is “some sort of old soul”; and the cultists were eager to use him as an offering to something they called the Great Kugapti. Mu-Jin hero-pointed a natural 20 on Willowshore Lore and got back almost nothing — an ancient and long-dead monster, and no more than that.

The rest of the camp came apart in pieces. Three hunting spiders in the storage room went straight for Weaver instead of the party (“Territorial,” said the GM) and put venom in him. Two more occultists were found praying to a misshapen effigy in a room lit by dozens of blood-red candles carved into the shapes of different ancestries, burning without smoke; one tried to take Zhen’s face and failed; Mu-Jin dropped the first with a natural-20 Needle Darts before it ever took a turn, and Zhen closed the second with an Earth’s Bile that also caught Kaelen in the blast. Six more grunts were staging a rat fight in the courtyard over roasting meat and didn’t even look up until the party opened on them. And in an office at the west end, Mu-Jin disturbed something on the floor and woke three elite air wisps, the only creatures all night that fought back properly: they knocked Mu-Jin unconscious on the first enemy turn of the fight — one round after he’d spent his own turn learning they had no weaknesses — then battered Zhen for 17. Kaelen answered with an Inner Upheaval crit for 36 — enough that a second wisp detonated itself trying to shield the first, and still not enough to save it. Mu-Jin, back on his feet with a potion in him and Weaver shaving a point off the thing’s AC, put the last one down himself.

They ended the night looking at a second courtyard to the north with five people in a cage, spaced out — and the GM called it three minutes early, on purpose, “just so Iryoku can take part in it when he comes back next week.” Six fights, one execution, two rescues, and a name nobody can look up.

Act II · Combat · The Island and the Kappa

The Log Pond, West

  • Cackling and taunting voices carried from a small island across a sagging bridge, where logs are floated for storage. Mu-Jin pushed to investigate (“We should investigate that first”); Zhen was ready to skip the whole thing — “Do we even want to encounter them?” — right up until he saw what they were doing to the kappa. “That thing, you gotta save the turtle.” Then he argued the party in, and they swam across.
  • Four figures who read as human guards were beating a kappa. They greeted the party in perfect good cheer: “We’re heroic adventurers, taking out this monstrous threat here. Would you like to join us?”
  • Kaoru saw through it immediately — “It looks like that thing’s suffering from shell shock” … “I question their heroism. By beating on a poor defenseless kappa.” Zhen tried diplomacy; Mu-Jin asked to roll Persuasion and the GM never called for it, going straight to the reveal. Kaoru: “Told you. Disguise.”
  • The kappa’s testimony — the party’s first hard intel on the camp’s internal politics: there is treasure nearby near some floating logs; and “the only thing I’ve heard is that there’s some friction between two of the individuals” — Zoudou and Mugirou.
  • Mu-Jin ran the folklore courtesy that has now worked on kappa twice (cf. S4’s elder kappa): “Greetings, kappa. We are friends with some cousins of yours to the southeast of Willowshore” — and afterwards directed it to those settlements as somewhere safer. It was Zhen’s follow-up question about the camp that pulled the two names loose. Kaelen sent the creature off with “Peace be with you, Brother Kappa.”
  • Party decision: do not fight the front-gate guards, who could raise the alarm. Back around through the secret stockade gap and work the buildings one at a time.
Act III · Mystery · The Man Behind the Curtain

The Southern Building

  • Zhen picked the southern buildings as the more self-contained target and handed Kaelen the door: “You want to take the honor of opening the door, Mr. Kaelen?”
  • Inside: red-robed noppera-bo with masks, one of them with “a human face trying to attach to itself.” They opened with recognition, not surprise: “Heroes. Here to rescue your prisoner, I see.” Zhen: “Yes, we are.” Straight to initiative. Kaoru: “Ain’t nobody got time for that, apparently.”
  • Steal Face on Kaoru. He rolled a 6, spent a hero point, and cleared DC 18 by exactly one — walking away merely Dazzled. Afterwards: “Since they like trying to grab my face — I know I’m cute and all, but damn.”
  • The second occultist spent the fight closing the door on the party, which triggered a table-wide grievance. Zhen solved it by opening the door himself and casting Earth’s Bile through the gap — the GM: “Because it failed, that is literally just enough damage to kill it.” The survivor tried to escape out a back window; Kaelen cut it off, and Zhen finished it with a hero-pointed natural 20.
  • Mu-Jin’s free Recall Knowledge (crit success), a rider off his exploration activity, bought two facts: they are noppera-bo occultists, “essentially the same as the grunts, except this one harnesses their natural magic further to frighten people,” and they have sensitive hearing — a weakness to sonic and anything with the auditory trait.
  • Behind the curtain: a human, near death. The GM: “That’s actually the guy in charge of the stables.” — Kum Soon-Chong, a South Bank man, and the beneficiary of the party’s own downtime work rebuilding the Willowshore Stables. Mu-Jin: “God, I just suck in medicine” — Zhen healed him with focus healing and rolled it out for the whole party.
  • He asked for one thing: “I know I asked for your help with the stables — but they took my son. Please, can you save them?” Kaoru made the call: “Just get out of here. We’ll deal with it. We will see what we can do about his son.”
  • They almost left before the payload. The GM: “I’m guessing you didn’t want to hear what else he had to say.” Zhen re-opened it, and got the session’s real intel: the son, Meng-Soon, was taken to the northwest, at the end of a trail; the son is “some sort of old soul”; and the cultists were eager to use him as an offering to something, or someone, they called a “Great Kugapti.”
  • Mu-Jin hero-pointed Willowshore Lore into a natural 20 — and the GM gated it hard: “Even with the nat 20, you barely recognize the name… it’s that of an ancient and long-dead monster of some sort, but other than that you don’t.”

Working the Camp, Building by Building

  • The spiders ignored the PCs and went for Weaver — the GM’s justification: “Territorial.” Weaver ate a crit and Hunting Spider Venom, and failed the DC 16 Fortitude save even with a hero-point reroll. Zhen: “Weaver, no.” Mu-Jin: “Stop attacking Weaver.”
  • Kaelen’s tech collapsed mid-fight and the GM rolled his turn for him — which still produced two bo staff hits for 16 and the first spider dead. A spider then lunged at Zhen and missed twice: “Not today, Spider. You need ten or a hundred more years if you want to do that.”
  • The candle room. “An elevated floor takes up the majority of this room. Dozens of blood-red candles carved into the shapes of various ancestries are lit here, their wicks burning without smoke.” At the centre of the ring of lights hunkered a misshapen effigy carved from a badly deformed burl. Two more noppera-bo occultists, praying to it, ignored the party entirely until the doors opened.
  • Steal Face on Zhen — Fortitude 25 vs DC 18, clean save, no hero point needed. Kaelen took Parry over a third attack on principle: “Because I don’t want to get my face taken off. Not today.” It cast Phantom Pain at him instead; he made the Will save and took 5.
  • Mu-Jin killed the first occultist with a natural-20 Needle Darts before it ever took a turn; Zhen killed the second with an Earth’s Bile that also caught Kaelen in the burst. Zhen: “Small price to pay. I’ll heal you up, don’t worry.” Kaelen: “Hey, I’m still alive, aren’t I?”
  • Break, and with it the GM’s top-of-the-hour hero points plus a 2nd-rank Guidance innate spell installed on Kaelen’s sheet. Kaelen minded the room and pinged everyone back.
  • On return the party spotted a second courtyard to the north with a cage — and people in it. Mu-Jin’s call: “Let’s try to take out the noppera-bo to the left first, and then we’ll go and rescue the guys to the north.”
  • The courtyard revel. “Sounds of revelry and the scent of cooking meat come from here.” Six noppera-bo grunts around a fire, staging a fight between two caged giant rats. Kaelen set his own threshold: “If they’re just tormenting rats, then cool. If they’re actually harming humans, then we’re going to have to do it.” No humans in that cage. They attacked anyway.
  • Kaoru drew fire in nearly every fight; Zhen noticed out loud — “Why does it always end up Kaoru being the target?” Mu-Jin: “That’s only because Iryoku’s not here.”
Act V · Combat · The Haunted Office

… and the Cage They Didn’t Open

  • Two buildings at the west end. The first was an office. Mu-Jin disturbed something on the floor, and it woke up. Kaelen: “Oh shit — or even worse. That’s a ghost. Fantastic.” Zhen: “A haunted office.”
  • Mu-Jin’s Recall Knowledge: air wisps, air and elemental traits. He asked for weaknesses. “No weaknesses.” Mu-Jin: “Goddammit.” One round later the wisp he had woken knocked him unconscious.
  • The fight’s defining mechanic: an air wisp’s Accord Essence reaction lets it detonate itself, permanently, to give its allies temporary hit points. Kaelen’s Inner Upheaval crit hit hard enough that one wisp blew itself up trying to save another — and the other died anyway.
  • Mu-Jin — dropped in round one — landed the killing blow on the last wisp in round three, with Weaver’s Familiar of Balanced Luck shaving the wisp’s AC to 17 to make the Needle Darts connect.
  • Cleanup: Kaelen force-opened a locked door (Athletics DC 23) on a hero-pointed natural 20 after critically failing the first attempt. Inside: a looted storeroom of empty shelves and open chests. All four Search checks failed DC 18 — and the GM pointed out a hidden trap door between two of the northern shelves regardless. Unexplored.
  • Final loot sweep, then the north courtyard: five captives in a cage, each one spaced apart. The party was mid-plan on how to pull the fight away from them when the GM called it — deliberately, three minutes early, “just so Iryoku can take part in it when he comes back next week.”
  • Closing table talk. Zhen, holding the line on what the party actually knows: “Taking their face seems like just one of the occultist’s abilities. We haven’t seen any of these others do it.” Mu-Jin, from the lore: “The noppera-bo run to two extremes — some are just pranksters who like being jackasses, but some are evil bastards who like inflicting harm and causing terror in mortals.” Zhen: “These are definitely on the latter side. Because they’ve been capturing people — and remember the house? It was like blood stuff.”

Round 1

  • Mu-Jin went first and spent his turn on Recall Knowledge: air wisps, air and elemental traits. He asked for weaknesses. “No weaknesses.” — “Goddammit.”
  • Seconds later the wisp he had woken came straight back at him — the GM: “you’re the one that stepped on it” — put three tendril strikes into Mu-Jin, and knocked him unconscious. “Well, I’m down.” Kaelen: “We got you, buddy.”
  • Both other wisps converged on Zhen — 7 and 5 from one, 5 from the other: 17 in a single round, 41 → 24. “I’m also in trouble.”
  • Zhen answered with a two-action Heal for 12, putting Mu-Jin back on his feet with Wounded 1. Kaoru’s Ignition rolled 18 — exactly the wisp’s AC — and was turned aside by a point of cover.
  • Kaelen closed the round with the best turn of the night: Inner Upheaval (spirit damage, over Zhen’s pitch for force) into Flurry of Blows — a natural 20, critical hit by +15, plus a second hit, for 36 damage on the wisp that had dropped Mu-Jin.
  • And the wisp beside it answered with Accord Essence: it detonated itself, permanently, pushing 11 temporary HP onto both survivors. GM: “We’ve completely gone and… removed [it] from this encounter” — then, “However, even with the temporary hit points, this one will go down.” Two of three wisps gone on one turn, and the sacrifice bought its friend a single round.

Round 2

  • Mu-Jin burned all three actions getting upright: one to pull the Minor Healing Potion off his body, one to drink it (5), one to stand. “I’m going to get that fucking bandolier when I get it back.”
  • The last wisp turned on Kaelen — “You’re the one that killed its friends” — for 5 and 7, then critically missed. Kaelen 47 → 35.
  • Zhen hit for 8 and watched all of it vanish into temporary HP, missed his second swing by 1, and cast Shield. Kaoru hero-pointed a Ray of Frost off a natural 1 and still missed. He finished the fight 0 for 3.
  • Kaelen’s Electric Arc was critically saved against (a 30 against DC 15), but the Flurry got there — a hero-point reroll turned a 6 into a 13, both staff hits landed for 14, and the last 3 points of temporary HP finally broke.

Round 3

  • Mu-Jin sent Weaver in, Familiar of Balanced Luck dropping the wisp’s AC to 17, and put Needle Darts through it for 11. GM: “That is just enough.” The PC who was unconscious in round one landed the killing blow.

Total on the last wisp: 22 HP + 11 temporary = 33, and it took precisely 8 + 14 + 11 = 33 — every point of the dead wisp’s sacrifice spent, and not one wasted.

Character Spotlight

Zhen Stillness · the Hand That Was Asked

  • He was asked to kill the prisoner, and he didn’t refuse. Zhen took this one alive in Session 6 for exactly this conversation, and when the conversation produced nothing, Mu-Jin turned to him. He said yes on a condition (“if you don’t want to”), pushed it back once (“you can also do it yourself”), and refused to make a ritual of it (“We’re not in medieval Japan”). Mu-Jin narrated the kill for the party. Whose hand it was never came up. His own sheet called this exactly: “It would not be a thing he chose. It would be a thing that was asked of him.”
  • He carried the leadership tier. Three of the four noppera-bo occultists died to him: both in the southern building (an Earth’s Bile through a door gap for exactly lethal damage, then a hero-pointed natural 20 crit on the one that ran out a window) and the second in the candle room. The fourth he crit down to 11 and Mu-Jin took. Six kills across the night — tied with Kaelen for the most.
  • He kept talking first. He tried to defuse the island before it was a fight, answered the prisoner’s telepathic hissing with “Shut the hell up. Can we talk?”, and re-opened the conversation with the stable master when the rest of the party was already walking out the door — which is the only reason anyone knows the words “Great Kugapti.”
  • He ran the party’s healing economy end to end: the opening Garden of Healing cycle, focus healing for Kum Soon-Chong, the in-combat Heal that brought Mu-Jin off the floor, and a full 20d4 pulse between every fight. Level 3 made it noticeably better and he noticed: “my healing is even more OP now” — followed immediately by “Can also be useless, depending on how you play.”
  • He caught Kaelen in his own AoE and owned it in one line — “Small price to pay. I’ll heal you up, don’t worry.”
  • Still the rules anchor, and not always right: he tried to rule Kaelen’s Inner Upheaval out as a second Flourish and the GM overruled him (though he was correct about the multiple attack penalty). He worked the cover math with the GM, coached positioning all night, and closed the session by refusing to over-generalize the party’s own intel about Steal Face.

Kaelen Ashford · the One Who Goes First

  • He took point all night, and he kept volunteering for it. Zhen handed him the honor formally (“You want to take the honor of opening the door, Mr. Kaelen?”), and he took it every single time — onto three giant spiders, onto a locked storeroom he critically failed to force and then broke open on a hero-pointed natural 20. After a night in which his microphone died, his Discord had to be restarted and his laptop rebooted itself out of the session, Mu-Jin said “this time you get in front of me” and Kaelen’s answer was “All right, are you guys ready?” — and he went first again.
  • He is the most generous player at the table about loot, and it never gets noticed. Offered the Oil of Potency, his first question was “Okay, does Kaoru need it?”, and he settled it with “Or just put it in the stash until someone needs it.” When Mu-Jin wanted the item off the occultist’s arm: “If it’s useful to you, take it.” When Mu-Jin beat himself up over a Medicine roll, Kaelen caught it: “That’s cool. Hey, don’t we all?”
  • He owns the Session 6 joke before anyone else can make it. Asked to distract an enemy: “Are you sure you want me to dance for him? We know how well that goes.” Three other people made that joke at him this session; this is the one time he made it himself, first, unprompted.
  • Six kills across the night — tied with Zhen for the most at the table — and his best turn effectively deleted two elite creatures at once. Inner Upheaval into Flurry of Blows: a critical hit by +15 plus a second hit, 36 damage — enough that a neighboring air wisp detonated itself to grant temporary hit points, and enough to kill through them anyway.
  • He set his own moral threshold out loud before the courtyard fight — “If they’re just tormenting rats, then cool. If they’re actually harming humans, then we’re going to have to do it” — had it checked with a Recall Knowledge, and held to the answer. He also chose defense over greed on principle, taking Parry instead of a third swing: “Because I don’t want to get my face taken off. Not today.”
  • The wit is his, not just aimed at him: the corpse-hand slapping proposal that got the GM to quietly post his own deity into chat; “At least they won’t shut doors on you” on opening a door onto giant spiders; and finishing a spider that had one hit point left, then being audibly cheated of the moment.
  • He also ran the break: minded the room, counted people down, pinged everyone back. When the GM asked the table how the campaign was going, he was the only player who answered — “It’s really fun.”

Kaoru · the One Who Called It

  • He read the disguise before anyone else and was proven right on the spot. “It looks like that thing’s suffering from shell shock” → “I question their heroism. By beating on a poor defenseless kappa” → and the instant the masks dropped: “Told you. Disguise.” Two sessions running, his instinct about what’s actually in front of the party has been the sharpest at the table.
  • He nearly lost his face and made it by one. Steal Face — a Curse/Polymorph effect with the Incapacitation trait — DC 18 Fortitude, natural 6, hero point, reroll, 19. A plain failure would have cost him his face only until the end of the occultist’s next turn; a critical failure is the one that sticks. His entire comment on the save: “Since they like trying to grab my face — I know I’m cute and all, but damn.”
  • He made the call on the stable master. With the party circling the question of whether to hold the dying man or send him home, Kaoru cut it: “Just get out of here. We’ll deal with it. We will see what we can do about his son.” That decision is what the back half of the session was about. He is also the one who spotted the captive cage first — 95 minutes before the party went to it.
  • Highest crit rate at the table: four critical hits, including the Sky Laughs at Waves flight into a flanking crit and the Spellstrike + Ignition for 20 in the courtyard that had Zhen counting the dice out loud (“Was that maximum damage? Five, five, six.”).
  • He scouted before anyone committed, reading the front gate off the map and asking the question nobody else did — “Or are we spotted?” The committed South-Banker of the party also clocked that the rescued man was South Bank before deciding what to do with him.
  • He was the enemy’s favorite target all night — spear crits on the island, the Steal Face, Phantom Pain, and spear hits in the courtyard — and Mu-Jin named the reason without being asked: “That’s only because Iryoku’s not here.” Kaoru absorbed the aggro the missing tank would have taken and kept swinging.
  • He advocated for other players’ turns, backing Mu-Jin’s ruling that got Kaelen moving during the spider fight, and pushing the party to check the courtyard cage because he’d spotted it first. The S6 callback is his to run now: “Alright, Kaelen. Go punch Ghost.”

Mu-Jin · the One Who Asks the Next Question

  • He ran the interrogation from open to close — Demoralize to shift it off hostile, three rounds of pressing it on motive and leadership, then Sense Motive to establish it truly didn’t care whether it died. When the well was dry he said so plainly and made the call: “I will speak plainly. Either you come up with a reason, or you give us something worth our while, or we’re just going to kill you here.”
  • His folklore-correct courtesy worked on a kappa for the second campaign running — “Greetings, kappa. We are friends with some cousins of yours to the southeast of Willowshore” — and he followed it up unprompted by telling the wounded creature where it could go to be safe. That courtesy is what opened the door; Zhen’s follow-up question is what pulled the Zoudou/Mugirou split out of it.
  • He is the knowledge engine and he keeps asking the right specific question. A critical success free Recall Knowledge on the occultists got him both their nature and their sonic weakness — and he immediately went looking for the Noise Blast he’d taken at level 3, then talked himself out of it on range. His hero-pointed natural 20 on Willowshore Lore is the only reason the party knows “Great Kugapti” is a monster and not a person.
  • He was knocked unconscious by the first air wisp turn of the fight and then killed the last one himself — potion, stand up, Weaver’s Familiar of Balanced Luck to drop its AC by 1, Needle Darts for exactly enough. He also spent the fight buffing rather than hoarding: Nudge Fate on Kaelen twice, and on Zhen.
  • He’s the party’s institutional memory and its conscience about the mission. He gave the session recap, tracked the loot, relayed every promise to the NPCs in character (“Thank you for the information. We’ll rescue your son ASAP”), and closed the night by putting the noppera-bo on a moral spectrum from the lore and placing this camp firmly at the wrong end of it.
  • Best line of the night is still his opener, and it’s now permanent table canon: “So that’s what Kaelen’s like towards his friends. Keep that in mind, everyone.”

What Zhen did this session

  • Opened the day healing the party to full, and did it again between every fight. The Garden of Healing cycle is meaningfully stronger at level 3.
  • Spoke into the bound prisoner’s mind when it would only hiss: Shut the hell up. Can we talk? It would not talk.
  • Agreed to kill the bound prisoner when Mu-Jin asked — conditionally, then pushed it back once. Refused the kneeling-execution ceremony Mu-Jin wanted. The prisoner died. Nobody said whose hand.
  • Tried to talk the four “heroes” off the kappa before it became a fight. It became a fight.
  • Killed three of the four noppera-bo occultists across two buildings. Two crits, one of them a hero-pointed natural 20; one Earth’s Bile through a door gap for exactly lethal damage; one closing burst. The fourth he crit down to 11 and Mu-Jin finished.
  • Solved the door-closing occultist by opening the door himself and casting through the gap.
  • Healed Kum Soon-Chong off death’s door, and was the one who stopped the party walking out before the man finished talking.
  • Won initiative on the courtyard and opened with Earth’s Bile across three, then Electric Arc for the first kill.
  • Took 17 damage in one round from the air wisps — the worst single round he’s had since the myceloid. Healed Mu-Jin up off the floor mid-fight.
  • Caught Kaelen in his own Earth’s Bile and healed it back.
  • Closed the night arguing the party should not assume Steal Face is universal to noppera-bo.

Things Zhen knows that the party does not

  • Six kills tonight — three of them with the nodachi, the rest with Earth’s Bile and Electric Arc. Four of the six were thinking creatures that spoke. It stays unwrapped. The rule his father set — never draw it until you are certain you are ready — has quietly stopped being the operative question.
  • He did not say no. He hedged, he handed it back, he stripped the ritual off it — and a bound, disarmed prisoner he had personally spared six days ago is dead, and he does not get to file that under someone else’s decision. Enkhar Glory would not have needed to be asked at all.
  • The lantern coins were blessed by the wrong gods. Granny Hu still does not know. Nothing has visibly gone wrong yet.

What Zhen is thinking about

  • “Old soul.” The stable master’s word for his son, and apparently the reason the cultists want that boy specifically rather than any of the five in the cage. Zhen is a samsaran. Does “old soul” mean what it sounds like it means — and if it does, is the offering after a person, or after a soul that has been through this before?
  • Zoudou and Mugirou. Two leaders, one of them tokened as the Rebel, and friction between them. A camp with a faction split can be turned. Worth asking the captives who they answer to before killing anything else.
  • Sonic is the lever. The occultists are weak to auditory damage. Mu-Jin has Noise Blast but a 10-ft burst; Kaoru was pointed at Thunderstrike last level. Somebody should be able to deliver it at range before the next occultist.
  • Earth’s Bile keeps catching allies. Twice now. It is the right spell and the burst is small; the fix is calling the target before placing it, not dropping the spell.
  • Dispel Magic has still never been cast. He took it for exactly this kind of enemy. Steal Face is a curse with the polymorph trait — but a plain failure wears off on its own by the end of the caster’s next turn. It is a critical failure that sticks, and that is the case Dispel is for.
  • The party has no Face and no Kineticist wall this week, and Kaoru absorbed every hit that would have gone to Iryoku. It held. It would not have held against something like the myceloid.
