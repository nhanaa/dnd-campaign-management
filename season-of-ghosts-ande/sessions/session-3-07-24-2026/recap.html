Session Summary

The party finished what Session 2 started: clearing monster-held downtown Willowshore and running down the "long-necked man" — Modoku, the invasion's rumored brain — all the way to his den in the Cerulean Teahouse. The morning ran long and comic before it turned grim. A house full of drunk jinkin (the GM went on a nat-20 tear and narrated the fey petting and cursing at their own clubs) nearly cost Kaoru his life at the very first fight, then the party rolled through a slaughterhouse-stable cockroach swarm and a chorus of animated cookware that demanded they join a parade for Willowshore's "new rulers."

A fiat level-up to 2 landed mid-session, and with it a round of build shopping. The teahouse itself was a horror set-piece: a giant frog that disarmed and tongue-grabbed Zhen, a hidden variant scorpion, a will-and-testament promising the deed to whoever recovers two pearls from Fumeiyoshi's eyes, and finally the rokurokubi himself — Mo Douqiu the Hedonist — lounging in a room of flayed and half-digested bodies with a pet weasel.

He pitched the party on how much fun his horror story had been. They declined, at length and with profanity. The bleed he handed out was the only thing all session that actually threatened them, and even that was cleaned up. Zhen cut Mo Douqiu down with the wrapped nodachi; Iryoku vaporized the weasel. With the yokai dead and the caged villagers freed, Willowshore is reclaimed from the monsters — and the session closed on a cutscene: a mysterious figure watching from the outskirts of town.

Timeline

Act I · Combat · The Drunken House

Six Nat-20s and a Near-Death

  • Following the S2 blood-trail, the party opened a mud-walled house to find two captive villagers (Yuli Sanmi, Hu Lelong) guarded by a mob of jinkin.
  • The GM went on an absurd nat-20 tear and leaned into it in fiction: the jinkin were drunk — praising their clubs, petting them, and yelling at them in Common for missing.
  • The jinkin were at effectively +0 to hit, so the only strikes that could land were auto-crits — and three nat-20 club crits all landed on Kaoru, dropping him to 3 HP (Near Death). The scariest moment of the night, at the weakest fight of the night, entirely on crit luck.
  • Kills came fast once the party found its feet: Kaelen's bo-staff dropped one for 29 (a crit plus a second hit — massive-damage instakill), Zhen's Needle Darts (his divine cantrip) dropped another, and Earth's Bile chipped the cluster.
  • Mu-Jin's Soothe pulled Kaoru back from the brink; the villagers were freed and escorted out.
Act II · Combat · The Slaughtered Stables

Have Bonk, Will Travel

  • The stable behind the guardhouse held a dozen slaughtered horses — the party's first hard look at the invaders' cruelty. "What barbaric actions."
  • A cockroach swarm boiled out of the carnage. Kaelen one-shot it — Flurry of Blows, first strike missed (a GM hero point made it hit), second strike crit, 21 after resistance. The entire encounter was one turn.
  • Cleanup and escorts: the party freed caged villagers ("thrown in to repent, or sent to the teahouse") and walked rescued Southbankers back across Dawnstep Bridge, banking reputation.
Act III · Combat · The Cookware Parade

Willowshore's "New Rulers"

  • A racket "like hundreds of pieces of cookware clattering, combined with off-key humming" resolved into three animated objects — cleavers, kettles, chopsticks, and a bloody butcher's apron.
  • One declared itself among Willowshore's new rulers and ordered the party to join the parade. Iryoku's Make an Impression actually landed (hero-pointed to a success) — but the party had no interest, and Kaelen torpedoed it: "No, you ain't. You're too small and short."
  • The engulfing cauldron-thing swallowed Kaelen (failed Reflex + two Fortitude saves, losing air) before the party cracked it open — "I landed on my face… thumbs up, I'm okay."
  • Kills: Iryoku solo-killed one with a crit blast; Kaoru's Phase Bolt dropped the engulfer (freeing Kaelen); Kaelen finished the third after Zhen cracked its construct armor.
  • Loot: a miscatalogued +1 silver dagger, oil of potency, potency crystals, and a Mesmerizing Opal talisman.
Act IV · Roleplay · Level 2 & the Build Table

Mixed XP, Live Retooling

  • The GM applied the mixed-XP catch-up he'd forgotten: everyone hit level 2. "Level two is important for class feats."
  • Kaelen dropped the long-planned Spirit Warrior (Zhen argued it's redundant with Monk/Flurry) for Staff Acrobat + Elemental Fist + Slippery Prey — "I like bonking people to death."
  • Mu-Jin leaned into Familiar Master (Enhanced Familiar → Cantrip Connection, Light, Willowshore Lore) to make Weaver tougher and more survivable.
  • Kaoru kept Shocking Grasp for the spellstrike; Zhen continues on Druid Dedication (Storm).
  • Heavy table-advice throughout, most of it Zhen and Iryoku walking the others through the 2024 Magus update and the finer points of PF2e archetypes.
Act V · Mystery · Into the Cerulean Teahouse

A Frog, a Will, and a Hidden Sting

  • The frog. A giant frog slept in the wreck of the teahouse floor; waking it, the party ate a tongue disarm (Zhen's nodachi knocked from his hand) and a tongue-grab on Zhen, who failed both Escapes and only came loose when the frog died. Mu-Jin's Needle Darts crit for 22 killed it.
  • The will. Teahouse paperwork yielded the Last Will & Testament of Qing Mai-Lai: with no heirs left, the deed passes to whoever recovers the two pearls she threw into Fumeiyoshi's eyes — two ponds north of town locally called the Eyes of Fumeiyoshi (a Tian deity). A standing quest.
  • The scorpion. A hidden variant forest giant scorpion clambered down; Zhen's Seek (25) caught it before it could drop on the party. Zhen's "burst combo" softened it and Kaoru finished it with a Shocking Grasp Spellstrike.
  • The body room. Flayed and partially-digested corpses hung from the beams — the yokai's larder. Mu-Jin recoiled: did something eat and digest them, then spit them back out?
Act VI · Combat · Mo Douqiu the Hedonist

The Rokurokubi's Bad Pitch

  • The long-necked man — Modoku, now named Mo Douqiu the Hedonist — lounged among the bodies with a weasel. He argued the town had been "the most boring place ever" and that he'd done everyone a favor by dropping them into a horror story: "Admit it — haven't you been having fun?"
  • The party refused across the board — Mu-Jin ("an unwanted guest… we ask you to leave"), Iryoku ("Violence."), Zhen ("Get the fuck out"), Kaoru ("you need a new definition of fun"), Kaelen ("you bring chaos… get the hell out") — and rolled initiative.
  • Mo Douqiu fought dirty: a 20-ft elongating neck, Jaws with 1d6 persistent bleed, Demoralize, and "Drop Corpse" — biting the ropes to drop hanging bodies onto the party. The bleed stuck on Iryoku, Zhen, and Kaoru and refused to clear.
  • Mu-Jin's signature play: dismount Weaver, send it in, land Elemental Betrayal (fire weakness) on the rokurokubi and drop its AC via Familiar of Balanced Luck.
  • Zhen cut the boss down — stepping to an off-guard angle and running him through with the wrapped nodachi. Iryoku vaporized the weasel after Kaoru's Foxfire crit softened it.
Act VII · Social · Willowshore Reclaimed

The Dead, and the Living

  • The bodies. Rather than presume to burn them, the party (led by Mu-Jin) chose to cut the corpses down, cover them, and notify the families and Granny Hu / Old Matsuki — "I don't know the customs of this village."
  • The prisoners. A locked back room held more bound, fatigued villagers — freed with Mo Douqiu's keys.
  • With the yokai dead and downtown cleared, the GM called it: "You have successfully reclaimed Willowshore from the monsters."
  • Everyone was healed to full by Zhen's Garden of Healing before the party moved on.

Character Spotlight

Zhen Stillness · the Steady Hand

  • Glue and closer both. In fiction he was the party's conscience — the one who told Mo Douqiu flatly, "We don't want any of this here. Get the fuck out. You have caused enough havoc" — and who insisted on letting the dead villagers' families know rather than presuming to burn the bodies.
  • The table's peacemaker: when Kaoru wanted Granny Hu "bitch-slapped," Zhen just shrugged the youngest off.
  • The Liturgist in one line: after the boss dropped, he begged off healing for a beat — "I do need to refocus… Talk to my spirits a bit — they're asking me how I'm doing."
  • Everywhere mechanically: Earth's Bile persistent-fire chip in every fight, the Garden of Healing that undid the boss's bleed, the Seek that spotted the scorpion, and the killing blow on Mo Douqiu himself.
  • Took a rough one physically — tongue-disarmed and grabbed by the frog, bled by the rokurokubi — and closed the session standing. Also spent the level-up steering Kaelen off a redundant archetype.

Iryoku-Yasha · the Oathbound

  • The party's Face, bookkeeper, and edge. Iryoku kicked off the whole session with a clean recap of S2, then spent the night tracking the party stash (the mystery +1 silver dagger, Kaelen's +1 spear), the reputation math, and everyone's scout bonus.
  • The oath keeps surfacing: he framed even the S2 jinkin as something "my oath demanded we confront."
  • When Mo Douqiu asked what entertainment the party had brought him, Iryoku answered for all of them in one word — "Violence."
  • When he unlocked Timber Sentinel, the delight was pure: "I make trees now. Probably the best class feat in the game — just cast a spell at will."
  • He put the boss fight to bed by overkilling the weasel and solo-killing a cookware object with a crit blast. "Goodbye."

Kaelen Ashford · Have Bonk, Will Travel

  • Played half the night around job applications and laptop lag, near-silent through stretches — and still posted two one-shot kills: the 29-damage jinkin drop and the swarm-ending Flurry crit.
  • His comedy is the load-bearing kind — and he frames it as deliberate, not distraction ("monks are supposed to have some sense of humor"). He told the cookware claiming to rule the town, "No, you ain't. You're too small and short," and stuck to the bit an hour later.
  • He met the weasel with "So we're fighting a damn weasel? … Let the weasel talk. Fuck it — what's its health?"
  • Not only the punchline: when Mo Douqiu tried the "aren't we the same" gambit, Kaelen drew the line cleanly — "We bring what we bring to the table. And you bring chaos with you. Get the hell out."
  • Settled his build this session, committing to Staff Acrobat because bonking is the plan.

Kaoru · the Deadpan Blade

  • Quiet at the table, sharp every time he speaks. Kaoru carries the party's dry wit and its South-Bank grudge: on Granny Hu, "She still needs to be bitch-slapped… watch out — bitch-slapper, with my tail."
  • He reads every room a half-beat ahead of the horror — "It's gonna be hopping mad" at the frog, "It looks like we're late to the party," and "we just jump to the Death Star" at the cliffhanger.
  • The party's quiet lore hand, walking Mu-Jin through external digestion to explain the corpses.
  • Mechanically the Spellstrike specialist — he channels Shocking Grasp (2d12) through the ninjato and used exactly that to end the scorpion in one burst.
  • To Mo Douqiu's "haven't you been having fun?" he had the cleanest reply in the room: "No. I think you need a new definition of fun."

Mu-Jin · the Support Mage Who Came Back

  • Back after missing S2, and glad to run his own game plan. Mu-Jin is unapologetically the support mage — Nudge Fate on Kaoru nearly every round, Familiar of Balanced Luck shaving enemy AC, Soothe when Kaoru cratered.
  • His proudest moment was tactical and he said so: dismount Weaver, send it in, land a Hex, drop the boss's AC — "That's why I have familiars to do this sort of shit. Pet fight." He picked Familiar Master precisely so Weaver can survive being sent out.
  • His best beat was human: faced with the flayed bodies he recoiled in genuine horror, and then insisted on doing it right — cover them, don't presume to burn them, notify the families — since he didn't know the village's customs for the dead and wouldn't presume.
  • He opened the boss scene with diplomacy first: "No entertainment, Yokai. You're an unwanted guest in our village, and we ask you to leave."
  • Killed the giant frog with a Needle Darts crit for 22.

But the teahouse deed hangs on two pearls in a death-god's ponds, a will written by a woman with no heirs, and — as the party catches its breath — a figure none of them can reach yet stands at the edge of Willowshore, watching. What Zhen did this session

  • Ran Earth's Bile as the workhorse in every fight (persistent-fire chip, sustained), swapping to Garden of Healing to clean up between and after.
  • Took a beating in the teahouse: tongue-disarmed and grabbed by the frog (failed both Escapes), bitten and bled by Mo Douqiu. Ended the session standing.
  • Killed Mo Douqiu the Hedonist — stepped to an off-guard angle to shave his AC, then cut him down with the wrapped nodachi. First boss kill of the campaign.
  • Caught the hidden scorpion with a Seek before it could drop on the party; steered Kaelen off Spirit Warrior toward Staff Acrobat.
  • Handled the dead with respect — pushed to notify the families and Granny Hu / Old Matsuki rather than burn the bodies.

Things Zhen knows that the party does not

  • Still carrying the wrong-gods secret from S2: the Eternal Lantern was relit with Abadar / Catephis / Pharasma, not the legendary Calistria / Kofusachi / Shelyn. Granny Hu suspects; Zhen has not confirmed it. Whether the ward holds is unknown.
  • His nodachi was his mother's — an oni's blade, still wrapped, drawn now to kill a yokai in a room full of the yokai's victims. He does not know its history. He does know it cut the boss down cleanly.
  • The persistent bleed was the only thing that hurt the party. Note it: our answer to ongoing damage is thin — flat DC-15 recovery, and we kept failing it. Hero points don't fix a flat check.

What Zhen is thinking about

  • The fights are still easy — even the boss only bit because of bleed, not because we were outmatched. Consider not burning Shield / hero points on one-round fights and holding them for whatever the stranger at the edge of town turns out to be.
  • Fumeiyoshi. A deity of envy and undeath, and now his name is on a dead aristocrat's will and on two ponds outside town. The teahouse deed is a lure; the deity is the thread. Watch it.
  • The stranger at the outskirts. Mo Douqiu called this a horror story he authored. If he was only a chapter, someone else is holding the pen.
